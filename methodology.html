Where our numbers come from

Every dataset on this site, what produced it, when we fetched it, how much of the site it actually covers, and where it falls short. The last of those is the reason this page exists: a number you cannot check should say so, and 7 of our 19 datasets are our own judgement rather than a measurement.

Primary10 datasets

Taken straight from whoever produces or governs the data. If you follow the link you land on the same number.

Climate normals

Primary

SourceWeather-station climate normals for 1991-2020 where a station stands for the city (WMO Climate Normals 1991-2020 via NOAA NCEI; IDEAM, Colombia's met service; Meteostat and its data providers, CC BY 4.0; and, for stations missing from the WMO set, the normals PAGASA, Nepal's DHM, the India Meteorological Department and Meteo-France publish themselves), Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis for 2019-2023 everywhere else

Retrieved5 October 2026

How

Each city takes the nearest station that publishes 1991-2020 normals (mean daily high, mean daily low, monthly precipitation) when it is within 25 km of the city coordinate and within 250 m of the city's elevation; airports and WMO-indexed stations are favoured by 3 km and a national met service file beats Meteostat for the same station. Stations absent from the WMO set are transcribed from the met service's own file (data/climate-national-normals.json, with the file URL per station); four of them have no published 1991-2020 normal, so their own period is used and named on the page: Iloilo 1991-2009 and Tagbilaran (Bohol) 1991-2013 from PAGASA, Kochi's naval air station and Madikeri (Coorg) 1981-2010 from IMD. Values are rounded, never adjusted for elevation or interpolated. Stations are dropped when their file coordinate disagrees with the station register or with the ground elevation, when a country's WMO submission reports extremes rather than daily means (Mongolia, Turkmenistan, Armenia, Oman, Niger), when any month fails a sanity check, and in three reviewed cases (a Meteostat Iloilo record 3-5C too cool against PAGASA's own sheet, Leh, one Nanaimo record). The remaining cities keep the ERA5 grid values from scripts/build_city_climate.cjs. Built by scripts/build_climate_stations.cjs; the source, station, distance and elevation difference for every city are in data/city-climate-source.json.

999 of 999 cities: 644 from a weather station (WMO 413, Meteostat 214, IDEAM 9, other national met services 8), 355 from the ERA5 grid

What this does not cover
Periods are mixed: station cities show the 1991-2020 normal (four show their station's own published period, see method), ERA5 cities the 2019-2023 average, a later and warmer window whose rain rests on five years only. Read the rest

ERA5 is a grid of roughly 25 km cells: against the 644 stations its annual rain was 1.12 times the measured figure at the median and off by a factor of 1.5 or more for 84 cities, mostly in mountains and on wet tropical coasts; its highs ran 0.7C cooler at the median and were more than 2C off for 80. The 355 ERA5 cities carry that margin; 12 of them are flagged in data/city-climate-source.json because the stations around them show the grid too wet or too dry. A station up to 25 km away can sit in a different microclimate (an inland airport for a coastal town has colder nights), and up to 250 m of height difference is about 1.6C.

Climate source per city

Primary

SourceThe station registers and normals files named in the climate entry

Retrieved5 October 2026

How

For every city: whether its climate normals come from a weather station or the ERA5 grid, and for a station its name, identifier (WMO index where it has one), provider, period, distance from the city coordinate and elevation difference, and the file URL for a station transcribed from a national met service. The ERA5 values a station replaced are kept beside it. Written by scripts/build_climate_stations.cjs and quoted under the weather chart of every city guide.

999 of 999 cities: 644 station, 355 ERA5, of which 12 flagged as doubtful against nearby stations

What this does not cover

Distances are measured from the coordinate the site stores for the city, which is a point in the centre, not the edge of the built-up area.

999 of 999 cities

city-airports

Primary

SourceOurAirports

Retrieved4 September 2026

How

Every city is mapped to the airport a traveller would actually fly into, from the airports.csv OurAirports publishes into the public domain. Read the rest

Only airports with an IATA code and scheduled service are eligible: an airfield you cannot buy a ticket to is not an answer. Distance is straight-line from the city coordinate and is discounted by 60km for a large hub and 25km for a medium one, so a hub slightly further out beats a nearer regional strip, capped at 250km. Straight-line distance knows nothing about water or hard borders, so eight cities are set by hand where the nearest airport is unreachable in practice, each with its reason recorded in scripts/build_city_airports.cjs. Cities with nothing in range are recorded as unserved rather than pointed at a guess.

890 of 891 cities, 1 unserved

Cost of living components

Primary

SourceNumbeo cost-of-living index

Retrieved1 October 2026

How

Component prices scraped per city in local currency exactly as Numbeo reports them, converted to USD at apply time. Read the rest

Numbeo is itself crowd-sourced, so it is primary only for the Numbeo index, not for ground truth. Retrieval dates: 2026-08-05 (most cities); 2026-09-29 and 2026-10-01 (86 cities fetched directly from numbeo.com).

What this does not cover

Numbeo sample sizes are small in less-visited cities and its 'last updated' date varies widely per city.

999 of 999 cities

Monthly budget range

Primary

SourceNumbeo cost-of-living components, converted to USD at exchangerate-api.com

Retrieved5 August 2026

How

The low end is a one-bedroom outside the centre plus the one-person no-rent basket; the high end is the same basket with a one-bedroom in the centre. Read the rest

Rent varies, the basket is held constant, because groceries and transport do not change with the neighbourhood and Numbeo does not split them by one anyway. Built by scripts/build_cost_range.cjs, rendered by apply_cost_range.cjs, checked by check_cost_range.cjs.

412 of 999 cities. Three more (Leon, Noumea, Plymouth) are excluded because Numbeo reports rent outside the centre as higher than or equal to rent in it, which would render backwards, and Punta Cana has no rent outside the centre on Numbeo.

What this does not cover
The 583 estimated cities show a single estimate and no range, deliberately. Read the rest

They hold no cost components, so the only way to derive a second end was to read a rent range out of their own page prose. Tested against the 330 cities where the true figures are known, that method missed by 23% at the median and by more than 50% on 72 of 488 endpoints, so it was not used: a range nobody measured would look exactly like one we did. The range is also only as good as Numbeo's idea of where the city ends, and it covers rent and a basket, not flights, visas, insurance or health care.

Service providers by language

Primary

SourceOfficial lists first: embassy and consulate lists of providers by language, statutory registers (Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare medical register, the Styrian doctors' chamber) and city and national government open data (Seoul, Daegu, Korea Health Industry Development Institute); then the provider's own website

Corroborated againstbangkok.diplo.de/th-de/service/aerzteliste-1506722, www.iryou.teikyouseido.mhlw.go.jp, www.aekstmk.or.at/cms/cms.php?pageName=46, data.seoul.go.kr/dataList/OA-12967/F/1/datasetView.do, Each provider's own page on its source, recorded per row as sourceUrl in data/service-languages.json

Retrieved24 September 2026

How

Every row records the exact URL the language claim was read on and an evidence tier: official (a government or embassy list defined by working language), self-declared (the provider's own site), directory (a third-party aggregator only), or visited (we confirmed in person; unused so far). Read the rest

scripts/build_services.cjs refuses to build if any row lacks a sourceUrl, a known evidence tier, a known language or a real city slug. The tiers are printed on every card. scripts/verify_service_links.cjs re-fetches every url and sourceUrl and reports status, redirect target and page title, so a link that rots or starts pointing somewhere else is caught rather than assumed; the 2026-08-12 runs removed one row whose cited article did not contain the claim, dropped two invented domains, corrected a link that redirected to the wrong branch, and later dropped five more rows whose sources turned out to be a dead domain, a 404, an unreachable directory, or a site that never actually states a spoken language. The checker retries three times before calling a link dead, because a single transient failure once reported six healthy URLs as dead. One live site (vetinlisbon.pt) refuses Node's fetch entirely and was confirmed by hand.

70373 providers across 566 of 999 cities, 15 service types and 56 languages. 70064 official, 174 self-declared, 115 directory. Prague 7315, Bucharest 5672, Hamburg 4714, Melbourne 4396, Tokyo 3634, Buenos Aires 3375

What this does not cover
THE EVIDENCE MIX SHIFTED ON 2026-08-12. Read the rest

An owner decision to grow volume via aggregator directories took the directory tier from 39 rows to 105, roughly half the set, so the average row is now weaker than when the dataset was 62 rows. Many directory rows rest on a ROSTER-LEVEL claim ("the clinics we list speak English") rather than a statement about the named business; each such row says so in its note. PRIMARY FOR THE CLAIM, NOT FOR FLUENCY. We source that a provider is stated to work in a language; we have not verified that it does, and no row carries the 'visited' tier. A hospital offering interpretation is counted under the languages it interprets, which is not the same as a doctor speaking them: Chiang Mai Ram and Thonglor Pet are interpreter desks, not multilingual clinicians. The 17 directory-tier rows rest on guides and aggregators that may sell placement, making them the weakest entries. The US Consulate Chiang Mai health-facilities and legal-provider PDFs and the US Embassy Georgia mental-health resource PDF are all image-only or protected and could not be read; retrieving those would upgrade several rows and is the obvious next improvement. A Russian-language directory of Russian-speaking doctors in Georgia (needdoc.ru) is an unexploited language-defined source of the same kind. Coverage is a pilot, not a map of any city's real provision, so absence from this list means nothing. REGISTERS (2026-09-24). Japan's register is the facility's own statutory report; only Tokyo's form asks whether a language is accepted without prior consultation, so Tokyo rows require that yes while the other seven cities publish the listed language unless the facility's own note hedges it (translation device, interpreter, call first, a few words). Facilities listing more than six languages and employer clinics are refused. Korean city lists are compilations by a city or district, not statutory reports, and Seoul's pharmacy list is dated 1 January 2023. The Korea Health Industry Development Institute lists language services a hospital offers, which may be an interpreter rather than the doctor.

internet-speeds

Primary

SourceOokla Speedtest Global Index

Retrieved1 October 2026

How

Median fixed-broadband and mobile download speeds read from each country page of the Global Index (the latest edition per metric, August 2026 for most), plus Ookla's own city medians where it publishes them. Read the rest

Stored in Mbps exactly as published and quoted rounded on the WiFi score tile of each city page that gives a speed. Metrics older than March 2026 or out of line with their own history are left out rather than filled. Extended on 2026-10-01 to every country in cities-data.js that has a Global Index page, under the same rules. Retrieval dates: 2026-09-29 (105 countries); 2026-10-01 (38 countries added).

143 countries: every country in cities-data.js for which Ookla publishes a current figure. 144 Ookla city medians, keyed by city id (Hong Kong from its own SAR market figures). 67 countries and territories in cities-data.js have no entry: 59 have no Global Index page at all, 7 have only a fixed series older than March 2026, and the Maldives has no usable figure (listed in _meta).

What this does not cover
National medians, not city measurements, except where a named Ookla city median is held. Read the rest

Speedtest results are user-initiated tests, so they lean toward people who test their connection. Ookla publishes no current mobile median for 39 of the 143 countries and no current fixed median for 2 (Eswatini, Eritrea). Small or new series (Barbados, Eritrea, Turkmenistan: two editions each) pass the same rules but carry more sampling noise.

Venue ratings and listings

Triangulated

SourceGoogle Maps ratings, mostly via aggregators that republish them, plus OpenStreetMap for location

Corroborated againstwww.google.com/maps, nominatim.openstreetmap.org

Retrieved6 August 2026

How

Venues gated at a 4.0 published rating, then geo-checked against OpenStreetMap within 25km of the city centre. Read the rest

65% resolve to an OSM record, 15% carry a verified official website, 20% rest on the research pass alone. Coworking spaces may instead be admitted on confirmed current operation without a rating. Liveness re-checked 2026-10-01 (data/venue-closures.json): OpenStreetMap via Overpass for 108 cities, Nominatim for every coworking and coliving card in the rest plus a 500-card sample of cafes and stays, an HTTP fetch of every venue website, and operator location pages for chain coworking. A card is removed only on evidence of closure; scripts/check_venue_liveness.cjs keeps removed venues off the pages.

5653 venue cards across 710 cities. 138 pages quote a published rating, which is the subset the 4.0 gate and the OpenStreetMap check apply to; the rest describe a venue without claiming a score

What this does not cover
Most ratings came from aggregators mirroring Google rather than Google directly, because the search and scraper budgets ran out. Read the rest

A handful in the Sendai and Tashkent sets were conservative estimates rather than read values. The 2026-10-01 liveness check looked up 3201 of the 5657 cards directly (an OpenStreetMap or Nominatim lookup, or a fetch of the card's own website). Of all 5657, 1054 matched a live OpenStreetMap record, 1065 more have a live own website, 4 were removed as closed (The Hub by Adeva in Skopje, which closed on 30 September 2026, and three WeWork buildings the operator no longer lists: Barranquilla, Cologne Friesenplatz, Portland Pioneer Place), and 3534 could not be confirmed either way. Unconfirmed is not closed: OpenStreetMap maps few coworking spaces, the public Overpass servers stopped answering after 108 cities, and no web search was available, so most cafe and stay cards outside that sample were checked only through their website. 29 cards with a dead, parked or re-registered website are listed for review in data/venue-closures.json.

Editorial7 datasets

Our judgement, not a measurement. Listed here in full because a number you cannot check should say so.

comfort-index

Editorial

SourceOur comfort model, applied to the climate normals above

How

Each month scores 0.55 x temperature + 0.45 x rain. Read the rest

Temperature is 100 when the mean of the average high and low is 18-25C, minus 6 per degree outside that band and minus 5 per degree the high exceeds 30C, floored at 0. Rain is 100 up to 50mm, minus 0.5 per mm above, floored at -60. The yearly score on /comfort-index is the mean of the 12 monthly scores; a comfortable month scores 80 or more; the best months on every city guide are the three highest. The same function is copied into eight scripts and pages, which must stay identical.

999 of 999 cities

What this does not cover
The comfort band, the penalties and the 55/45 weighting are judgement, not a standard. Read the rest

Humidity, sunshine, wind and sea temperature are not modelled. Rain is penalised harder than cold: a dry month far below freezing keeps 45 points, a very wet month can go below zero. Inputs are station normals for 644 cities and ERA5 grid cells for 355, and the grid cities, mostly in mountains and on wet tropical coasts, carry a wider margin.

The 13 category scores

Editorial

SourceOur own assessment

How

13 category scores per city. Read the rest

Ten are editorial judgement. Three now follow published rules: Visa Ease is one score per country from a written rubric over the entry rules for UK, US and German passports (stay on arrival, border paperwork, routes to stay longer, shared limits such as Schengen), with three documented within-country exceptions (Hong Kong, Sabah, the Aqaba zone), see docs/visa-score-rubric.md; Safety keeps each country's editorial level and orders cities inside Canada, the Nordic countries, Greenland, New Zealand and other checked countries by official police-recorded crime relative to the national rate (StatCan, Statistics Finland, SSB, BRA, Statistics Denmark, NZ Police, Eurostat NUTS3, NPA Japan, ABS, ONS, FBI), see docs/safety-score-method.md. Cost scores of cities whose monthly cost was corrected on 2026-09-29 were moved toward the score sourced cities at the same price carry. WiFi: cities with an Ookla Speedtest Global Index city median carry a score measured from it (fixed and mobile medians, August 2026); other cities are capped at one point above their country's measured score, see docs/wifi-score-method.md.

999 of 999 cities

What this does not cover
Still the most visible number on the site, and ten of thirteen categories remain judgement. Read the rest

Safety country levels are inherited editorial values; a cross-national baseline (UNODC homicide plus Gallup safety-at-night) is drafted in docs/safety-score-cross-national-proposal.md and not applied. The safety adjustment constants (one point per doubling of the crime ratio, the 70/30 violent/property split, the caps) are judgement calls. Visa Ease reflects three reference passports only. Wifi is measured only where Ookla publishes a city median; elsewhere the measurement is a ceiling, not a value. Air quality and English remain editorial.

Monthly budget figure

Editorial

SourceNumbeo for 416 cities, our own estimate for the other 583

How

Where Numbeo data exists the headline figure is reconciled against it; otherwise it is an editorial estimate. Read the rest

On the sourced cities it is rent for a one-bedroom in the city centre plus a one-person basket without rent, which is the TOP of the range, not a typical spend. Those cities show the range in the hero instead, described under Monthly budget range.

416 of 999 sourced, 583 estimated, each labelled on its own page

What this does not cover
The 583 estimated figures carry more uncertainty than the 416 sourced ones. Read the rest

On 2026-09-29, 24 estimates that sat outside their own page's researched range or above every Numbeo city in their country were rebuilt the way the sourced figure is built, a central rent plus a one-person Numbeo basket: rent from official statistics for Hiroshima and Kanazawa (Japan 2023 Housing and Land Survey) and Wanaka (Tenancy Services bond data), otherwise the top of the central rent range researched on the page, and the basket from the nearest Numbeo city. The working is logged per city; they stay labelled as estimates. Each city page now states which of the two it is, in a note under its Cost of Living heading; check_cost_basis.cjs is the gate. The page title and meta description still hedge with "about" rather than naming the basis, because they carry one figure and no room for the caveat. This figure is still the single number every ranking, filter and comparison sorts on, and on nine cities it describes a wider or cheaper area than a reader would live in, so those cities rank cheaper than anyone can actually manage there. Goa is the clearest and is worth stating precisely: Numbeo reports the district honestly, and the inland rents in our own guide match it, but almost every visitor lives in the Anjuna belt where rent alone exceeds the headline. Those nine pages (Amritsar added 2026-10-01) now carry a note saying what the figure covers; the ranking is unchanged, because the only substitute available is editorial rent that tested 23% off at the median.

Tiers are defined in our provenance manifest, which this page is generated from, so the two cannot drift apart. Primary means taken directly from the body that produces or governs the data (a met agency, IANA, a government visa portal, the photographer's licence). Triangulated means no usable primary source, so agreed across >=2 independent secondary sources, each listed in corroboration. Editorial means our own judgement. Not a fact. Must be labelled as opinion wherever it is shown.
