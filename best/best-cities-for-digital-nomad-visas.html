Best Cities for Digital Nomad Visas

Fifteen bases where staying legally on remote income is straightforward, from year-long visa-free entry to formal nomad permits.

See the ranking ↓
Home/Rankings/Best Cities for Digital Nomad Visas

Visa access is the quiet factor that decides whether a city becomes a real base or just a two-week stop. You can find perfect weather, cheap rent, and fast internet, but if the border only grants you thirty days and no clear way to extend, you spend more energy tracking exit dates than doing your job. For a remote worker earning abroad, the legality of your stay shapes everything else: how long you can sign a lease, whether you can open a local bank account, and how relaxed you feel each time you cross a border.

The cities that score highest here solve this in one of a few ways. Some grant unusually long visa-free entry, so you can settle in for months without paperwork. Others run formal digital nomad visa programs built specifically for people working for foreign clients or employers. A third group makes extensions or border logistics simple enough that a longer stay is a formality rather than a gamble. Each approach carries tradeoffs around cost, tax exposure, and how welcome remote income actually is.

Read this ranking as a shortlist of places where the visa question is largely answered, then weigh the rest. A top score means the legal path exists and is well trodden, not that every city suits your budget or lifestyle. Pair the visa access below with the cost figures and category scores, and treat any duration or eligibility detail as something to confirm against current official rules before you book a flight.

Cities are ranked by their visa access score across our 410-city index, with ties broken by overall Nomad Score. Explore the numbers yourself on the comparison tool or browse all 410 city guides.

At a glance

Best all-rounderTbilisi

A year of visa-free entry for many passports plus a strong Nomad Score means you can settle deeply into Tbilisi without a single form or fee to worry about.

 Easiest for AmericansSan Juan

As US territory, San Juan needs no visa or immigration process for Americans at all, so a US remote worker can base here indefinitely with zero border admin.

 Cheapest legal baseKutaisi

At around 1,200 a month with the same generous Georgian visa-free window, Kutaisi lets budget-focused nomads stay long and legal for less than almost anywhere ranked.

What to weigh before you book

A high visa score confirms that a legal path exists, but it says nothing about how that path interacts with your tax situation. A long visa-free stay does not exempt you from becoming a tax resident somewhere, and formal nomad visas sometimes come with local reporting duties. Before you commit to a base, map how many days you can stay, what happens when that window closes, and whether leaving and re-entering resets the clock. The friendliest borders still expect you to respect the rules you were admitted under.

Eligibility also varies enormously by passport, and this ranking cannot account for yours. A stay that is visa-free for one nationality may require a visa on arrival or an embassy application for another. Income thresholds, proof of health insurance, and background checks attach to some programs and not others. Treat every specific here as a prompt to verify current official guidance for your citizenship, since immigration policy shifts faster than any published list can track.

The best visa city is the one whose legal path matches how long you actually want to stay and which passport you hold. If you want a year with no paperwork, the Georgian cities and Yerevan are hard to beat. If you are American and want simplicity above all, San Juan removes the border entirely. Frequently asked questions

What is a digital nomad visa?

It is a residence permit designed for people who work remotely for foreign clients or employers rather than local ones. It typically lets you stay longer than a tourist stamp, often with income or insurance requirements. Not every city here uses one; some rely instead on long visa-free entry or easy extensions, which can be simpler for shorter stays.

Do I need a nomad visa if I can just stay visa-free?

Often not, if the visa-free window covers your planned stay. Georgia's long allowance, for example, removes the need for paperwork for many nomads. A formal visa matters when you want to stay past the free window, open local accounts, or establish residency. Match the tool to your timeline rather than defaulting to the most formal option.

Does a long visa-free stay affect my taxes?

It can. Many countries treat you as a tax resident once you spend enough days there, usually around 183, regardless of your visa type. A generous entry allowance does not shield you from that. Where you owe tax depends on your home country, the local rules, and any treaties. Get professional advice before assuming a long stay is tax-free.

Which of these cities is easiest for US citizens?

San Juan, since Puerto Rico is US territory and requires no visa or immigration process for Americans at all. Beyond that, Mexico's cities offer very easy entry and a long tourist stay for US passport holders, and Georgia's long visa-free allowance applies too. Americans have the widest choice on this list, so budget and lifestyle can drive the decision.

Can I extend my stay without leaving the country?

Sometimes yes, sometimes you must do a border run. Mexico and Cambodia offer in-country extension or residency paths that many nomads use, while other places expect you to exit and re-enter. Rules change and depend on your nationality, so confirm the current process with official sources before your initial stay runs out rather than improvising at the last minute.
