Best Digital Nomad Cities in Turkey

From the Bodrum marina to the stone lanes of Mardin, fifteen places to work from in Turkey, priced from $700 to $1,520 a month.

See the ranking ↓
Home/Rankings/Best Digital Nomad Cities in Turkey

Turkey keeps its own count of your days. Holders of most Western passports can spend 90 days in any 180 here, tallied separately from the Schengen area, so a season on the Turkish coast leaves your European allowance untouched. Staying longer as a remote worker now runs through the Digital Nomad Visa, which comes in two steps. The first is a Digital Nomad Identification Certificate, granted through the official GoTürkiye platform to university graduates aged 21 to 55 who hold the passport of a listed country. The income test is $36,000 a year, or $3,000 a month, earned from a company outside Turkey, and with the certificate in hand you apply for the visa itself at a consulate. The old fallback of a tourist residence permit has mostly closed, since migration offices have turned down the majority of first-time tourism applications since 2022. Compare it with other countries' nomad visas.

The list leans hard towards the southwest coast. Four resort towns between Bodrum and Alanya sit level at the top, each offering sea, sun and rents well below Spain or Greece, and each weaker on coworking than a city would be. Istanbul is the one true metropolis, with the deepest food, culture and workspace choice in the country, held back by its price and its city air. Inland, Cappadocia, Safranbolu and Mardin swap the beach for stone architecture and history, and Amasra brings in the much wetter Black Sea.

Two practical facts apply everywhere. The lira loses value fast, so prices in local currency climb through the year and a budget holds its shape better in dollars. And Turkish networks cut off an unregistered foreign handset after 120 days; registering it means paying a steep tax, so long-stayers usually just buy an inexpensive Turkish phone.

This page takes every Turkish city in our index and orders it by overall Nomad Score, the composite of the 13 categories we rate each city on, among them cost, climate, internet, safety, food and visa access. Bodrum, Fethiye, Alanya and Marmaris share the same overall value, so the order among those four says little. Monthly costs for Bodrum, Fethiye, Antalya, Istanbul and Izmir are built from Numbeo data; the other ten are our own estimates. At a glance

Best town to settle inFethiye

Three coworking spaces, a walkable centre and the Lycian Way in the hills behind, for $1,250 a month. The largest space, Hipokampus, charges about $62 a month for a hot desk.

 Best big cityIstanbul

The widest coworking choice in Turkey, food from balık ekmek to fine dining, and a ferry between continents as a commute. It is the priciest base here at $1,520 a month.

 Best winter baseAlanya

January highs of 17C and a large foreign population that stays all year keep the town open when other resorts close, at about $950 a month.

What to weigh before you book

Season matters more than rank on this coast. Bodrum, Fethiye, Antalya and Marmaris all reach July highs of 35C and fill with package holidaymakers, while from November many cafes, dive shops and boat operators close; Alanya and Marmaris keep going through winter because thousands of northern Europeans overwinter there. Real coworking exists only in Istanbul, Izmir, Antalya, Fethiye and Bodrum, so anywhere else means a guesthouse terrace and a Turkish SIM as backup. The visa picture is the other filter. The Digital Nomad Visa excludes anyone outside the age band, without a degree or from a country not on the list, and the residence permit is harder than it was: Turkey closes neighbourhoods to new foreign registrations once foreigners pass a set share, which has hit Istanbul districts such as Fatih and Esenyurt as well as resort areas.     Bodrum

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.6$1,050/mo
    Safety 7WiFi 5Value 7

    Turkey's glamour coast centres on this marina town under the Castle of St. Peter, where the year revolves around boats, beach clubs and ferries across to the Greek islands. Serious work happens at CoBodrum, whose month runs about $210 before VAT, because cafe connections sag when the summer crowd shares a router. The baseline is $1,050 a month, though short lets climb well past that in July and August, when the town is hot, packed and loud. Winter brings the cheapest rents and shuttered cafes, and beyond the centre you need a dolmus or a scooter to reach anything.

    Fethiye

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.6$1,250/mo

    Of all the coastal towns, Fethiye gives a remote worker the most to work with: three coworking spaces, led by Hipokampus by the sea with its silent zones and garden. The bazaar, the fish market and the marina are a short walk from most flats, dolmuses run out to Ölüdeniz and Çalış beach, and the Lycian Way starts in the hills. Living here comes to $1,250 a month. The catch is company, because the nomad crowd is small and English fades quickly away from tourist venues, and Dalaman airport lies about 45 km off with no train.

    Alanya

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.6$950/mo
    Safety 7WiFi 6Value 7

    Winter is Alanya's quiet strength. With January highs of 17C, sun more days than not and a big foreign population that never leaves, restaurants and services stay open while other resorts board up. That community has built a rental market run in English, Russian and German, and cafes in Oba and Cikcilli are used to laptops. Roughly $950 covers a month. The foreigners are mostly retirees and property buyers rather than remote workers, so the nomad scene is thin, summer humidity is hard going, and some neighbourhoods are closed to new residence registrations.

    Marmaris

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.6$950/mo
    Safety 7WiFi 6Value 7

    An hour's catamaran ride from Rhodes, this pine-backed bay puts an EU border within a morning, which is rare on the Turkish coast. Several thousand northern Europeans spend the winter, so internet, pharmacies and English-speaking accountants keep working in January, and the cafes that stay open are used to someone sitting for four hours, all for about $950 a month. Formal coworking is small and seasonal, Bar Street stays loud until very late in season, and from June the same cafes fill with holidaymakers and stop being any use for work.

    Antalya

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.4$1,220/mo
    Safety 7WiFi 6Value 7

    Big-city services with a beach attached: Antalya has fibre from Turk Telekom and TurkNet, an airport 13 km from the centre with direct flights across Europe, and Coworking Antalya under the Kaleiçi walls, which runs weekly events for the local nomad crowd. Many long-stayers settle along the pebble beach at Konyaaltı, on one bus line into the old town. A month here runs to $1,220. Away from the tourist zones English is limited, July highs reach 35C, and landlords favour short summer lets, so a long lease is hard to land in season.

    Istanbul

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.2$1,520/mo
    Safety 5WiFi 6Value 6

    No other place on this list comes close for food and culture: Byzantine and Ottoman monuments, two continents joined by a 20-minute ferry, and the cafes of Kadıköy, where a laptop can stay all afternoon. Kolektif House and Atölye at Bomontiada lead the deepest coworking choice in the country, with monthly memberships from about $63. The price of all that is the dearest month on this list, $1,520, plus severe traffic, poor winter air, a VPN for some calling services, and districts closed to new residence applications.

    Kaş

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.2$800/mo
    Safety 8WiFi 6Value 7

    Divers and sea kayakers pick Kaş for what lies offshore: the sunken ruins at Kekova, clear water, and dive shops along a whitewashed harbour beneath a Lycian rock tomb. Daily life costs around $800 a month, more in July and August when short-let demand spikes. With no coworking space, work means harbour cafes and your own flat, on wifi that copes with calls but wobbles when summer multiplies the town. The streets are steep, plenty of businesses close for winter, and the nearest big hospital is in Antalya, three hours or more by road.

    Cappadocia

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 7.1$1,300/mo
    Safety 8WiFi 5Value 8

    Expect to build your own office here. Cappadocia has no coworking hub, thick tufa walls swallow wifi inside cave rooms, and the setup that works is a guesthouse terrace before 11am with the afternoon left for the valleys. In return come dawn balloon flights, underground cities, horse riding and hiking from the door, in a region with very little crime. Allow $1,300 a month, more if a cave hotel is the plan. Dolmuses stop in the early evening, so a scooter or car soon becomes essential, and January brings highs of 5C and snow.

    Göreme

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 6.9$900/mo
    Safety 8WiFi 6Value 7

    Think of Göreme as the village at the centre of the Cappadocia postcard: fairy chimneys at the edge of town, trails into the Pigeon, Rose and Red valleys straight from the streets, and balloons over the terraces on most clear mornings. Nearly every cave house has become a hotel and almost all of its roughly two thousand residents work in tourism, which makes a long-lease address hard to find; Ürgüp and Nevşehir have more. It comes in at $900 a month. At about 1,100 metres, nights turn cold, and there is no coworking and little nightlife.

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 6.9$900/mo

    The lagoon is the reason to come: a protected Blue Lagoon where building is banned, a Blue Flag beach, and tandem paragliders dropping from Babadağ onto the sand. Working here is a hotel-wifi affair, with no coworking desk in the village and solid 4G across the bay. Count on $900 a month, less on an off-season lease in Hisarönü or Ovacık, where landlords cut rates once the crowds leave. Most of the resort shuts for winter and the nomad scene is close to nil, and the answer to both is Fethiye, 20 minutes away by dolmus.

    If Istanbul sounds like too much, Izmir is the gentler big city: liberal, seafront, with a Kordon promenade cooled by the summer meltem wind.     Nomad Score 6.3$800/mo

    Treat Pamukkale as a place for a few slow weeks rather than a full-time office. You wake beneath the white travertine terraces, walk into the ruins of Hierapolis before breakfast, and eat very well for little, all on about $800 a month. Village wifi is basic and sometimes unreliable, there is no coworking, and English drops away outside the tourist trade. Denizli, 20 minutes by dolmus, has the faster cafes, the hospitals and the coach station, and some longer-stayers live there and ride out to the terraces instead.

    Several hundred timber-framed Ottoman houses, still lived in by families rather than turned into hotels, are why this hill town is on the UNESCO list, and they make an unusually atmospheric month for $700.     Nomad Score 6.1$700/mo

    Black Sea weather sets the terms in Amasra. The season runs from June to September, rain falls in every month, with even July averaging 61 mm, and in winter much of the town simply closes.     Mardin

    Turkey
    Nomad Score 6$700/mo
    Safety 7WiFi 6Value 8

    Honey-coloured limestone houses step down a hill above the Mesopotamian plain, and Kurdish, Arab, Syriac and Turkish heritage runs through everything from the Artuqid madrasas to the food. It is one of the cheapest bases on this list at $700 a month. Work happens on hotel terraces and in bazaar-street cafes, with a Turkcell or Vodafone SIM as backup, because there is no coworking and connections drop during outages. July highs reach 36C, English is limited, and the UK Foreign Office advises against travel within 10 km of the Syrian border, a zone that takes in Nusaybin but not the old town.

A good first stay in Turkey pairs a shoulder-season month on the coast, May and June or September and October, with time in Istanbul or Izmir, where the coworking and services live. Fethiye is the easiest coastal town to settle into, with three workspaces and Dalaman airport within reach, and its city guide covers neighbourhoods and rents in detail. Turkey stays three hours ahead of GMT all year, so your overlap with Europe shifts twice a year when European clocks change, and a standing call can drift by an hour without anyone noticing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I stay in Turkey longer than 90 days as a remote worker?

Yes, through the Digital Nomad Visa. Step one is an online application on the official GoTürkiye platform for what Turkey calls a Digital Nomad Identification Certificate. It requires a university degree, citizenship of a listed country and an age between 21 and 55. The income test is $3,000 a month or $36,000 a year, paid by a company outside Turkey. With that certificate, you lodge the visa application at a Turkish consulate. The tourist residence permit, once the usual route, is now refused in most first-time cases.

Do days in Turkey count towards the Schengen 90 days?

No. Turkey runs its own allowance of 90 days in any 180, completely separate from Schengen, so time here leaves your European days untouched. The reverse also holds: a trip from Bodrum or Marmaris to a Greek island spends Schengen days and does nothing to reset the Turkish count, because both windows are rolling.

Which Turkish city is best for coworking?

Istanbul, by a wide margin, with Kolektif House in Levent and near Galata and the Atölye creative hub at Bomontiada, from about $63 a month. Izmir has Withco in Konak with 24/7 access, Antalya has Coworking Antalya in Kaleiçi, and Fethiye has three small spaces. Most of the other towns on this list have no coworking at all.

What is the cheapest base in Turkey on this list?

Safranbolu, Amasra and Mardin each come to about $700 a month, but all three are small towns with no coworking and little English. On the coast, Kaş is the cheapest at around $800, rising sharply in July and August. Istanbul is the most expensive at $1,520.

How is this ranking calculated?

Every Turkish city in our index is ordered by its overall Nomad Score, a composite of the 13 categories we rate, from cost and climate to internet, safety and visa access. Bodrum, Fethiye, Alanya and Marmaris share the top value, so their order among themselves means little. Costs for five cities come from Numbeo data and the rest are our own estimates, which every city page labels.
