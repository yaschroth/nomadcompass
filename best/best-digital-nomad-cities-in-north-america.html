Best Digital Nomad Cities in North America & the Caribbean

From pricey coastal US hubs to Mexican value cities and Caribbean island bases, all on American time zones.

See the ranking ↓
Home/Rankings/Best Digital Nomad Cities in North America & the Caribbean

North America and the Caribbean split neatly into two halves. The United States and Canada give you native English, fast and reliable wifi, and strong infrastructure in cities like San Diego, Vancouver and Toronto, but you pay for it: budgets here run $3,500 to $5,000 a month, and long-term visas are hard for non-citizens.

The southern half is where the value lives. Mexican cities such as Cancún, Mexico City, Mazatlán and Oaxaca run $1,400 to $2,200 a month, welcome you on a generous entry stamp of up to 180 days, and pile on beaches, colonial streets and some of the best food on the continent. San Juan adds a Caribbean island base with US convenience and no separate visa for Americans.

Every city on this list sits in a time zone that overlaps the Americas, so calls with US and Canadian clients stay easy. The tradeoff between the two halves is simple: pay more for polish and English up north, or stretch your money and tolerate patchier wifi and area-by-area safety further south.

What to weigh before you book

Budget is the clearest divide on this list. The US and Canadian cities cluster between $3,500 and $5,000 a month, with cost scores of 2 or 3, so Los Angeles and Miami at $5,000 sit at the top end. The Mexican and Caribbean cities run far cheaper: Mazatlán at $1,400, Oaxaca at $1,600 and Cancún, Mexico City and San Miguel de Allende all around $2,200. If money is the deciding factor, the southern half wins easily.

Visas and safety pull the other way. US and Canadian long-term stays are hard for non-citizens, which is why cities like Austin and Miami score just 4 on visa; Mexico by contrast hands most visitors up to 180 days on arrival, and Puerto Rico needs no separate visa for Americans. Safety, though, varies by neighborhood rather than by country: Mexico City and San Juan both score 4 on safety, so choosing the right area matters more than the headline number.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best digital nomad city in North America and the Caribbean?

By overall Nomad Score, San Diego tops the list at 8.9, thanks to its 9/10 climate, fast 9/10 wifi and flawless English. Cancún ties it at 8.9 for a fraction of the cost. The best pick depends on your budget: San Diego runs about $4,500 a month, while Cancún delivers a similar score at roughly $2,200.

Which cities are cheapest for digital nomads in this region?

The value end is firmly in Mexico and the Caribbean. Mazatlán is the cheapest base on the list at about $1,400 a month, followed by Oaxaca at $1,600, with Cancún, Mexico City and San Miguel de Allende all around $2,200. By comparison, US and Canadian cities run $3,500 to $5,000, so the southern half stretches a remote income much further.

Is Mexico part of this list?

Yes. This region covers the United States, Canada, Mexico and Caribbean islands including Puerto Rico. Mexican cities feature heavily, with Cancún, San Miguel de Allende, Mexico City, Mazatlán and Oaxaca all ranked, and San Juan in Puerto Rico adds a Caribbean base. They form the affordable, warm counterweight to the pricier US and Canadian cities.

How hard are visas for digital nomads here?

It depends on the half. Long-term stays in the US and Canada are difficult for non-citizens, which is why cities like Austin and Miami score just 4 on visa. Mexico is far easier, granting most visitors up to 180 days on arrival, and Puerto Rico requires no separate visa for US citizens. If flexible entry matters, the Mexican and Caribbean cities are the simpler route.

How are these cities ranked?

Cities are ranked by overall Nomad Score among the destinations we rate across North America and the Caribbean. The score blends thirteen factors including cost, wifi, safety, climate, food, community and visa access, so the ranking reflects the all-round experience rather than any single metric.
