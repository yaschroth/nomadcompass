Best Digital Nomad Cities in North America & the Caribbean

From pricey coastal US hubs to Mexican value cities and Caribbean island bases, all on American time zones.

See the ranking ↓
Home/Rankings/Best Digital Nomad Cities in North America & the Caribbean

North America and the Caribbean split neatly into two halves. The United States and Canada give you native English, fast and reliable wifi, and strong infrastructure in cities like San Diego, Vancouver and Calgary, but you pay for it: budgets here run $2,250 to $5,200 a month, and long-term visas are hard for non-citizens.

The southern half is where the value lives. Mexican cities such as Cancún, Mexico City and San Miguel de Allende run $1,340 to $1,680 a month, welcome you on a generous entry stamp of up to 180 days, and pile on beaches, colonial streets and some of the best food on the continent. San Juan adds a Caribbean island base with US convenience and no separate visa for Americans.

Every city on this list sits in a time zone that overlaps the Americas, so calls with US and Canadian clients stay easy. The tradeoff between the two halves is simple: pay more for polish and English up north, or stretch your money and tolerate patchier wifi and area-by-area safety further south.

Cities are ranked by overall Nomad Score among the destinations we rate across North America and the Caribbean. Explore the numbers yourself on the comparison tool or browse all 410 city guides.

What to weigh before you book

Budget is the clearest divide on this list. The US and Canadian cities cluster between $2,250 and $5,200 a month, with low cost scores throughout, so San Francisco at $5,200 and Honolulu at $4,600 sit at the top end. The Mexican and Caribbean cities run far cheaper: Mazatlán at $1,400, Oaxaca at $1,600 and Cancún, Mexico City and San Miguel de Allende all around $2,200. If money is the deciding factor, the southern half wins easily.

Visas and safety pull the other way. US and Canadian long-term stays are hard for non-citizens, which is why cities like Austin and Miami score just 4 on visa; Mexico by contrast hands most visitors up to 180 days on arrival, and Puerto Rico needs no separate visa for Americans. Safety, though, varies by neighborhood rather than by country: Mexico City and San Juan both score 4 on safety, so choosing the right area matters more than the headline number.

The right base here comes down to which half of the map fits your budget and your paperwork. If you have the funds and want native English, fast wifi and top infrastructure, the US and Canadian cities from Calgary to San Francisco deliver polish, at $2,250 to $5,200 a month. If you would rather stretch your money and lean on Mexico's easy 180-day entry, Cancún, Mexico City, Mazatlán and Oaxaca offer warmth, food and beaches for a fraction of the cost.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best digital nomad city in North America and the Caribbean?

By overall Nomad Score, San Diego tops the list at 8.9, thanks to its 9/10 climate, fast 9/10 wifi and flawless English. Cancún ties it at 8.9 for a fraction of the cost. The best pick depends on your budget: San Diego runs about $4,400 a month, while Cancún delivers a similar score at roughly $1,340.

Which cities are cheapest for digital nomads in this region?

The value end is firmly in Mexico and the Caribbean. Montego Bay is the cheapest base on the list at about $1,300 a month, followed by Cancún at $1,340, with San Miguel de Allende and Mexico City both under $1,700. By comparison, US and Canadian cities run $3,500 to $5,000, so the southern half stretches a remote income much further.

Is Mexico part of this list?

Yes. This region covers the United States, Canada, Mexico and Caribbean islands including Puerto Rico. Mexican cities feature heavily, with Cancún, San Miguel de Allende, Mexico City, Mazatlán and Oaxaca all ranked, and San Juan in Puerto Rico adds a Caribbean base. They form the affordable, warm counterweight to the pricier US and Canadian cities.

How hard are visas for digital nomads here?

It depends on the half. Long-term stays in the US and Canada are difficult for non-citizens, which is why cities like Austin and Miami score just 4 on visa. Mexico is far easier, granting most visitors up to 180 days on arrival, and Puerto Rico requires no separate visa for US citizens. If flexible entry matters, the Mexican and Caribbean cities are the simpler route.

How are these cities ranked?

Cities are ranked by overall Nomad Score among the destinations we rate across North America and the Caribbean. The score blends thirteen factors including cost, wifi, safety, climate, food, community and visa access, so the ranking reflects the all-round experience rather than any single metric.
