The Nomad Comfort Index

How comfortable is a city's climate across the whole year? Every one of our 999 cities, scored month by month on temperature and rain from five years of climate records, then ranked.

Photo: Diego Delso / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Each city gets a comfort score from 0 to 100 for every month, and its yearly score is the average of the twelve. Las Palmas, Huanchaco and Trujillo top the list; the median city scores 73. The strip beside each city shows where its comfortable months fall, so a city with a perfect summer and a hard winter is easy to tell from one that is merely pleasant all year.

How the comfort score works

Every month of every city gets a score out of 100, made of two parts.

Temperature, 55% of the month. Take the average of the month's typical daytime high and night-time low. Anything from 18 to 25°C scores the full 100. Every degree outside that band costs 6 points, and every degree the daytime high climbs past 30°C costs another 5, so a city that is mild on average but scorching in the afternoon still pays for the heat. This part never drops below zero.

Rain, 45% of the month. Up to 50 mm of rain in the month is free. Every millimetre beyond that costs half a point, and this part can fall as far as minus 60, so a monsoon month drags the whole month down however warm it is.

Yearly score. The plain average of the twelve monthly scores. A comfortable month is one that scores 80 or more, the same line our best weather by month finder draws for ideal weather. In a dry month that rules out anything averaging below about 12°C, and most months with afternoon highs in the mid-30s.

Three months, worked through. Lisbon in July: highs of 28°C, lows of 18°C, a mean of 23°C and 3 mm of rain. Temperature scores 100, rain scores 100, and the month comes out at 100, about as good as a month gets. Chiang Mai in August: highs of 32°C, lows of 24°C, a mean of 28°C and 234 mm of rain. Temperature scores 72, rain scores 8, and the month comes out at 43, because the rain wipes out a pleasant temperature. Bergen in January: highs of 5°C, lows of 1°C, a mean of 3°C and 256 mm of rain. Temperature scores 10, rain scores -3, and the month comes out at 4: the cold wipes out the temperature half and only the rain half is left.

The 18 to 25°C band, the penalties and the 55/45 split are our judgement about what makes a month easy to live and work in. They are not an international standard, which is why every number is written out here. The same model picks the best months to visit on every city guide, so this index and the guides cannot disagree about a city.

What the index cannot tell you

  • It describes climate, not weather. For 636 cities the figures are the 1991-2020 normals of a weather station near the city, the 30-year averages national weather services publish; for the other 363 they are averages of five years, 2019 to 2023. Either way they describe a typical January, not the one you will get.
  • Humidity, sunshine and wind are not in it. Lima scores 99 because it is mild and almost rainless, yet the model cannot see the low grey cloud that sits over the coast for much of the southern winter. Equally, 30°C feels very different in dry Andalusia than on a humid tropical coast.
  • It is harder on rain than on cold. A month far below freezing but dry keeps 45 points from its rain half, while a very wet month can fall below zero. 3 cities have at least one month that does. That is a choice, made on the view that rain disrupts a working day more than cold does. If cold is what you cannot stand, read the strip rather than the yearly number.
  • Cities without a nearby station carry a margin. A city takes a station's numbers only when the station is within 25 km and 250 m of height. For the other 363 the data comes from ERA5, a reanalysis that averages the weather over grid cells about 25 km wide: on a coast a cell can take in open sea, in the mountains slopes far above or below the town. 20 of them sit above 2,000 m, and those deserve the most caution. Manizales shows the size of the error: its grid cell put 6,120 mm of rain a year on it and a comfort score of 18, while the Aeropuerto La Nubia station measures 1,612 mm, and it scores 78.

Frequently asked questions

What is a comfort index?

A comfort index turns a city's climate into one number for how pleasant it is to live in. This one scores every month from 0 to 100 on temperature and rain, then averages the twelve months into a yearly score, so a city that is mild and dry all year scores near 100 and one with a long monsoon or a hard winter scores far lower.

How is the yearly comfort score calculated?

Each month scores 0 to 100: 55% for temperature, where a day-night average of 18 to 25°C gets full marks, and 45% for rain, where up to 50 mm a month is free. The yearly score is the plain average of the twelve months. We also count the comfortable months, those scoring 80 or more.

Which cities have the most comfortable weather year round?

Las Palmas (100), Huanchaco (100), Trujillo (100), Tenerife (99), Fuerteventura (99) and Lima (99) lead the index. 47 cities stay comfortable in all twelve months, most of them on dry coasts cooled by the ocean, such as the Canary Islands, the Peruvian coast and Morocco's Atlantic shore, or on high plateaus near the tropics, such as central Mexico and East Africa.

What are the best places to live for weather?

By yearly comfort, the leader in each region is Las Palmas in Europe (100); Mysore in Asia (88); Huanchaco in Latin America (100); Mindelo in Africa (98); Abha in the Middle East (96); San Diego in North America (96); Adelaide in Oceania (91). Filter the table by region to see the rest, then weigh humidity and sunshine, which the index does not measure.

Why does a cold, dry city outscore a hot, rainy one?

The temperature half of a month's score stops at zero, but the rain half keeps falling to minus 60. A frozen month with no rain keeps 45 points; a monsoon month can drop below zero. We built it that way on the view that daily rain disrupts a working day more than cold does. If you hate cold more than rain, read the 12-month strip rather than the yearly score.

Is this the same as the BestPlaces comfort index?

No. BestPlaces publishes its own comfort index for places in the United States, with its own method. This index covers 999 cities worldwide, uses 1991-2020 weather-station normals for 636 cities and Open-Meteo's ERA5 grid for 2019 to 2023 for the other 363, and its whole formula is written out on this page, so any score can be recomputed by hand.
