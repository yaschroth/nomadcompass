# How we score Visa Ease

Visa Ease answers one question: how easy is it for a remote worker with a Western passport to arrive, stay a while, and stay longer legally? It is scored from 0 to 10 from published entry rules, not from impressions.

## One country, one score

A visa is a national rule, so every city in a country gets the same Visa Ease score. A city is scored differently only when the rules really differ there, for example Hong Kong inside China or Sabah inside Malaysia. Each of those exceptions is listed below with the rule and its source.

## Whose passport

We score three reference passports: the United Kingdom, the United States and Germany (standing in for the EU). The country's score is the average of the three, rounded to the nearest whole number (halves round up).

A passport is left out where its holder is not a visitor: citizens in their own country, EU citizens anywhere in the EU and EEA, US citizens in US territories and the Compact states (Palau, the Marshall Islands, Micronesia), and UK citizens in Ireland and the Crown Dependencies. So France is scored for UK and US passports, the United States for UK and German passports, and Puerto Rico under the same federal rules as the mainland.

If you hold a different passport, your own rules may be very different. The [Visa Finder](/visa) shows the entry rule for your passport.

## The four parts

For each passport we add up four parts.

### 1. How long you can stay on arrival (up to 6 points)

The longest single stay you can get without applying at an embassy: visa-free entry, an electronic travel authorisation, a visa on arrival or an e-visa all count. Where there is a choice, we take whichever scores best in parts 1 and 2 together.

| Stay per visit | Points |
|---|---|
| 365 days or more | 6 |
| 180 days | 5.5 |
| 90 days | 5 |
| 60 days | 4.5 |
| 30 days | 4 |
| 14 days | 3 |
| Under 14 days | 2 |
| A visa from an embassy is needed first | 2 |
| Only on a pre-arranged guided tour | 1 |

A stay between two rows takes the lower row, so 45 days scores as 30 and 120 days as 90.

### 2. Paperwork at the border (up to 1 point)

| Before or at the border | Points |
|---|---|
| Nothing to apply for (free stamp on arrival counts) | 1 |
| An online travel authorisation costing $25 or less | 0.5 |
| A costlier authorisation, a paid visa on arrival, or an e-visa | 0 |
| Any of these costing $100 or more | minus 0.5 |

Small arrival or airport taxes that every traveller pays are not counted as a visa fee.

### 3. Staying longer (up to 3 points)

We take the single best of these options, not the sum:

| Option | Points |
|---|---|
| The stay on arrival is already a year or more (Georgia, Svalbard) | 3 |
| A remote-work or long-stay residence route (see below) | 1 to 2.5 |
| Tourist stay extendable inside the country to a year or more | 1.5 |
| Extendable to 180 days or more | 1 |
| Extendable by at least a month | 0.5 |

A remote-work route counts when it lets you live in the country while working for employers or clients abroad, is open to UK or US citizens without a local job offer, and is actually taking applications. It scores:

- 1 point for existing and being open,
- plus 0.5 if the income floor is $3,000 a month or less, there is no fixed figure, or the test is savings of $20,000 or less,
- plus 0.5 more if the floor is $2,000 a month or less,
- plus 0.5 if the first grant lasts 12 months or more,
- minus 0.5 if the floor is above $5,000 a month,
- minus 0.5 if the whole route lasts 6 months or less,
- minus 0.5 if eligibility is restricted (a degree, a profession, an age band, a list of nationalities, or registering a local business),
- minus 0.5 if the law already sets a closing date.

Routes that are announced but not open, closed, or not confirmed by any government source score nothing until they are.

### 4. Shared limits (deductions)

| Rule | Points |
|---|---|
| Schengen area: 90 days in any 180 are shared across 29 countries, so leaving one member for another does not reset the clock | minus 1 |
| CA-4 (Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua): 90 days shared across the four | minus 0.5 |
| The length is routinely set lower than the headline by the officer at the desk | minus 0.5 |

The best possible total is 10 (6 + 1 + 3).

## Worked examples

- **Georgia, 10.** 365 days visa-free (6), nothing to apply for (1), and that year is the long stay (3).
- **Colombia, 9.** 90 days visa-free (5), no paperwork (1), Digital Nomad Visa at about $1,640 a month for up to two years (2.5). Total 8.5, rounded up.
- **Mexico, 8.** Up to 180 days (5.5), no paperwork (1), temporary residence at about $4,600 a month (1.5), officers often grant fewer days (minus 0.5). Total 7.5.
- **Spain, 7.** 90 days (5), no paperwork (1), Digital Nomad Visa at about $3,280 a month for a year (1.5), Schengen (minus 1). Total 6.5.
- **Poland, 5.** 90 days (5), no paperwork (1), no remote-work route (0), Schengen (minus 1).
- **United States, 5.** 90 days on an ESTA (5), the ESTA costs $40 (0), no route and no extension (0).
- **Algeria, 2.** A visa from an embassy is needed first (2), nothing else.
- **Turkmenistan, 1.** Entry only through a licensed tour operator with a state guide.

## City exceptions

| City | Country score | City score | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hong Kong | China 4 | 6 | Hong Kong runs its own immigration: UK 180 days, US and Germany 90 days visa-free. Mainland China needs a visa for US citizens and gives UK and German citizens 30 days. |
| Kota Kinabalu | Malaysia 8 | 6 | Sabah controls its own immigration and the DE Rantau Nomad Pass does not cover it, so only the 90-day visa-free entry applies. |
| Aqaba | Jordan 5 | 6 | Arrivals through the Aqaba Special Economic Zone get the Jordanian visa free of charge, with extensions at the ASEZA office. |

Places with their own rule that does not change the score: Gangtok (Sikkim's Restricted Area Permit, 30 days extendable to 60), Kuching and Miri (Sarawak's own nomad pass, scored like the federal one), Jeju and Phu Quoc (30 days visa-free for any nationality, shorter than what UK, US and German passports already get or equal in points), Dahab (15-day South Sinai stamp) and Zanzibar (compulsory local travel insurance).

## Sources

Entry rules come from the UK Foreign Office's entry-requirements pages and the US State Department's country pages, and, for German passports where they differ, the German Foreign Office. Remote-work routes come from the destination's own immigration or government site. Where a government page could not be reached, we used two independent sources that agree and say so; a route we could not confirm at all is not scored.

## What this score does not measure

- Tax. A long stay can make you tax resident; the score ignores that.
- Whether remote work is formally allowed on a tourist stay. Most countries do not say, and a few forbid it outright. The score measures how long you may stay, not what the stamp permits.
- Safety or travel advisories. Iran and Myanmar score low here for their entry rules; the safety score covers the rest.
- Passports other than the three above.

## Keeping it current

Entry rules change often; Thailand cut its visa-free stay from 60 to 30 days in September 2026 and Kyrgyzstan from 60 to 30 at the end of 2025. Every country's facts and sources are kept in one file, and a change to a rule changes that country's score for every city at once.
