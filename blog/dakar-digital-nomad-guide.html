Dakar Digital Nomad Guide: Fibre, Power Cuts and Visas

A home broadband line in Senegal delivers a median 44.5 Mbps down and 13.3 Mbps up, according to Ookla's Speedtest Global Index for August 2026. That is enough for video calls, far short of Lisbon or Busan, and it decides whether Dakar works for a digital nomad. The second number is 90: US, UK, Canadian and most EU passports enter Senegal visa-free for up to three months. This guide covers the connection, the power grid, the costs and where to live.

We rate Dakar 7 out of 10 for cost of living and 4 for wifi, and those two scores describe the trade. You get an Atlantic capital of four million people, on GMT all year, for about $1,490 a month. You pay for it in patience with the infrastructure.

Why Dakar works for a digital nomad, and where it does not

The time zone is the quiet advantage. Senegal sits on GMT with no daylight saving, so your working day matches London in winter, trails Berlin by one or two hours, and still overlaps the US East Coast morning: 9:00 in New York is 13:00 or 14:00 in Dakar.

The city is large. Senegal's statistics office, ANSD, counted 4,004,426 residents in the Dakar region at the 2023 census, up from 3,137,196 in 2013, packed at 7,478 people per square kilometre on a narrow peninsula. That density is why traffic dominates daily life, and why a digital nomad in Dakar should choose the district before the apartment.

The weak points are equally concrete. Our scores put cleanliness at 4, English at 4 and safety at 5. Arrive with working French or a phrasebook, a second mobile network as backup and a coworking membership already chosen.

Dakar entry rules: who gets 90 days visa-free in Senegal

Senegal's foreign ministry states that since 1 May 2015, citizens of the countries on its exemption list need no visa for a stay of under three months, provided their passport is valid for at least six more months. The list names 59 countries: 20 African states from Benin to Tunisia, 26 EU members plus the United Kingdom, and Brazil, Canada, China, Djibouti, India, Japan, Malaysia, Mauritius, Singapore, South Korea, the UAE and the United States.

Read the list before you book, because it has gaps. Ireland is the one EU member missing from it, and Switzerland, Norway, Australia and New Zealand do not appear either. If your passport is not named, confirm your entry route with a Senegalese embassy rather than assuming the EU rule covers you.

At the border, the UK's travel advice (updated May 2026) says you need to show proof of onward travel and of booked accommodation, and can be refused entry without them. To stay beyond three months, Canada's advice (updated 24 September 2026) says you either leave and come back later or apply for a foreign identity card, the carte d'étranger, at the Direction de la police des étrangers et des titres de voyage before your 90 days run out.

One rule is still pending. Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko announced a return to visa reciprocity in his policy speech of 27 December 2024, and the Council of Ministers adopted an electronic visa for reciprocity countries on 30 July 2025. As of September 2026, both the UK and Canadian governments still list visa-free entry. Senegal has no digital nomad visa, which is why it does not appear in our nomad visa finder.

Internet in Dakar: fibre, 4G and 5G by the numbers

The regulator, ARTP, counted 951,809 fixed broadband lines (ADSL and fibre) in Senegal on 30 June 2026, against 23 million mobile internet lines. Most people are online through a phone, and the fixed network is still small for a country of 19.6 million. In Dakar, whether you get fibre depends on the building, so check the address before you sign.

Orange, Sonatel's consumer brand, is the only operator ARTP lists on every market segment, and it held 62.7% of mobile internet lines at the end of June 2026. Yas, which traded as Free until a November 2024 rebrand, held 23.4%, and Expresso and the virtual operator Promobile share the rest. Orange runs an online eligibility checker for its fibre and 5G that takes a street address, which is the fastest way to vet a listing.

On the mobile side, 69.4% of Senegal's mobile internet lines were on 4G and only 5.7% on 5G in June 2026, per the ARTP internet dashboard. Treat 5G as a bonus in parts of the city, not a plan. Speedtest's index publishes no mobile median for Senegal, so run your own tests: ARTP's Sama Réseau app measures speed and latency against servers inside the country.

The practical digital nomad setup is a fibre line where you live, an Orange or Yas SIM with a data bundle as backup, and a coworking desk for the days that matter. The fixed median's latency of 7 ms is good; the 13 Mbps median upload is the figure that will bother anyone who ships large video files.

Power cuts in Dakar: what Senelec's own figures show

The national utility publishes its own reliability indicators for Dakar. Senelec's figures show that in 2024 the average Dakar customer had 9.12 interruptions across the year, adding up to 6 hours 40 minutes without power, or roughly 44 minutes per cut. The second half of the year was worse than the first: 5.30 interruptions against 3.82.

The second half includes most of the rainy season, and Canada's travel advice notes that outages happen across the country, particularly during heavy rain or strong winds, and that not all buildings have generators. The rains fall mainly from June to October, and August is the wettest month at about 145 mm.

For a digital nomad, two things follow. Ask any landlord whether the building has a generator or an inverter for the router, and keep a charged power bank that can run the router for an hour. The first half of 2025 came in at 4.08 interruptions totalling 2 hours 39 minutes: slightly more cuts than the first half of 2024, but shorter ones.

Where to live in Dakar: Almadies, Ngor, Mermoz, Ouakam and Plateau

Dakar is a peninsula, and the districts a digital nomad considers sort themselves by distance from the western tip. Numbeo's August 2026 data gives only two rent levels: a one-bedroom in the centre at $830 a month, and outside the centre at $440. We have no sourced rent for individual districts, so use those two figures as the frame and negotiate from there.

Les Almadies sits on the Pointe des Almadies, the westernmost point of mainland Africa, with restaurants, beach clubs and the most expat-oriented housing stock. Ngor, next door, is a fishing village on the beach facing Ngor Island, with simpler facilities and the surf on its doorstep. Ouakam lies between the two and the city, below the African Renaissance Monument on the Mamelles hills.

Mermoz and neighbouring Sacré-Coeur are residential and central: Jokkolabs is in Sacré-Coeur and Impact Hub in nearby Keur Gorgui. Plateau, at the southern tip, is the administrative downtown: ministries, banks, the TER's Dakar terminus and the ferry to Gorée. It empties after office hours.

For a first month, Mermoz puts you near both coworking spaces and midway between Plateau and the beaches. Pick Ngor if the surf is the reason you came.

Cost of living in Dakar: the CFA franc, the euro peg and dollars

Senegal uses the West African CFA franc (XOF), and its value against the euro never moves. The central bank, BCEAO, fixed the parity on 1 January 1999, and the franc has been pegged at 655.957 XOF to 1 euro since. Against the dollar, the franc moves exactly as the euro does. Every dollar figure in this guide uses $1 = 570 XOF, the rate our city page applied on 1 August 2026; the ECB's rate of 25 September 2026 implies about 575.

Our cost of living index puts a solo digital nomad month at $1,490: the $830 central one-bedroom plus about $660 for groceries, meals out, utilities, phone, internet and transport. A couple sharing a one-bedroom lands near $2,010, and a lean month outside the centre near $1,100.

Day to day, a meal in an inexpensive restaurant costs about $5 and a monthly transport pass $79. The mobile money apps Orange Money and Wave are widely used, per the UK's advice, which also warns that Dakar ATMs have low withdrawal limits and regularly run out of cash.

Planning Dakar against other West African bases? Our Dakar city profile breaks the budget into rent, living-cost and transport lines and scores the city on 13 categories, so you can see exactly where the $1,490 goes before you commit to a lease.

Coworking in Dakar: two spaces with published prices

Our city page lists six places to work. Two of them publish enough on their own websites to plan around, as of September 2026. Impact Hub Dakar, in Cité Keur Gorgui, opens Monday to Friday from 9:00 to 18:00 and charges $7 for a day, $53 for 15 days and $105 for 30 days, all before VAT.

Jokkolabs, which calls itself a pioneer of innovation in Africa, lists its Dakar office at Sacré-Coeur 3. We found no desk prices on its website, so ask before you commit.

Notice the opening hours. A space that closes at six on weekdays and all weekend cannot be a digital nomad's only connection, which is another reason the fibre line at home matters.

French, Wolof and English in Dakar

French is the official language, and it runs government offices, leases, bank forms and most business email. It is not what the street speaks. At the 2023 census, 68.7% of Dakar region residents aged three and over said Wolof was the language they used most, followed by Pulaar at 15.7% and Sereer at 5.7%. Only 1.8% named French.

For a digital nomad, that means French gets the paperwork done and Wolof gets you the smile. A handful of greetings in Wolof goes a long way in markets and taxis. We rate English 4 out of 10: expect it in coworking spaces, upmarket restaurants and embassies, and very little elsewhere.

Getting around Dakar: TER train, BRT and taxis

Two modern lines now cut through the traffic. The TER regional express train runs 36 km from central Dakar to Diamniadio, with 13 stations, and carries nearly 80,000 passengers a day according to its operator. Trains leave every 10 minutes from about 5:45 to 20:55 Monday to Saturday, then every 20 minutes until 22:05; Sundays run every 20 minutes. A second-class ticket costs $0.88, $1.75 or $2.63 depending on zones, and first class $4.39.

Sunu BRT, the bus rapid transit line, runs from Papa Gueye Fall (Petersen) downtown to Guédiawaye, seven days a week from 6:00 to 21:00 with a bus every six minutes. A single costs $0.70 for one zone or $0.88 for all zones, and a 30-day all-zone pass $39. The operator lists the extension of the TER to Blaise Diagne airport as planned, so the airport run is still by road.

For a digital nomad without a car, taxis cover everything else. Canada's advice says they have no meter, so agree the fare before you get in, and warns against the jakarta motorbike taxis. Our facts panel lists Yango and Heetch as the ride-hailing apps operating in Dakar.

Surf, Ngor Island and the Dakar beaches

Senegal was one of the stops in The Endless Summer, the 1966 surf film. In Dakar the surf life centres on Ngor, the fishing village at the western end of the peninsula. Pirogues run from its beach to Ngor Island, a short boat ride offshore, where visitors swim, surf and dive. For a digital nomad it is the easiest half-day off the laptop, with time to be back for an afternoon call.

The ocean is the real hazard here. Canada's advice warns that riptides are common, several drownings happen each year, and beaches are generally unsupervised, with no warning system. Swim where locals swim, surf within your level, and use a surf school for the first sessions if you are new to Atlantic swell.

Health in Dakar: malaria, yellow fever and English-speaking doctors

Malaria is present throughout Senegal, Dakar included. The CDC's Senegal page recommends preventive medication for all areas, naming atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine and tafenoquine, and notes chloroquine resistance. Canada's advice recommends antimalarials for most travellers and suggests seeing a travel clinic about six weeks before you fly.

The CDC recommends the yellow fever vaccine for everyone aged nine months and over. It becomes a legal requirement only if you arrive from, or transit through, a country with yellow fever risk. The CDC also lists meningococcal vaccination, especially for the dry season from December to June.

The UK calls medical facilities in Dakar reasonable and those in the rest of the country limited. The ambulance number is 15 and the police 17. Our Dakar services directory lists 14 doctors, clinics and dentists who work in English, each taken from the UK or US embassy lists of local providers.

Is Dakar safe for digital nomads? What the advisories say

Canada sets Senegal at "exercise a high degree of caution" because of crime. The UK's advice says robbery, burglary and assault have become more common in Dakar, sometimes with weapons, that hotels used by foreigners have been robbed at gunpoint, and that pickpocketing and bag-snatching are rising. We score safety 5.

The advice is specific about where. Canada names Yoff beach, Independence Square, the Gorée ferry terminal and both Corniches in the evening as places where pickpockets are very active, and notes that snatching from passing motorbikes is frequent. The UK warns of bogus taxi drivers at the airport and fake banknotes when changing cash.

Two legal points apply to every visitor. Demonstrations are frequent and can close the road to Blaise Diagne airport, and foreigners have been prosecuted for joining unapproved protests. Same-sex relations are illegal, and on 30 March 2026 Senegal raised the sentence to 5 to 10 years and criminalised the "promotion, support or funding" of homosexuality, bisexuality and transsexuality.

Who Dakar suits as a remote-work base

Dakar fits a digital nomad with a flexible schedule, working French and a job that tolerates a 44 Mbps line with a backup. It rewards people who want a real African capital on a European time zone, surf before work and a month that costs well under $2,000. It suits a long stay better than a short one, because finding a building with fibre and a generator takes time.

It is the wrong base if a dropped call costs you a client, if you need English to run daily life, or if you want a ready-made nomad scene. The 90-day limit and the pending e-visa also make it a poor fit for anyone who needs certainty about next year.

If Dakar is on your shortlist, weigh it against Cape Verde, 570 km offshore, which has the nomad visa Senegal lacks. Our Praia remote work guide covers its six-month permit and island choice, and the Cape Verde city profile puts its costs and scores next to Dakar's.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa to work remotely from Dakar, Senegal?

Not if your passport is on the foreign ministry's list of 59 exempt countries, which includes the US, UK, Canada and 26 EU states. Stays must be under three months, on a passport valid six more months. Ireland, Switzerland and Australia are not on the list, so check with an embassy first.

How fast is the internet in Dakar for remote work?

Senegal's fixed broadband median is 44.5 Mbps down and 13.3 Mbps up (Speedtest, August 2026), with 7 ms latency. That handles video calls but not heavy uploads. Only 5.7% of mobile internet lines were on 5G in June 2026, so a 4G SIM is your realistic backup.

How much does a month in Dakar cost a digital nomad?

About $1,490 for one person: $830 for a central one-bedroom and roughly $660 for food, utilities, phone and transport, based on Numbeo data from August 2026. Living outside the centre brings it near $1,100. The CFA franc is pegged to the euro at 655.957 XOF.

Does Senegal have a digital nomad visa?

No. Senegal has no dedicated remote-work visa as of September 2026. Visitors use the visa-free stay of under three months, then either leave and return or apply for a carte d'étranger before the 90 days end. Cape Verde, next door, offers a six-month remote working visa.

Do I need malaria tablets for Dakar?

The CDC says malaria is present in all areas of Senegal and recommends preventive medication, naming atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine and tafenoquine. Canada recommends antimalarials for most travellers. See a travel clinic about six weeks before you fly, and avoid mosquito bites as well.

Is Dakar safe for digital nomads?

It takes care. Canada advises a high degree of caution because of crime, and the UK reports rising robbery, bag-snatching and pickpocketing. Hotspots include Yoff beach, Independence Square, the Gorée ferry terminal and the Corniches at night. Use pre-arranged airport transport and avoid walking alone after dark.

Which SIM card should a digital nomad buy in Dakar?

Orange is the largest network, with 62.7% of Senegal's mobile internet lines in June 2026, and it offers 5G and fibre. Yas, formerly Free, is second at 23.4%. Carrying one SIM from each is the cheapest insurance against a network outage.
