How to Get a Portugal Digital Nomad Visa (Step by Step)

Yannick Schroth January 2025 14 min read

Portugal's digital nomad visa is decided by the contents of one folder. You hand it over at a visa centre in New York, Washington or London, or at the Portuguese consulate that covers your country, and on top lies the application form, headed "Pedido de visto de longa duração" and printed with the line "Este impresso é gratuito": this form is free. In one box you tick "Duas entradas (residência)", two entries, for residence. Above your signature you accept that the fee will not be refunded if the visa is refused.

Underneath come two identical passport photos, your passport and a copy of its photo page, a criminal record certificate with a stamp from your own government headed in French, a travel insurance policy, the contract that pays you, three months of bank statements and a document showing where you pay tax today. Each sheet answers one question the Portuguese state has about you, and a missing one sends the folder back.

The D8, as the visa is known, is a residence visa for people who work remotely for employers or clients outside Portugal: salaried employees, freelancers and owners of companies abroad. It is the right tool if you want Portugal as your base for years, earn at least about $4,240 a month, and accept Portuguese tax on your income once you live there. It is the wrong tool for a few months in Lisbon, for which a shorter visa exists, and for a tax break, which it does not bring.

The top sheet: who the visa is for

The form only makes sense if you fit the category. The D8 was written into Portugal's immigration law in 2022 and has been issued since late that year. It covers employees and self-employed professionals working remotely for people or companies domiciled outside Portugal, and they must show that relationship on paper. You must be a citizen of a country outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland, whose citizens can simply move. A job with a Portuguese employer is a different visa.

There is a second, lighter version. Portugal also issues a temporary-stay visa for remote work, for stays under a year, valid for multiple entries and decided within 30 days. The income test is the same, and it adds a return ticket to the folder. If what you want is a winter in Lisbon rather than a new home, that is the visa to look at; everything below is about the residence version, which leads to a permit.

The sheets everyone worries about: three months of income

The money test is stated in one line: your average monthly income from employment or self-employment over the last three months must equal at least four guaranteed minimum monthly wages. The minimum wage for 2026 is about $1,060 a month, so the floor is about $4,240 a month, or about $12,700 across the three months. Portuguese wages are paid fourteen times a year, with a holiday and a Christmas bonus, but the test uses the monthly figure, not an annual one.

The number moves. The floor follows the minimum wage, which is set by decree each year, and the 2026 rise took effect on 1 January, so a figure from an older guide is probably low. Because the rule is set in euros, its dollar value also moves with the exchange rate.

Two details decide most files. First, only income from contracts counts: the ministry considers money that comes from an employment contract, a company contract or a service contract, so savings do not replace it. Employees bring the contract or a letter from the employer; freelancers bring service contracts, a company contract or documents showing work done for one or more clients. Second, it is an average, which helps freelancers with uneven months. Make it easy for the officer: every payment in the statements should match an invoice or a contract in the folder.

The sheet with a French heading: your criminal record

The certificate comes from your country of nationality, or from any country where you have lived for more than a year, and it needs an apostille. That is a certificate attached to the document or to a sheet stapled to it, always headed in French, "Apostille (Convention de La Haye du 5 octobre 1961)". It confirms the signature and seal on your certificate, not what the certificate says.

Order this first, because it runs on another government's clock. In the UK, for example, a police certificate from England and Wales cannot get an electronic apostille, so it needs the paper version, about $61 and usually up to 25 working days plus post. Later, AIMA, the immigration agency, refuses the permit to anyone convicted of a crime that carries more than a year in prison in Portugal.

Insurance, tax residence, and what is not in the folder

The insurance must cover medical costs, including urgent care and repatriation. The requirement can fall away under a health agreement between Portugal and your country, such as the UK's S1 or Brazil's PB4 form. Next to it goes a document from the tax authority at home confirming where you are currently tax resident.

Just as useful is what the ministry's base list leaves out: no Portuguese tax number, no Portuguese bank account, no lease. Each consulate may ask for more, and the form does ask for an address where you will stay, so read your consulate's own page before you book. Proof of an address in Portugal becomes compulsory only at the next stage, at AIMA.

The number you will want anyway: the NIF

Even if your consulate does not ask for it, you will need a NIF, Portugal's nine-digit tax number, for a job, a lease or a bank account. You can request one in person at any tax office or Loja do Cidadão, or remotely through a legal representative using the tax authority's online counter. A power of attorney given to a lawyer or solicitor needs no certified signature, and documents in a foreign language must come with a certified translation; our directory lists translators in Lisbon by the languages they work in.

The tax authority's leaflet on NIFs for foreigners is explicit that no fiscal representative is required when the number is assigned. The obligation comes later. Once you have a tax relationship with Portugal, such as a job, self-employment, a car or property, a resident of a country outside the EU and EEA has 15 days to appoint a fiscal representative or sign up for the tax authority's electronic notifications. A freelancer charging VAT still needs a representative for VAT.

The NIF is first registered with your foreign address. When you become tax resident, you change the address yourself, which is the step that tells Portugal you have arrived. Our guide to the NIF and Portuguese tax for nomads walks through it.

Across the counter, and what comes back

Where the folder goes depends on where you live. In the US, the embassy in Washington and the consulates in New York and San Francisco take applications through VFS Global, the ministry's outside contractor; in the UK, London, Manchester and a VFS centre in Edinburgh do. You give your fingerprints and pay the visa fee of about $127, which is not refunded if you are refused.

The ministry gives itself 60 days to decide. The New York consulate quotes four to eight weeks, and San Francisco advises applying at least three months before you want to travel. What comes back is a vignette in your passport, valid for four months and two entries. Within those four months you must be in Portugal and file for the residence permit.

The vignette usually carries the next appointment too: the AIMA appointment is normally scheduled when the visa is issued and printed with it. If AIMA had no free slot that day, the vignette is printed without the link, and you request an appointment through AIMA's contact form. Check which kind you have before you book a one-way flight.

The second folder: AIMA

In Portugal you start a new, thinner folder. AIMA, the agency that took over from the old border service SEF, takes residence applications at any of its 35 Lojas AIMA, one-stop counters for migrants from Lisbon to the Algarve, not only the one nearest your home. You hand it in in person with your passport, the visa, AIMA's application form and two sworn statements.

The first comes from your employer or client abroad, confirming the work; AIMA publishes a template in which the company declares, "sob compromisso de honra", on its word of honour, your role, the type of contract and your monthly pay. The second is your own declaration of where you live and on what basis, backed by a land-registry certificate if you own the home or a declaration from your landlord or host if you do not.

The fees are two: about $115 to receive and analyse the application and about $99 to grant the permit, $214 in all, after a 25 percent reduction that applies until these services move online. With the visa, the government's share of the whole process is about $340. The permit runs for two years and renews for three at a time, for the same two fees.

The folder you open a year later: tax

The visa does not make you tax resident; your days do. Under article 16 of the income tax code, you are resident if you spend more than 183 days in Portugal in any twelve months that start or end in the tax year, counting any day that includes a night here, or if you keep a home in conditions that suggest you mean to live in it. Once either test is met, you are resident from your first day.

A D8 holder who actually lives in Portugal meets one of those tests within the first year, and a resident is taxed on worldwide income. A salary for work done at a desk in Lisbon is Portuguese-source income, whoever pays it. The rates run from 12.5 percent to 48 percent above about $99,700 of taxable income, with a solidarity surcharge of 2.5 percent on income above about $92,100.

The famous Non-Habitual Resident regime was revoked from 1 January 2024, and a request from someone who became resident in 2025 or later is refused. Its successor, IFICI, taxes qualifying income at 20 percent for ten years, but only income earned in listed activities, such as research, certified startups and qualified jobs with companies in Portuguese incentive programmes. Remote work for a foreign employer or foreign clients is not on the list. If you do qualify, register by 15 January of the year after you become resident.

For your first, split tax year, our directory of English-speaking tax advisers in Lisbon is the place to start, and our digital nomad tax guide covers what your home country may still claim, which for US citizens never stops.

What the permit buys, and what it asks of you

The card opens more than Portugal. A residence permit from a Schengen country lets you travel to the other Schengen countries for 90 days in any 180, and your time in Portugal no longer counts against any visa-free limit.

In return, Portugal expects you to live there. AIMA can cancel a temporary permit if you are away without good reason for six months in a row, or eight months in total, during its validity; a longer absence has to be justified by a request filed before you leave. The D8 does not suit a life spent mostly elsewhere.

The long road is clear. After five years on temporary permits you can apply for permanent residence, with proof of basic Portuguese, one accepted proof being an A2 certificate from a Portuguese-for-migrants course, and proof that your taxes and social security are in order. Citizenship is further away than it was: since Organic Law 1/2026 took effect on 19 May 2026, naturalisation needs ten years of legal residence for most nationals from outside the EU, and seven for citizens of Portuguese-speaking countries such as Brazil.

Is the D8 worth it?

Yes, if Portugal is going to be home. For someone who clears the income floor with room to spare and plans to spend most of the year there, the D8 is one of the clearest residence routes Europe offers a remote worker: a defined document list, fees under $350 and a path to permanent residence. If you want a few months in Lisbon or Porto, take the temporary-stay version instead; if you want a tax deal, look elsewhere.

If you do apply: order the criminal record and its apostille first, keep three clean months of income and the contracts that explain them, get a NIF early, and check whether your vignette carries an AIMA date. Then start on the second folder. For the months in between, our digital nomad guide to Lisbon covers neighbourhoods, coworking and costs.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Portugal Digital Nomad Visa

What is the Portugal D8 digital nomad visa?

A residence visa for non-EU citizens who work remotely for employers or clients outside Portugal. It is valid for four months and two entries and leads to a two-year AIMA residence permit, renewable for three years at a time.

How much income do I need for the Portugal D8 visa?

An average of at least four times the Portuguese minimum wage over the last three months: about $1,060 a month in 2026, so about $4,240 a month. Only income from employment or service contracts counts, not savings.

What documents are required for the Portugal D8 visa?

The application form, two photos, a passport valid three months beyond the stay, a criminal record certificate with an apostille, travel medical insurance, your work contracts, proof of three months of income and proof of your current tax residence. Consulates may ask for more.

How long does the Portugal D8 visa take?

The foreign ministry allows itself 60 days to decide. The visa is then valid for four months, in which you file for the residence permit at AIMA, usually at an appointment printed with the visa.

How much does the Portugal D8 visa cost?

About $127 for the visa and $214 in AIMA fees for the first permit, about $340 in total. Apostilles, translations, insurance and any lawyer come on top.

Do I need a fiscal representative to get a NIF in Portugal?

No, not when the NIF is assigned. Once you have a tax relationship in Portugal, a non-EU resident has 15 days to appoint a fiscal representative or opt into the tax authority's electronic notifications.

Does the D8 visa come with a tax break?

No. The NHR regime closed to newcomers on 1 January 2024, and its successor, IFICI, covers only listed activities. Remote work for foreign employers or clients is taxed at the normal rates, from 12.5 to 48 percent.
