Is Nashville Good for Digital Nomads? Yes, for US Citizens

Is Nashville good for digital nomads? For US citizens, yes: Tennessee takes no income tax from wages, gigabit fibre sells for $70 a month, and typical asking rent sat at $1,834 a month in August 2026 on Zillow's index, about where it stood in mid-2022. For foreign digital nomads the answer is narrower: an ESTA gives 90 days, federal rules bar visitors from "employment" without naming laptop work, and an uninsured emergency visit at Metro Nashville General Hospital lists a $657 cash price.

Is Nashville good for digital nomads? The verdict by passport

Split the question by passport, because the rules differ more than the city does. A US digital nomad moving from a state that taxes wages stops paying state income tax on them, lands in the Central time zone (one hour behind New York, two ahead of California), and pays asking rents that were flat from July 2022 to August 2026. That is a strong case.

A foreign digital nomad is in a different position. The Visa Waiver Program caps a stay at 90 days, there is no US visa class for remote work, and health care is priced as if you will pay cash, because you will. Nashville still works for a one to three month stretch built around the music calendar. It rarely works as a long base.

Two things apply to everyone. The city is built around the car, and spring brings tornado warnings that can arrive after midnight. Neither is a reason to skip Nashville. Both should decide where you rent.

Nashville rent by neighbourhood: HUD and Zillow figures

Two public datasets price Nashville rent ZIP by ZIP, and they measure different things. HUD's fiscal 2027 Small Area Fair Market Rents start from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey: the 40th-percentile gross rent, utilities included, adjusted upward for recent movers and inflation. Zillow's Observed Rent Index (ZORI) is the typical asking rent on listed homes of every size, smoothed, for August 2026.

HUD's figure is a one-bedroom with utilities; Zillow's blends studios with family houses, so the two disagree most in 12 South and East Nashville. Price the actual listing, not the ZIP. The Census Bureau's 2024 survey puts the median one-bedroom gross rent at $1,470, but that counts tenants who signed years ago. Digital nomads signing today should plan on the HUD column, which is adjusted for recent movers.

All three sources price unfurnished long leases. A furnished month-to-month apartment is a separate market that none of them measures, so treat these numbers as the floor for a digital nomad budget, not the budget itself.

Which Nashville neighbourhood fits a digital nomad

East Nashville, the indie music and coffee end of town, centres on Five Points, and WeGo route 56 runs down Gallatin Pike every 15 minutes or better. Germantown sits half a mile north of downtown, with the Farmers' Market on its edge. The Nations and Sylvan Park share ZIP 37209 and the frequent route 50 on Charlotte Pike.

12 South has the priciest one-bedroom in the table and is served only by route 17, which runs every 20 to 60 minutes, so living there without a car means walking or ride-hail. If rent is the constraint, Madison in 37115 is about $330 a month below 37209 on HUD's figures.

Cost of living in Nashville beyond the rent

Our cost data for Nashville, built from Numbeo prices in August 2026, puts one person's living costs excluding rent at $1,020 a month: groceries, some meals out, utilities, a phone, internet and transport. Add a central one-bedroom at about $1,950 and a solo digital nomad spends around $2,970; a leaner month in a cheaper area comes to about $2,420.

A meal at an inexpensive restaurant runs about $19 before tax and tip, and a tip of 15 to 20% is expected at table-service restaurants. Every restaurant bill and most shop receipts then carry Nashville's 9.75% sales tax, covered below. A monthly bus pass costs $65, which matches WeGo's own monthly fare cap.

The coworking spaces on our Nashville city guide sell day passes from $20 to $25 and hot desks for $100 to $200 a month. Our cost of living index sets Nashville beside every other city we cover.

Internet in Nashville: fibre, cable and how to check an address

Home internet is not the weak point. In the Census Bureau's 2024 survey, 80% of Nashville households (264,905 of 331,090) had a cable, fibre or DSL subscription. Google Fiber sells symmetric plans in the city at $70 a month for 1 Gig, $100 for 3 Gig and $150 for 8 Gig, as listed on its Nashville page in September 2026.

Providers differ from building to building, and fibre versus cable shows up mostly in upload speed on video calls. Before you sign a lease, type the exact address into the FCC National Broadband Map at broadbandmap.fcc.gov, which lists each provider reporting service there and the technology it uses.

Outside the apartment, the coworking spaces on our city page run their own fibre, and a phone-based hotspot on a US carrier covers the gap on days the cafe network is crowded.

Tennessee taxes: nothing on wages, 9.75% at the till

Tennessee is one of eight US states with no individual income tax, according to the Tax Foundation's 2025 state table. The last piece, the Hall tax on interest and dividends, was phased down one point a year and fully repealed from 1 January 2021. Federal income tax still applies as anywhere else in the country. Once Tennessee is your home state, your wages owe it nothing.

The state collects instead at the register. Since 1 February 2025, the rate in Nashville and the rest of Davidson County is 9.75%: the 7% state rate plus a 2.75% local rate. The local share rose by half a point after Davidson County voters approved the "Choose How You Move" transit referendum on 5 November 2024.

On $1,500 of taxable monthly spending, the sales tax is about $146. How your home country or home state treats a stay abroad is a separate question, which our digital nomad tax guide walks through.

Getting around Nashville without a car, and why most people drive

Public transport exists and is cheap. WeGo charges $2 for two hours of unlimited rides, and tapping a contactless card or the QuickTicket app caps the cost at $4 a day and $65 a month, according to WeGo's fare page. American Express is not accepted. The network has 27 local routes and nine regional ones.

Frequency is the problem. Only eight corridors run every 15 minutes or better, among them route 3 on West End, route 7 on Hillsboro, route 50 on Charlotte Pike and route 56 on Gallatin Pike. Local routes run every 20 to 60 minutes. The WeGo Star commuter train runs on weekday mornings and afternoons only. From the airport, route 18 costs $2 and takes about 20 minutes on express trips, 35 to 45 minutes on local ones.

The Census Bureau's 2024 commuting data shows the result: of Nashville workers who did not work from home, 93% travelled by car; 1.6% of all workers took public transport and 2.0% walked, while 18% worked from home. The transit referendum is funding new sidewalks, smart traffic signals and bus stop upgrades, but the city you will live in this year is the one in those figures.

For a digital nomad without a car: rent within a short walk of a frequent route, use ride-hail for the rest, and hire a car only for trips out of town.

Nashville weather and tornado season for digital nomads

Our weather data (Open-Meteo, 2019 to 2023 averages) has July highs around 32°C (90°F) with heavy humidity, and January lows near freezing. May, June and September are the most comfortable months to work from here.

Tornadoes are the risk to plan for. In the National Weather Service's climatology for Middle Tennessee, 66% of tornadoes struck in March, April and May, with a second, smaller peak around November and the fewest in September. 58% touched down between 2pm and 9pm, but not all. On 3 March 2020 an EF-3 tornado crossed Nashville between 12:32 and 1:35am, causing EF-2 damage in Germantown and EF-3 damage at Five Points in East Nashville, according to the NWS Nashville survey. That tornado killed five people and injured 220.

It happened again on 9 December 2023, when an EF-2 tracked through north Nashville and Madison and killed three people. For a spring stay, keep emergency alerts switched on in your phone settings, and ask the landlord where the building's interior shelter space is on the day you move in.

Healthcare costs in Nashville if you are uninsured

An ESTA or visitor visa includes no health cover, so the useful numbers are cash prices. Every US hospital must publish them, and Metro Nashville General Hospital, open since 1890, updated its file on 1 September 2026. Its listed discounted cash prices for the facility side of a visit are:

  • Emergency department visit, level 3: $657 (gross charge $1,095)
  • Emergency department visit, level 5: $1,510.80 (gross charge $2,518)
  • Clinic visit, new patient, level 3: $104.40
  • Chest X-ray, two views: $243.60

The hospital's price information page states that doctor charges may not be included and can arrive as a separate bill. Under the federal EMTALA law, a hospital that takes Medicare and runs an emergency department must screen and stabilise you regardless of your ability to pay, which guarantees treatment, not a free bill.

For anything planned, ask for a Good Faith Estimate. Under federal rules, a provider must give an uninsured or self-paying patient one when care is scheduled at least three business days ahead, and you need it to dispute a bill later. The simplest fix remains travel medical insurance that covers the United States, bought before you fly.

Can foreign digital nomads work from Nashville on ESTA or a B visa?

The Visa Waiver Program lets citizens of 42 countries visit "for business or tourism for stays of up to 90 days without a visa" (CBP). An ESTA has cost $40 since 30 September 2025 and is generally valid for two years, according to the US Embassy in London. CBP adds that an ESTA "does not determine whether a traveler is admissible": the officer at the airport decides.

Nationalities outside the programme apply for a B-1/B-2 visitor visa. Federal regulation 8 CFR 214.2(b) admits B visitors for not more than one year, B-2 visitors for a minimum of six months, with extensions in six-month steps.

On work, the texts are short. 8 CFR 214.1(e) says a B-2 visitor "may not engage in any employment", and that unauthorised employment is a failure to maintain status. 22 CFR 41.31 defines permitted business as "conventions, conferences, consultations and other legitimate activities of a commercial or professional nature", which "does not include local employment or labor for hire". The State Department's Foreign Affairs Manual adds that a B-1 visa "is not appropriate for applicants who intend to obtain and engage in employment while in the United States".

None of these texts mentions remote work for a foreign employer by name, and none defines it. The manual calls the Matter of Hira decision "the clearest legal definition": it approved a B-1 activity because the "principal place of business and the actual place of accrual of profits" were in the foreign country. The list of nonimmigrant classes in 8 CFR 214.2 runs from (a) to (w) and contains no remote-work or digital nomad visa. For the countries that do offer one, see our digital nomad visa finder.

The Nashville music scene: the real draw for digital nomads

Music is the lifestyle case for Nashville, and its institutions are old. The Grand Ole Opry first went on air as the WSM Barn Dance on 28 November 1925; it now plays the Opry House at 600 Opry Mills Drive and stages many shows at the Ryman Auditorium downtown. The Ryman opened in 1892 and was the Opry's home from 1943 to 1974.

The rooms that suit a long stay are smaller. The Bluebird Cafe opened on 1 January 1982 as a 100-seat room in a strip mall five miles from downtown, and has run its in-the-round format, four songwriters trading songs at the centre of the room, since 1985. It sells tickets only through its own website with TicketWeb and warns that resale seats are fraudulent.

The Station Inn, at 402 12th Avenue South in The Gulch, has live bluegrass, classic country and Americana nearly every night. CMA Fest, which sells four-night stadium passes, runs 10 to 13 June 2027.

Nashville vs Austin and Atlanta for a remote-work base

Our Austin guide is Nashville's closest match, and on rent Austin now wins. Zillow's index for the city of Austin fell from $1,806 in July 2022 to $1,586 in August 2026, while Nashville's stayed near $1,830. Texas also has no state income tax, so for a US digital nomad Austin keeps the tax advantage with lower asking rents, and our cost data puts the two monthly budgets within $50 of each other.

Atlanta is the other neighbour. Its asking rent was $1,944 in August 2026, higher than both, and Georgia levies a flat state income tax. Atlanta sits in the Eastern time zone, an hour closer to Europe. Nashville's case against both rests on the music institutions above, not on price or tax.

Who should move to Nashville, and who should look elsewhere

Nashville suits a US digital nomad who wants to live near live music, can afford about $3,000 a month, and will either drive or rent next to a frequent bus corridor.

It suits a foreign digital nomad for a 90-day stay timed to avoid the spring tornado peak, with travel medical insurance bought in advance and a clear reading of the employment rules above. It does not suit digital nomads who want a car-free life, a cheap base, or a long legal stay with a laptop job; the countries in our visa finder offer a status the United States does not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Nashville good for digital nomads?

For US citizens, yes: Tennessee has no income tax on wages, fibre is available, and asking rent was flat at about $1,834 in August 2026. For foreign digital nomads it suits stays of up to 90 days, since there is no US remote-work visa and health care is priced for cash payment.

How much does it cost to live in Nashville as a digital nomad?

Plan on about $2,970 a month for one person with a central one-bedroom, or about $2,420 in a cheaper area, based on our Numbeo-sourced cost data for August 2026. HUD's fiscal 2027 one-bedroom rents run from $1,530 in Madison to $2,110 in 12 South, utilities included.

Which Nashville neighbourhood is best for digital nomads without a car?

East Nashville, on route 56 along Gallatin Pike, and The Nations or Sylvan Park, on route 50 along Charlotte Pike, both have buses every 15 minutes or better. Germantown is half a mile from downtown. 12 South is pleasant but relies on route 17, which runs every 20 to 60 minutes.

Can I work remotely in Nashville on an ESTA?

Federal rules say B-2 visitors "may not engage in any employment", and permitted business "does not include local employment or labor for hire". None of the official texts mentions remote work for a foreign employer by name. The Visa Waiver Program allows stays of up to 90 days for business or tourism.

Does Tennessee have a state income tax for remote workers?

No. Tennessee is one of eight states with no individual income tax, and its last tax on interest and dividends, the Hall tax, was repealed from 1 January 2021. Federal income tax still applies. The state relies on sales tax instead, which is 9.75% in Nashville since February 2025.

Can you live in Nashville without a car?

It is possible but uncommon. WeGo caps fares at $65 a month, yet only eight corridors run every 15 minutes or better. In 2024 Census data, 93% of Nashville workers who commuted went by car, and just 1.6% of all workers used public transport. Live on a frequent route and use ride-hail for the rest.

When is tornado season in Nashville?

March to May: the National Weather Service counts 66% of Middle Tennessee tornadoes in those three months, with a smaller second peak around November and the fewest in September. The March 2020 tornado struck Germantown and East Nashville after midnight, so keep phone emergency alerts on.

Is the internet in Nashville fast enough for video calls?

Yes, if the building has fibre or cable. Eighty percent of Nashville households had a cable, fibre or DSL subscription in 2024, and Google Fiber sells symmetric 1 Gig for $70 a month. Check any address on the FCC National Broadband Map before signing a lease.
