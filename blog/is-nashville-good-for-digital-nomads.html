Is Nashville Good for Digital Nomads? Yes, for US Citizens

Yannick Schroth September 2026 15 min read

Yes, if you carry a US passport and want to live inside the music rather than visit it. No, if you need a long legal stay on a foreign passport, a life without a car, or a cheap base. Tennessee takes nothing from your wages, gigabit fibre sells for $70 a month, and one person with a central one-bedroom spends about $3,000 a month. In return, Nashville asks you to drive or to pick your street around a bus line, and to take its spring storms seriously.

What the yes buys is easiest to see on Lower Broadway. On the blocks below Fifth Avenue, the honky-tonks put live bands on stage from around ten in the morning until three the next morning, every day of the year, mostly with no cover: the bands play for tips. Tootsie's Orchid Lounge, painted purple, has its back door on the alley that performers once crossed from the Ryman Auditorium, the 1892 gospel tabernacle with church pews and stained glass that housed the Grand Ole Opry for three decades.

Walk on to the Cumberland and the city changes pace. The Shelby Street Bridge, closed to cars in 1998, carries walkers and cyclists 3,150 feet across the river on boardwalk sidewalks, lit up at night. Keep going east and you reach streets of Victorian and craftsman houses with covered front porches, the coffee shops of Five Points and a three-mile riverside greenway: the Nashville a remote worker actually lives in.

Rent is the pleasant surprise: typical asking rent across the city was $1,834 a month in August 2026, almost exactly where it stood in mid-2022. That makes the budget predictable. The harder questions are the car and the passport.

Does the music live up to the name?

Yes, and not only on Broadway. The Grand Ole Opry first went on air as the WSM Barn Dance in November 1925; today it plays the Opry House out at Opry Mills and returns to the Ryman for many of its shows. The Ryman, the "Mother Church of Country Music", is a working concert hall by night and open for tours by day.

The rooms that reward a long stay are smaller. The Bluebird Cafe has 100 seats in a strip mall five miles from downtown, and since 1985 it has seated songwriters "in the round", four in the middle of the room trading songs. Tickets are sold only on its own website, so plan ahead; a month in town gives you more nights to choose from. The Station Inn, on 12th Avenue South in the Gulch, has bluegrass, classic country and Americana nearly every night.

For a visitor, all of this is a weekend. For someone working from the city for three months, it is a weeknight habit, and this is the part of the answer Nashville wins outright. If music is not why you are coming, the case rests on tax and rent, and on those Austin now does better.

Where should you live?

Choose the bus line before the flat. Rents differ by a few hundred dollars from ZIP to ZIP, but only eight bus corridors run every 15 minutes, and living on one decides whether you need a car. The figures below are HUD's fiscal 2027 fair market rents for a one-bedroom with utilities, set for people moving now. They price unfurnished long leases, so treat them as the floor: a furnished month-to-month flat is a dearer market no public dataset measures.

East Nashville: porches, Five Points and the river bottoms

Across the river from downtown, East Nashville is the stomping ground of the city's creative class: tree-lined streets of Victorian and craftsman houses in Lockeland Springs, dive bars, record stores, vintage shops and coffee at Five Points, where Woodland and 11th Street meet. Its eastern edge is Shelby Bottoms, 960 acres of bottomland forest and open fields along three miles of the Cumberland, with paved trail running its length.

Route 56 runs down Gallatin Pike every 15 minutes or better, and Center 615, a coworking campus on Main Street, sells day passes for $25. A one-bedroom in 37206 runs about $1,720. Without a car, try here first.

Germantown: the market on the doorstep

A few blocks north-west of downtown, Germantown is named for the European immigrants who settled it in the mid-1800s. Its southern edge is the Nashville Farmers' Market, trading since the early 1800s: two covered open-air sheds and a food hall of about 20 restaurants and shops, open all year. Rent is about $1,780, and downtown is a walk.

12 South: a half-mile of shops ending in a park

12 South is a half-mile stretch of 12th Avenue South, from Wedgewood Avenue down to Sevier Park, lined with restaurants, coffee houses, bakeries and boutiques. The original red, white and blue "I Believe in Nashville" mural, painted in 2012, is on a wall at 2700 12th Ave S. At the south end, Sevier Park has nearly 20 acres of playgrounds, trails and a small creek around Sunnyside, a mansion built in 1852.

It is also the dearest one-bedroom in our sample at $2,110, and it is served only by route 17, which comes every 20 to 60 minutes. Live here if you will walk the strip and drive everywhere else.

West, north and the Gulch

The Nations and Sylvan Park, west of downtown, share ZIP 37209 at about $1,860 and the frequent route 50 on Charlotte Pike. The Gulch and Music Row cost about $1,960 and put the Station Inn on your doorstep. Madison, to the north, is the cheapest ZIP we checked, at $1,530.

What will a month really cost?

On our one-person basket, priced from Numbeo, a solo month with a central one-bedroom costs about $2,970, and a leaner month in a cheaper area about $2,420. Everything that is not rent comes to about $1,020. The full breakdown sits on our Nashville city guide.

Eating out costs more than the menu says: a cheap restaurant meal is about $19, before a 15 to 20 percent tip and the 9.75 percent sales tax. Working from a desk is cheap by comparison: day passes run $20 to $25 and a hot desk $100 to $200 a month. Our cost of living index sets that total against every city we cover.

Why the tax math favours a Tennessee address

Tennessee is one of eight US states with no individual income tax. Its last tax on income, the Hall tax on interest and dividends, was cut a point a year and disappeared on 1 January 2021. Federal income tax still applies, but once Tennessee is your home state, your wages owe the state nothing. If you are leaving a state that taxes wages, that is a raise.

The state collects at the till instead. Since 1 February 2025 the sales tax in Nashville and the rest of Davidson County is 9.75 percent, after voters approved half a point more in a November 2024 transit referendum. How your home state or country treats a stay here is a separate question, which our guide to how digital nomads are taxed works through.

What a working day could look like

Mornings can start at home, because the connection is rarely the problem. Four in five households have a cable, fibre or DSL line, and Google Fiber sells symmetric 1 gigabit for $70 a month. Fibre on the street is not fibre in the flat, so check the exact address on the FCC's National Broadband Map before you sign.

Lunch could be the city's own dish. Hot chicken is fried chicken coated in a paste of cayenne and lard, served on white bread with pickle chips; the story goes that Thornton Prince's girlfriend spiked his chicken with pepper as revenge for a late night, and he liked it. Prince's Hot Chicken Shack, still run by his family and named an American Classic by the James Beard Foundation, has a counter in Assembly Food Hall at Fifth + Broadway.

At five o'clock you could cross the pedestrian bridge, stopping at one of its four river overlooks, and walk back once it is lit for the night, or run the paved trail through Shelby Bottoms. The evening is what no other city offers in quite this way: a songwriter round at the Bluebird, bluegrass at the Station Inn, or a band on Broadway that has been playing, in one form or another, since breakfast.

Can you manage without a car?

Just, if you plan for it. WeGo, the city's bus system, is cheap: $2 buys two hours of unlimited rides, and a contactless card or the app caps you at $4 a day and $65 a month. From the airport, route 18 costs the same $2 and takes about 20 minutes on an express trip.

Frequency is the catch. Of 27 local routes, only eight corridors come every 15 minutes or better; the rest come every 20 to 60 minutes, and the WeGo Star train runs only on weekday mornings and afternoons. Of the workers who did not work from home in 2024, 93 percent went by car.

The transit referendum is paying for sidewalks and better bus stops; until they arrive, rent within a short walk of a frequent route, use ride-hail for the rest, and hire a car only for trips out of town.

What do you do at the weekend?

Head for the green edges. Radnor Lake, a state natural area in Oak Hill, has five miles of unpaved trails round a lake where otters and bald eagles live. The Warner Parks, nine miles south-west, cover more than 3,000 acres behind a stone gateway on Belle Meade Boulevard. Closer in, Centennial Park holds a full-scale copy of the Parthenon with a 42-foot gilded Athena inside. Most of this needs a car; the greenways by the river do not.

When should you come?

Spring is storm season. In the National Weather Service's records for Middle Tennessee, 66 percent of tornadoes struck in March, April and May, with a smaller second peak around November and the fewest in September. Most touch down in the afternoon and evening, but not all: on 3 March 2020 an EF-3 tornado crossed the city between 12:32 and 1:35 in the morning, damaging Germantown and Five Points and killing five people.

An EF-2 followed in December 2023 through north Nashville and Madison. In spring, keep emergency alerts on and ask on move-in day where the building's shelter space is.

Then the calendar fills up. In June, CMA Fest brings four nights of stadium concerts, next held on 10 to 13 June 2027. Summer is hot and humid, with July highs around 32°C (90°F), and on the Fourth of July the free Music City Hot Chicken Festival opens in East Park with an antique fire-truck parade before the fireworks at Riverfront Park that night. In August, Five Points dresses up for the free Tomato Art Fest, costumes and parade included.

Aim for September: the heat eases, tornadoes are at their rarest, and the music never takes a season off. May and June come next. Winter is mild, with January highs around 9°C, workable but quieter.

Where the answer turns to no: a foreign passport

The Visa Waiver Program lets citizens of 42 countries visit for business or tourism for up to 90 days without a visa. An ESTA costs $40.27 and is generally valid for two years, but it does not decide whether you get in: the officer at the airport does. Other nationalities need a B-1/B-2 visitor visa, which admits a tourist for six months to a year.

What neither status allows is employment. A visitor for pleasure "may not engage in any employment", and the business a visitor may do means meetings, conferences and consultations, not "local employment or labor for hire". The State Department's manual for visa officers leans on a test of whether the principal place of business and the profits stay abroad.

No rule mentions laptop work for a foreign employer, and no US visa is made for it. Read plainly, that makes Nashville a place to visit with work in the background, not a base for a working year. For countries that do offer a legal remote-work stay, see our digital nomad visa finder.

What happens if you get sick?

A visitor has no health cover, so the number that matters is the cash price. At Metro Nashville General Hospital, a level 3 emergency visit lists a discounted cash price of $657 and a level 5, the most serious, about $1,510. That is only the facility: the hospital's price page says the doctor may bill separately.

An emergency department must screen and stabilise you whatever you can pay: treatment is guaranteed, a free bill is not. For planned care booked three business days ahead, a self-paying patient is owed a Good Faith Estimate, which you need to dispute a bill later. The real fix is travel medical insurance that covers the United States, bought before you fly.

Nashville, Austin or Atlanta?

Austin is Nashville's closest match, and on rent it now wins: typical asking rent in the city fell from about $1,806 in mid-2022 to $1,586 in August 2026, while Nashville's stood still. Texas has no state income tax either, and our monthly budgets for the two sit within $50.

Atlanta asks more on both counts: asking rent near $1,944 and a flat state income tax, though Eastern time puts it an hour closer to Europe. So Nashville's case against both is not price. It is the Ryman, the Bluebird and a street that plays music from breakfast to three in the morning.

So, is Nashville good for digital nomads?

Section by section, the answer from the top holds. For a US remote worker who cares about music, it is a strong yes: no state tax on wages, fair-priced fibre, rents flat for four years and a nightly choice of rooms few cities match. Rent in East Nashville near route 56 and come in September.

On a foreign passport, it shrinks to an insured visit of up to 90 days with the job in the background. And if neither the music nor the tax break draws you, the car and the storms make other cities the better base.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Nashville good for digital nomads?

Yes for US citizens who want the music: no state income tax on wages, $70 gigabit fibre and rents flat since mid-2022. On a foreign passport it suits a visit of up to 90 days.

How much does it cost to live in Nashville as a digital nomad?

About $2,970 a month for one person with a central one-bedroom, or $2,420 in a cheaper area. A one-bedroom with utilities runs from $1,530 in Madison to $2,110 in 12 South; furnished short lets cost more.

Which Nashville neighbourhood is best for digital nomads without a car?

East Nashville, on route 56 along Gallatin Pike, or The Nations and Sylvan Park, on route 50 along Charlotte Pike: both have buses every 15 minutes or better. 12 South relies on route 17, every 20 to 60 minutes.

Can I work remotely in Nashville on an ESTA?

Visitors may not take up employment, and the rules do not mention remote work for a foreign employer. The US has no visa for it, so treat an ESTA stay of up to 90 days as a visit with work in the background.

Does Tennessee have a state income tax for remote workers?

No. Tennessee is one of eight states with no individual income tax, and the Hall tax on interest and dividends ended on 1 January 2021. Federal income tax still applies.

Can you live in Nashville without a car?

Just. WeGo caps fares at $65 a month, but only eight corridors run every 15 minutes or better, and 93 percent of commuters go by car. Live on a frequent route and use ride-hail for the rest.

What is the best time of year to work from Nashville?

September, when the heat eases and tornadoes are rarest. May and June come next, but March to May brings 66 percent of Middle Tennessee's tornadoes, so keep phone emergency alerts on.
