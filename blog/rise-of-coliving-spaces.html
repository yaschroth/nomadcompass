Coliving for Digital Nomads: What a Month Costs in 2026

Yannick Schroth January 2025 10 min read

Contents

Getting a front-door key in a new country is harder than it looks. In Spain, a lease on the home you live in is extended year by year, at the tenant's choice, until it has run five years, or seven if the landlord is a company. In Portugal a residential lease cannot be shorter than a year unless it is written for a temporary purpose. In Germany the landlord may ask for a deposit of up to three months' rent before you get the key, which on a central one-bedroom in Berlin means up to about $4,500 tied up for the length of the lease.

Coliving sells the opposite: a key for a month, sometimes for a week or two, to a furnished private room in a shared house, with the desk, the internet, the bills and at least some of the cleaning in one price, and a group of other remote workers in the kitchen. For a digital nomad that is the whole product. You are paying for a short contract and for company, not for cheap space.

That makes coliving a good fit for your first month somewhere new, for working alone after too many weeks of silence, and for places where a month's lease barely exists, such as a Galician village or a Bulgarian ski town. It is a poor fit if you travel as a couple, want a home to yourself, or plan to stay six months in a cheap city, where a local flat will cost you far less.

It also pays to check that the house still exists. The brands of the last boom have thinned out: Selina went through insolvency and its surviving hotels now trade as Socialtel, Roam's website says it is "taking the year off", and Sun and Co., the coliving in Jávea on Spain's east coast, stopped taking bookings in June 2025. Every operator below was open for bookings on its own website in September 2026.

What the monthly price actually buys

The headline price is rarely the whole bill, and the extras are where colivings differ most. Outpost's Nyuh Kuning house in Ubud starts at $849 a month, but tax and service add 15%, which takes it to about $976. Sende's private room costs about $1,105 a month, and food is extra at about $15 a day for four meals, so a month of full board comes to roughly $1,555. Anceu includes utilities and the basics for breakfast but charges about $5 a day to join the dinners and about $3.50 a load for laundry.

Deposits exist too, just smaller ones: Sende asks about $345 for stays longer than 15 days. And some prices only apply if you stay the full month. At Co404, the month-long rate is half the nightly price, while Nine Coliving takes 10% off stays of 28 nights or more.

So before you compare two houses, write out one month in each: room, tax, food you cannot cook yourself, laundry, deposit and the transport to get there. The cheapest headline is often not the cheapest month.

Is coliving cheaper than renting a flat?

Usually not, room for room. In Bansko, a Valentina studio from about $865 costs close to the $900 a month we estimate for a remote worker's whole budget there, rent, food and all. In Ubud, Outpost's cheapest room after tax, about $976, is more than half of our $1,800 estimate for a full month. Both figures are our estimates rather than measured prices, but the direction is clear: in a cheap town, a local flat on a longer lease costs less.

The sum changes once you count what a flat does not include. A coworking hot desk in Lisbon costs $175 to $290 a month on our Lisbon page, a furnished short let costs more than a long lease almost everywhere, and a Berlin deposit can lock up three months' rent. A coliving folds the desk and the bills into one price and asks for a far smaller deposit, if any.

The honest rule: for one or two months, coliving is often the cheapest furnished option with a proper desk. For six months in one city, find a flat.

Galicia: colivings that fill an empty village

The most distinctive colivings in Europe are not in cities. In Galicia, in Spain's green north-west, two of them are in villages small enough that the coliving is a real part of the place. Sende calls itself a village of 20 inhabitants, with mountain houses, gardens and coworking rooms, views towards the Portuguese border and meals cooked for the house. It opens in the warmer months, until 15 October this year, and caps stays at a month.

Anceu, near Pontevedra, half an hour from the city and an hour from Santiago airport, pairs private ensuite rooms with 1 Gbps symmetric fibre, and says its aim is to "revive rural" Galicia. It is open from March to November for stays of 15 to 60 nights. Both suit deep work and walks rather than nightlife. For a reader who wants a Spanish address and fast internet without a city rent, they are the obvious first look.

Tenerife and the Atlantic islands: winter work

The Canary Islands are Europe's winter office, and Tenerife has one of the best-known houses. Nine Coliving sits in La Orotava, the old town in the valley of the same name on the island's north side, with Teide, Spain's highest peak at 3,718 metres, above it. The minimum stay is 14 nights, and the price includes cleaning, a daily breakfast and a workspace open around the clock.

On the Portuguese side, Outsite, a membership network of coliving houses, lists houses at Ponta do Sol on Madeira and at Ericeira, the surf town north of Lisbon, alongside city houses from Lisbon and Barcelona to Mexico City and Cape Town. If you are weighing Lisbon itself, our complete Lisbon digital nomad guide prices the neighbourhoods for a normal lease.

Bansko: a ski town that became a coliving town

Bansko lies at 925 metres at the foot of the Pirin Mountains, next to the national park of the same name, and a gondola runs up from the town to 75 km of ski runs. It has become a base for remote workers, and the housing followed: Coworking Bansko runs a coliving building of 64 apartments on six floors, and Valentina rents private studios with a kitchenette and bathroom from about $865 a month, with coworking, phone booths and room cleaning included.

Bulgaria is in the Schengen area, so your days here count against the same 90 in any 180 as the rest of the zone on a US, UK, Canadian or Australian passport. Come for the ski season or the summer, when the spa and sauna are open, rather than the shoulder months.

Bali and Thailand: the tropical houses

In Bali, Outpost now concentrates on Ubud, with houses in the villages of Nyuh Kuning and Penestanan; the second starts at $1,149 a month before the 15%. Its Canggu house and its Cambodian location are no longer on its site, a reminder that even an established brand moves around. For desks beyond your coliving, our guide to the best coworking spaces in Bali covers the island.

On Koh Lanta, in southern Thailand, KoHub sells all-inclusive packages for the dry season, 1 November to 30 April: a private garden bungalow with air conditioning, two meals a day and coworking, booked by the month. Bookings for the 2026 to 2027 season are open.

Mexico and Colombia: the community houses

Co404 runs three houses: in Oaxaca, whose colonial centre is a UNESCO World Heritage site, in San Cristóbal de las Casas and in Medellín. It is built around the group, with a family dinner every Wednesday, daily activities and soundproofed call booths for work. Rooms in Oaxaca start at $553 a month, the cheapest verified price in this guide.

How to choose a coliving

Start with the calendar. Many of these houses are seasonal: Sende closes in mid-October, Anceu runs from March to November, KoHub from November to April. A house that is perfect in June may be shut, or empty, in January.

Then check the work setup, not the photos. Ask for a speed test from the desk you will use, and ask where you take calls: Co404 and Valentina both advertise call booths, and Anceu publishes its fibre speed. If your day is back-to-back video calls, a house that only offers the dining table will not work.

Size decides the atmosphere. A village house with a dozen people and shared dinners feels nothing like a 64-apartment building, and neither is better. Decide which you want before you book. Finally, read the cancellation terms: the closures above are why a refundable deposit matters more than a small discount.

Who coliving is for

Book a coliving if you are new to working on the road, arriving somewhere you do not know anyone, or staying one or two months in a place where short leases barely exist. It solves the key, the desk and the loneliness in one booking, and a good house gives your days a steady remote-work routine from the first morning.

Skip it if you travel as a couple, need silence to work, or plan to stay long in a cheap city: a flat and a coworking membership will cost less and give you more room. Either way, pick the city first and the house second.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is coliving for digital nomads?

A furnished private room in a shared house, rented by the week or month, with internet, bills, a workspace and some cleaning included, and other remote workers as housemates.

How much does coliving cost per month?

Among the houses in this guide, from $553 a month in Oaxaca to about $1,105 plus meals in rural Galicia. Check the extras: Outpost adds 15% tax and service, and food and laundry are often charged separately.

Is coliving cheaper than renting an apartment?

For one or two months, often, because a furnished short let costs more and a coworking desk is extra. For six months or more in a cheap city, a normal lease usually costs less.

What is the minimum stay at a coliving?

Usually one to four weeks: 14 nights at Nine Coliving in Tenerife, 15 at Anceu, one week at Sende. Many give the best rate only to stays of 28 nights or more.

Is Selina still open for coliving?

Not under that name. Selina went through insolvency, and its remaining hotels now trade as Socialtel, which does not advertise a monthly coliving product on its website.

Where are the best places for coliving in Europe?

Rural Galicia for quiet and fast fibre, Tenerife and Madeira for winter, Ericeira for surf and Bansko for skiing and a nomad scene. All are in Schengen, so the 90-day rule applies.
