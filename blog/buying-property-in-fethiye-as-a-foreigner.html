Buying Property in Fethiye as a Foreigner: Tapu and Lawyers

Turkish law does not require a lawyer for buying property in Fethiye as a foreigner. What it does require is a currency conversion certificate from a Turkish bank, a valid earthquake insurance policy, a court-authorised translator if you do not speak Turkish, and title deed fees of 4 percent of the price, split by law between buyer and seller. This guide walks through each step at the Fethiye land registry, the rules that changed in 2023 and 2024, and the points where a lawyer earns the fee.

All prices are in US dollars at the Central Bank of Turkey exchange rate of 48.88 lira to the dollar (forex selling, 25 September 2026). Two thresholds frame most decisions: a home worth at least $200,000 can support a residence permit, and $400,000 opens the citizenship route.

Buying property in Fethiye as a foreigner: who is allowed

The legal basis is Article 35 of the Land Registry Law No. 2644. It lets citizens of countries designated by the President buy property and limited real rights in Turkey. The land registry authority, Tapu ve Kadastro Genel Müdürlüğü (TKGM), says citizens of 185 countries qualify under Cabinet decision 2012/3504, and that the list is not published. Any land registry office will confirm your nationality on request.

Dual nationals are assessed on the more restricted passport, so a second citizenship from an excluded country blocks the purchase. If one of your nationalities is Turkish, you buy as a Turkish citizen and none of the foreigner rules in this guide apply.

Two area caps sit in the same article. One foreign individual may hold at most 30 hectares across the whole of Turkey, and foreigners together may hold no more than 10 percent of the privately owned area of any district. For a flat in Çalış or a villa in Ovacık, neither cap comes close to binding, and the registry checks the district figure itself.

Restricted land around Fethiye: military, security and heritage zones

Under the Military Forbidden Zones and Security Zones Law No. 2565, foreigners cannot acquire property inside second-degree military zones, and the President can bar foreign ownership near them. This used to mean a separate clearance letter from the local command. It no longer does: TKGM says the maps for all 81 provinces are now recorded in the land registry, so the Fethiye office answers from the register without writing to the military.

Two other zones still involve another authority. A plot inside a special security zone needs permission from the provincial governor, the Muğla Valiliği. A plot inside a protected heritage site (sit alanı) is referred to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism or the Ministry of Environment, depending on the site. Fethiye has real exposure here: Kayaköy, the abandoned Greek village 8 km south of the town, is a historical monument under state protection.

Bare land carries its own obligation. A foreigner who buys an unbuilt plot must submit a development project to the relevant ministry within two years. Miss that, or fail to finish the approved project on time, and the land is liquidated: the owner gets up to a year to sell, after which the state sells it and pays over the proceeds. If you want land in the hills above Üzümlü, budget for an architect from day one.

The Fethiye property purchase, step by step

TKGM's own Your Key Türkiye portal sets out the sequence for a foreign buyer. In practice it runs in nine steps.

  1. Agree the price and get the plot details: the ada (block), parsel (parcel) and, for a flat, the independent unit number. Every later document quotes them.
  2. Check the title and the building: the land registry record, the occupancy certificate and the zoning status (covered in the next section).
  3. Get a Turkish tax number, because the deed records the tax ID of both parties.
  4. Convert the price at a Turkish bank and have it issue a foreign currency purchase certificate (DAB), sent to the registry electronically.
  5. Buy compulsory earthquake insurance (DASK) for the building or flat.
  6. Apply through WebTapu or at any land registry office, with passport, photo and identity declaration form.
  7. Pay the title deed fee and the registry service fee once the office texts you the amounts.
  8. Sign the deed at the time the office sends by SMS, with a sworn translator if either side does not speak Turkish.
  9. After completion, file a property tax declaration with Fethiye Municipality and move the utilities into your name.

Steps 2 and 4 carry most of the risk, and both happen before the registry is involved. After you apply, TKGM runs the rest by text message.

Checks on a Fethiye title deed before you pay anything

Start with the land registry record for the exact unit. It shows the registered owner, any mortgage (ipotek), any attachment by a creditor (haciz) and any annotation (şerh), such as a pre-emption right or an earlier sale promise. Ask the seller for a current copy before any deposit changes hands. If the name on the record is not the person you are negotiating with, stop until someone explains why.

Next, read the type of title. Kat mülkiyeti is full condominium ownership of a finished, certified flat. Kat irtifakı is construction servitude: a share in land with the right to a future flat, which is what an off-plan or unfinished building carries. It is legal to buy, but it means the building has not been signed off.

The sign-off is the occupancy certificate, yapı kullanma izin belgesi, known locally as iskan. Under Article 30 of the Zoning Law No. 3194 it is issued by the municipality once the building matches its permit, and the municipality has 30 days to decide. A villa without iskan may have been extended beyond its permit, which matters twice over: the earthquake insurer may refuse to cover a building altered against its project.

Some Fethiye houses instead hold a Yapı Kayıt Belgesi, the building registration certificate from the 2018 zoning amnesty. With it, the law allows conversion to full condominium title without iskan, provided every owner consents and a double fee is paid. It regularises the paperwork, not the structure.

Paying for a Fethiye home: the currency rule and the declared price

Since 2022 a foreign buyer cannot simply transfer lira. Article 13 of the Central Bank's Capital Movements Circular says anyone without Turkish citizenship must pay for Turkish property in foreign currency, sold to a bank before the deed is signed, with the seller receiving lira. The bank issues a Döviz Alım Belgesi (DAB) and sends it to the land registry the same day by registered e-mail. You cannot carry it in by hand.

The DAB must name you, your passport or foreign ID number, the property number and the dollar equivalent. Only the buyer, the seller or their proxies may make the conversion. TKGM confirms there are no exemptions: long-term residence permit holders and foreigners married to Turks need one too. The price can be converted in several tranches, in which case the certificates are added together.

The same article fixes the declared price. The lira amount on the DAB is what you declare as the sale price, and the deed is drawn up on that figure. Separately, the Fees Law No. 492 says the title fee is charged on the declared price but never less than the municipal tax value, and it adds back the missing fee plus a penalty if an under-declaration is found. Declaring a low price on a Fethiye deed is not available to a foreign buyer.

The valuation report for foreign buyers in Fethiye: now for citizenship only

Many guides still say a foreign buyer must commission an appraisal report from a valuer licensed by the Capital Markets Board (SPK). That rule applied from March 2019. TKGM's official FAQ for foreigners now answers the question directly: a valuation report is not compulsory for all foreign transactions, and its foreign-buyer page says the report requirement for foreign individuals has been lifted.

The report survives in one place. Under TKGM circular 2024/2, in force from 1 March 2024, a buyer seeking citizenship needs a report prepared by GEDAŞ Gayrimenkul Değerleme A.Ş., ordered through WebTapu. From it the system issues a Tutar Tespit Belgesi (TTB), an amount confirmation certificate valid for six months, which must cover the $400,000 threshold.

For an ordinary purchase, an independent valuation is optional. The DAB fixes the price you pay, not what the property is worth, and an SPK-licensed valuer's figure is the one neutral number you can get before converting your money.

Title deed fees in Fethiye: who pays what

The title deed fee (tapu harcı) is set in tariff 4 of the Fees Law: 2 percent from the buyer and 2 percent from the seller, each charged separately on the declared price. Buyer and seller can agree in their contract that one side pays both halves. That is a private arrangement. The registry still treats each party as liable for its own share.

  • Title deed fee, buyer's share: 2 percent. On a $200,000 home that is $4,000.
  • Title deed fee, seller's share: 2 percent, the same amount again.
  • Registry service fee (döner sermaye): a fixed charge set yearly by TKGM. The office texts you the exact figure with the fee.
  • Earthquake insurance (DASK): priced from a national tariff of 14 rates, by earthquake risk zone and building type, and renewed every year.
  • Sworn translator: a fee agreed directly with the translator.
  • Lawyer: optional, agreed in a written fee contract.

The order is fixed. Once your file is complete, the registry sends the fee and service charge amounts by SMS, and only after payment does it draw up the deed and text you a signing time.

At the Fethiye land registry: the tapu appointment

You do not have to sign in Fethiye. Since 1 January 2020 any land registry office in Turkey can complete a transfer outside its own district, so a buyer and seller in different cities can use different offices. TKGM also runs an overseas office in its Berlin consulate. Appointments are booked online at randevu.tkgm.gov.tr or through the Alo 181 line.

Bring your passport, a recent 6x4 cm photo and the identity declaration form, the Kimlik Bilgileri Beyan Formu, which every foreign party fills in. If you do not yet have a foreign ID number, the registry allocates one on the spot. The office also needs the municipal declared value of the property, which some municipalities feed into WebTapu directly, plus the DAB and the DASK policy.

If either party does not speak Turkish, a sworn translator must attend. It has to be one authorised by a regional judicial commission, not a friend who is fluent. Our Fethiye services directory lists 62 translators in the town taken from the Ministry of Justice register, with the languages each one covers.

Buying from abroad means a power of attorney. One signed at a foreign notary is accepted in the original or as a certified copy, with a Turkish translation. It must carry your photograph, sealed or stamped by the notary: TKGM rejects one without it, and a passport copy stapled to it does not count. Foreign consulates inside Turkey cannot issue one for Turkish property.

Buying off-plan property in Fethiye: what the consumer law guarantees

New developments around Fethiye are often sold before completion. Consumer Protection Law No. 6502 treats this as a pre-paid housing sale, and a foreign buyer is protected like any other consumer. The first rule is the one to test: no pre-paid sale may be made before the building permit (yapı ruhsatı) is issued. Ask for the permit number and confirm it with the municipality before paying a deposit.

The contract itself has a form requirement. It must be registered at the land registry, or made as a sales promise contract drawn up by a notary. The developer must hand you a pre-information form at least one day before signing, and cannot ask for money under any label before a valid contract exists.

Four more protections follow. You can withdraw within 14 days without reason or penalty. Delivery must happen by the contract date and never later than 48 months after signing. You can cancel for any reason within 24 months, owing compensation that rises from 2 percent of the price in the first three months to 8 percent in months 12 to 24. Projects above a size set by the Ministry of Trade must also carry building completion insurance or an equivalent guarantee.

The currency rule applies to off-plan too: a DAB covering at least the amount paid up front, issued no later than the date of the notarised contract. A deposit requested by ordinary transfer before the notary falls outside every protection above.

Where a Fethiye lawyer fits in a property purchase

Nothing in the land registry process makes a lawyer compulsory. The deed is drawn up by the registry itself, and the parties sign in front of a registry official. What the law does reserve for lawyers is legal advice: Article 35 of the Attorneys Law No. 1136 says giving opinions on legal questions and drafting the documents for legal proceedings belongs only to lawyers registered with a bar.

In a Fethiye purchase, a lawyer can read the registry record, check iskan and zoning at the municipality, review the sale contract, sign for you under a power of attorney while you are abroad, and assemble a citizenship file. On an off-plan purchase, checking the notarised contract against Law 6502 is where the fee pays for itself.

Two rules help you check who you are hiring. Only Turkish citizens can be admitted as lawyers in Turkey, and each must be on the roll of a bar association, which is organised by province. A lawyer practising in Fethiye belongs to the Muğla Bar Association, which also covers Bodrum and Marmaris. Ask for the bar registration number and a written fee agreement before you hand over a power of attorney.

Turkey residence permit by property: the $200,000 rule in Fethiye

Owning a home is one of the listed grounds for a short-term residence permit under Article 31 of the Law on Foreigners and International Protection No. 6458. The old split between big cities and smaller towns is gone. The Presidency of Migration Management's document checklist now sets one national figure: the home must have been worth at least $200,000 at the date you acquired it, converted at the Central Bank rate.

The conversion uses the rate on the day you bought, so a later fall in the lira does not push an existing home under the threshold. The checklist asks for a copy of the title deed, a numbering certificate (numarataj belgesi) from the municipality confirming the address on first application, and a current title continuation document at renewal. A home received by inheritance or gift needs a fresh valuation report instead of a purchase price.

The permit only works if you live in the property. The checklist states that the home must be residential, used by the foreigner as a residence, and cannot be used for rent or similar income.

That rules out a villa let to holidaymakers in summer. Short lets are also regulated separately: Law No. 7464 of 2023 requires a Ministry of Culture and Tourism permit for any letting of up to 100 days, and in a building with several units, the unanimous consent of every owner.

Each permit runs for up to two years and is renewed while you still own the home; applications are decided within 90 days, and renewals open 60 days before expiry. Eight uninterrupted years on residence permits make you eligible for long-term residence. If you want to stay without buying, Turkey's digital nomad visa asks for $3,000 a month in income for applicants aged 21 to 55; our nomad visa finder compares it with neighbouring countries.

Turkish citizenship through a Fethiye property: the $400,000 route

The citizenship threshold rose from $250,000 to $400,000 for purchases from 13 June 2022. TKGM's citizenship page sets out the conditions: property worth at least that sum, a land registry annotation that it will not be sold for three years, the full price converted through a bank with a DAB, and a TTB from the GEDAŞ valuation confirming the value.

Two cut-off dates matter on the Fethiye market. Since 1 February 2023, shares of a property no longer count. Since 12 December 2023, agricultural land and unbuilt plots do not count either, so a field with olive trees in the hinterland cannot carry an application. You may combine several properties to reach $400,000, as long as none falls into those excluded categories.

After signing, TKGM issues a Real Estate Investment Certificate to the migration and civil registry authorities. You apply for a residence permit on that basis, then for citizenship, which is granted by presidential decision.

Owning property in Fethiye: annual property tax and insurance

Property tax (emlak vergisi) is levied by the municipality, not the state. The Real Estate Tax Law No. 1319 sets the rate for homes at 0.1 percent of the tax value, doubled inside metropolitan municipalities. Muğla became a metropolitan municipality under Law No. 6360, so a home in Fethiye pays 0.2 percent a year. The base is the municipal tax value, worked out from land unit values and construction cost tables, not your purchase price.

You pay Fethiye Municipality in two equal instalments, the first in March to May and the second in November. The Revenue Administration's property tax guide says a buyer must file a declaration by the end of the year of purchase, or within three months if buying in the last quarter, and liability starts the following year. Homes in neighbourhoods designated rural by the metropolitan council are exempt.

Earthquake insurance is renewed every year. Under the Disaster Insurance Law No. 6305, water and electricity companies check for a valid policy when you open or move a subscription, and the land registry will not register a later transaction without one. The policy carries a 2 percent deductible on any claim. Fethiye has reason to take it seriously: a pair of 1957 earthquakes damaged most of the town.

Selling property in Fethiye: capital gains tax and moving money home

Hold the property for five full years and the gain on sale is not taxed in Turkey. Sell sooner and it is taxable income under Article 80 of the Income Tax Law, counted in calendar days from the purchase date. The Revenue Administration's 2026 guide to capital gains sets the annual exemption for sales in 2026 in lira; at the rate above it is worth about $3,070.

Indexation softens the bill further. The purchase price is adjusted by the producer price index whenever that index has risen by 10 percent or more between purchase and sale. The Revenue Administration's own table shows it climbing from 3,035.59 in January 2024 to 4,783.04 in December 2025, a rise of 58 percent. The taxable remainder is then charged at the progressive income tax rates, from 15 to 40 percent.

How you declare depends on residence. A tax resident reports the gain in the annual return each March. A non-resident seller files a separate one-off return with the tax office where the property sits, within 15 days of the sale. The seller also pays their own 2 percent title deed fee on the way out.

Getting the money out is not restricted. The first paragraph of Article 13 of the Capital Movements Circular states that non-residents may transfer abroad the net proceeds of selling Turkish property, after tax and similar obligations, through a bank. Keep your purchase DAB as the record of what came in.

Bodrum sits in the same province, so everything above, from the 0.2 percent tax rate to the Muğla Bar Association, applies there too. If it is also on your list, our Bodrum digital nomad guide covers what living there is like before you look at a deed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer to buy property in Fethiye?

No. The land registry draws up the deed and no law requires a lawyer at the signing. You do need a court-authorised sworn translator if you do not speak Turkish. A lawyer becomes useful for title and iskan checks, contract review, off-plan purchases, citizenship files and signing by power of attorney while you are abroad.

Can foreigners buy property in Fethiye?

Yes, if your country is among the 185 whose citizens may buy in Turkey; any land registry office confirms this. Limits are 30 hectares per person nationwide and 10 percent of a district's private land for all foreigners together. Military zones are marked in the register, and unbuilt land needs a development project within two years.

How much are title deed fees when buying property in Fethiye?

The title deed fee is 4 percent of the declared price: 2 percent from the buyer and 2 percent from the seller, though the contract can assign both to one side. A fixed registry service fee is added, and the office sends both amounts by SMS. On a $200,000 home, each 2 percent share comes to $4,000.

Is a valuation report still mandatory for foreigners buying in Fethiye?

Not for an ordinary purchase. TKGM's foreigners FAQ says a valuation report is not compulsory for all foreign transactions. Since circular 2024/2 took effect on 1 March 2024, a GEDAŞ report and the resulting amount certificate are required for citizenship applications. An independent valuation remains a sensible check on price.

What is the minimum property value for a Turkish residence permit in Fethiye?

$200,000, measured at the date you acquired the home and converted at the Central Bank rate on that day. The figure is the same in every Turkish city. The home must be residential, lived in by you, and not rented out for income.

Can I get Turkish citizenship by buying a home in Fethiye?

Yes, with property worth at least $400,000, a three-year no-sale annotation on the deed, the price converted through a bank with a DAB, and a GEDAŞ-based amount certificate. Agricultural land and unbuilt plots bought after 12 December 2023 do not qualify, nor do shares bought after 1 February 2023.

How much property tax do you pay on a home in Fethiye?

0.2 percent of the municipal tax value each year, because Fethiye sits inside the Muğla metropolitan municipality, where the 0.1 percent residential rate is doubled. The tax value comes from municipal tables, not your price. It is paid to Fethiye Municipality in March to May and November.

Do I pay capital gains tax when selling property in Fethiye?

Only if you sell within five years of buying. The gain above the 2026 exemption, set in lira and worth about $3,070, is taxed at 15 to 40 percent after inflation indexing. Non-residents file a one-off return with the local tax office within 15 days. After five full years, the gain is tax-free.
