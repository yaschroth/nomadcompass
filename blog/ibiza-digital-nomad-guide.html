Ibiza Digital Nomad Guide: Costs and the Off-Season

Yannick Schroth August 2026 9 min read

Ibiza scores 10 for nightlife and 3 for cost, and those two numbers are the same fact stated twice. The island that made itself famous in July and August is close to unworkable in July and August, and genuinely good the rest of the year. Everything below assumes you came to work.

Off-peak, a solo nomad month runs $2,530 to $4,030. In summer the same apartment costs two or three times what it does in November. Getting the season right is worth more here than any other decision you will make.

Cost of living in Ibiza: the most expensive island in Spain

A one-bedroom in Ibiza Town runs $1,610 to $2,530 a month in low season. In peak summer the same unit doubles or triples, if it is available at all, because the holiday-let market outbids anyone looking for a long stay. Our Ibiza city page puts a full month at around $4,000 all in.

Groceries and restaurants sit above mainland Spain but are not extreme outside the peak months. What breaks budgets is not the supermarket, it is the summer surcharge on everything: rent, taxis, a sunbed, a table at a beach restaurant.

The honest comparison is not Ibiza against Valencia. It is Ibiza in November against Ibiza in August, and that gap is larger than the gap between most European cities. Our Spain ranking has cheaper bases if the island itself is not the point.

The season decides everything

For remote work the window is October through May. That is not a preference, it is the difference between an island that functions and one that does not.

Autumn, September to November, gives you 20 to 26C days, clear skies, and an island that empties out once the clubs close for the season. Rents drop sharply, restaurants revert to local pricing, and you can get a table without booking. September is also the wettest month, around 94mm.

Spring, April to June, is the mirror image: 18 to 25C, long days, the island waking up without the crowds. Winter, December to February, sits around 10 to 15C, is very cheap and very quiet, and closes a lot of the beach infrastructure. It suits people who want solitude.

July and August are genuinely difficult for focused work. Highs hit 31C, accommodation costs two to three times more, club noise in the tourist zones runs until dawn, and a basic errand takes twice as long. If you have no choice, base yourself in the quiet north around Sant Joan.

Internet: fibre in the towns, 4G in the hills

We rate wifi 6 out of 10, and the score is an average across a split island. Fibre reaching 100 to 300 Mbps is available in Ibiza Town, Santa Eulalia and Sant Antoni. In the rural and northern parts you may be on slower ADSL or a 4G connection.

Coworking is reliable. Short-term apartments are the variable: speeds vary enormously and listings are optimistic. Ask the host for a screenshot of a speed test before you book, not after. This is the single most common avoidable mistake on the island.

Where to work in Ibiza

The coworking set is small but real, and concentrated around Ibiza Town.

theHUB Ibiza

In Santa Eulalia des Riu, and the largest purpose-built option: private offices, meeting rooms, music studios and a coworking floor on reliable fibre. Day and monthly passes.

Origen Ibiza

On Carrer de s'Illa Plana near Talamanca Beach, with 24/7 access on a monthly fibre subscription, private offices, and a small tight community. The best pick if you want the same faces every day.

The Business Space Ibiza

On Passeig de Vara de Rey, the most central option, a few steps from the old town. Daily and 10-day passes make it the easy choice for a short stay.

Cowork Group Ibiza

Carrer de Sant Cristofol in Ibiza Town, with 500 Mbps fibre, individual lockers and a relaxed wood-and-plants aesthetic.

Cafes for laptop work

Cafe culture here is built around brunch and evening socialising rather than laptops. Two exceptions: Passion Cafe, with several Ibiza Town locations, reliable wifi and a menu of bowls and smoothies, welcoming to laptops in the quieter morning hours; and Croissant Show near Dalt Vila, an institution for morning sessions, busy and social rather than hushed.

Where to base yourself

Five areas carry almost everyone, and they are not interchangeable.

Dalt Vila, the UNESCO fortress above Ibiza Town, is the cultural heart: medieval streets, panoramic views, steep climbs, expensive dining. Marina Botafoch is the superyacht address and priced accordingly. Playa d'en Bossa is where the big clubs are, which makes it the one place you should not live if you have morning calls.

San Antonio is the sunset strip and the more accessible party town. Santa Gertrudis, inland and quiet, is where a lot of long-stayers actually end up. Santa Eulalia, on the east coast, is the other sensible base and where theHUB sits.

Getting around: budget for a scooter

The island is about 45 km north to south and you can reach any corner in 45 minutes by car. The bus network is cheap but not frequent enough for most working schedules.

ALSA runs 17 metropolitan and 33 interurban lines, with year-round routes linking Ibiza Town to Sant Antoni and Santa Eulalia. Fares are $2.50 to $4.50 a trip. The airport bus into Ibiza Town takes about 20 minutes for roughly $4 to $4.50.

A rental car is $29 to $46 a day. For a long stay a small scooter at $230 to $405 a month is the practical answer. Taxis work through the TaxiClick app or ranks, with the airport run to Ibiza Town at roughly $23 to $29. Parking in town in summer is genuinely hard.

Community: real, small, and off-season

Community scores 6 out of 10. There is a growing international nomad and expat scene, mostly in Ibiza Town and Santa Eulalia, and theHUB and Origen both run regular events.

It is smaller and much less organised than Lisbon or Barcelona. The important detail is when it exists: from October to May, long-stay workers outnumber party tourists, and that is when the scene is actually visible. Arrive in August and you will meet holidaymakers.

The 10 for nightlife, and what it means in practice

Ibiza is one of six cities in our index with a perfect nightlife score, alongside Berlin, Madrid, Miami, New York and Cali, and it is the only one of the six that is an island of 150,000 people. The club season runs roughly May to early October. Berlin takes first place in our nightlife ranking on the strength of everything around the clubs; Ibiza wins on the clubs themselves.

The trade is direct. Peak nightlife and peak workability are the same months in opposite directions. Most people who work from Ibiza long-term settle on a compromise: arrive in May or September, catch the tail of the season, and stay through the quiet months.

Visas: Schengen 90 days, or the Spanish nomad visa

EU citizens can simply live and work here. Non-EU nationals, including US, UK, Canadian and Australian passport holders, get up to 90 days visa-free in the Schengen area, which covers a season but not a year.

Spain's Digital Nomad Visa, running since 2023, allows non-EU remote workers to stay for one to five years. It requires proof of income above $3,280 a month and remote work for clients or employers outside Spain. That floor is 200% of Spain's minimum wage, so it rises whenever the minimum wage does. Check the current figure rather than a number in an article, including this one. Our nomad visa finder lists what we hold and the rule behind it.

Language and everyday admin

English scores 7 out of 10. It is widely spoken in tourist-facing businesses, hotels, restaurants and coworking spaces across the island. Locals and long-term residents mostly use Spanish and Catalan, in the Eivissenc dialect.

Outside the tourist zones, English drops off quickly. Basic Spanish smooths markets, pharmacies and anything involving paperwork. For professional help our Ibiza services directory lists providers by the language they actually work in, currently 17 across English, French, Italian and German.

Who Ibiza is for

It suits an established earner who wants Mediterranean nature, a good climate and an island that is beautiful when nobody is looking, and who can afford a 3 out of 10 for cost. Nature scores 8 and air quality 9, and both are easy to forget in the club coverage.

It does not suit anyone on a tight budget, anyone arriving in July without checking rents first, or anyone who needs a large organised nomad community. If the beach matters more than the brand, our city index has far cheaper Mediterranean options.

Frequently asked questions about Ibiza

Can you actually work remotely from Ibiza?

Yes, from October to May. Fibre reaching 100 to 300 Mbps covers Ibiza Town, Santa Eulalia and Sant Antoni, and there are four dedicated coworking spaces. July and August are the hard months, on both cost and noise.

How much does a month in Ibiza cost?

Between $2,530 and $4,030 for a solo nomad off-peak, with a one-bedroom at $1,610 to $2,530. In peak summer accommodation costs two to three times more, which is what pushes a month well past $4,000.

When is the cheapest time to stay in Ibiza?

December to February. Temperatures sit around 10 to 15C, rents are at their lowest, and the island is very quiet. Many beach restaurants and some amenities are closed for the season.

Do I need a car in Ibiza?

Not necessarily, but you need something. Buses are cheap at $2.50 to $4.50 a trip and not frequent enough for most schedules. A scooter at $230 to $405 a month is the usual long-stay answer; a car runs $29 to $46 a day.

What visa do I need to stay in Ibiza longer than 90 days?

Non-EU nationals need Spain's Digital Nomad Visa, which covers one to five years and requires income above $3,280 a month from clients or employers outside Spain. That threshold is pegged at 200% of the Spanish minimum wage and moves with it.

Which part of Ibiza should a remote worker stay in?

Ibiza Town or Santa Eulalia for the connection and the coworking, Santa Gertrudis if you want quiet and are willing to drive. Avoid Playa d'en Bossa and San Antonio in season unless you enjoy working after a night of club noise.

Is the nomad community in Ibiza any good?

It is small but real, scoring 6 out of 10, and concentrated in Ibiza Town and Santa Eulalia. theHUB and Origen both run events. It is most visible between October and May, when long-stay workers outnumber tourists.
