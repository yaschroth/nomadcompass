Luxembourg Digital Nomad Guide: Internet, Rent and Visas

Internet for digital nomads in Luxembourg is close to a solved problem. At the end of 2025, fibre reached 86.6% of homes and premises and a gigabit-capable fixed line reached 95.9%, according to the national regulator, the ILR. Its own speed-test tool measured an average fixed download of 207 Mbit/s. The connection is not what decides whether Luxembourg works for you. Rent and the visa are, and this guide covers both after the internet.

We rate Luxembourg City 9 out of 10 for wifi and 10 for safety, and 1 out of 10 for cost, the lowest score we give. Our comfortable solo budget is about $5,500 a month, which is our own estimate rather than a measured basket. Prices below are in US dollars at the European Central Bank reference rate of 25 September 2026 (EUR 1 = $1.1403).

Internet in Luxembourg for digital nomads: fibre, speeds and coverage

The ILR's 2025 telecoms statistics report, published in June 2026, is the source for coverage. Fibre to the premises covered 86.6% of households and premises at the end of 2025. Cable on DOCSIS 3.1 covered 81.5%. Counting every technology able to deliver 1 Gbps, coverage reached 95.9%. Fibre is also what people actually buy: 203,500 lines, or 72.8% of all fixed internet subscriptions.

Coverage is uneven at the edges. The ILR names Walferdange (95.8%) and Bettembourg (95.7%) as the best-covered communes for fibre, and Vichten (22.3%) and Dalheim (24.5%) as the weakest. Twelve communes were still below 50% at the end of 2025. If you are renting outside the capital, check the address on the ILR coverage maps at myilr.lu or geoportail.lu before you sign.

For measured speed, the ILR runs a free test tool, checkmynet.lu. Its open internet report for May 2024 to April 2025 gives an average fixed download of 207 Mbit/s, upload of 128 Mbit/s and latency of 23.7 ms. About 19% of fixed tests came in below 30 Mbit/s. The ILR notes that home wifi drags these results down, so a wired fibre line should beat the average.

Gigabit plans are now mainstream rather than premium. Subscriptions at 1 Gbps or more rose 66.9% in 2025 to 78,900, and more than half of the 294 fixed internet offers on the ILR's price comparator, smartcompare.lu, were at least 1 Gbps in May 2026. The average fixed internet customer paid $64 a month in 2025. Use the comparator to price a line for a longer stay.

Luxembourg mobile operators and the prepaid SIM rules

Three networks carry almost the whole market. By subscribers at the end of 2025, POST Luxembourg held 50.3%, Proximus Luxembourg, which sells to consumers under the Tango brand, held 32.9%, and Orange held 15.8%. The ILR's coverage data separates them on 5G: POST's gigabit-class 5G reached more than 75% of the population, Proximus almost 70%, and Orange less than both.

Across all networks, 5G able to deliver 1 Gbps covered 78.7% of households and premises, but only about 29% of the territory, because the operators concentrated it in the south, the capital and the larger northern towns. On checkmynet.lu, 5G tests averaged 229 Mbit/s down and 45 Mbit/s up, and 4G tests 102 Mbit/s down. That makes a phone hotspot a realistic backup in the towns where 5G is concentrated.

Prepaid is a niche here. Of 1,016,900 SIM cards at the end of 2025, only 24,500 were prepaid, and that number fell 22.4% in a year. There is no anonymous option: a law of 7 June 2017 amending the electronic communications act obliged operators to deactivate every prepaid card with a Luxembourg number whose holder had not identified themselves, with a deadline of 12 July 2017. Bring a passport or national ID card to the shop.

A SIM from another EU or EEA country may be enough for a short stay, since the EU's roam-like-at-home rules charge domestic prices. The limit is residence: your operator can review roaming over a four-month window, and if you spend more time abroad than at home and use more data abroad than at home, it can warn you and then add surcharges. Anyone basing themselves in Luxembourg for a season should expect to switch.

Free public wifi and free public transport in Luxembourg

Every bus, tram and train inside Luxembourg has been free since 1 March 2020, per mobiliteit.lu, the national transport portal. Two exceptions matter: first class on trains still needs a ticket, and free travel ends at the border, so a trip to Trier, Arlon or Thionville needs a cross-border ticket. For a remote worker living in the country, transport drops out of the budget entirely.

The rail operator CFL says every station and stop in the country has had a CFL Free WiFi hotspot since the beginning of 2024. In the city itself, the citywifi network run by HOTCITY advertises more than 1,000 antennas and over 50,000 connections a day across partner municipalities including Luxembourg City, Esch-sur-Alzette, Dudelange and Strassen.

The citywifi Free tier costs nothing and has no data cap, but it has limits for work. You need a mobile number to receive an SMS code when you sign up, and the network is built for outdoor use, so coverage inside buildings is not guaranteed. VPN connections are listed as a feature of the Premium tier, at $5.59 a month with speeds up to 100 Mbit/s. For a corporate VPN, plan on your own line or a SIM, not the square outside.

Coworking and free places to work in Luxembourg City

Our Luxembourg City guide lists the coworking spaces we have priced. The Office, in the Ville-Haute and near the station, sells hot desks from about $220 a month and day passes around $29. Paladium, in Belair, charges about $23 a day or $345 a month with 24-hour access for members. Silversquare Liberté, near the station, prices flex desks around $455 a month. Expect to budget $220 to $460 a month for a desk.

The free option is the National Library (BnL) on Avenue John F. Kennedy in Kirchberg. Registration is free for anyone aged 14 or over with valid ID and lasts two years. The library opens 10:00 to 20:00 on weekdays and 10:00 to 18:00 on Saturdays. The main reading room bans phone calls, but members can book one of eleven group rooms free of charge, for up to two hours a day and four bookings a week, which is enough for a standing call.

Our city guide rates cafe working as thinner than in Lisbon or Berlin: many restaurants turn tables over at lunch and do not want laptops, so budget for a desk rather than a cafe.

Luxembourg rent by commune and city quarter

The government's housing observatory, the Observatoire de l'Habitat, publishes advertised rents by commune from listings on the IMMOTOP.LU portal. For apartments advertised between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026, the Luxembourg City average was $2,318 a month, or $57.21 per square metre, across 5,655 listings. The national average was $2,101.

Inside the capital the spread is wide. Average advertised rents by quarter over the same period:

  • Gare: $3,066, 731 listings
  • Kirchberg: $2,908
  • Limpertsberg: $2,686
  • Ville-Haute: $2,650
  • Belair: $2,408
  • Gasperich: $1,956
  • Cessange: $1,822
  • Bonnevoie: $1,618, 542 listings

These are averages across all apartment sizes, so a quarter full of studios looks cheap per month and expensive per square metre. Bonnevoie is a case in point: the lowest monthly average of the big quarters, and $62 per square metre. Outside the capital, the per-metre figure falls fast: Dudelange $34.63, Ettelbruck $25.52, Diekirch $24.09, and Clervaux in the north $20.65.

Under Luxembourg's lease law as summarised on guichet.public.lu, a residential lease must be written, the deposit cannot exceed two months' rent, and an agent's fee is split equally between landlord and tenant. Advertised rents rose 3.0% in the year to the fourth quarter of 2025, close to inflation of 2.9%, according to the housing ministry and STATEC.

Cost of living in Luxembourg for a remote worker

Rent dominates, and everything else is Western European. Our city guide puts groceries for one at $345 to $460 a month, a casual lunch at $17 to $29, and a sit-down dinner without wine at $35 to $69. Transport is zero. Add a coworking desk and a mobile plan and a remote worker in a mid-priced quarter lands between $3,220 and $4,600 a month, by our estimate.

Our headline figure of $5,500 assumes a central apartment and eating out often. It is an estimate, because no published cost basket covers Luxembourg City. The advertised rents above are the part you can check yourself, and the part that moves your total most. Our cost of living index shows where that puts Luxembourg against the rest of Europe.

The practical conclusion is short. Our city guide sets the floor at about $3,450 a month: below that, Luxembourg City is hard to make work on a long lease, and the border towns start to look like the answer.

Living across the border from Luxembourg: France, Belgium and Germany

Around 237,200 people commute into Luxembourg from France, Germany and Belgium to work, according to the government portal luxembourg.public.lu, set against a resident population of about 691,000 on 1 January 2026. Rent is the obvious draw: our own estimate for a month in Trier, the nearest German city in our guides, is $1,650, less than a third of our Luxembourg City figure.

For a nomad whose employer or clients are abroad, living across the border is simple: your tax position follows the country you live in, not Luxembourg's cross-border regime, and you visit Luxembourg as anyone else would. Transport stops being free at the frontier, so price in a cross-border pass. Our digital nomad tax guide covers how residence decides where you pay.

The 34-day rule applies to a different person: someone employed by a Luxembourg company who lives in a neighbouring country. According to the Luxembourg tax administration, the tax treaties let residents of Germany, Belgium and France work up to 34 days a year outside Luxembourg while Luxembourg keeps the right to tax the whole salary. Every working day counts, including part days. Above the threshold, Luxembourg loses the right to tax the pay earned outside it.

The 34 days apply to Belgium since 2022, France since 2023 and Germany since 2024. Social security follows its own threshold. By default a cross-border employee who works 25% or more of their time in their country of residence changes scheme, and since 1 July 2023 a European framework agreement lets them telework up to 49.9% of their time and stay in Luxembourg's system, on conditions and after a declaration, per the employers' federation UEL and the law firm Tiberghien.

Luxembourg visa rules: no digital nomad visa, three real routes

Luxembourg has no digital nomad visa and no freelancer visa. Non-EU visitors from visa-exempt countries get the Schengen allowance of 90 days in any 180, counted across the whole Schengen area. One local rule catches people out: within three days of arriving, a short-stay visitor must either declare their arrival at the commune or fill in an accommodation form where they stay, and a hotel's form counts.

Guichet.lu also states that any third-country national needs a work permit to carry out a paid activity during a stay of less than three months, with exemptions for business travellers, visiting lecturers, athletes and a few others. Its page does not address remote work for a foreign employer. Our nomad visa finder lists the European countries that do issue a remote work permit, if a legal long stay is the priority.

EU citizens have the easy route. Staying beyond three months, you declare your arrival at the commune within eight days and apply there for a registration certificate within three months, which is issued on the spot. You must be employed, self-employed, or able to show enough resources not to rely on social assistance, and those living on their own resources must also show health insurance covering all risks in Luxembourg.

Non-EU nationals have three realistic routes. The self-employed residence permit must be applied for and approved before you enter, costs $91, is normally decided within three months and lasts up to three years, but the activity must show an economic or social benefit to Luxembourg.

The EU Blue Card needs a job offer of at least six months paying $74,863 a year. The standard salaried permit needs a signed contract with a Luxembourg employer, who must first declare the vacancy to the employment agency ADEM and wait three weeks for a local candidate.

Languages in Luxembourg: which one you will actually use

The 1984 languages law allows French, German or Luxembourgish in administrative and judicial matters, but only the French text of legislation is authentic. In practice French runs the paperwork: leases, commune forms and tax letters usually arrive in it. A 2018 education ministry study cited on luxembourg.public.lu found 98% of residents speak French, 80% English, 78% German and 77% Luxembourgish.

We rate English 8 out of 10. With 46.6% of residents holding a foreign passport and 180 nationalities recorded, English is the working language of much of the finance sector and the EU institutions in Kirchberg. You can live here in English. Expect to read your lease in French.

Who Luxembourg suits as a digital nomad base

Luxembourg suits a remote worker on a high salary who wants infrastructure that never gets in the way: gigabit fibre by default, free transport, and safety we score 10. It works well as a two-to-four-week stop inside a Schengen rotation, or as a long-term base for an EU citizen earning in a strong currency. Its community is small, and we rate it 4.

It suits nobody on a tight budget, and it offers non-EU nomads no dedicated route past 90 days. For a cheaper base in the same region, our estimates put Trier at $1,650 a month and Brussels at $2,580. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the internet in Luxembourg good enough for digital nomads?

Yes. At the end of 2025 fibre covered 86.6% of homes and premises and gigabit-capable lines 95.9%, according to the regulator ILR. Its checkmynet.lu tool measured an average fixed download of 207 Mbit/s and latency of 23.7 ms. Coverage is weakest in a few rural communes, so check an address first.

Which Luxembourg mobile network covers the most people with 5G?

On the ILR's end-2025 data, POST Luxembourg's gigabit-class 5G reached more than 75% of the population, Proximus (Tango) almost 70%, and Orange less than both. POST also holds 50.3% of mobile subscribers. On checkmynet.lu, 5G tests averaged 229 Mbit/s down across all networks.

Can you buy a prepaid SIM in Luxembourg without ID?

No. Since a law of 7 June 2017, operators must deactivate any prepaid card with a Luxembourg number whose holder has not identified themselves, so shops ask for a passport or national ID card. Prepaid is rare anyway: 24,500 of Luxembourg's 1,016,900 SIM cards were prepaid at the end of 2025.

Is public transport free in Luxembourg for visitors too?

Yes. Buses, trams and trains inside Luxembourg have been free since 1 March 2020, for residents and visitors alike. First class on trains still needs a ticket, and free travel ends at the border, so journeys to Trier, Arlon or Thionville need a cross-border ticket.

Does Luxembourg have a digital nomad visa?

No. Luxembourg has no digital nomad or freelancer visa. Non-EU visitors get 90 days in any 180 across Schengen. Longer stays need a residence permit, such as the self-employed permit ($91, approved before entry) or the EU Blue Card with a salary of at least $74,863. EU citizens simply register at their commune.

How much is rent in Luxembourg City for a remote worker?

The average advertised apartment rent in Luxembourg City was $2,318 a month between July 2025 and June 2026, per the Observatoire de l'Habitat. Gare averaged $3,066 and Bonnevoie $1,618. Deposits are capped at two months' rent, and agency fees are split with the landlord.

Is it cheaper to live across the border from Luxembourg?

Usually, which is why about 237,200 people commute in from France, Germany and Belgium. Our estimate for a month in Trier is $1,650 against $5,500 for Luxembourg City. Free public transport ends at the border, and if you work for a Luxembourg employer the 34-day telework tax threshold applies.

Do you need French to live in Luxembourg?

Not day to day: 80% of residents speak English, per a 2018 study cited by the government. French is what paperwork uses, since only the French text of legislation is authentic and leases and commune forms usually come in French. Luxembourgish is spoken by 77% of residents.
