Tax Adviser in Portugal: NIF, IFICI and IRS for Nomads

The 20% regime most people mean when they talk about moving to Portugal for tax reasons almost never applies to a remote worker. IFICI, which replaced the old NHR in 2024, taxes income from qualifying work for qualifying entities in Portugal, and a remote job for a foreign employer is not on its list. So the real questions a tax adviser in Portugal answers for a nomad are more basic: when you become resident, what you owe on work done from a Lisbon flat, and which deadlines you cannot miss.

This guide works through those questions in the order they arrive, from the first tax number to the first annual return, with each rule tied to the Portuguese law or official page that states it, as of September 2026. It ends with who is allowed to act as your tax adviser and how to check a registration before you pay anyone. Euro amounts are converted at the European Central Bank reference rate of 25 September 2026, $1.1403 per euro.

When you need a tax adviser in Portugal, and when you can file alone

Many nomads never need one. If you are an employee of a foreign company, have no Portuguese property and file a single return a year, the Portal das Finanças lets you do everything yourself: request changes, file the annual return and pay. The law only forces a professional on you in a few places, and they are narrower than the market for "NIF services" suggests.

Three situations change that. The first is organised accounting (contabilidade organizada): a certified accountant must sign returns built on it, and you move into it once your freelance income passes the simplified regime's ceiling, covered below. The second is a tax court case, where Portugal's tax procedure code makes a lawyer mandatory. The third is a deadline you only get once, the IFICI application above all, where a missed date costs years of the benefit.

Between those, a Portugal tax adviser earns a fee on judgement calls: whether your home abroad still counts, how a treaty splits your income, whether a freelance service is taxed where you sit or where your client sits. Those are the questions to bring. Form-filling is not.

Getting a NIF: the Portuguese tax number you need first

Everything starts with the NIF (número de identificação fiscal), a nine-digit number that stays the same whether the tax authority records you as resident or non-resident. The Autoridade Tributária's own guide for foreign citizens notes that it is needed well beyond tax: for employment, contracts, bank accounts and social security. A rental contract or a bank will ask for it before any tax office does.

There are two official routes, set out in the tax authority's NIF leaflet for non-residents. In person, you book an appointment at any Serviço de Finanças or Loja do Cidadão, through the Portal das Finanças or the tax authority's phone line on +351 217 206 707, and bring your passport plus a document showing your foreign address. A non-EU citizen applying in person needs an entry visa in the passport unless their nationality is visa-exempt for Schengen.

Remotely, the request goes through the e-balcão messaging service, filed by a legal representative holding your power of attorney, which is a different role from a fiscal representative. The same leaflet says a power of attorney given to an advogado or solicitador needs no signature certification, which is why a tax adviser offering a remote NIF service is so often a law office. Documents not in Portuguese need a certified translation.

The NIF is issued with your foreign address, as a non-resident. When you later meet the residency conditions, you have to request the change of address yourself. Nothing updates automatically.

Fiscal representative rules for EU and non-EU nomads in Portugal

The fiscal representative is where most advice online is out of date. The tax authority states plainly that appointing one is not required when the NIF is assigned. The obligation only arises later, and only for some people, which its fiscal representation FAQ splits by where you live.

If you live in an EU country, Norway, Iceland or Liechtenstein, a representative is always optional. If you live in a third country and form a tax relationship with Portugal, you have 15 days to either appoint a representative or sign up to electronic notifications, through the Portal das Finanças or the ViaCTT electronic mailbox. The leaflet lists what counts as that relationship: a car registered in Portugal, property there, a Portuguese employment contract, or self-employed activity in Portugal.

One exception survives the electronic option. A non-resident from a third country who works self-employed in Portugal must still appoint a VAT fiscal representative, who has to be a VAT-registered taxpayer resident in Portugal. Anyone domiciled in Portugal, person or company, can otherwise act as representative, including a foreigner with a valid residence permit who is registered as resident.

Every one of these rules is about non-residents. Once you live in Portugal and have moved your tax address there, the representative question falls away and the residency rules take over. A tax adviser still billing you for representation after that is charging for something the rules no longer ask of you.

When you become a Portuguese tax resident: 183 days or a habitual home

Residency is decided by article 16 of the IRS code, not by your visa. You are resident for a year if you spent more than 183 days in Portugal, consecutive or not, in any 12-month period starting or ending in that year. You are also resident if, on any day of that period, you had a home in conditions suggesting you intend to keep and occupy it as your habitual residence. The second test needs no day count at all.

The same article, in number 3, makes residency start on the first day you are in Portugal and end on the last. The year you arrive is split: before your arrival you are a non-resident, from arrival onward a resident taxed on worldwide income. That is why the arrival date matters more than most nomads expect.

The habitual-home test is the one that catches people. Signing a long lease on a flat you furnish and keep while travelling looks like a habitual residence, whatever your day count says. If your plan is to stay under the line, a year-long lease undermines it. That is a fact pattern worth putting in front of a tax adviser before signing, not after.

What the D8 digital nomad visa does to your Portuguese tax position

Portugal's consular visa list includes both a residency visa and a temporary stay visa for "a professional activity done remotely", the official label for digital nomads. The residency route is the D8. Its application steps, documents and timeline are in our step-by-step guide to the Portugal D8 visa, and the income floor is four times the Portuguese minimum wage, about $4,196 a month for 2026; our comparison of digital nomad visa income requirements sets it beside the other countries.

The visa itself does not flip your tax status. Article 16 does. In practice, though, a D8 holder who actually lives in Portugal passes the 183-day test or the habitual-home test within the first year, and from the day of arrival is taxed on worldwide income.

The larger consequence sits in article 18 of the IRS code: employment income from work performed in Portugal counts as Portuguese-source income, whoever pays it. A remote salary from New York earned at a desk in Lisbon is Portuguese income. For a resident it falls under the general rates, which article 68 of the IRS code sets from 12.5% up to 48% on taxable income above $98,800 (a bracket the code sets in euros), as the code stood in September 2026.

The old NHR regime is closed to new arrivals in Portugal

The Non-Habitual Resident regime was revoked from 1 January 2024 by the 2024 State Budget Law (Lei 82/2023). The tax authority's NHR FAQ says so in one line, and articles that still offer it as an option to someone planning a move in 2026 are describing a door that has shut.

A transitional rule let a last group in. It covered people who became resident in 2024, had not been resident in 2019 to 2023, and could show a job contract or promise signed by 31 December 2023, a lease signed by 10 October 2023, a visa or residence permit valid on 31 December 2023, or an immigration process started by that date. Their registration deadline was 31 March of the following year, so 31 March 2025. Nobody arriving in 2025 or later qualifies.

People already registered keep NHR until their ten years run out. If you meet one of them in Lisbon, their tax bill is no guide to yours, and a tax adviser's page that still sells NHR to new clients is out of date.

IFICI, Portugal's 20% tax regime: who qualifies and the 15 January deadline

The replacement is the Incentivo Fiscal à Investigação Científica e Inovação, article 58.º-A of the Tax Benefits Statute, added by the same budget law and regulated by Portaria 352/2024/1 of 23 December 2024. It is widely called "NHR 2.0". The name oversells it for remote workers.

What the IFICI tax benefit is

According to article 58.º-A, qualifying employment and self-employment income (categories A and B) earned in the listed activities is taxed at a special rate of 20% for ten consecutive years from registration as a resident. The Ordem dos Contabilistas Certificados' March 2025 guide adds that foreign-source income is generally exempt, though it still counts in setting the rate on your other income. The tax authority's FAQ carves out pensions and dividends from blacklisted jurisdictions, which are taxed at 35%.

Who can register for IFICI in Portugal

You must become tax resident, must not have been resident in any of the previous five years, and must never have used NHR or the returning-residents regime of article 12.º-A. The regime can be used once per person. Then comes the part that excludes most nomads: the income has to come from one of the listed activities, each defined by the entity you work for.

  • Higher-education teaching and scientific research, certified by the science foundation FCT.
  • Qualified jobs under productive-investment contracts, through AICEP.
  • Highly qualified professions at companies in eligible sectors that export at least 50% of turnover, through AICEP.
  • Qualified jobs at entities recognised as relevant to the economy, through IAPMEI.
  • R&D staff whose costs qualify for the SIFIDE II credit, through ANI.
  • Jobs at certified startups, through Startup Portugal.
  • Qualifying jobs in the Azores and Madeira.

For a highly qualified profession, the Portaria makes the company prove by 15 March that you meet the conditions. A foreign employer with no Portuguese presence has nothing to prove it with. The D8 is built on income from outside Portugal, and IFICI on work for a qualifying entity inside it, so the two overlap only when a Portuguese employer or startup hires you. A tax adviser can file the registration for you, but the certifying documents have to come from that employer.

The IFICI application deadline

You apply on the Portal das Finanças by 15 January of the year after the year you become resident. Someone resident from 2026 applies by 15 January 2027. The tax authority's IFICI FAQ states that a late registration takes effect only from the year it is filed, so the years before are lost from the ten. The return itself declares the regime on Annex L of the Modelo 3 form.

Qualifying for IFICI turns on documents from your employer and the right certifying agency, and a missed 15 January cannot be undone. Freelancing in Portugal: recibos verdes, IRS coefficients and VAT

Freelancers are the group for whom a Portuguese tax adviser most often pays for itself, because freelancing creates obligations the moment you start. Article 112 of the IRS code requires a declaration of start of activity before any work that produces category B income. From then on, article 115 requires an invoice, receipt or invoice-receipt for every payment, issued through the tax authority's own invoicing application or under the VAT code's invoicing rules. These are known as recibos verdes, green receipts.

Most freelancers are taxed under the simplified regime, open to anyone whose gross category B income in the previous year stayed under $228,000, a ceiling set in euros. It taxes a fixed share of what you bill. Under article 31, that share is 75% for the professions on the official activity table in article 151 and 35% for other services. In the year you start and the one after, those coefficients fall by 50% and 25% respectively, provided you have no employment or pension income in that year.

There is a catch built into the 75% figure. The code expects documented expenses worth at least 15% of gross fees, and any shortfall is added back to taxable income. Keep every invoice with your NIF on it. Cross that $228,000 line for two years running, or by more than 25% in one year, and you move into organised accounting, where a certified accountant becomes a legal requirement.

VAT runs on its own track. Article 53 of the VAT code exempts taxpayers domiciled in Portugal whose turnover in Portugal stayed under $17,100, converted from a threshold set in euros, in the previous calendar year and who do not carry out export operations, under the version in force since Decreto-Lei 35/2025. Whether fees from a client abroad count toward that figure depends on where each service is taxed, which is decided client by client and it is worth putting to a tax adviser before your first invoice goes out.

Social security contributions for self-employed nomads in Portugal

Income tax is only half the bill for a freelancer. Portugal's social security institute sets the rules in its practical guide for self-employed workers, and they work differently from income tax. You declare what you earned each quarter, in January, April, July and October, and pay monthly between the 10th and the 20th of the following month.

The contribution is 21.4% of "relevant income", which for services is 70% of what you invoiced in the previous three months. A freelancer billing $4,000 a month has relevant income of $2,800 and pays about $599 a month. You may raise or lower that base by up to 25% in 5% steps at each quarterly declaration, and if nothing is due the minimum is $23 a month (a floor set in euros).

New freelancers get a long start. A first registration as self-employed only takes effect on the first day of the twelfth month after activity begins, so most of the first year runs without contributions unless you ask to start early. Put the date in your calendar, or your tax adviser's. The first quarterly declaration after that month is the one people miss.

The Portuguese tax calendar: Modelo 3 and the dates that matter

The Portuguese tax year is the calendar year, and the annual IRS return, the Modelo 3, is filed electronically from 1 April to 30 June of the following year under article 60 of the IRS code. The tax authority's Modelo 3 page sets 31 August as the date by which tax due must be paid and refunds are paid. Foreign income goes on Annex J, which has a column for tax already paid abroad.

Your first return covers the arrival year, split at the arrival date. Getting that split right, and declaring the non-resident months correctly, is the most common reason a new resident pays a tax adviser for a single year and then files alone.

Double tax treaties and your home country's claim on Portuguese residents

Portugal has signed 79 double tax conventions, 78 of them in force, according to the tax authority's treaty page. A treaty decides which country may tax which income and which one gives credit. To claim its benefits abroad, you ask the Portal das Finanças for a certificate of tax residence, which the same page offers for exactly that purpose.

Two treaties matter most to English-speaking nomads. The US and Portugal have an income tax treaty dating from 1994, but Americans are taxed by the US on citizenship, so a Portuguese resident still files a US return every year; our digital nomad tax guide covers the exclusions and credits on the US side. The UK and Portugal signed a new convention on 15 September 2025, in force since 29 December 2025 and applying to UK income tax from 6 April 2026.

A treaty does not remove the Portuguese filing duty. As a resident you declare worldwide income, and foreign income goes on Annex J with the tax paid abroad beside it, which is how the credit is claimed. Leaving it off because "it was already taxed" is the mistake a tax adviser's review of your first year exists to catch.

Who may give tax advice in Portugal: contabilista, advogado or solicitador

Portugal protects professional titles more tightly than the advice itself. Since the Lei 10/2024 of 19 January 2024, which replaced the 2004 law on lawyers' acts, legal consultation is listed among the competences of advogados and solicitadores, but the law declares it is not an act reserved to them and lets law graduates and notaries give it too. Tax advisory work is therefore open to more people than the titles are.

What stays exclusive is court work. Article 4 of the same law reserves the mandato forense, representation before a court, to advogados and solicitadores, and article 6 of the tax procedure code requires a legal representative in the tax courts. A dispute that leaves the tax office needs a lawyer.

The contabilista certificado, a certified accountant registered with the Ordem dos Contabilistas Certificados, has one exclusive act under article 10 of that Ordem's statute: taking technical responsibility for, and signing, the accounts and tax returns of anyone with organised accounting. The same article gives certified accountants competence to advise on accounting and tax and to represent the clients whose books they keep before the tax authority, up to the point where a lawyer becomes mandatory.

In practice that gives three profiles. A contabilista certificado handles returns, recibos verdes, social security and IFICI filings. An advogado handles disputes, appeals, court and the legal side of residency. A solicitador shares the lawyer's listed competences under Lei 10/2024, including contracts and complaints against tax decisions. Anyone else calling themselves a tax adviser or "tax consultant" holds no protected title at all.

How to check a Portugal tax adviser's registration before paying

The Ordem dos Contabilistas Certificados keeps a public register of certified accountants under article 21 of its statute, showing whether a registration is active or suspended. Ask the tax adviser who will sign your returns for their name as registered and look it up. A firm is not a person: the register lists the individual who carries the responsibility.

For an advogado or solicitador, the matching check is membership of the Ordem dos Advogados or the Ordem dos Solicitadores e dos Agentes de Execução, the two bodies Lei 10/2024 names. Ask for the professional number and confirm it with the Ordem. If you are handing over a power of attorney, read which powers it grants, and whether it also names the holder as your fiscal representative, which the NIF leaflet says must be written into it.

Scope matters as much as status. Get in writing which returns are included, whether the quarterly social security declarations are, and who files the IFICI registration if you might qualify. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a tax adviser in Portugal as a digital nomad?

Not by law, unless your freelance income pushes you into organised accounting, which a certified accountant must sign, or you end up in tax court, which requires a lawyer. Remote employees with one foreign employer can file the Modelo 3 themselves. Freelancers and anyone who might qualify for IFICI get the most value from one.

Do I still need a fiscal representative in Portugal to get a NIF?

No. The tax authority states a representative is not required when the NIF is assigned. Non-EU residents who later form a tax relationship, such as property or a car in Portugal, or a Portuguese job, have 15 days to appoint one or join electronic notifications. Self-employed non-residents from outside the EU still need a VAT representative.

When do I become a tax resident in Portugal?

When you spend more than 183 days there in any 12-month period starting or ending in the tax year, or when you hold a home there that you appear to intend as your habitual residence. Residency starts on the first day you are present, so the arrival year is split into non-resident and resident parts.

Can a digital nomad get the 20% IFICI tax rate in Portugal?

Rarely. IFICI covers income from listed activities: research, teaching, certified startups, qualifying jobs at exporting or recognised companies, and similar roles certified by a Portuguese body. A remote job for a foreign employer has no entity in Portugal to certify it. You must also not have been resident in the previous five years.

Is the NHR tax regime still available in Portugal?

Not to new arrivals. NHR was revoked from 1 January 2024. A transitional rule covered people who became resident in 2024 with a contract, lease, visa or immigration process dated 2023, and they had to register by 31 March 2025. Existing NHR holders keep it until their ten years end.

What is the IFICI application deadline in Portugal?

15 January of the year after you become tax resident, filed on the Portal das Finanças. If you became resident in 2026, the deadline is 15 January 2027. A late registration only takes effect from the year it is made, so the earlier years are lost from the ten-year period.

How much social security does a freelancer pay in Portugal?

21.4% of relevant income, which for services is 70% of what you invoiced in the previous quarter. Billing $4,000 a month means about $599 a month in contributions. First-time self-employed workers only start contributing on the first day of the twelfth month after opening their activity.

When is the Portuguese IRS tax return due?

The Modelo 3 is filed online between 1 April and 30 June for the previous calendar year. Tax due must be paid, and refunds are paid, by 31 August. Foreign income goes on Annex J, and anyone taxed under IFICI or the old NHR declares it on Annex L.
