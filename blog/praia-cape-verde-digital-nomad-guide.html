Praia Digital Nomad Guide: Cape Verde Costs and Visas

Yannick Schroth August 2026 11 min read

Cape Verde runs on UTC-1, which is a time zone almost nothing else uses. From Praia you are one hour behind Lisbon and four ahead of New York, which puts a working day inside reach of both. The archipelago also issues one of the most straightforward remote-work permits in the Atlantic: six months, renewable once, on proof of about $1,635 a month. This guide covers what living there actually costs, which island to pick, and the two things that will frustrate you.

Start with the honest number. We rate Praia 8 out of 10 for cost and 8 for climate. We rate it 2 out of 10 for community, which is close to the bottom of our entire data set. Cape Verde is a good place to work remotely and a poor place to arrive expecting a scene.

Why Cape Verde suits some digital nomads and not others

The islands sit 570 kilometres off Senegal, which is far enough into the Atlantic to catch trade winds year-round and close enough to Europe for a four-hour flight from Lisbon. That geography is the whole argument. You get African-Portuguese Creole culture, dry heat, and an ocean that produces world-class wind and swell, in a country that is politically stable and has held free elections since 1991.

What you do not get is density. A digital nomad arriving in Praia will find a handful of remote workers at the coworking spaces and a larger population of NGO and development staff. There is no weekly meetup calendar. Connections happen through the coworking desk or the Cape Verde Digital Nomads group online, and they take longer to form than in Lisbon or Las Palmas.

That suits some people exactly. If you have a settled remote job and want quiet, warm, cheap and well-connected to Europe, this is a serious option. If you are between contracts and need the network, it is the wrong island.

Cost of living in Praia: real monthly numbers

Budget around $1,400 a month as a solo remote worker. Our cost of living index builds that from local rent and a one-person basket, and a comfortable nomad month with coworking and regular restaurant meals lands between $1,200 and $1,800.

A one-bedroom in the central Plateau district runs $470 to $830. Move out to the surrounding neighbourhoods and the same apartment costs $210 to $365, which is the single biggest lever on your budget. Groceries land around $155 to $230 a month if you eat local staples: tuna, cachupa, cassava and imported rice.

Imports are where Cape Verde stops being cheap. Almost everything that is not fish or produce arrives by container, and electronics, cheese, wine and anything branded carry a visible premium. Bring what you depend on.

Which island to base yourself on as a remote worker

Cape Verde is ten islands and the choice matters more than the neighbourhood choice does in a normal city. Getting between them means a flight or a ferry, so pick deliberately.

Praia, on Santiago, is the capital and the only place with full urban infrastructure: hospitals, ministries, the best connectivity, the coworking spaces. It is also the least scenic option. Mindelo, on São Vicente, is the cultural capital, home of the morna music that Cesária Évora carried worldwide, and the one most visitors fall for.

Santa Maria on Sal and Sal Rei on Boa Vista are the beach and watersports islands, flat, dry and tourist-shaped, with the kitesurfing that draws people back every winter. Tarrafal, in northern Santiago, is the quiet alternative for anyone who wants the coast without the resorts.

For a first month with real work commitments, base in Praia. Move to Mindelo or Sal once you know your connection holds.

Internet in Cape Verde: the constraint to plan around

This is the weakest part of the proposition and the reason we score wifi 5 out of 10. Central Praia has fibre and 5G, and the dedicated coworking spaces, Workin' CV and Prime Coworking, deliver a reliable 40 to 50 Mbps. That is enough for calls and cloud work and well short of what European cities offer.

Away from those spaces it degrades quickly. Cafe and restaurant wifi is inconsistent and rarely holds up for a full working day. Buy a local SIM on arrival from CV Movel or Unitel T+, both cheap with monthly data bundles, and treat mobile data as your primary backup rather than an afterthought.

Anyone whose work depends on large uploads or uninterrupted video should test before committing to a lease. This is a place where the connection is the thing that breaks.

The Cape Verde remote working visa, step by step

Cape Verde created a dedicated Remote Working Programme visa, and it is one of the less demanding ones in the Atlantic. It grants six months of residence, renewable once, to remote workers earning at least the equivalent of about $1,635 a month.

Applications need proof of income over the preceding six months, health insurance valid in Cape Verde, a clean criminal record check, and proof of accommodation. Processing is done online ahead of travel. Compare it against the alternatives in our nomad visa finder, which tracks income floors across 60 countries.

If six months is more than you need, most Western passport holders enter visa-free for up to 30 days. Two things are still mandatory: pre-registration through the EASE platform before you fly, and payment of the Airport Security Tax. Neither is expensive and both are easy to forget, which is how people end up arguing at check-in.

Getting to Cape Verde and moving between the islands

Flights from Lisbon reach Praia or Sal in about four hours, and TAP, TACV and a rotating cast of charter operators serve the route. From most of northern Europe you connect through Lisbon. There are seasonal direct flights from the UK, Germany and the Netherlands, weighted heavily toward Sal and Boa Vista because that is where the package holidays go.

Inter-island movement is the part that catches people out. The islands are far enough apart that hopping between them means a domestic flight, and the domestic schedule is thin and prone to change. Ferries exist and are slow, cheap and weather-dependent. Neither is something to build a Monday deadline around.

On Santiago itself, shared minibuses called hiaces cover the routes between Praia and the smaller towns for a couple of dollars, leaving when they fill rather than to a timetable. Taxis in Praia are inexpensive and metered in principle, negotiated in practice. Renting a car makes sense only if you intend to explore the interior, which is worth doing: Santiago's mountains are greener and steeper than the coast suggests.

Plan any island change with a day of slack on either side. That is the single most useful piece of logistics advice for the archipelago.

Weather in Cape Verde: dry heat and the harmattan

Temperatures sit between 24°C and 30°C all year, and rain is genuinely scarce: the islands are semi-desert, and some years the short August to October wet season barely arrives. We score the climate 8, and air quality 9, because the wind here has crossed open ocean.

The qualifier is Saharan dust. From roughly December to February the harmattan carries fine sand west from the mainland, hazing the sky and occasionally disrupting inter-island flights. It is the same weather system that gives the Canaries their calima, and it affects Cape Verde more often because the islands are closer to the source.

Wind is a feature rather than a problem if you came for it. Sal and Boa Vista are among the most reliable kitesurfing destinations on earth from November through March.

Language: what a digital nomad needs beyond English

We score English 4 out of 10 here, and that number does real work. Portuguese is the official language and Kriolu, Cape Verdean Creole, is what people actually speak to each other. English appears in tourist restaurants, better hotels and the coworking spaces, and disappears at the pharmacy, the market and any government counter.

A few weeks of Portuguese before arriving changes the experience more than it would in most destinations. Younger professionals in Praia often have functional English, especially in business settings, but you cannot rely on it for errands.

Is Praia safe for remote workers?

Broadly, yes. We rate safety 7 out of 10. Cape Verde has one of the better stability records in West Africa and violent crime against visitors is uncommon. Petty theft is the realistic concern, concentrated around Praia's Plateau at night and on the beaches when bags are left unattended.

Standard urban caution covers most of it. Medical facilities are limited outside Praia and Mindelo, which matters more than crime does: insurance that includes evacuation is worth having on an archipelago where the nearest specialist may be on another island.

Who Cape Verde suits, and who should look elsewhere

Choose it if you have stable remote income, want an Atlantic base that is warm in January and cheap against Europe, and are happy with your own company. The six-month visa is generous, the flights to Lisbon are short, and the UTC-1 clock is genuinely useful if your team spans both sides of the ocean.

Look elsewhere if you need reliable high-bandwidth internet, if you want to arrive into a ready-made community, or if you expect a broad restaurant and cultural scene. On our data, food and culture both sit at 6 and community at 2, and no amount of good weather compensates for the last one if it is what you came for.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to live in Praia as a digital nomad?

Around $1,400 a month solo. A central Plateau one-bedroom is $470 to $830, outer neighbourhoods $210 to $365, and groceries $155 to $230 if you eat local staples. Coworking and regular restaurant meals push a comfortable month toward $1,800.

Does Cape Verde have a digital nomad visa?

Yes. The Remote Working Programme grants six months of residence, renewable once, for remote workers earning roughly $1,635 a month. You need six months of income proof, health insurance valid locally, a criminal record check and proof of accommodation. Applications are handled online before travel.

Is the internet in Cape Verde good enough for remote work?

At the coworking spaces, yes: Workin' CV and Prime Coworking hold 40 to 50 Mbps reliably. Cafe wifi is inconsistent and rarely lasts a full day. Buy a CV Movel or Unitel T+ SIM on arrival and treat mobile data as your main backup, not a fallback.

Which Cape Verde island is best for digital nomads?

Praia on Santiago for infrastructure, hospitals and the best connectivity. Mindelo on São Vicente for culture and music. Sal and Boa Vista for beaches and kitesurfing. Base your first month in Praia, then move once you know your connection and your routine hold up.

Do I need a visa to enter Cape Verde for a short stay?

Most Western passport holders enter visa-free for up to 30 days. Two steps are still compulsory: pre-registration on the EASE platform before you fly, and payment of the Airport Security Tax. Both are inexpensive, and airlines do check the EASE registration at boarding.

Is English spoken in Cape Verde?

Not reliably. Portuguese is official and Cape Verdean Creole is the everyday language. English works in tourist restaurants, better hotels and coworking spaces, and not at pharmacies, markets or government offices. Basic Portuguese makes daily errands considerably easier and is worth preparing.

What time zone is Cape Verde in for remote work?

UTC-1, with no daylight saving. That is one hour behind Lisbon and London in winter, and four hours ahead of New York. Few places sit on this offset, which makes the islands unusually workable for anyone with colleagues on both sides of the Atlantic.

Is Praia safe for foreign remote workers?

Generally yes, with a safety rating of 7 in our data. Violent crime against visitors is uncommon; petty theft around the Plateau at night and on beaches is the real risk. Medical facilities outside the two main cities are limited, so insurance covering evacuation is sensible.

When is the best time of year to work from Cape Verde?

November through March for wind and kitesurfing, with warm dry days throughout. Expect Saharan dust haze from December to February, which occasionally disrupts inter-island flights. April to June is calmer and clearer if you would rather have the beaches than the wind.
