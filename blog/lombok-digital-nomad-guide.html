Lombok Digital Nomad Guide: Community, Wifi and Visas

Yannick Schroth September 2026 15 min read

From the beach on Gili Air, Bali is part of the view. On a clear evening the cone of Mount Agung stands on the western horizon, and turning round you face Rinjani, Lombok's own volcano. About 40 km of water separate the two islands, and the Lombok Strait between them is a real border: naturalists draw the line between Asian and Australasian wildlife down it, and on the far shore Hindu Bali gives way to the Sasak, a mostly Muslim people, on what is nicknamed the Island of a Thousand Mosques.

The crowds do not make the crossing. In 2024 Bali counted about 6.3 million direct foreign arrivals, while Lombok's airport counted 81,544, roughly one for every 78. Others come over by boat, but the hotel figures show what most of them are doing: foreign guests in the province's smaller hotels stay about two nights. They climb Rinjani, dive off the Gilis or chase a swell, then leave. The pace drops with them, and so does the size of any community you might hope to join.

The remote workers who stay a month or more gather in one place. Kuta, on the south coast, is a fishing village that became Lombok's main beach town, about 15 km from the airport, and every dedicated coworking space we list on the island sits in or near it. Around the town the coast breaks into bays between hills and rocky headlands, most of them a short scooter ride away.

So Lombok suits a remote worker who has done Bali and wants the same heat, the surf and emptier beaches, and who will build a social life from a small group. It is the wrong choice if you want a ready-made network, internet you never think about, or a hospital close by.

The saving is almost all rent: our estimate for a solo remote worker is about $800 a month in Kuta against $2,200 in Canggu, both editorial estimates. A warung meal costs a few dollars on either island.

Kuta and its bays: where the laptops are

Kuta itself is small enough to walk. Most of the guesthouses and restaurants sit along Kuta Beach, and the old village clusters, the kampung, are still woven between them. The point of living here is what lies either side. East of town, Tanjung Aan has sand whose grains look like pepper, and above it Bukit Merese, a hill with views in every direction, fills up at sunrise and sunset.

West of town the road dips to Mawun, a half-moon bay, and on to Selong Belanak, a wide bay held between two rocky promontories, with the surf beach of Mawi just beyond. Further east, Gerupuk is a fishing village whose best waves break out on the reefs and are reached by boat. Both villages are quieter than Kuta, with fewer services, and both mean riding in to work.

The desks are in town. The most detailed public claims come from The Spot, a garden café with bungalows on Jl. Sawe-Batu Riti, whose own site describes a 950 Mbps fibre line shared out at up to 60 Mbps per device, a second fibre line with Starlink behind it, and a generator for power cuts. Its outdoor desks are free and an air-conditioned focus room starts at about $5.60. We have not measured any of it.

The rest of the list is short: KARIA by TIKA, the coworking floor of the TIKA hotel complex, South Lombok Cowork above Kuta beach, and Xeno Hub, a restaurant with desks beside a gym. Against our guide to the best coworking spaces in Bali, that is a handful, so if a proper desk matters, rent within scooter distance of Kuta town.

Housing is where Lombok pays you back. A room with air conditioning runs $165 to $275 a month on our estimates, a studio or small house a little more, and our city page notes that committing to six months typically takes 20 to 30 percent off the monthly price.

A working day on the south coast

Start with the connection, the weakest part of the day. No published speed figure covers Lombok alone. Across Indonesia the median fixed line in August 2026 managed about 51 Mbps down and 36 Mbps up, and Bali, the obvious benchmark, is not much better: more than a fifth of Bali's fixed-line tests fail a basic 25 Mbps down, 3 Mbps up threshold.

Mobile is your safety net. In the Bali and Nusa Tenggara region, 4G was available about 95 percent of the time in the first half of 2025, while 5G reached only 17 percent. Two of the Kuta spaces keep Starlink as a backup. Ask any host for a speed test showing the upload before you pay a month's rent, carry a local SIM with a large data package, and do not plan around 5G.

Lunch is the island on a plate. Ayam taliwang is chicken flattened, rubbed with chilli, garlic and shrimp paste and grilled over charcoal, a dish named after the old kingdom of Taliwang on neighbouring Sumbawa and now a Lombok staple. It comes with plecing kangkung, blanched water spinach under a fierce Lombok sambal, and often beberuk terong, raw aubergine in spiced relish.

At five o'clock you could hire a boat from Gerupuk out to the reef breaks, or ride to Bukit Merese and watch the light go over Tanjung Aan. Five times a day the call to prayer carries across town. During Ramadan many restaurants stay shut until the fast breaks at dusk, and eating in public during the fast is considered impolite. Every errand runs on the scooter, and anything Kuta's shops do not stock means Mataram.

Staying legally: which visa fits how long

Lombok follows Indonesia's national rules. Most Western passports, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and most of the EU, are on the visa-on-arrival list rather than the visa-free list, which covers ASEAN members and a short list including Brazil, Colombia and Turkey.

For a first stay, the B1 visa on arrival costs $28 for 30 days, extendable once to 60. You can buy it online as an e-VoA before you fly, and Lombok's airport is a listed e-VoA entry point.

For a season, look at the C1 visit visa: $56 for 60 days, extendable up to 180. Many guides still call it the B211A, a code that no longer appears on the official list. You show three months of bank statements with at least $2,000, most nationalities need no sponsor, and you may not take payment from anyone in Indonesia.

For a year, Indonesia has a real remote worker visa. The E33G is for someone employed by a company established outside Indonesia who earns at least $60,000 a year, with a contract to prove it, the same $2,000 bank statement and a passport valid for six months. No sponsor is needed, the state fees come to about $391, and the stay permit is issued when you land. If the income bar rules you out, the C1 is the honest route for a long Lombok winter; our comparison of digital nomad visas sets the $60,000 against what other countries ask.

Mandalika: a racetrack, a princess and a weaving village

East of Kuta the road widens into boulevards, promenades and cycle paths. This is The Mandalika, a 1,175-hectare resort area run by the state developer ITDC and a special economic zone since 2017. Inside it sits the Pertamina Mandalika International Circuit, 4.3 km and 17 turns, which has hosted MotoGP since 2022.

For a remote worker the zone cuts both ways: it brings wide new roads, and it is turning the stretch from Kuta to Tanjung Aan into a resort strip. The 2026 Indonesian Grand Prix runs from 9 to 11 October, and our Kuta page notes that prices climb around race weekends, so book early if your stay overlaps. Outside those peaks there is room to spare: star-hotel occupancy across the province stood at about 50 percent in July 2025, the busiest month for foreign arrivals since at least early 2024.

Older calendars still run here. On the 20th day of the tenth month of the Sasak calendar, which falls in February or March, people camp on Seger beach for Bau Nyale. The evening fills with traded rhymes and boat rides, and after dark they go down to the water with lights and nets to catch the nyale, sea worms said to be the hair of Princess Mandalika, who threw herself into the sea rather than set her suitors to war. The catching goes on until dawn.

Twenty-five to thirty minutes from the airport, the village of Sade keeps around 150 traditional houses under mountain-shaped roofs of reed grass. Their floors are smeared with buffalo dung to harden them and keep insects out, and weaving, tenun, is a skill Sasak girls are expected to learn from childhood.

Senggigi, the Gilis and the view back to Bali

On the west coast, facing the strait, Senggigi was Lombok's first resort strip, stalled by the violence of 2000 and the Bali bombing of 2002 and since overtaken by Kuta and the Gilis. Its sun sets behind Bali, and Pura Batu Bolong, a Hindu temple with 14 altars on a rock on the beach, is a favourite place to watch it. Senggigi is closer to Mataram's hospitals, but we list no dedicated coworking there.

Off the north-west coast, the three Gilis ban cars and motorbikes, so you get about on foot, by bicycle or in a cidomo, a small horse cart. Gili Trawangan, about 3 km long, is the biggest, the busiest and the one for diving and nights out. Gili Meno is the quietest, with a salt lake and turtles on its reefs. Gili Air, closest to Lombok, sits between the two in mood.

Foreign visitors pay a $1.10 fee to land on the islands; a rise to $2.80, proposed in April 2026, was still a plan in August. The islands suit a week of snorkelling better than a month of calls.

Dry months, wet months and the mountain

The south of Lombok is the drier side of the strait. Our weather data gives Kuta 32 mm of rain in July, 35 in August and 44 in September, about half of what Canggu gets, and from May to October Kuta is the drier of the two every month. Daytime highs sit between 28 and 30°C all year.

January is the wettest month, with 268 mm, and from December to February the beach roads get hard going on a scooter. Aim for May to September; September keeps the dry weather with fewer arrivals than July.

The season matters most for the mountain. Rinjani rises to 3,726 metres, the second-highest volcano in Indonesia. Its caldera, about 6 by 8.5 km, holds Segara Anak, a crater lake as blue as the sea, with a young cone, Barujari, rising out of the water and hot springs around it. Treks start from Senaru or the high valley of Sembalun.

Plan around the rains: the national park closed all six trails from 1 January to 31 March 2026 to limit landslide and flood risk. If the climb is on your list, plan it for the dry months.

Crossing over: flights, ferries and the Bali levy

Lombok International Airport sits inland, about 15 km north of Kuta, with DAMRI buses to Mataram and Senggigi. It is a small gateway, so long-haul trips need a connection. By sea, the public car ferry links Padang Bai in east Bali with Lembar in south-west Lombok, and fast boats run from Bali to the Gilis and the north-west coast.

How you cross decides whether you pay Bali's tourist levy, about $8.40, a Bali charge rather than an Indonesian one. Paid through the official Love Bali portal before or on arrival, it applies to every foreign tourist entering Bali, directly from abroad or via another part of Indonesia, and one payment covers you until you leave the country.

So fly into Bali and take the boat across, and you pay once, in Bali. Fly straight into Lombok and never touch Bali, and you pay nothing. Pop back across the strait later in the same trip, and your first payment still covers you. Stay-permit holders, E33G holders included, can apply for an exemption on the same portal.

If you get sick on Lombok

This is the real cost of the quiet. Lombok's main public hospital, the General Hospital of West Nusa Tenggara Province, is in Mataram, with every specialisation and English-speaking staff on the UK Foreign Office's list of medical facilities in Indonesia. The same list shows two more hospitals there, clinics on the Gilis and in Sembalun, and nothing in Central Lombok, where Kuta is.

From Kuta, anything beyond basic care means the drive to Mataram. Buy insurance that covers medical evacuation, save 112, Indonesia's emergency number, and use our directory of Lombok providers by language to find clinics whose English is documented, with the source on each entry.

Lombok or Bali: which side of the strait?

Stand on Gili Air at sunset and the choice is in front of you. On one side, the island with the network: the coworking choice, a community you can join on arrival, and a Canggu budget close to three times Kuta's. On the other, pepper-grain sand, reef breaks reached by boat, a volcano to climb and a bay to yourself, paid for with slower internet, a longer drive to a hospital and a social life you have to build.

If you choose Lombok, come between May and September, base yourself within scooter distance of Kuta town, and take the C1 for a long stay or the E33G if your income clears $60,000. Ask for a speed test before you sign, carry a SIM as your second line, and fly straight into Lombok if you want to skip the Bali levy. Then take the boat out from Gerupuk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a digital nomad community in Lombok?

Yes, but a small one, centred on Kuta on the south coast, where every dedicated coworking space on the island is. You meet people through those spaces and the surf breaks rather than a large scene like Canggu's.

Is Lombok cheaper than Bali for digital nomads?

Yes, mainly on rent. Our estimate for a solo remote worker is about $800 a month in Kuta Lombok against $2,200 in Canggu, and a room with air conditioning in Kuta runs $165 to $275 a month.

Is the internet in Lombok good enough for remote work?

Usually, with a backup. Indonesia's median fixed line managed about 51 Mbps down in August 2026, and 4G was available about 95 percent of the time in the region in early 2025. Carry a local SIM as your second connection.

Which visa do digital nomads need for Lombok?

For a short stay, most Western passports take the $28 visa on arrival, good for 30 days plus one extension to 60. The C1 visit visa allows up to 180 days. For a year of remote work, the E33G requires $60,000 a year in foreign income and costs about $391 in state fees.

Do I pay the Bali tourist levy if I only visit Lombok?

No. The $8.40 levy applies only when a foreign tourist enters Bali. Fly straight into Lombok and never visit Bali, and you do not pay it. Enter through Bali first, and you pay once, which covers return trips to Bali until you leave Indonesia.

Where is the nearest hospital to Kuta Lombok?

In Mataram, which has the public General Hospital of West Nusa Tenggara Province and two other hospitals. The UK Foreign Office's list shows no facility in Central Lombok, where Kuta is.

When is the best time to work from Lombok?

May to September. Kuta averages 32 mm of rain in July and 35 mm in August, drier than Canggu in every month from May to October. Rinjani's trails close from January to March, and the MotoGP weekend on 9 to 11 October 2026 pushes up prices around Kuta.
