Lombok Digital Nomad Guide: Community, Wifi and Visas

Lombok's digital nomad community is small, and the arrival figures show how small. In 2024, 81,544 foreign visitors landed at Lombok International Airport, according to Indonesia's statistics office (BPS). Bali recorded 6,333,360 direct foreign arrivals in the same year, about 78 for every one on Lombok. The remote workers who do come gather in one place, Kuta on the south coast, with thinner spreads in Selong Belanak, Senggigi and the Gili islands.

You get the Bali setup with a fraction of the people, a south coast that is drier than Canggu from May to October, and rent that our estimates put at well under half of Canggu's. You give up the scene, the coworking choice and quick hospital access. Prices are in US dollars at Bank Indonesia's JISDOR rate of IDR 17,917 to the dollar on 25 September 2026.

How big the digital nomad community in Lombok really is

No agency counts digital nomads, so the size of the scene has to be read from proxies. The most direct is the monthly BPS count of foreign arrivals at the airport, known locally as BIZAM after its official name, Zainuddin Abdul Madjid. Between January 2024 and July 2025 it ran from 4,947 in November 2024 to 10,512 in July 2025, the highest month in the series.

The airport count understates Lombok, because many foreigners arrive by boat from Bali. The hotel survey fills part of that gap. BPS counted 88,232 foreign guests in West Nusa Tenggara's non-star hotels in July 2025, and they stayed an average of 2.01 nights. That is a province of short visits: surfers, divers on the Gilis, Rinjani trekkers. The people who stay a month or longer are a small share that the statistics do not separate out.

Our own ratings say the same. We score the digital nomad community 5 out of 10 for Lombok as a whole and 3 for Kuta Lombok on its own, against 10 for Canggu and for Bali overall. In practice, you build a network here through the handful of coworking spaces and surf breaks. Nobody hands you one on arrival.

Where digital nomads base themselves on Lombok

Kuta and Mandalika on the south coast are the centre of gravity. Every dedicated coworking space we list on the island sits in or near Kuta town, which is compact enough to walk, with the beach at its edge. East of town, the road to Tanjung Aan runs through the Mandalika resort zone and past the MotoGP circuit.

Selong Belanak is the wide beginner-surf bay west of Kuta, quieter and with fewer services. Gerupuk, east of Kuta, is a fishing and surf village where the best waves are reached by boat. Both suit people who want the south coast without the town, and both mean riding in to work.

Senggigi on the west coast is Lombok's original resort strip, faded but closer to Mataram's hospitals, with no dedicated coworking space on our city page. The Gili islands off the north-west coast have no motor vehicles: Gili Trawangan is the party island, Gili Air and Gili Meno the quieter pair. Mataram, the provincial capital, is where you go for a hospital or a mall, not a beach.

Coworking in Kuta Lombok: what exists and what it claims

Lombok's dedicated coworking spaces fit in one short list, and all of them cluster around Kuta. The most detailed public claims come from The Spot, a garden café with bungalows on Jl. Sawe-Batu Riti. Its own site states a 950 Mbps fibre line capped at up to 60 Mbps per device, a second fibre line with Starlink as failover, and a UPS and generator for power cuts. It lists an air-conditioned focus room from about $5.60, priced in rupiah, and free outdoor desks.

We have not measured those claims. The other options on our city page are KARIA by TIKA, the coworking floor of the TIKA hotel complex, South Lombok Cowork above Kuta beach, and Xeno Hub, a restaurant with desks next to a gym. Our listed monthly hot-desk prices across the island run from $28 to $115.

Outside the Kuta area the dedicated spaces stop, and you work from cafés or your own connection, the sharpest contrast with the choice in our guide to the best coworking spaces in Bali. If a dedicated desk every day is non-negotiable, rent within scooter distance of Kuta town.

Lombok internet speeds: fixed, 4G and 5G by the numbers

No independent body publishes a speed figure for Lombok alone. Ookla's Speedtest Global Index for August 2026 puts Indonesia's median fixed broadband at 51.40 Mbps down and 36.23 Mbps up, 113th in the world, and its median mobile connection at 66.34 Mbps down and 19.80 Mbps up, 70th.

Ookla's Q2 2026 study of ASEAN digital nomad hubs measured Bali's fixed median at 67.04 Mbps, second-lowest of 13 destinations, with 22.1% of fixed samples failing a basic 25 Mbps down and 3 Mbps up threshold. Bali's 5G availability was 42.3%. Lombok is not in the study, so treat those as the neighbouring benchmark rather than a Lombok reading.

For Lombok's own region, Ookla's 2025 Indonesia report groups it into "Bali and Nusa Tenggara", where 4G was available 95.2% of the time in the first half of 2025 and 5G just 17%. Starlink has been licensed in Indonesia since May 2024, and two of the Kuta spaces we list use it as a backup. We rate wifi 4 out of 10 for Lombok and 5 for Kuta, against 6 for Canggu.

So ask any host for a speed test that shows the upload figure before you pay a month's rent, and carry a local SIM with a large data package as your second line. Do not plan around 5G yet.

Visas for digital nomads in Lombok: E33G, visa on arrival and C1

Lombok follows national immigration rules, quoted here from Imigrasi's visa pages as of September 2026. Most Western passports, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and most of the EU, are on Imigrasi's visa-on-arrival list, not its visa-free list, which covers ASEAN members and a short list including Brazil, Colombia and Turkey.

Visa on arrival (B1) for a first Lombok trip

The B1 visa on arrival costs $28, a fee set in rupiah and converted at the rate above, for 30 days, extendable once to a 60-day total. You can buy it as an e-VoA at evisa.imigrasi.go.id before you fly, and Lombok's airport is a listed e-VoA entry point. An e-VoA can be extended online, a paper VoA at an immigration office.

C1 visit visa for Lombok stays up to 180 days

Many guides still call it the B211A, but that code no longer appears on Imigrasi's visa list. The current single-entry tourist visit visa is the C1: $56, likewise set in rupiah, for 60 days, extendable several times up to 180 days in total. Most nationalities need no sponsor, and you must show three months of bank statements holding at least $2,000. It bars you from taking payment from anyone in Indonesia.

E33G digital nomad visa for a year in Lombok

The E33G is Indonesia's digital nomad visa: a one-year stay for someone working for a company established outside Indonesia. It requires a bank account showing income of at least $60,000 a year, an employment contract with that foreign company, the same $2,000 bank statement, and a passport valid for six months. You need no sponsor.

The state fees are set in rupiah and come to $391 at the rate above: $28 for the visa, $112 for verification, $167 for the one-year stay permit and $84 for the re-entry permit. Processing takes five working days after payment, you must enter within 90 days of issue, and the stay permit is issued at the border and can be extended online. Our nomad visa finder sets that income bar against other countries'.

Digital nomad costs in Lombok compared with Canggu

No cost survey covers either place, so every figure below is our own editorial estimate, not a measured basket. Our monthly figure for a solo remote worker is $800 in Kuta Lombok and $2,200 in Canggu.

Rent is the whole difference. A Canggu villa costs roughly three times a Kuta studio, and our Canggu cost of living breakdown shows why: Bali's rents rose faster than supply after 2022, and landlords there now expect months paid up front. On Lombok, our city page notes that a six-month agreement typically takes 20 to 30 percent off the monthly price.

Food changes little, because warung meals cost a few dollars on both islands. What Lombok charges you instead is time: a scooter for every errand, and a trip to Mataram for anything a Kuta shop does not stock.

The Mandalika circuit and what it changes in south Lombok

The state tourism developer ITDC runs The Mandalika, a 1,175-hectare resort area in Central Lombok that has been a special economic zone since 2017, according to ITDC. Inside it sits the Pertamina Mandalika International Circuit, which hosts MotoGP.

The 2026 Indonesian Grand Prix runs from 9 to 11 October, according to the MotoGP calendar. If your stay overlaps with it, book early: our Kuta page notes that prices climb around race weekends. Outside those peaks there is spare room. BPS put star-hotel occupancy across West Nusa Tenggara at 49.92% in July 2025, the busiest month for foreign airport arrivals since at least January 2024.

For a digital nomad the zone cuts both ways. It shares Pujut district with the airport, which is why the south coast is the easiest part of Lombok to reach. It also means the stretch from Kuta to Tanjung Aan is turning into a resort strip. If you chose Lombok for the quiet, look west to Selong Belanak or east to Gerupuk.

Getting to Lombok from Bali and abroad

Lombok International Airport sits in Pujut district, about 15 km north of Kuta and 26 km south-east of Mataram, both as the crow flies. The airport's own site lists DAMRI buses to Mataram and Senggigi.

With 81,544 foreign arrivals in 2024, fewer than 7,000 a month on average, it is a small international gateway, and any long-haul trip needs a connection. By sea, the public car ferry links Padang Bai in east Bali with Lembar in south-west Lombok, and fast boats run from Bali to the Gili islands and Lombok's north-west coast.

Tourist levies for Lombok travellers: the Bali levy and the Gili fee

The Bali levy is a Bali provincial charge, not an Indonesian one. Under Article 5 of Bali Regional Regulation 6 of 2023, since amended by Regulation 2 of 2025, foreign tourists pay a charge set in rupiah, $8.40 at the rate above, each time they enter Bali, whether directly from abroad or indirectly through another part of Indonesia. Payment is electronic, due before or on arrival, through the official Love Bali portal.

The same article says one payment covers you until you leave Indonesia. For a Lombok stay that gives three cases. Fly into Bali and take a boat across, and you pay once, in Bali. Fly straight into Lombok and never touch Bali, and you pay nothing. Nip back to Bali from Lombok on the same trip, and your first payment still covers you. The portal also has an exemption route for stay-permit holders, which includes E33G holders.

The Gilis have their own charge. North Lombok Regency collects a rupiah fee of $1.10 from each foreign tourist entering the three islands under its Regional Regulation 9 of 2023. In April 2026 the regency proposed raising it to $2.80, and in August 2026 the rise was still reported as a plan, not a rate in force.

Best time for remote work in Lombok: dry and wet season

South Lombok's dry season is drier than Canggu's. Our weather data, built from Open-Meteo's 2019 to 2023 averages, shows Kuta Lombok getting 32 mm of rain in July, 35 mm in August and 44 mm in September, against 71, 65 and 84 mm in Canggu. From May to October Kuta is the drier of the two in every month. Daytime highs stay between 28 and 30°C all year.

January is the wettest month, with 268 mm in Kuta, followed by February and December, when our city page notes the beach roads get hard going on a scooter. For remote work, target May to September.

July is the peak: BPS counted 8,652 foreign airport arrivals in July 2024 against 7,461 that September, and September keeps the dry weather. October brings the MotoGP weekend.

Healthcare in Lombok: Mataram hospitals and Gili clinics

The UK Foreign Office's list of medical facilities in Indonesia, updated 9 July 2026, is the clearest map. Its main public entry for Lombok is the General Hospital of West Nusa Tenggara Province (RSUD Provinsi NTB), on Jl. Prabu Rangkasari in Mataram, listed with every specialisation and English-speaking staff.

The list adds two more hospitals in Mataram, the police hospital RS Bhayangkara, where it marks English as "some", and one private hospital. It lists clinics on Gili Trawangan and Gili Air, one offering medical evacuation, and one in Sembalun at the foot of Rinjani. It lists nothing in Central Lombok. From Kuta, anything beyond basic care means the drive to Mataram.

Choose insurance that covers medical evacuation, and save 112, Indonesia's emergency number. Our directory of Lombok providers by language lists the clinics whose English is documented, with the source named on each entry.

Is Lombok the right quieter alternative to Bali for digital nomads?

Lombok suits a remote worker who has already done Bali and wants the same climate for less, with surf and empty bays a scooter ride away. It rewards people who stay for months, who can work on a 4G backup when the fibre drops, and who build a social life from a small group rather than a scene.

It is the wrong choice if you want to arrive into an established nomad network, need fast internet you can take for granted, or want a hospital nearby. For those, Canggu remains the better base, and its higher rent is what you pay for it. Before you book, read the full Lombok city profile for all thirteen scores and the monthly weather.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a digital nomad community in Lombok?

Yes, but a small one. It centres on Kuta on the south coast, where the island's few coworking spaces are. We rate the Lombok nomad community 5 out of 10 and Kuta's 3, against 10 for Canggu. Expect to meet people through coworking spaces and surf breaks rather than organised nomad events.

Is Lombok cheaper than Bali for digital nomads?

Yes, mainly on rent. Our estimate for a solo remote worker is $800 a month in Kuta Lombok against $2,200 in Canggu. A room with air conditioning in Kuta runs $165 to $275 a month, while a private one-bedroom villa in Canggu costs $750 to $1,125. Both are editorial estimates rather than survey data.

Is the internet in Lombok good enough for remote work?

Usually, with a backup. There is no Lombok-only measurement, but Ookla puts Indonesia's median fixed broadband at 51.40 Mbps in August 2026, and 4G was available 95.2% of the time in the Bali and Nusa Tenggara region in early 2025. Some Kuta coworking spaces add Starlink. Carry a local SIM as your second connection.

Which visa do digital nomads need for Lombok?

For a short stay, most Western passports take the $28 visa on arrival, good for 30 days plus one extension. The C1 visit visa allows up to 180 days. To work remotely for a year, the E33G requires $60,000 a year in foreign income and costs $391 in state fees.

Do I pay the Bali tourist levy if I only visit Lombok?

No. The $8.40 levy applies only when a foreign tourist enters Bali. If you fly straight into Lombok and never visit Bali, you do not pay it. If you enter through Bali first, you pay once, and that payment covers return trips to Bali until you leave Indonesia.

Where is the nearest hospital to Kuta Lombok for digital nomads?

The nearest hospitals on the UK Foreign Office's list are in Mataram, including the public General Hospital of West Nusa Tenggara Province. The list shows no facility in Central Lombok, where Kuta is, and Mataram lies about 39 km from Kuta in a straight line, further by road.

How do you get from Bali to Lombok as a remote worker?

Fly into Lombok International Airport, about 15 km from Kuta, or go by sea. The public car ferry runs from Padang Bai in Bali to Lembar on Lombok, and fast boats run to the Gili islands and Lombok's north-west coast. If you entered Indonesia through Bali, you pay the Bali levy there.

When is the best time to visit Lombok as a digital nomad?

May to September. Kuta Lombok averages 32 mm of rain in July and 35 mm in August, drier than Canggu in every month from May to October. July is the busiest month, September keeps the dry weather with fewer arrivals, and the MotoGP weekend on 9 to 11 October 2026 raises accommodation demand around Kuta.
