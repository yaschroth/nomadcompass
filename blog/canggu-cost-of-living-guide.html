Canggu Cost of Living 2026: What Bali Really Costs Now

Yannick Schroth August 2026 11 min read

Contents

Canggu is no longer a cheap place to live. A private one-bedroom villa runs $750 to $1,125 a month, rents climbed roughly 18 percent year on year through 2025 and 2026, and most landlords now want two months upfront. A realistic monthly budget here is $1,500 to $2,500, which puts this stretch of Balinese coast level with Lisbon and above Kraków. This guide is the arithmetic behind that, and what you still get for it.

The reputation lags the prices by about five years. People arrive expecting the $600 months that Bali sold in 2018 and find a village that priced itself into the same bracket as southern Europe. Our data rates the cost of living here 6 out of 10, the same as Valencia.

What Canggu actually costs a digital nomad now

Budget $2,200 a month as a solo remote worker. That is the figure our cost of living index carries, and a comfortable month covering a villa, food, a coworking desk and a scooter lands between $1,500 and $2,500.

Housing is the whole story. A private one-bedroom villa with a pool is $750 to $1,125 monthly on a longer lease. A room in a shared villa cuts that substantially. Guesthouse and monthly Airbnb rates run higher again, and the gap between a walk-in price and a six-month price is wide enough to be worth a week of patience.

The rest is manageable. Local warung meals cost a few dollars, a coworking membership is modest, and a scooter rental is cheap. It is rent, and specifically the deposit convention, that decides whether Canggu fits your number.

Two months upfront is now standard, and many landlords ask for six or twelve months paid in advance for the best rates. Arrive with the cash or accept the monthly premium.

Why Bali rents rose so fast

Three things happened at once. Indonesia reopened after 2022 with an enormous backlog of demand. Remote work went from a fringe arrangement to a normal one, which turned three-week visitors into six-month residents. And Canggu specifically became the default answer to "where should I go", which concentrated all of that into a few square kilometres of former rice paddy.

Supply responded, but villa construction takes time and the good land near Batu Bolong and Berawa was gone early. What followed is the familiar shape: new build pushed north and inland, prices in the established pockets kept climbing, and the long-term rate stopped being a bargain.

The knock-on effects are visible. Traffic on the Canggu shortcut is genuinely bad, and we score cleanliness 4 out of 10, the lowest of any category here, which reflects waste infrastructure that did not scale with the arrivals.

Where to stay in Canggu on a remote work budget

The name covers several distinct areas, and the price difference between them is the main lever you have.

Batu Bolong is the centre of gravity: cafes, the main surf break, the highest density of other remote workers and the highest rents. Berawa is the polished option, with beach clubs and newer villas, and prices to match. Echo Beach sits west, a little quieter, still walkable to everything that matters.

Pererenan is where people move when Batu Bolong gets expensive or loud. It has absorbed a lot of the growth and is now most of the way to being what Canggu was. Nelayan is the quietest of the five, a fishing village edge with less around it.

For a first month, base in Batu Bolong or Echo Beach and look for a longer lease in Pererenan once you know the area. That single move is worth a few hundred dollars a month.

Internet in Canggu: the number to check before you book

Average speeds sit around 25 Mbps. That is enough for video calls and cloud work and slow by global standards, which is why we score wifi 6 out of 10 despite the density of people working here.

Coworking spaces are the reliable option. BWork and Tribal both run steadier connections than most villas, and cafe wifi is generally adequate for a few hours. The real issue is not bandwidth, it is power: outages during heavy rain are routine, and they take the wifi with them.

Carry a Telkomsel or XL SIM with a data package. On this island, a 4G backup is not a precaution, it is part of the setup. Our fuller picture of where to work is in the Bali coworking guide.

Indonesia visa options for digital nomads in Canggu

Most passport holders enter visa-free for 30 days, or buy a Visa on Arrival for about $31, which extends once for another 30 days through an in-person immigration appointment. That covers a two-month stay and is what the majority of people use.

Indonesia also launched a dedicated digital nomad visa, the E33G, valid for up to five years, which requires proof of roughly $60,000 in annual income from foreign sources. The threshold is high compared with the Southeast Asian alternatives, and it is the reason many long-stayers still cycle through visa extensions instead.

Rules here change with little notice and enforcement varies. Verify at imigrasi.go.id before you travel, and compare the regional alternatives in our nomad visa finder. Nothing here is immigration advice.

The Canggu nomad community: the strongest reason to come

We score community 10 out of 10 here. Nowhere else in our data set gets that, and it is the honest counterweight to everything about the cost.

Coliving houses, coworking spaces and beach bars all work as social infrastructure. Slack groups, Facebook communities and weekly meetups mean most people have plans within days of landing. The crowd skews Western, English-speaking and late twenties to thirties, which is either exactly what you want or exactly what you are trying to escape.

If your reason for going somewhere is to not be alone while working, Canggu solves that faster than anywhere. That is what the premium buys.

What the money still buys in Canggu

A pool. That sounds glib, and it is the honest answer: for a Lisbon budget you get a private villa with a garden and a pool, ten minutes from a surf break, in 30°C. No European city at that price offers the same physical life.

Beyond that, the practical stack is unusually complete. English works everywhere in the nomad-facing economy, so daily life needs no Indonesian at all. Scooters make the area navigable. Cafes are built for laptops rather than tolerating them. Nature scores 8, with the rice terraces inland and the Bukit peninsula's better beaches an hour south.

The trade is against cleanliness at 4, traffic that has outgrown the roads, and a food scene we rate 6, heavy on the same international cafe menu repeated a hundred times.

Getting around Canggu and the scooter question

Everything here runs on two wheels. Monthly scooter rental is cheap, and the area is spread out enough along the coast that walking between Batu Bolong, Berawa and Pererenan in the heat stops being appealing by day three. Grab and Gojek cover the gaps, though some areas have informal restrictions on where app drivers may pick up.

The scooter comes with a genuine warning attached. An international driving permit with the motorcycle category is required, police checks do happen, and travel insurance will not pay out for an accident without one. Bali's road injury numbers among visitors are bad, and most of it is inexperienced riders on unfamiliar roads in the wet.

Traffic on the Canggu shortcut is the daily frustration. A journey that looks like ten minutes on a map can take forty at the wrong hour. Factor it in when choosing how far from your coworking space to live.

Ngurah Rai airport is around an hour away in normal traffic and considerably more in bad. Build in the slack for early flights.

Cheaper alternatives to Canggu in Bali and beyond

If the number is the problem, the island still has answers. Ubud inland is calmer, greener and cheaper, with a yoga and wellness crowd instead of a surf one. Sanur on the east coast is quieter, flatter and older in profile, with rents below Canggu's.

Further afield, Lombok is the next island east, considerably cheaper and considerably less developed. Off the island entirely, Chiang Mai remains the value benchmark of Southeast Asia, and our Medellín versus Chiang Mai comparison covers how it stacks up against the Latin American options.

Is Canggu still worth it for digital nomads?

Yes, for a specific person. If you want the densest remote-work community anywhere, a villa with a pool, surf within walking distance and a life that runs entirely in English, Canggu delivers all of it and the price is now honest about that.

No, if you came for cheap. The gap between Canggu's reputation and its rent roll is the single biggest source of disappointment among people arriving in Bali for the first time. Set the budget at European levels and you will enjoy it. Set it at 2018 levels and you will spend a month arguing with landlords.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to live in Canggu per month?

Budget $1,500 to $2,500 a month, with $2,200 a realistic middle. A private one-bedroom villa runs $750 to $1,125, and food, a coworking desk and a scooter make up most of the rest. Sharing a villa is the biggest single saving available.

Is Canggu still cheap for digital nomads in 2026?

No. Rents rose roughly 18 percent year on year through 2025 and 2026, and we now score the cost of living 6 out of 10, level with Valencia. It is comparable to southern Europe rather than to the rest of Southeast Asia, and the reputation for cheapness is out of date.

What is the internet like in Canggu for remote work?

Average speeds are around 25 Mbps, workable for calls and cloud tools but slow globally. BWork and Tribal offer steadier connections than most villas. Power cuts during heavy rain are the bigger problem, so a Telkomsel or XL SIM as 4G backup is essential rather than optional.

Which area of Canggu should a digital nomad stay in?

Batu Bolong for the centre of the scene and the highest rents, Berawa for polish and beach clubs, Echo Beach for a slightly quieter alternative. Pererenan is where people move as prices rise, and Nelayan is the quietest. Start central, then sign a longer lease inland.

Do digital nomads need a visa for Bali?

Most passports get 30 days visa-free or a Visa on Arrival for about $31, extendable once in person. Indonesia's E33G digital nomad visa runs up to five years but requires roughly $60,000 in annual foreign income. Rules change often, so check imigrasi.go.id before travelling.

How much deposit do landlords in Canggu ask for?

Two months upfront is now the norm, and the best long-term rates often require six or twelve months paid in advance. That upfront requirement, more than the monthly figure itself, is what decides whether a Canggu villa fits a given remote worker's cash flow.

Is Canggu good for meeting other remote workers?

It is the best in our data, scoring 10 out of 10 for community. Coliving houses, coworking spaces and beach bars all function socially, and weekly meetups plus active Slack and Facebook groups mean most arrivals have company within days. The crowd skews Western and under 40.

What are the downsides of living in Canggu?

Traffic on the shortcut roads, waste infrastructure that did not scale with the growth, and a food scene that repeats the same international cafe menu. We rate cleanliness 4 out of 10, the lowest of any category here. Rain-season power cuts are a recurring practical annoyance.

What is a cheaper alternative to Canggu in Bali?

Ubud inland is calmer and cheaper with a wellness rather than surf crowd, and Sanur on the east coast is quieter with lower rents. Off the island, Lombok is considerably less developed and less expensive, and Chiang Mai remains Southeast Asia's value benchmark.
