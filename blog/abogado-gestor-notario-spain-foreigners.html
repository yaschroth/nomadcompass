Lawyer in Spain: Abogado, Gestor or Notary for Foreigners

Hiring a lawyer in Spain covers less than most foreigners expect. The deed that makes you the owner of a flat in Alicante is authorised by a notario, a public official who works for neither buyer nor seller and whose fee is fixed by royal decree: about $420 on a $288,000 sale. Your residence paperwork is often filed by a gestor administrativo, who is not a lawyer at all. The abogado is the one who represents you, and the law makes one compulsory only in defined cases.

This guide shows which lawyer in Spain, or which other professional, handles the NIE, the digital nomad visa, a property purchase, an inheritance and tax residency, what each charges, and how to check registration and working language. Prices are in US dollars at $1 = €0.8687, the 1 August 2026 rate our city pages use; the legal thresholds behind them are fixed in euros.

Which lawyer in Spain? Abogado, gestor and notary compared

Spain divides legal work by task rather than under one licence. Six roles cover what a foreign resident will meet, and only two of them charge on an official tariff.

ProfessionalWhat they doWhen the law requires oneHow the fee is set
Abogado (lawyer)Advice, contracts, appeals, defence in courtSigning most civil court claims (LEC art. 31)Freely agreed, with a written quote on request
ProcuradorFormal court representative: files papers, receives noticesAppearing in most civil proceedings (LEC art. 23)Official tariff, Real Decreto 1373/2003
NotarioAuthorises public deeds: sales, mortgages, wills, powers of attorneyAny title you want entered in the Land Registry (Ley Hipotecaria art. 3)Official tariff, Real Decreto 1426/1989
Gestor administrativoFiles forms with public offices: residence, vehicles, tax, companiesNeverFreely agreed

Traductor-intérprete juradoSworn translations with official statusWhen an authority asks for an official translation of a foreign documentFreely agreed; the ministry publishes no scale

The practical rule: the notary protects the legality of the document, not your interests in it, and a gestor moves forms through an office. The abogado is the one whose job is to tell you whether the purchase or the application is a good idea, and the only one who can defend you in court if it goes wrong.

The abogado: when Spanish law requires a lawyer

An abogado must belong to a Colegio de la Abogacía, the local bar. The Ley Orgánica del Poder Judicial (art. 544.2) makes membership compulsory for acting before the courts. Bars are local rather than regional: Alicante province alone has four, in Alicante, Elche, Alcoy and Orihuela, and the provincial one, ICALI, says it regulates about 4,500 lawyers.

Court is where the obligation bites. Under the Ley de Enjuiciamiento Civil (art. 31), a civil claim must carry a lawyer's signature, except in verbal proceedings over amounts up to $2,300 and the opening petition of a debt-collection claim (proceso monitorio). Article 23 adds the procurador on the same exceptions: a law graduate who files and receives the court papers, charges on an official tariff and is normally brought in by your lawyer.

Outside court, nothing forces you to hire a lawyer in Spain. You can buy a flat, apply for a visa or file a tax return alone. The catch is that nobody else in the chain works for you: the notary authorises the deed for both sides, the registry records title, and the estate agent is paid when the sale completes.

Lawyers qualified elsewhere in the EU can practise here too. A German Rechtsanwalt or an Italian avvocato may work in Spain permanently under their home title, but only after registering with the Spanish bar where they keep their office (Real Decreto 936/2001, arts. 3 and 4). A German-speaking lawyer on the Costa Blanca should therefore appear on a Spanish bar's register either way.

The gestor administrativo: Spain's paperwork professional

A gestor administrativo files paperwork with public offices on your behalf: vehicle transfers at Tráfico, tax forms, company registrations and residence applications. The profession has its own colegios under the Consejo General de Colegios de Gestores Administrativos de España, which publishes a public register of members. Alicante has its own colegio, separate from Valencia's, while Málaga shares one with Melilla, and Santa Cruz de Tenerife and the Balearic Islands each have their own.

A gestor is not a lawyer. When an application is refused, the appeal (recurso) is legal work, and so is any dispute. For a routine filing and an ordinary case, a gestor is built for the job. For anything contested, or a case with a history such as an earlier refusal or an overstay, hire an abogado from the start. Either way, ask who signs the filing and which colegio that person belongs to: both registers are public.

The notario: a public official, not your lawyer

The Spanish notary authorises public deeds (escrituras) and gives them legal force. The Land Registry only records titles contained in a public deed or an equivalent official document (Ley Hipotecaria, art. 3), so every purchase you want registered and every mortgage passes through a notary. Wills, powers of attorney and, where an estate includes registered property, the deed in which the heirs accept it are signed there too.

You choose the notary. On a mortgage, the 2019 real estate credit law (Ley 5/2019, art. 14) makes the bank hand you the loan documents at least ten calendar days before signing, and gives you the right to personalised advice, free of charge, from the notary you pick.

Who pays is set by the Civil Code (art. 1455): the cost of granting the deed falls on the seller and the first copy on the buyer, "salvo pacto en contrario", unless the contract says otherwise. Check the clause on gastos in the private contract before you sign it, because that is where any change to the split is written.

Sworn translators and tax advisers in Spain

A traductor-intérprete jurado holds a title granted by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and their translations "tendrán carácter oficial" before courts and administrations. The Spanish consulate in Washington, for instance, wants the FBI record for the digital nomad visa with a Hague apostille and "an official translation into Spanish by a sworn/certified translator".

The ministry publishes a searchable list of sworn translators. Its interpretation office says it "no hace de intermediaria" in these jobs and publishes no fee scale, so you agree the price directly. Our directory lists 120 translators and interpreters in Alicante by working language; check a name against the ministry list before you rely on its stamp.

Tax advice comes from lawyers, economists, gestores and specialist firms, and no law requires you to use any of them. The two questions worth paying for, first-year residence and the six-month window of the regime for new arrivals, are covered in the tax section below.

NIE and TIE in Spain: the official fees and who files them

The NIE is the foreigner identity number that Spanish banks, notaries and employers ask for. The Policía Nacional charges $11.30 to assign one at your request, paid with form 790, code 012. EU citizens who settle register instead and receive the green certificado de registro, which carries the NIE and costs $13.80.

Non-EU residents carry a TIE, the physical residence card. As of September 2026 the same police fee form lists $18.50 for the card that documents a first temporary residence or stay authorisation and $22.20 for the card after a renewal. These are the state fees. Anything above them on an invoice is the professional's own charge.

None of this needs a lawyer. You can file it yourself, or pay a gestor if Spanish or time is the obstacle. An abogado earns the fee only when there is a complication: a refusal, an earlier irregular stay, or a family member whose status depends on yours.

The digital nomad visa: when a lawyer in Spain earns the fee

Spain's international telework permit comes from the 2022 Startups Law (Ley 28/2022), which added articles 74 bis to 74 quinquies to Ley 14/2013. You must work remotely for companies based outside Spain. If you are self-employed, Spanish clients may make up no more than 20% of your professional activity. You need a degree or at least three years of professional experience, the company must have been operating for at least a year, and an employee must have worked for it for the three months before applying.

The income floor is 200% of the minimum wage, which Real Decreto 126/2026 sets at $1,410 a month in 14 payments, or $19,680 a year. The consulate in Washington applies it to the monthly figure and asks for $2,810 a month, plus $1,050 for the first family member and $350 for each further one. Spread the annual minimum wage over twelve months and double it, and the floor is $3,280, the figure our nomad visa finder carries. Evidence of income above the higher number clears both readings.

There are two routes. From abroad, a consulate issues a visa valid for up to one year (art. 74 quater). From inside Spain, if you are there legally, you apply for a residence authorisation of up to three years, renewable in two-year periods (art. 74 quinquies). That application is filed online with the Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos (UGE), which has 20 days to decide. If it does not answer in time, the permit counts as granted (art. 76).

Filing online needs a Spanish digital certificate or a representative who holds one. An abogado is worth the fee when the case is not clean: a freelancer near the 20% line, dependants, or a refusal that needs a recurso de alzada. On the Canary Islands the tax side differs, which our Las Palmas digital nomad guide covers.

Buying property in Spain: notary, registry and a lawyer of your own

A resale purchase usually runs in two steps: a private deposit contract (contrato de arras), then the public deed at the notary, which goes on to the Registro de la Propiedad. Before the deposit, someone on your side should pull the nota simple from the registry, which shows the registered owner and any mortgage or charge. That check is the core of what a property lawyer in Spain does for a buyer.

Notary and Land Registry fees on a Spanish home

Both tariffs fall by bracket as the price rises. The notary's scale (Real Decreto 1426/1989, Annex I, number 2) starts with a fixed $100 on the lowest bracket, then charges 4.5, 1.5, 1, 0.5 and 0.3 per thousand on successive slices of the price, less a compulsory 5% reduction. On a $288,000 home that comes to about $420 for the deed, plus $3.46 per page of the authorised copy, halved from the twelfth page, plus 21% VAT.

The Land Registry runs a separate scale (Real Decreto 1427/1989) with the same 5% reduction. On the same home the entry costs about $220, and no single entry can exceed $2,510. Both fees follow from the price alone, so you can calculate them before you make an offer.

Property transfer tax by region: Alicante, Málaga, Tenerife, Mallorca, Madrid

The largest cost is regional tax. A resale home pays transfer tax (ITP). A new build pays 10% VAT under the VAT law (Ley 37/1992, art. 91), or IGIC on the Canary Islands, plus stamp duty (AJD) on the deed. The finance ministry's 2026 survey of regional taxes gives these general rates:

  • Comunitat Valenciana (Alicante): ITP 9% since 1 June 2026, 11% on properties above about $1.15 million; AJD 1.4%.
  • Andalucía (Málaga, Seville): ITP 7%; AJD 1.2%.
  • Canarias (Tenerife, Las Palmas): ITP 6.5%; AJD 0.75%.
  • Illes Balears (Palma, Ibiza): ITP on a scale from 8% on the first $460,000, rising in steps to 13% on the part above $2.3 million; AJD 1.5%.
  • Comunidad de Madrid: ITP 6%; AJD 0.75%.

On a $288,000 resale that is $25,900 in Alicante against $20,150 in Málaga and $17,270 in Madrid. Alicante's rate was 10% from August 2013 until 31 May 2026, so guides written before this summer overstate it. Reduced rates exist for young buyers, large families and protected housing, and most of them require the home to be your habitual residence.

Selling Spanish property as a non-resident: the 3% retention and the plusvalía

If you sell while non-resident, the buyer must withhold 3% of the agreed price and pay it to the tax agency on account of your tax (non-resident income tax law, art. 25.2). On a $288,000 sale that is $8,630 held back at the notary's table and credited against your capital gains tax when you file.

The municipal plusvalía taxes the rise in land value. The seller owes it, but when the seller is a non-resident individual the buyer becomes the substitute taxpayer (local finance law, art. 106.2), which gives the buyer a reason to hold the estimated amount back.

Since the 2021 reform no tax is due where there was no gain (art. 104.5), and you can ask to be taxed on the real gain when it is lower than the formula (art. 107.5). The return is due within 30 working days of the sale (art. 110).

Inheritance in Spain: the will, the notary and the six-month tax deadline

Which law governs the estate is decided by the EU Succession Regulation (650/2012): by default the law of the state where you were habitually resident at death (art. 21). A Briton or an American living in Alicante therefore gets Spanish succession law, which reserves a share for children. You can choose the law of your nationality instead, but the choice "shall be made expressly" in a will or follow from its terms (art. 22).

After a death, the heirs request the certificate from the Registro General de Actos de Última Voluntad, which shows whether a Spanish will exists; the Ministry of Justice accepts the request 15 working days after the death. The heirs then accept the estate in a notarial deed, which the registry needs before it records the property in their names. The plusvalía is due within six months here, extendable to a year.

Inheritance tax is due within six months of the death (Real Decreto 1629/1991, art. 67), and a six-month extension has to be requested within the first five. Non-resident heirs have the right to apply the rules of the region where the deceased lived or, for a non-resident deceased, where most of the Spanish estate lies (Ley 29/1987, second additional provision), a right Spain wrote in after an EU court ruling in 2014.

Those regional rules are generous for close family. For children and spouses (tax groups I and II) the 2026 reliefs are a 99% reduction of the tax bill in Valencia, Andalucía and Madrid, 100% in the Balearic Islands, and 99.9% in the Canary Islands, which extends it to group III relatives such as siblings. The notary, registry and plusvalía still fall due.

Tax residency in Spain: the 183-day rule and the Beckham regime

Under the income tax law (Ley 35/2006, art. 9) you are resident if you spend more than 183 days of the calendar year in Spain, or if your main base of economic interests is here. Sporadic absences count as days in Spain unless you prove tax residence elsewhere, and Spain presumes residence if your spouse and minor children live here. A remote worker based in Alicante all year is therefore resident and taxed on worldwide income.

The special regime for new arrivals, known as the Beckham law (art. 93), lets qualifying people pay 24% on income up to about $690,000 and 47% above it, for the year of the move and the five following years, if they were not resident in the previous five. Since the 2022 reform it covers remote work, but the text names employees (trabajadores por cuenta ajena) holding the telework visa. A self-employed digital nomad does not qualify through that clause.

The option must be exercised within six months of the start date on your Social Security registration (income tax regulation, art. 116), and choosing it also makes you liable to wealth tax on your Spanish assets. That deadline, and the question of whether you are resident at all in your first year, are the two points where an asesor fiscal or a tax lawyer earns the fee.

What a lawyer in Spain charges, and why the bar will not say

There is no official fee scale for lawyers. The bar's own statute (Real Decreto 135/2021, art. 26) says fees are "libremente convenida", freely agreed between client and lawyer. The law on professional bodies goes further: bars "no podrán establecer baremos orientativos ni cualquier otra orientación, recomendación" on fees (Ley 2/1974, art. 14). No bar in Alicante or Málaga can therefore quote you a going rate.

You may find a bar's fee criteria online. They exist "a los exclusivos efectos de la tasación de costas" (the same law, fourth additional provision), for assessing the costs a court orders a losing party to pay. They are not a price list for your case.

What you get instead is a right to a quote. Since 4 December 2024 the Ley Orgánica 5/2024 on the right of defence (art. 15) lets any client ask for the engagement in writing, in a hoja de encargo with a "presupuesto previo con los honorarios y costes". The statute adds that the lawyer must tell you the likely cost of losing (art. 48.4). Get both in writing before paying any advance, and compare two quotes on the same scope. Fees carry 21% VAT.

How to check a lawyer in Spain is registered

Start with the lawyer. The statute (art. 48.1) obliges every lawyer to give you their name, tax number, the bar they belong to, their membership number (número de colegiado) and a professional address. Someone who calls themselves a lawyer and cannot give you a bar and a number is not practising as an abogado in Spain, and cannot represent you in court.

Then check the number. The Consejo General de la Abogacía Española compiles the lists every bar sends into a national census of practising lawyers, the Censo de Letrados, published on its website with the bar where each one practises (statute, art. 15). The census is built as the register of practising (ejerciente) lawyers, so a lawyer missing from it owes you an explanation.

Each bar also runs its own search. In Alicante that is the Ilustre Colegio Provincial de la Abogacía de Alicante, with separate bars in Elche, Alcoy and Orihuela. Málaga has its own bar (ICAMálaga), Seville the Colegio de Abogados de Sevilla (ICAS), Tenerife the Ilustre Colegio de Abogados de Santa Cruz de Tenerife, and Palma and Ibiza the Ilustre Colegio de Abogados de las Illes Balears. An EU lawyer under a home title appears with the Spanish bar that registered them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer to buy property in Spain?

No law requires one. What the law requires is a notary, because the Land Registry only records a purchase made in a public deed. The notary acts for both sides, so a buyer's lawyer is the only professional who checks the registry extract, the charges on the property and the contract terms from your side.

How much does a notary cost in Spain for a property purchase?

The fee is fixed by Real Decreto 1426/1989 and depends on the price. On a $288,000 home the deed costs about $420 after the compulsory 5% reduction, plus $3.46 per page of the authorised copy and 21% VAT. The Land Registry entry adds about $220 on the same price.

What is the difference between a gestor and a lawyer in Spain?

A gestor administrativo files paperwork with public offices, such as NIE, residence and vehicle forms. A lawyer, an abogado, gives legal advice, drafts contracts, appeals refusals and represents you in court. For a routine filing a gestor is enough. For a refusal, a dispute or an unusual case you need an abogado.

How do I check a lawyer is registered in Spain?

Ask for the bar (colegio) and membership number, which every lawyer must give you. Check it in the national Censo de Letrados kept by the Consejo General de la Abogacía Española, or in the search of the local bar, such as ICALI in Alicante. The entry should show the lawyer as practising (ejerciente).

Can a German or Italian lawyer work in Spain?

Yes. A lawyer qualified in another EU country can practise in Spain permanently under their home title, as Rechtsanwalt or avvocato, after registering with the Spanish bar where they have their office (Real Decreto 936/2001). We list 66 German-speaking and 80 Italian-speaking lawyers in Spain, each with a published source.

How much does an NIE cost in Spain in 2026?

The Policía Nacional fee to assign an NIE is $11.30, paid with form 790, code 012. EU citizens who settle get the registration certificate for $13.80, and non-EU residents pay $18.50 for a first residence card (TIE) and $22.20 after a renewal. Any higher total is a professional's fee.

How much income does the Spain digital nomad visa require in 2026?

Twice the minimum wage. The Spanish consulate in Washington applies that as $2,810 a month for a single applicant, plus $1,050 for the first family member and $350 for each further one. Computed on the annual minimum wage the floor is $3,280, so income above that satisfies either reading of the Spanish rule.

Do non-resident heirs pay inheritance tax in Spain?

Yes, on Spanish assets, but they can apply the regional rules. For children and spouses those cut the tax by 99% in Valencia, Andalucía and Madrid, 100% in the Balearic Islands and 99.9% in the Canaries. The return is due within six months of the death, with a six-month extension available on request.
