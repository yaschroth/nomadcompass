St Kilda Digital Nomad Guide: Melbourne Rent, Trams, Visas

A one-bedroom flat in St Kilda let for a median of $332 a week in the latest official Victorian rental data, less than in Fitzroy, Collingwood, Brunswick, Richmond, Carlton or South Yarra. For a St Kilda digital nomad that is the whole case in one number: the beach suburb is the cheapest of Melbourne's popular inner neighbourhoods, and tram routes 96 and 16 run from it straight through the city. This guide tests it against trams, internet, visas and weather, using government data.

Prices are in US dollars, converted at the Reserve Bank of Australia rate of A$1 = US$0.70 (25 September 2026). Rent is quoted per week in Australia; monthly figures here are weekly times 52, divided by 12.

Why St Kilda works as a Melbourne base for digital nomads

St Kilda sits on Port Phillip Bay south of the city centre, with Luna Park, the Acland Street cake shops and a beach at the end of the tram line. It is also a deep rental market: 338 new one-bedroom leases were lodged there in the September 2025 quarter, against 60 in Fitzroy. Among the suburbs in this guide, only South Yarra and the CBD recorded more.

The trade is distance and noise. St Kilda is outside the Free Tram Zone, so every trip into the centre costs a fare, and our own neighbourhood notes say the nightlife can get rowdy and the tourist crowds thick. If your day is a laptop, a flat white and a walk on the foreshore, neither matters much. If you want to walk to a CBD coworking desk, it does.

St Kilda rent against Melbourne's inner suburbs: the official medians

The Victorian Government's Homes Victoria Rental Report is built from bonds lodged for new leases, so it records signed rents, not asking prices. The September quarter 2025 edition, the latest published as of September 2026, puts the metropolitan Melbourne median at $407 a week, up 3.5% on a year earlier. Here are the one-bedroom flat medians for the suburbs a remote worker usually weighs, from the Homes Victoria rent lookup:

  • St Kilda: $332 a week, about $1,440 a month. 338 new leases.
  • Brunswick: $350 a week, about $1,515 a month. 124 new leases.
  • South Yarra: $365 a week, about $1,580 a month. 381 new leases.
  • Collingwood and Abbotsford: $368 a week, about $1,600 a month. 151 new leases.
  • Richmond and Burnley: $368 a week, about $1,600 a month. 221 new leases.
  • Carlton and Parkville: $372 a week, about $1,610 a month. 278 new leases.
  • Fitzroy: $378 a week, about $1,635 a month. 60 new leases.
  • CBD and St Kilda Road: $406 a week, about $1,760 a month. 1,634 new leases.

Two-bedroom flats tell a similar story. St Kilda's median is $448 a week, against $421 in Brunswick, $491 in Collingwood, Richmond, Carlton and South Yarra, $526 in the CBD and $540 in Fitzroy. Split between two people, a St Kilda two-bedroom costs about $224 a week each. East St Kilda, the next suburb inland, is lower again at $298 a week for a one-bedroom.

Read these as the floor, not the price you will pay for a month. The report counts leases with a rental bond lodged, and it leaves out rooms in shared houses, rooming houses and student housing. A furnished flat booked by the month is a different market, and our Melbourne city profile carries a Numbeo figure of $1,740 a month for a central one-bedroom, about $300 above St Kilda's lease median.

St Kilda vs Fitzroy, Collingwood, Brunswick, Richmond, Carlton and South Yarra

Fitzroy is the one most remote workers picture: Brunswick Street, the bars and the cafe density. It is also the dearest one-bedroom on the list outside the CBD and the thinnest market, with 60 new leases in a quarter. Collingwood and Abbotsford, its eastern neighbours, run $10 a week cheaper with more than twice the supply, and The Commons, one of the coworking spaces on our Melbourne list, is there.

Brunswick is the value pick on the north side, only $18 a week above St Kilda for a one-bedroom and cheaper for a two-bedroom. Carlton puts you beside the University of Melbourne and the Italian restaurants of Lygon Street, north of the city grid. Richmond has Inspire9, which our listing records as Melbourne's first coworking space, open since 2008.

South Yarra is the closest match to St Kilda: south of the river, with 381 new one-bedroom leases, the most here outside the CBD. It costs $33 a week more and has no beach, which is the one thing none of the others can offer.

Getting from St Kilda to the CBD: tram routes, myki and half-price fares

Four Yarra Trams routes serve St Kilda. Route 96 runs from East Brunswick via Fitzroy, Carlton, the city and South Melbourne to St Kilda Beach and Luna Park, and the operator lists it as a night route. Route 16 comes down from Melbourne University past the Shrine of Remembrance to St Kilda Beach and Acland Street, then continues to Kew. Route 12 links St Kilda with South Melbourne, St Vincent's Hospital and Victoria Gardens shopping centre, and route 3 reaches St Kilda Town Hall on weekdays.

Route 96 matters most: one tram, no change, links the beach with Carlton, Fitzroy and East Brunswick.

Fares are unusually cheap right now. The Victorian Government has cut public transport fares to half price until the end of 2026. Fares are set in Australian dollars; converted, the adult fare is $2.00 for two hours and $4.00 as a daily cap, with weekends and public holidays capped at $2.81. A 7-day myki Pass is $20, and longer passes cost $2.40 a day, about $72 for 30 days. Expect those numbers to roughly double if the discount ends on schedule.

Since 21 June 2026 you can tap on any tram with a bank card, phone or smartwatch instead of a myki, according to the Victorian Government's announcement. The Free Tram Zone covers the CBD and Docklands only: a ride that starts in St Kilda needs a valid fare, even if it ends inside the zone.

Internet in St Kilda: NBN technology and what the ACCC measured

Home broadband in Melbourne runs over the national NBN, and nbn assigns the connection type premises by premises, so the suburb name tells you little. One flat may have fibre to the premises (FTTP), another copper from a street node (FTTN), and an apartment block may use fibre to the building (FTTB). Before you sign, put the address into nbn's address checker or ask the agent which technology the flat uses.

It matters because of what the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission measured. In its 33rd and final Measuring Broadband Australia report, released on 17 June 2026, NBN fixed-line services averaged 100.5% of plan speed between 7pm and 11pm on weeknights. An NBN 100 plan delivered 92.2 Mbps in those busy hours and an NBN 500 plan 500.3 Mbps. Across all hours, 88.2% of tests reached at least 90% of plan speed and 4.6% fell below half.

The ACCC notes that FTTN has accounted for most of the underperforming services throughout the program. Upload is the other catch: services averaged 90.4% of plan upload speed, because upload tiers are not over-provisioned the way download tiers are, and upload is what a video call or a large file transfer leans on. According to nbn, selected FTTN and FTTC premises can now upgrade to full fibre with an eligible plan, but that is a decision for a long-term tenant, not a monthly guest.

Our city profile lists coworking in the CBD, Richmond and Collingwood, with hot desks at $210 to $350 a month, and none in St Kilda itself, so plan to ride route 96 or 16 in on desk days.

Australian visitor visas and remote work in St Kilda

Australia has no digital nomad visa, so it is absent from our nomad visa finder, and most remote workers arrive as visitors. Home Affairs sets its charges in Australian dollars, converted here. The ETA (subclass 601) costs a $14 app service charge, is valid for 12 months, and allows stays of up to 3 months after each entry. European passport holders use the free eVisitor (subclass 651) on the same 3-month terms. The Visitor visa (subclass 600) costs from $175 and generally grants 3 months, up to 12 in certain circumstances.

The Department of Home Affairs is plain about work. The ETA page says: "The ETA is not a work visa. You cannot undertake paid work for an Australian employer." The Visitor visa page says "You can't work", and a business visitor "can't work for or provide services to a business or organisation based in Australia". None of those pages mentions remote work for an employer abroad.

The Department's own procedural guidance fills that gap. As quoted by the International Bar Association in December 2021, it says online work that is "incidental to a holiday" is "not of concern in terms of condition 8101", the no-work condition. Keeping up with a job at home during a holiday is tolerated. Working full time from a St Kilda flat for three months is not what that sentence describes.

Repeat visits carry their own risk. Home Affairs says long or frequent stays may prompt questions about your travel purpose on arrival, and it can cancel the visa of anyone it finds is not a genuine visitor. Back-to-back three-month stays are exactly that pattern.

The working holiday visa: the Melbourne option that allows work

If you are young enough, the Working Holiday visa removes the grey area. The subclass 417 is open at 18 to 30, or 18 to 35 for passports including the UK, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Denmark, Finland and South Korea. The subclass 462 covers other countries at 18 to 30, with a ballot for citizens of China, India and Vietnam. Either costs $590, set in Australian dollars, and gives 12 months in Australia.

On it, you "can do any kind of work", in the Department's words, though usually for no more than six months with one employer, a limit called condition 8547. You apply from outside Australia, without dependent children, and Home Affairs puts processing at around three months, so book flights only after the grant. Earning in Australia also raises where you owe tax, which our digital nomad tax guide explains through the residency tests.

For help with a visa application in your own language, our directory lists 4,396 lawyers in Melbourne by the language they work in, 965 of them in Chinese and 480 in Greek, each with the source of the language claim.

What a month in St Kilda costs a remote worker

Start with the official rent and add the rest from our city data. A St Kilda one-bedroom at the lease median is about $1,440 a month. Our Melbourne profile prices a one-person basket of groceries, a few meals out, utilities, phone, internet and transport at $1,000 a month from Numbeo's August 2026 figures. That makes about $2,440 before a workspace, or $2,650 to $2,790 with a monthly hot desk.

That sits at the top of the $2,410 to $2,740 range our profile gives for Melbourne. A furnished monthly rental adds roughly the $300 gap between the Numbeo centre rent and the St Kilda median.

Our Sydney profile puts a month there at about $3,490, roughly $750 more. Across the region, our ranking of the best digital nomad cities in Oceania has Melbourne tied with Sydney on overall score, with cost as the weakest category for both.

Melbourne weather from St Kilda: four seasons in a day, measured

The Bureau of Meteorology's Melbourne Olympic Park station, open since 2013, averages a January high of 26.8°C, a July high of 14.5°C and 575 mm of rain a year. Averages hide Melbourne's reputation, though. At the long-running Melbourne Regional Office station, one January day in ten stays below 19.4°C and one in ten tops 35.6°C.

That spread is the "four seasons in a day" in numbers: a summer week can hold both a cool day and a very hot one. The record is 46.4°C, set on 7 February 2009, and the long-term average is ten days a year at 35°C or more, most of them between December and February. It also logs 179 cloudy days a year against 49 clear ones.

For a St Kilda base, March to May is the sweet spot: highs of 18 to 24°C at Olympic Park and the summer heatwaves mostly over. June to August is grey and cool, with lows of 7 to 8°C.

Healthcare for visitors in Melbourne: Medicare agreements and insurance

Home Affairs is direct on this: "You are personally liable for all your healthcare costs while you are in Australia", and it recommends health insurance for every visitor. The exception is the eleven countries with a Reciprocal Health Care Agreement: Belgium, Finland, Ireland, Italy, Malta, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Slovenia, Sweden and the United Kingdom.

For a UK visitor, Services Australia says the agreement covers medically necessary care outside hospital, treatment as a public patient in a public hospital, and some prescription medicines at the general PBS rate. You must have lived in the UK before arrival, and you enrol in Medicare through myGov with your visa, passport and proof of UK residence. Italian visitors get six months of cover.

Hospital cover under the agreements is as a public patient only, and for UK visitors it stops when the visa does, so travel insurance still earns its place. Visitors from anywhere else, including the US and Canada, need insurance from day one. The emergency number is 000.

Who St Kilda suits, and when another Melbourne suburb wins

St Kilda suits a remote worker who wants the bay, the lowest one-bedroom rent among Melbourne's popular inner suburbs and a direct tram to the north side, and who works mostly from home or a cafe. It suits a working holiday maker especially well, because the visa question disappears and the rental market is one of the deepest in the inner city.

Choose Fitzroy or Collingwood if your week revolves around coworking desks and late cafes, and pay the $36 to $46 a week extra. Choose Brunswick if you are sharing: its two-bedroom median of $421 a week is below St Kilda's $448. Choose the CBD if Free Tram Zone rides matter more than the view.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is St Kilda a good base for digital nomads in Melbourne?

Yes, if you want the beach and lower rent. St Kilda's one-bedroom lease median was $332 a week, below Fitzroy, Collingwood, Brunswick, Richmond, Carlton and South Yarra. Tram routes 96, 16, 12 and 3 serve it. The downsides are a paid fare into the centre and nightlife that can get rowdy.

How much is rent in St Kilda compared with Fitzroy and Brunswick?

In Homes Victoria's September quarter 2025 data, a one-bedroom flat let for a median $332 a week in St Kilda, $350 in Brunswick and $378 in Fitzroy. Monthly, that is about $1,440, $1,515 and $1,635. These are new bonded leases, so a furnished monthly stay usually costs more.

Can digital nomads work remotely from St Kilda on an Australian visitor visa?

Only incidentally. Home Affairs says the ETA "is not a work visa" and the Visitor visa page says "You can't work". Its procedural guidance treats online work "incidental to a holiday" as no breach of the no-work condition. Full-time remote work needs a visa that allows work, such as a Working Holiday visa.

Which trams go from St Kilda to Melbourne's city centre?

Route 96 runs from St Kilda Beach through the city to Carlton and East Brunswick. Route 16 goes past the Shrine of Remembrance to Melbourne University, route 12 via South Melbourne to Victoria Gardens, and route 3 stops at St Kilda Town Hall on weekdays. A two-hour fare is $2.00 until the end of 2026.

How fast is the internet in St Kilda for remote work?

It depends on the building's NBN technology, which is set per address. The ACCC's final 2026 report found NBN 100 plans averaged 92.2 Mbps in evening peak hours and NBN 500 plans 500.3 Mbps. Fibre to the node accounted for most slow services, so check the address before signing a lease.

Does Medicare cover digital nomads visiting Melbourne?

Only visitors covered by one of Australia's eleven Reciprocal Health Care Agreements, such as residents of the UK, Ireland, New Zealand, Italy and the Netherlands, and only after enrolling in Medicare. It covers medically necessary care, public hospital treatment and some medicines. Everyone else pays their own costs, so needs insurance from arrival.

When is the best time of year to work remotely from St Kilda?

March to May. Bureau of Meteorology averages put highs at 18 to 24°C, and the heatwave season is mostly over. Summer brings about ten days a year at 35°C or more, and June to August is cool and grey, with July highs near 14.5°C and lows of 7 to 8°C.
