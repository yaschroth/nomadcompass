Safest Cities for Female Digital Nomads: What the Data Says

Only 47 percent of women in New Zealand tell Gallup they feel safe walking alone at night where they live, against 86 percent in Norway. Yet six of the fifteen places in our ranking of the safest cities for female digital nomads are in New Zealand. This guide shows how to judge a city's safety as a woman working remotely alone with data instead of reputation, and checks ten cities against it.

Our ranking of the best cities for female digital nomads puts Oulu in Finland first, then Turku, Queenstown and Trondheim. It rests on our own editorial scores, so each city below faces four outside tests: a women-specific country index, a peace index, United Nations violence data and national police statistics.

How our female digital nomad ranking is built

The Match score on our ranking is a weighted blend of six inputs. Safety carries 30 percent. Cleanliness and ease in English carry 15 percent each, air quality and nomad community 10 percent each, and affordability the remaining 20 percent, scaled from the cheapest to the most expensive city in our data of 1,000. Oulu scores 8.6 on that blend, Turku 8.5 and Queenstown 8.4. Ties are broken on the overall Nomad Score.

The inputs matter more than the weights. Our methodology page lists the 13 category scores, safety included, as editorial: assigned by judgement, with "no measurement, no external index, no sample". The same page names replacing the safety score with a sourced input as the most valuable provenance fix still outstanding. The ranking page adds that we hold no gender-specific data at all, no harassment reports and no crime statistics.

The cost column has two bases. For Oulu, Turku, Tampere, Trondheim, Queenstown, Wellington and Calgary it comes from Numbeo's August 2026 prices and is the top of the range: a one-bedroom in the centre plus a one-person basket. For Torshavn, St John's and Rarotonga it is our own labelled estimate, because no cost survey covers them. Treat the ranking as a shortlist, and the rest of this article as the test it should pass.

The Women, Peace and Security Index: country safety measured for women

The Women, Peace and Security Index 2025/26 comes from Georgetown University's Institute for Women, Peace and Security with the Peace Research Institute Oslo. It scores 181 countries on 13 indicators grouped into inclusion, justice and security. Denmark ranks first with a score of .939. Norway and Sweden share third, Finland is fifth, New Zealand ninth, Canada 16th, the United Kingdom 23rd and the United States 31st.

Two of the 13 indicators speak directly to female digital nomads living alone in a new city. Community safety is the share of women aged 15 and over who answer yes to the Gallup World Poll question "Do you feel safe walking alone at night in the city or area where you live?", using data from 2022 to 2024. Intimate partner violence is the share of ever-partnered women who experienced physical or sexual violence from a partner in the previous 12 months.

Globally, 66 percent of women say they feel safe walking alone at night. Singapore is highest at 97 percent and Syria lowest at 17. Among the countries on our list, Norway reaches 86 percent, Finland 78, Canada 63, the United States 58 and New Zealand 47. New Zealand ranks ninth overall on the strength of its other indicators; on this one it sits below the global average.

The lesson for female digital nomads: read the indicator, not the rank. A composite blends parliamentary seats and maternal mortality with street safety, so a country can rank highly for reasons unrelated to your walk home. And the survey is residents describing their own neighbourhoods, the best proxy for public space and still a perception.

What the Global Peace Index 2026 adds, and where it misleads

The Global Peace Index 2026, published in June 2026 by the Institute for Economics and Peace, ranks 163 countries on 23 indicators in three domains: Societal Safety and Security, Ongoing Domestic and International Conflict, and Militarisation. Iceland is first for the 19th consecutive year. New Zealand is second, Singapore eighth, Finland ninth, Denmark 11th and Canada 14th. The United States fell to 134th after a 4 percent deterioration in a single year.

Norway shows why the headline rank is the wrong number for this question. It sits 33rd overall, pulled down by the militarisation domain, which counts things like military spending and weapons exports. On the Societal Safety and Security domain alone, Norway ranks first in the world and Finland second. New Zealand drops to 14th on that domain and Canada shares 16th with South Korea.

The domain score is the one to read. Its perceptions-of-criminality indicator uses the same Gallup question as the women's index, but counts everyone who answers no, men included. For female digital nomads choosing a base, the women-only figure in the WPS Index is the sharper version of the same measurement.

Violence against women data, and the question it actually answers

The United Nations publishes the most complete global count. The UNODC and UN Women brief Femicides in 2024, released in November 2025, estimates that 83,000 women and girls were killed intentionally in 2024. Around 50,000 of them, 60 percent, were killed by an intimate partner or another family member: 137 a day. Relative to population, those killings run at 3 per 100,000 women in Africa, 1.5 in the Americas, 1.4 in Oceania, 0.7 in Asia and 0.5 in Europe.

These numbers describe danger inside homes and relationships: in Europe 64 percent of those killings were by intimate partners, in the Americas 69 percent. For a female digital nomad who arrives alone and rents her own flat, they do not measure the street. They matter once a relationship starts abroad, which is where the WPS partner-violence indicator helps: 8.1 percent in Finland, 6.0 in the United States, 4.4 in Norway, 4.2 in New Zealand and 2.6 in Canada.

Both the Australian and US governments treat this as travel advice rather than background. The US State Department lists domestic and dating violence among the crimes women travellers can be targeted by. Australia's Smartraveller has a section on new relationships abroad: if you travel to meet someone you met online, meet in a public place, tell someone you trust where and with whom, and never hand over your passport or bank details.

City crime data for female digital nomads: where national averages mislead

Every index above is national, and your decision is local. Three countries on our list publish police data by city. None of it is split by the victim's sex, and none compares across borders, because each country counts offences differently. Within one country it is useful.

Canada. Statistics Canada's Crime Severity Index weights each offence by how serious it is. For 2025 the national index is 75.01; Calgary's is 61.66 and St John's 79.22. On the violent-crime version the gap widens: Canada 97.19, Calgary 68.49, St John's 104.32. We score both cities 9 for safety. The police data puts one well below the national average and the other above it.

Finland. Statistics Finland records assault offences per 1,000 residents by the municipality where they happened. In 2025 the national rate was 8.00. Oulu recorded 7.24, Tampere 8.94, Turku 9.97 and Helsinki 10.76. For assaults in public places other than shops, events and restaurants, the national rate was 2.72, against 2.29 in Oulu, 3.33 in Tampere, 3.43 in Turku and 4.92 in Helsinki.

Norway. Statistics Norway averages offences reported to the police over two years. For 2024 to 2025, violence and maltreatment ran at 8.2 per 1,000 residents nationally, 8.4 in Trondheim and 12.8 in Oslo.

One caution applies to all three. Recorded crime counts what people report and police record. A city where more women report sexual offences can show a higher rate without being more dangerous, so use assault in public places and violent-crime indices as the comparison, and treat sexual-offence counts with care.

What government travel advisories tell women travelling alone

The UK Foreign Office keeps one general page of advice for women travelling abroad, last updated on 8 September 2026. Its country pages for Finland, Norway, Denmark (which covers the Faroe Islands), New Zealand, Canada, the United States and the Cook Islands each link to that page rather than carrying a women-specific warning of their own. For female digital nomads that absence is itself information: none of the seven flags a gender-specific risk.

The general warnings that do appear are practical. The US page advises against walking through quieter areas alone at night, and the New Zealand page notes thieves targeting hire cars and camper vans in major tourist areas.

Ride-hailing apps or street taxis for women travelling alone

The US State Department gives female digital nomads the clearest reason to prefer an app. App-based companies, it says, "offer a record of your ride. This is unlike hailing a ride on the street." Some let you require the driver to confirm a PIN at pick-up, and you can share the live trip with another phone. Smartraveller (updated 13 February 2026) adds three habits: book the airport transfer before you land, use only licensed taxis, and sit in the back behind the driver.

Our city pages list Bolt and Uber in Finland and Trondheim, Uber and Ola in New Zealand, and Uber and Lyft in Canada. Torshavn lists buses and ferries instead, and Rarotonga the island bus and scooters. Those are our own country notes, so confirm the app works locally before relying on it at 1am.

Accommodation safety checks for a solo female remote worker

Smartraveller's list is the most specific of the three governments. Book before you go and check in with your first initial and surname only, without Miss, Ms or Mrs; the State Department gives the same advice. Avoid rooms on the ground floor and buildings whose entrance is on a back street. On arrival, ask to see the room and check the door has a peephole, deadbolt or chain.

One line in the Australian advice cuts against how many nomads find housing: avoid sharing accommodation with strangers or people you have just met, "including renting a spare room or couch". For a first month, that makes a self-contained flat the safer default.

Drink spiking advice for women abroad

The UK page is specific about something often misunderstood: rape drugs "also work in non-alcoholic drinks, such as coffee and tea", and are normally colourless and tasteless. If you feel strange, sick or drunk after a small amount, it advises telling a trusted friend or calling the local police.

Shortlisting two of these cities? Put them side by side in the city comparison tool, which lines up monthly cost and all 13 of our category scores, then bring the outside figures from this article to the result.

Emergency numbers and healthcare for women in the top-ranked countries

These are the numbers female digital nomads should save before landing in each country, taken from the UK Foreign Office pages and the national health services and checked in September 2026.

  • Finland: 112 for police, fire and ambulance. The Medical Helpline 116117 gives free advice on urgent health problems around the clock, everywhere except Åland.
  • Norway: 112 for police, 113 for a medical emergency, and 116 117 for the out-of-hours doctor (legevakt). Helsenorge says calls to 113 and 116 117 are free.
  • Faroe Islands: 112, per the UK advice for Denmark, which covers the islands.
  • New Zealand: 111. Healthline, 0800 611 116, gives free medical advice 24 hours a day.
  • Canada and the United States: 911.
  • Cook Islands: 999 for ambulance, fire and police.

Paying for care differs sharply. In Norway anyone present is entitled to urgent care, but tourists from outside the EU, EEA and Switzerland are not reimbursed. The UK advice for Finland says British visitors need a GHIC or EHIC for state care. In New Zealand, the Accident Compensation Corporation helps pay for local care after an injury. For Canada and the US the same advice is blunt: treatment is expensive and there are no special arrangements for British visitors.

Official health sources confirm non-prescription emergency contraception in three of these countries. Health New Zealand says people of any age can buy the pill over the counter at most pharmacies; it works best within 24 hours, can work up to 72, and does not after 96. Norway's health portal says every type is non-prescription, with no age limit in pharmacies. The US FDA made Plan B One-Step non-prescription for all ages on 20 June 2013.

After a sexual assault, the State Department's advice is to get medical care within 72 hours, for HIV prevention and emergency contraception. New Zealand's Safe to talk line runs 24 hours on 0800 044 334 or by text to 4334. For English-speaking care in northern Finland, our Oulu services directory lists Oulu University Hospital, taken from the UK government's list of medical facilities in Finland.

Ten safe cities for female digital nomads, checked against outside data

Each profile gives our scores first, then the outside evidence a female digital nomad can check herself. Costs are our monthly figures in USD.

Oulu: the safest Finnish city on police figures

Oulu tops our ranking with safety 10, air quality 10 and English 9 at $1,550 a month, and the police data backs it. Its 2025 assault rate of 7.24 per 1,000 residents is below the national 8.00 and the lowest of the four Finnish cities compared above; public-place assaults, at 2.29, are under Finland's 2.72. The trade is social and seasonal: community 3, climate 2, and January highs around minus 4°C.

Turku: a perfect safety score, and assault rates above Finland's average

We give Turku the same safety 10 as Oulu, at $1,660. Statistics Finland does not separate them so kindly: Turku recorded 9.97 assaults per 1,000 residents in 2025, above the national 8.00, with 3.43 in public places against 2.72. It remains below Helsinki on both. The country figures behind it are strong, with 78 percent of Finnish women feeling safe at night, but this is one of two cities in this article where our score outruns the police data.

Tampere: safety in the middle of the Finnish data

Tampere scores safety 9 with us, at $1,780, and a community score of 4, one point above Oulu and Turku. Its 2025 assault rate of 8.94 per 1,000 sits between Oulu and Turku, and its public-place rate of 3.33 is above the national 2.72. Finland's weakest women's indicator applies here as everywhere in the country: partner violence at 8.1 percent, the highest of any country on our list.

Trondheim: Norway's safety for women, at Norway's prices

Trondheim pairs safety 10 with the highest cost on this list, $2,610 a month. Norway has the strongest outside record here: third on the women's index, 86 percent of women safe at night, first in the world on the peace index's societal safety domain. Trondheim's 8.4 violence offences per 1,000 sit level with the national 8.2 and far below Oslo's 12.8. St. Olavs Hospital is on the UK government's list of medical facilities in Norway.

Torshavn: high safety scores, no outside index at all

We score Torshavn 10 for safety and 10 for cleanliness, at an estimated $2,400, and no index here can check that: the women's index scores Denmark, not the self-governing Faroes, and the peace index omits them. The UK advice notes the Faroes are outside the EU. Tourists need no visa, but living and working there needs a work and residence permit.

Queenstown: safe streets by our score, and a national figure that disagrees

Queenstown scores safety 9, English 10 and community 6, at $2,390. New Zealand ranks second on the peace index and ninth on the women's index, yet only 47 percent of its women feel safe walking alone at night, the lowest share on our list. The UK advice on thefts from hire cars and camper vans in tourist areas fits a resort town. Neither our score nor the survey is city-level data.

Wellington: a safe capital with a community score of 6

Wellington scores safety 9 and community 6 at $2,140, and its culture score of 8 is the highest of the New Zealand entries. The national caveats from Queenstown apply. Healthline and the Accident Compensation Corporation work nationwide, and British visitors are covered for urgent treatment under the reciprocal agreement, not for routine care or prescriptions.

St John's: safety score 9, violent crime above Canada's average

We score St John's 9 for safety at an estimated $1,700, the third cheapest of the ten. Statistics Canada's figures do not support that score. Its 2025 violent Crime Severity Index was 104.32 against a national 97.19, and its overall index 79.22 against 75.01. None of that is gender-specific, but it is the city-level evidence that exists, and it points the other way from our rating.

Calgary: the safest Canadian city here on police data

Calgary scores safety 9, wifi 9 and English 10 at $2,250, with a climate score of 3. Here the police data agrees with us and then some: a 2025 violent Crime Severity Index of 68.49, about 30 percent below Canada's 97.19, and an overall index of 61.66. At country level, 63 percent of Canadian women feel safe walking alone at night, and Canada's partner-violence rate of 2.6 percent is the lowest of any country in this article.

Rarotonga: island safety, with evacuation as the hospital plan

Rarotonga scores safety 9 at an estimated $1,800, with wifi 5, the weakest connection on this list. Like the Faroes it falls outside both indices. The deciding fact is medical: the UK advice says facilities in the Cook Islands are limited and evacuation to mainland New Zealand is likely the only option in an emergency. Budget for insurance that covers medical evacuation, and treat that cover as part of the monthly cost.

A data checklist for judging any city's safety as a woman

The same seven steps let female digital nomads assess any city, including ones that are not on our list.

  1. Look up the country's community safety figure in the WPS Index: the share of women who feel safe walking alone at night. Compare it with the global 66 percent.
  2. Read the Societal Safety and Security domain of the Global Peace Index, not the overall rank.
  3. Check the partner-violence indicator if you expect to date while abroad.
  4. Find the national statistics office's crime table by municipality and compare your city with the national rate, using assault or violent crime rather than sexual-offence counts.
  5. Read the UK, US and Australian advice for the country, and note any women-specific warning.
  6. Save the emergency number, the medical helpline and the rules on emergency contraception.
  7. Plan the first month around a self-contained flat and an app-booked ride from the airport.

Run that test on our full list of fifteen cities for female digital nomads and most pass, with St John's and Turku the two where police figures argue with our scores. For the working side once you have a base, see our guide to staying productive while working remotely abroad.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the safest city for female digital nomads?

On our ranking, Oulu in Finland, with safety 10 and a Match score of 8.6. Outside data supports it: Oulu's 2025 assault rate of 7.24 per 1,000 residents is below Finland's 8.00, and 78 percent of Finnish women feel safe walking alone at night. Our scores are editorial, so check them against police data for any city you shortlist.

Which country is safest for women on the Women, Peace and Security Index?

Denmark ranks first on the 2025/26 index, followed by Iceland, with Norway and Sweden joint third and Finland fifth. On the single indicator of women feeling safe walking alone at night, Singapore leads at 97 percent. Norway reaches 86 percent, Finland 78 and Canada 63.

Is New Zealand safe for female digital nomads?

It depends which measure you trust. New Zealand ranks second on the Global Peace Index 2026 and ninth on the women's index. Yet only 47 percent of New Zealand women say they feel safe walking alone at night, below the global 66 percent. We score its cities 9 for safety, but that is our judgement.

Are ride-hailing apps safer than taxis for women travelling alone?

The US State Department recommends considering app-based rides because they keep a record of the trip, unlike hailing a car on the street, and some apps let you require a driver PIN and share the ride live. Australia's Smartraveller advises licensed taxis only, a pre-booked airport transfer and sitting in the back.

How can female digital nomads check whether accommodation is safe?

Smartraveller's checklist: book ahead, check in with your initial and surname only, avoid ground floor rooms and entrances on back streets, and confirm a peephole, deadbolt or chain. It also advises against sharing with strangers, including renting a spare room or couch from someone you have just met.

What emergency number should female digital nomads call in Finland, Norway, New Zealand or Canada?

Finland uses 112 for everything, plus 116117 for medical advice. Norway uses 112 for police, 113 for medical emergencies and 116 117 for the out-of-hours doctor. New Zealand uses 111, Canada and the United States 911, the Faroe Islands 112 and the Cook Islands 999.

Can women get emergency contraception without a prescription in these countries?

Yes in New Zealand, Norway and the United States, on official sources. Health New Zealand says anyone of any age can buy it over the counter at most pharmacies. Norway sells every type without prescription, with no age limit in pharmacies. The US FDA removed the age limit on Plan B One-Step in June 2013.

Does the Global Peace Index measure safety for women?

Not directly. Its perceptions-of-criminality indicator uses Gallup's question about walking alone at night, but counts all respondents, men included. For women specifically, use the community safety indicator in the WPS Index, and read the peace index's Societal Safety and Security domain rather than its headline rank.
