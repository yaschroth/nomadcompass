Cost of Living in Nha Trang

Nha Trang sits at the more affordable end of Vietnam's coastal cities. Here is what a typical month looks like in Vietnamese Dong and USD:

  • Rent, central apartment (1-bedroom): 8,000,000-12,000,000 VND ($320-480)
  • Rent, quieter outer areas: 5,000,000-7,000,000 VND ($200-280)
  • Groceries (self-catering): 1,500,000-2,500,000 VND ($60-100)
  • Eating out (local pho, banh mi, seafood stalls): 1,500,000-2,500,000 VND ($60-100), assuming one or two meals out per day at 50,000-120,000 VND each
  • Coworking hot-desk (monthly): 1,800,000-4,000,000 VND ($70-160); daily passes at Stockholm Bistro or LIVIN run around 150,000 VND ($6)
  • Local transport (Grab bike, occasional taxi): 500,000-1,000,000 VND ($20-40)
  • Mobile data SIM (10GB+): 200,000-300,000 VND ($8-12)
  • Utilities (if not included in rent): 500,000-1,000,000 VND ($20-40)

Realistic monthly total: $700-1,100 for a solo nomad living modestly but comfortably. Push closer to $1,200-1,400 if you cowork full-time, eat out often at international restaurants, and rent beachside. Cash still dominates most transactions, and you will want to budget for bottled water since tap water is not safe to drink.

Where to Work

Nha Trang has a small but functional coworking scene. Stockholm Bistro and Co-Working on Biet Thu Street is the most popular option with nomads: four floors, fast Wi-Fi, ergonomic seating, and a restaurant on the ground floor. A day pass (150,000 VND) includes a coffee. LIVIN Collective in the city center is a beloved hybrid: coworking upstairs, American barbecue and craft beer below. It attracts a mix of expats and locals and has a genuine community feel. SMART SPACE in the Vinh Hai area caters more to local professionals and offers longer-term packages.

Cafe work culture is alive but uneven. Cong Caphe (rated 4.3 on Tripadvisor) has reliable Wi-Fi and a distinctive retro-Vietnamese atmosphere. KAT Coffee offers two floors, plenty of outlets, and stays quiet enough for focused work. Highlands Coffee at Vincom Plaza is a fallback with consistent connectivity and long opening hours.

Fiber broadband is widespread in Nha Trang and speeds are genuinely fast, typically 50-100 Mbps at good cafes. Power outages are infrequent. The main risk is peak-hour cafe crowding, especially on weekends near the beach strip.

Getting Around

Nha Trang's city center is compact. If you stay near the main beach boulevard (Tran Phu), many restaurants, cafes, and the central market are within a 15-20 minute walk. Sidewalks in the tourist core are passable, though expect parked motorbikes to eat into pedestrian space away from the waterfront.

For anything beyond walking distance, Grab is your best tool. Grab Bike is cheap and fast for short hops (around 20,000-30,000 VND for a typical 2-3 km trip). Grab Car is reliable for longer journeys. The Maxim app also operates here. Renting a motorbike yourself costs around 150,000-200,000 VND per day and opens up the surrounding coastline, waterfalls, and Po Nagar towers.

Airport: Cam Ranh International Airport (CXR) is about 35 km south of the city center, a 45-60 minute drive. A Grab Car costs roughly 250,000-350,000 VND ($10-14). Airport shuttle buses run for about 50,000 VND ($2) but have fixed stops and less flexibility. There is no rail link from the airport to the city, though Nha Trang has a train station served by the North-South line if you are traveling to or from Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Vietnam's e-visa is available to citizens of all countries, costs $25 (single entry) or $50 (multiple entry), and allows stays of up to 90 days. Processing takes 3-5 business days and is handled entirely online at evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn. This is the simplest and most common route for nomads.

One important caveat: as of 2025, the Vietnam e-visa cannot be extended in-country. If you want to stay longer than 90 days, your options are to exit Vietnam and re-enter on a new e-visa (a "visa run" to a neighboring country), or to obtain a longer-stay visa through a Vietnamese embassy before arrival. Some nationalities qualify for short visa-free stays (15-45 days depending on passport), but the e-visa at 90 days is the most practical choice for remote workers.

Vietnam does not currently offer a dedicated digital nomad or remote worker visa. Always confirm the latest rules with the official immigration portal or your nearest Vietnamese embassy before traveling.

Best Time to Visit

Nha Trang has a more favorable climate than much of Vietnam, with over 300 sunny days a year. The dry season runs roughly January through August, with the clearest skies and calmest seas falling between February and May. Temperatures hover between 26-31 degrees Celsius (79-88F) during this window, with low humidity and reliable sunshine. This is the ideal period for remote work combined with beach access.

June through August is still dry but hotter, with temperatures regularly hitting 33-35 degrees Celsius. You will spend more time indoors or air-conditioned coworking spaces.

The rainy season runs October through December, driven by the northeast monsoon. October and November bring the heaviest rain, occasional flooding in low-lying areas, and rough seas. Some days during this period are perfectly fine; others can be washed out. If work is your priority, the December-January shoulder period offers improving conditions and lower accommodation prices before peak tourist season kicks in.

Pros and Cons

  • Low cost of living: comfortable nomad life under $1,100/month is realistic
  • Fast, reliable internet at coworking spaces and most cafes
  • Long dry season with genuine beach access for nine months of the year
  • Fresh seafood at consistently low prices
  • Compact city center: easy to walk or Grab to most places you need
  • Growing expat and nomad community with coworking meetup spots
  • Nightlife strip can be rowdy near Tran Phu, especially with large Russian tourist groups
  • No dedicated digital nomad visa: the 90-day e-visa requires exit to extend
  • Walkability is limited outside the beachfront strip due to motorbike-clogged sidewalks
  • Community is smaller than Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, or Da Nang
  • Air quality is moderate and tap water is not drinkable, adding cost and friction
  • Rainy season (Oct-Nov) can disrupt outdoor plans and cause brief flooding

Who Nha Trang Is Best For

Nha Trang suits nomads who want a beach-town base without paying beach-town prices. If your priority is reliable internet, low overhead, warm weather for most of the year, and the option to snorkel or swim after your workday, it delivers. It also works well as a short-term stint of one to three months within a longer Southeast Asia loop, particularly when paired with a Vietnam e-visa.

It is a stronger fit for solo nomads or couples who are self-sufficient and do not need a large or vibrant co-working community. The nightlife, while present, skews toward beach bars and clubs rather than a craft cocktail or independent music scene.

Skip Nha Trang if you want a dense digital nomad hub with daily meetups and a strong tech scene: Da Nang or Ho Chi Minh City serve that need better. Also reconsider if you are sensitive to heat, need walkable urban infrastructure, or plan to stay beyond 90 days without doing a visa run.

Frequently Asked Questions About Nha Trang

How much does it cost to live in Nha Trang as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,400 a month in Nha Trang for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Nha Trang?

Nha Trang rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Nha Trang safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Nha Trang is 7/10, good for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Nha Trang a good city for digital nomads overall?

Nha Trang stands out for low costs and nature among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,400 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
