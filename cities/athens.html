Athens is one of the most affordable capitals in the European Union, and that makes a real difference when you are running your life on a remote income. Expect to spend between 1,300 and 2,200 EUR per month (roughly 1,400-2,400 USD) for a comfortable nomad lifestyle.

  • Rent, central neighborhoods (Kolonaki, Syntagma, Exarchia): 800-1,300 EUR/month for a furnished one-bedroom
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Pangrati, Neos Kosmos, Kypseli): 550-850 EUR/month for a furnished studio or one-bedroom
  • Groceries: 200-300 EUR/month cooking at home
  • Eating out: A sit-down meal at a local taverna runs 10-20 EUR per person; street souvlaki is under 3 EUR. Budget 250-400 EUR/month mixing both.
  • Coworking hot-desk: 120-200 EUR/month at a dedicated space; many cafes let you work all day for the price of two coffees (around 8-10 EUR)
  • Local transport: A monthly transit pass costs around 30 EUR and covers metro, bus, and tram
  • Total range: 1,300-2,000 EUR/month (1,400-2,200 USD) living comfortably; 1,800-2,500 EUR/month with a central apartment and frequent dining out

Alcohol and dining out are markedly cheaper than in Western Europe, and everyday Greek food at neighborhood spots is excellent value.

Where to Work

Athens has a growing but still compact coworking scene. The best dedicated spaces are reliable and priced reasonably by European standards.

  • Vios Coworking (near Omonia) is the most wellness-focused option, with gym, nap room, showers, and 500 Mbps fiber. Hot desks start around 150 EUR/month.
  • Impact Hub Athens (Psirri) is built around an entrepreneurship community with fiber internet and regular events. Around 170 EUR/month for hot-desk access.
  • Stone Soup (central, near Omonia) was one of the city's first coworking spaces and retains a community-driven feel with views of the Acropolis from its rooftop terrace.
  • Selina Theatrou (Omonia) offers flexible passes (day, week, month) and is popular with short-stay nomads.

Cafe-working culture is strong. Athens has a well-developed specialty coffee scene and most independent cafes are accustomed to people working for hours. Dope Roasting Co in Psirri, Third Place near Syntagma, and Papia in Pangrati are consistently recommended for reliable WiFi and a welcoming attitude toward laptops.

The honest WiFi reality: coworking spaces deliver 100+ Mbps consistently. Cafes are hit or miss, typically 20-50 Mbps on a good day. Many short-term apartments have mediocre broadband, so if stable connection is critical for your work, factor a coworking membership into your budget from day one.

Getting Around

Athens is a walkable city in its historic core. Monastiraki, Plaka, Syntagma, Psirri, and Thissio are all connected on foot, and the terrain is mostly flat except around the Acropolis hill. For anything further, the metro is clean, punctual, and covers the main areas you will use. The M1, M2, and M3 lines intersect at Syntagma and Monastiraki, making them the natural transit hubs.

Buses fill the gaps but can be slow in traffic. Taxis and Bolt (rideshare) are plentiful and cheap by Western European standards. A monthly transit pass costs around 30 EUR.

Getting to and from the airport: the Airport Express metro (M3 Blue Line) runs directly from the airport to central Athens in about 40 minutes and costs 10 EUR. Taxis to the city center cost around 40-50 EUR during the day.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of EU and Schengen countries can stay indefinitely. Most other nationalities, including US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders, get 90 days within any 180-day window under Schengen rules, which is enough for a medium-length stay but not a full year.

Greece does offer a dedicated Digital Nomad Residence Permit for non-EU nationals who work remotely for employers or clients based outside Greece. As of early 2026, the requirements include proof of a minimum monthly net income of 3,500 EUR and valid health insurance. The permit is initially granted for 12 months and is renewable for a further year, giving you up to two years total. A significant rule change in February 2026 means you can no longer apply for this permit from within Greece: you must apply at a Greek embassy or consulate in your home country before arriving.

Always verify current requirements directly with the Greek embassy or the official Greek immigration service before making plans, as rules have been changing.

Best Time to Visit

For remote work, the sweet spot is March through May and September through November. Temperatures sit in the mid-teens to low twenties Celsius (60s-70s Fahrenheit), crowds are manageable, and accommodation prices are reasonable. Spring in Athens is genuinely pleasant, with long days and the city at its most photogenic.

Summer (June through August) is harsh. Temperatures regularly hit 35-40 C (95-105 F) in July and August, air conditioning bills spike, and tourist pricing inflates accommodation. If you must visit in summer, plan to work morning and evening and rest midday, and look for apartments with reliable AC.

Winter (December through February) is mild by Northern European standards, with temperatures around 10-15 C (50-60 F) and occasional rain. The city empties of tourists and prices drop significantly. It works well for focused work, though some attractions run reduced hours.

Pros and Cons

  • Very affordable cost of living compared to most EU capitals
  • Rich culture and history on your doorstep: the Acropolis, ancient markets, world-class museums
  • Excellent food at every price point, from street souvlaki to Michelin-recommended tavernas
  • Good metro and walkable core make day-to-day navigation easy
  • Warm, sunny climate for most of the year outside peak summer
  • Friendly, English-proficient population, especially among younger Athenians
  • Digital nomad visa available for non-EU nationals who meet income requirements
  • Summer heat is punishing and makes outdoor life unpleasant from mid-July through August
  • WiFi in apartments can be inconsistent: short-term rentals often have weak broadband
  • Coworking scene is growing but still limited compared to Lisbon or Barcelona
  • Air pollution and noise are real in the city center, particularly during summer
  • Bureaucracy can be slow if you need any official paperwork or banking as a foreigner
  • Nomad community is smaller than in Lisbon, Tbilisi, or Chiang Mai

Who Athens Is Best For

Athens works especially well for remote workers who care about history, culture, and food, and who want a major European capital without major European prices. You get genuine urban life with excellent food and nightlife, walkable neighborhoods, and a sense that you are living somewhere with real depth. It suits people who are happy to invest in a decent coworking membership and treat their accommodation WiFi as backup only.

It is also a solid base for those who want to use it as a launchpad: flights to the islands, Turkey, the Balkans, and across Europe are cheap from Athens International Airport.

Athens is less suited to people who need a large, organized nomad community to feel at home, who struggle in extreme heat (if visiting in summer), or who want a settled long-term base without meeting the income threshold for the Digital Nomad Permit. It can also feel gritty and visually chaotic in parts of the center, which does not suit everyone.

Frequently Asked Questions About Athens

How much does it cost to live in Athens as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $2,400 a month in Athens for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Athens?

Athens rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Athens safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Athens is 6/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Athens a good city for digital nomads overall?

Athens stands out for culture and nightlife among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,400 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
