Nairobi for Digital Nomads: The Honest Take

Experience safari gateway with a thriving tech scene and incredible wildlife. For remote workers, Nairobi leans on English and climate, with a typical budget near $1,600 a month. The main trade-off is safety.

Cost of Living in Nairobi

Nairobi is one of Africa's most expensive capitals, though still affordable by Western standards. You can live comfortably on $1,000-1,800 per month depending on your choices.

  • Rent (central, Westlands/Kilimani): KES 60,000-120,000/month ($460-920) for a one-bedroom
  • Rent (quieter areas, Ngong Road corridor): KES 25,000-55,000/month ($190-420) for a one-bedroom
  • Groceries: KES 15,000-25,000/month ($115-190) cooking at home
  • Eating out: KES 500-1,000 ($4-8) at a local restaurant; KES 1,500-3,000 ($11-23) at a mid-range spot
  • Coworking hot-desk: KES 10,000-20,000/month ($75-155); day passes run KES 1,500-3,500 ($11-27)
  • Local transport (Uber/Bolt within city): $1-4 per ride; matatu minibuses $0.30-0.80 per trip
  • Total realistic range: $900-1,500/month on a lean budget; $1,500-2,200 with a comfortable lifestyle

Currency: the Kenyan shilling (KES) trades at roughly 129-132 per USD as of mid-2026. Prices in expat neighborhoods rise quickly, so choosing accommodation a few kilometers further out is one of the best ways to cut costs.

Where to Work

Nairobi has one of the strongest coworking ecosystems in Africa, driven by its position as the continent's leading tech hub. You will not struggle to find a decent desk.

Coworking spaces are the most reliable option. Nairobi Garage (multiple locations including Kilimani, Westlands, Karen, and Spring Valley) is the largest network and offers 24/7 access with fast fibre connections hitting 100-150 Mbps. iHub in Kilimani (Senteu Plaza) is another institution, with a strong tech and startup community. Ikigai has four wellness-focused locations around the city and is popular with freelancers who value a calm environment.

Cafe work culture exists but is uneven. Artcaffe branches across Nairobi are reliably laptop-friendly with free WiFi and power outlets, making them a solid fallback. Upscale cafes in Westlands and Kilimani generally tolerate laptop workers during off-peak hours.

WiFi reality: Nairobi's fibre infrastructure is genuinely good in established neighborhoods. Dedicated coworking spaces consistently deliver 50-150 Mbps. Residential fibre from Safaricom or Zuku is affordable (KES 3,500-6,000/month for 20-100 Mbps). Mobile data on Safaricom's 4G network averages around 70 Mbps and serves as a solid backup. Power outages do occur and surge protectors are worth bringing; most coworking spaces have UPS backup.

Getting Around

Nairobi is a car-centric city with real traffic congestion, and you will quickly learn to plan your day around it. Rush hours from 7-9am and 5-7pm can turn a 5km journey into a 45-minute crawl.

Ride-hailing via Uber and Bolt is the go-to for most nomads. Rides within central areas cost $1-4; a trip from Westlands to Karen might run $5-8. Bolt tends to be slightly cheaper. Both apps work reliably.

Matatus (minibuses) are cheap at $0.30-0.80 per trip and cover most of the city, but routes can be confusing without local knowledge. The Nairobi Express Way has improved north-south travel considerably.

Walking is practical only within your immediate neighborhood. Pavement quality varies widely, and walking at night is not recommended anywhere in the city. Stick to ride-hailing after dark.

Airport (JKIA): Jomo Kenyatta International Airport is about 18km southeast of the city center. Bolt or Uber to Westlands takes 30-50 minutes outside rush hour and costs roughly $10-15. There is no direct rail link.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Kenya dropped most tourist visas in January 2024. Citizens of most countries can now enter visa-free for up to 90 days after completing an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) online before departure. The eTA costs around $30-50 and is typically approved within 72 hours. You can extend for an additional 90 days (total 180 days) for a $50 fee at the Department of Immigration.

Digital Nomad Permit (Class N): Kenya launched this permit in October 2024. It allows remote workers employed by or contracted to non-Kenyan entities to live and work legally in Kenya for one year, renewable for up to five years. The income requirement was revised down to $24,000 USD per year. You will need proof of remote employment or freelance contracts, police clearance, and documentation of accommodation. Working on a tourist eTA is technically illegal.

Always verify current entry requirements with the Kenya Department of Immigration (immigration.ecitizen.go.ke) before you travel, as policies have changed rapidly in recent years.

Best Time to Visit

Nairobi sits at roughly 1,700m elevation near the equator, which gives it a mild, springlike climate year-round. Daytime temperatures hover between 20-27C (68-80F) and rarely exceed that. Nights are cool, dropping to 10-14C (50-57F), so pack a layer.

The city has two wet seasons: the long rains from March to May (heaviest in April) and the short rains from October to December. The long rains bring persistent afternoon downpours that cause flooding in low-lying areas and significantly worsen traffic. The short rains are lighter and less disruptive.

Best window for remote work: June to September. This dry season brings consistent sunshine, minimal rain disruption, clear skies, and cooler temperatures that are ideal for productivity. January and February are also dry but can feel hotter and dustier. Avoid April if you can; it is genuinely difficult to get around the city.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Africa's strongest tech and startup ecosystem, with a thriving nomad and expat community
  • Pro: Excellent coworking infrastructure with fast, reliable fibre internet
  • Pro: Incredible nature access: Nairobi National Park is 20 minutes from the city, and safaris to Maasai Mara are a weekend trip
  • Pro: English is widely spoken and used in business, signage, and everyday life
  • Pro: Diverse, cosmopolitan food scene at every price point
  • Pro: Legal digital nomad permit available since late 2024
  • Con: Traffic congestion is severe and can consume hours of your day
  • Con: Petty crime (bag snatching, phone theft) is common, especially in the CBD and at night
  • Con: Air quality is noticeably poor, driven by traffic, open burning, and informal industry
  • Con: More expensive than most African cities and some Southeast Asian alternatives
  • Con: Power outages occur, requiring backup plans for critical work
  • Con: The long rains (March-May) can make getting around genuinely miserable

Who Nairobi Is Best For

Nairobi works well for nomads who want to be in a real working city rather than a resort town. If you are in tech, entrepreneurship, media, or development work, the connections and community here are unmatched on the continent. It suits people who appreciate cultural depth, want genuine urban texture, and are excited by the proximity to world-class wildlife and landscapes.

It is also a strong choice if you want to use Nairobi as a base for wider East Africa travel: Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda, and Ethiopia are all short flights away.

Skip Nairobi if you are easily stressed by chaotic traffic and urban grit, if personal safety concerns weigh heavily on you (the city requires more vigilance than, say, Lisbon or Chiang Mai), or if you are on a very tight budget and looking for maximum value per dollar. Nomads who prioritize beach or outdoor lifestyle would be better served by Diani or somewhere further from the city.

Frequently Asked Questions About Nairobi

How much does it cost to live in Nairobi as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Nairobi is around $1,600 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Nairobi?

With a WiFi score of 5/10, connectivity in Nairobi is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Nairobi safe for digital nomads?

Nairobi scores 3 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is patchy for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Nairobi a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Nairobi is worth a look if you value English and climate. Costs sit near $1,600 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
