Setting Up as a Nomad in Eze

A medieval eagle-nest village perched high above the French Riviera between Nice and Monaco, its car-free stone lanes winding up to an exotic garden with sweeping views of the Mediterranean. For remote workers, Eze leans on safety and climate, with a typical budget near $1,300 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Cost of Living in Eze

Eze sits on the French Riviera between Nice and Monaco, and prices reflect that neighborhood. A studio or one-bedroom apartment in or near the village runs roughly 1,200 to 1,900 EUR a month, when you can find one at all, since much of the perched village's housing is short-term tourist rental or second homes. A simple lunch menu in the village costs 20 to 30 EUR, a coffee 3 to 4 EUR, and a sit-down dinner with wine easily 50 EUR or more per person. Groceries are cheaper if you shop at the Carrefour or Casino in nearby Beaulieu-sur-Mer or Nice rather than the small village shop. All-in monthly budgets for a single remote worker typically land between 2,200 and 3,200 EUR, higher in July and August when the whole coast raises prices for tourists. This is not a budget destination. Nomads chasing lower costs do better basing in Nice and treating Eze as a day trip or a splurge weekend, which is in fact how most people who work remotely near Eze actually live.

Where to Work in Eze

There is no dedicated coworking space in the village itself, and that is unlikely to change given its size and protected medieval status. Realistic options are hotel lounges and cafe terraces with wifi in Eze and Eze-sur-Mer, or the wider selection of coworking spaces and cafes eight to ten kilometers away in Nice, which has a genuine remote-work scene. Wifi in village cafes is generally serviceable but not fast, and can wobble during peak tourist hours when everyone is on it. If video calls and reliable bandwidth matter, plan to work from Nice on weekdays and enjoy Eze on evenings or weekends when day-tripper crowds thin out. A handful of guesthouses and small hotels in Eze offer quiet work corners for guests, and the terraces overlooking the sea are hard to beat for a slow-morning writing session, just do not count on them for back-to-back client calls.

Getting Around Eze

Eze is genuinely two places: the hilltop Village Medieval, which is entirely car-free and reached only on foot up steep cobbled lanes and stairs, and Eze-sur-Mer down on the coast, which has the train station and beach access. The Lignes d'Azur bus 82 and the seasonal T100 coastal shuttle connect Eze to Nice, Monaco, and Beaulieu-sur-Mer along the Moyenne and Basse Corniche roads. The regional TER train stops at Eze-sur-Mer and is the fastest way in from Nice (about 10 to 15 minutes) or Monaco, though from the station you still face a steep climb or a connecting bus up to the village itself. The famous Nietzsche path is a scenic but genuinely strenuous hiking trail linking Eze-sur-Mer to the village, roughly 45 minutes to an hour uphill. Parking in the village is scarce and expensive, with most spaces in a paid lot below the ramparts, so a car is more hassle than help unless you are based outside town. Nice Cote d'Azur Airport is about 20 to 30 minutes away by car or bus, making Eze an easy add-on to a Riviera trip rather than a place you fly into directly.

Visas for Eze

Eze follows standard French and Schengen rules. Citizens of the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and most other visa-exempt countries can stay up to 90 days within any 180-day period without a visa, which covers most short nomad stints or Riviera getaways. For longer stays, France's Passeport Talent visa (which includes a specific track for remote workers and freelancers) and the longer-stay visitor visa (VLS-TS) are the realistic routes, both requiring proof of income, health insurance, and accommodation, and both processed through a French consulate before arrival. There is no local Eze-specific process; anyone settling here for months rather than weeks handles paperwork the same way they would for Nice or anywhere else in the Alpes-Maritimes, and in practice most long-stay nomads register their address in Nice or a nearby town rather than in Eze itself, given the scarcity of long-term housing in the village.

Best Time to Visit Eze

Late spring (May, June) and early autumn (September, October) are the sweet spot, with warm but not scorching days, thinner crowds than peak summer, and the Jardin Exotique and village lanes far more pleasant to walk without jostling tour groups. Summer (July, August) brings hot, dry, sunny weather and the best sea conditions for a swim at Eze-sur-Mer, but also the heaviest coach-tour traffic through the village during the day; visit early morning or evening to have the lanes mostly to yourself. Winters are mild by most standards (daytime highs often 12 to 15C) but noticeably quieter, with many village restaurants and shops reducing hours or closing for a few weeks. Whatever the season, plan to see the village early morning or after 5pm, when the day-trip buses have left and the light on the coastline is at its best.

Pros and Cons of Eze

Pros: extraordinary views over the Mediterranean from nearly every corner of the village, a genuinely atmospheric medieval core with stone lanes and the Jardin Exotique at the top, easy access to Nice, Monaco, and Cap Ferrat, safe and clean, and a peaceful, almost magical feel once the day-tripper buses depart in late afternoon. Cons: very small, with limited long-term housing and essentially no coworking infrastructure; expensive Riviera-level prices for food and lodging; overwhelmed by tour groups and coach traffic for much of the day; steep stepped lanes throughout the village make it impractical for luggage, strollers, or mobility issues; and almost no nightlife or resident community to speak of once shops close. Parking is limited and a car is often more burden than benefit if you are staying in the village itself.

Who Eze Is For

Eze suits nomads who want a strikingly beautiful, quiet home base for a short stay, remote workers who can handle occasional patchy wifi in exchange for sea views from their desk, and anyone treating the Riviera as a multi-base trip who wants a few nights of postcard scenery between working stints elsewhere. It is a poor fit for anyone on a budget, anyone needing dependable fast internet for daily video calls, or anyone wanting an active coworking or nomad community, since that scene lives in Nice, not Eze. The honest recommendation for most remote workers is to base in Nice, which has real coworking spaces, more housing options, lower costs, and a broader social scene, and to visit Eze regularly as a short train or bus ride away rather than trying to live and work from the village full time.
