Setting Up as a Nomad in Alanya

A Mediterranean resort town with a year-round foreign population and Seljuk walls above it. For remote workers, Alanya leans on climate and low costs, with a typical budget near $950 a month.

Best Neighborhoods in Alanya

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Castle Hill and the Old Harbour

$$$
Historic & Scenic

Below the Seljuk walls, with the Red Tower on the quay

The headland where the thirteenth century Seljuk fortifications climb the rock, with the Red Tower and the old shipyard on the harbour below and a quarter of Ottoman houses on the slopes. It is the historic heart of Alanya and the quietest part of the central town.

Best for: Stays that want the castle, the harbour and the old quarter
Pros
  • Seljuk walls and the Red Tower on the doorstep
  • Views over both bays from the hill
  • Quieter than the resort strip
Cons
  • Steep, with a long climb to the upper castle
  • Limited everyday shopping
  • Day-visitor traffic to the castle

Cleopatra Beach and the Western Strip

$$$
Beachfront & International

The main beach, with the promenade and the sea-view blocks

The long sand beach running west from the headland, backed by a promenade and rows of apartment blocks, many of them owned by the town's large foreign resident population. It is where most arrivals base themselves: swimming from the door, restaurants along the front, everything walkable.

Best for: Almost anyone arriving, for the beach access and the foreign community
Pros
  • The town's best beach directly there
  • Restaurants, gyms and services along the promenade
  • The largest concentration of foreign residents
Cons
  • Highest rents in Alanya, and higher again in summer
  • Very busy in the season
  • Traffic on the coastal road behind

Oba and Tosmur

$$
Modern & Residential

East of the centre, newer complexes and better value

The residential districts east of the river, where much of Alanya's newer apartment building has gone up in complexes with pools and gardens, aimed at both Turkish families and foreign buyers. It is quieter than the strip, cheaper for a better flat, and ten minutes from the centre.

Best for: Longer stays wanting a modern flat with a pool at a moderate rent
Pros
  • Newer, better-built apartments with shared pools
  • Rents well below the beachfront
  • Quieter, with a year-round resident population
Cons
  • Ten to fifteen minutes to the main beach
  • Generic complex architecture
  • Car or dolmus needed for most things

Mahmutlar

$
High-rise & Expat

The high-rise suburb east, where the foreign residents concentrate

A dense strip of apartment towers about twelve kilometres east, built almost entirely for foreign buyers over the last two decades, with supermarkets, its own market day and a large permanent Russian, Ukrainian and northern European population. Rents are the lowest for a sea view anywhere here.

Best for: Long stays wanting a cheap sea-view flat and an established foreign community
Pros
  • The cheapest sea-view apartments in the area
  • Large, established foreign resident community
  • Own market, supermarkets and services
Cons
  • Dense high-rise development with little charm
  • Twenty minutes into central Alanya
  • Very little Turkish character

Dim Cayi and the Taurus Foothills

$$
Valley & Cool

Up the river valley, cool water and pine forest

The valley of the Dim river inland from the town, where riverside restaurants sit on platforms over cold running water and the road climbs into the Taurus pine forest towards the Dim cave. It is noticeably cooler than the coast and a genuine escape from the summer heat.

Best for: Stays that want the river and the mountains rather than the beach
Pros
  • Cool river water and forest at the door
  • Considerably cooler than the coast in summer
  • Low rents and quiet nights
Cons
  • Twenty minutes to the beach and the town
  • A car is essential
  • Very few services and slower internet

Cost of Living in Alanya

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Alanya, so the $950 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 831 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget roughly $950 a month as a solo remote worker in Alanya, which buys a Mediterranean seafront life at a fraction of the Spanish or Italian equivalent. A one-bedroom apartment a few streets back from the beach runs about $300 to $450 on a long lease, with sea-view and newer complexes reaching $600 or more. Alanya has a large foreign resident population and a correspondingly organised rental market, much of it conducted in English, Russian and German. Groceries land around $200 to $260, with the weekly pazar market far cheaper than supermarkets. A lokanta lunch is $4 to $6, a restaurant dinner with drinks $15 to $25, and a monthly transit pass about $20. Utilities add $60 to $130, with summer air conditioning the main variable.

Where to Work in Alanya

Alanya's working infrastructure is built around its resident foreign population rather than around a nomad scene. There are a couple of small coworking spaces and a number of cafes, particularly in the Oba and Cikcilli districts where many foreign residents live, that are entirely used to laptops. Rates where coworking exists run about $80 to $140 a month. Turkish fixed-line internet is fast and inexpensive, and this is a well-connected town rather than a remote resort. The one recurring problem for foreigners is Turkey's handset registration rule: a foreign phone stops working on Turkish networks after 120 days unless registered and taxed, which is expensive, so most long-stayers buy a cheap local handset or plan around it.

Getting Around Alanya

Alanya is strung out along the coast, with the castle headland at the centre and residential districts running several kilometres either side, so walking works within a district and not between them. Dolmus minibuses run constantly along the coastal road, cost under a dollar and are the way almost everyone moves; they stop wherever you flag them. Taxis are metered and cheap. Many foreign residents keep a scooter or a small car, which is genuinely useful for the Taurus mountains behind the town. Antalya airport is about two hours west by bus, and the newer Gazipasa airport is 40 minutes east with a growing list of European routes. Long-distance buses from the otogar reach most of Turkey overnight and comfortably.

Visas for Alanya

Turkey grants most Western passports 90 days in any 180, with EU, UK and Canadian citizens entering visa-free. There is no digital nomad visa as such. The standard route for longer stays is the short-term residence permit, the kisa donem ikamet, applied for online through the e-ikamet system with proof of address, health insurance and funds. Alanya has a very large foreign resident community and a corresponding industry of agents who handle these applications, which makes the process considerably less painful here than doing it alone. Note that Turkey has restricted new residence permit registrations in some neighbourhoods where the foreign population exceeds a threshold, and Alanya has been affected, so check the current status of a specific address before signing a lease.

Best Time to Visit Alanya

April, May, September and October are the best months in Alanya: sea warm enough to swim, air in the mid twenties, and the resort machinery running without the July crush. Summer is hot and humid, regularly into the mid thirties with high humidity off the sea, and the town fills with package holidaymakers. Winter is the quiet secret: mild at 15 to 18 degrees in the day, sunny more often than not, cheap, and with a substantial population of northern Europeans who overwinter here precisely for that. Some restaurants and hotels close from November to March but far from all, because the resident foreign community keeps enough of the town open. The sea is swimmable from roughly May to November.

Pros and Cons of Alanya

Alanya offers a genuinely warm Mediterranean coast, a long swimming season, low prices and an established international community, which makes the practical business of settling in far easier than in most Turkish towns. Healthcare is good and cheap, food is excellent, the Taurus mountains are twenty minutes inland, and Gazipasa airport is close. Against that, it is a resort, and in summer it behaves like one: crowded, loud in places, and oriented towards package tourism. Much of the architecture is undistinguished concrete. The foreign community is more retiree and property-buyer than remote worker, so the nomad scene is thin. Turkish inflation makes budgeting in lira unpredictable, residence permit rules have tightened, and summer humidity is genuinely uncomfortable.

Who Alanya Is For

Alanya suits remote workers who want warm sea, low costs and an easy landing, and who do not need a nomad community to be waiting for them. It works well for people who plan to overwinter somewhere sunny, for anyone with family in tow who values an established foreign infrastructure of schools, doctors and English-speaking agents, and for water sports and hiking. It is a reasonable base for exploring the Turkish coast and Cappadocia. It is a poor fit for anyone wanting a young remote-work scene, an unspoilt town, or a city's cultural depth. Antalya an hour and a half west is larger and more varied, and Izmir is the better choice for anyone who wants a real Turkish city on the sea.

Frequently Asked Questions About Alanya

How much does it cost to live in Alanya as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Alanya is around $950 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Alanya?

Alanya rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is good for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Alanya safe for digital nomads?

Alanya scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Alanya a good city for digital nomads overall?

Alanya stands out for great weather and affordability among remote workers. With a typical budget near $950 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
