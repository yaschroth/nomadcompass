Why Accra Works for Remote Work

West Africa's rising star with vibrant culture and entrepreneurial spirit. A tropical climate and strong English make Accra an easy base, and at about $1,800 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep cleanliness in mind.

Cost of Living in Accra

Accra is one of West Africa's more expensive capitals for foreigners, but it still offers solid value compared to European or North American cities. Prices are quoted in Ghanaian Cedis (GHS); 1 USD buys roughly 15-16 GHS in 2025-2026.

  • Rent, central (Osu, Labone, Cantonments): GHS 8,000-15,000/month ($500-950)
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Spintex, Tema, Adenta): GHS 4,500-8,000/month ($280-500)
  • Groceries (self-catering): GHS 1,200-2,000/month ($75-125)
  • Eating out, local spots: GHS 50-120 per meal ($3-8)
  • Eating out, expat-level restaurants: GHS 300-700 per meal ($19-44)
  • Coworking hot-desk: GHS 750-1,500/month ($47-95); some spaces charge GHS 150/day ($9-10)
  • Local transport (trotro + occasional Bolt/Uber): GHS 800-1,500/month ($50-95)
  • Utilities (electricity, water, internet at home): GHS 800-1,600/month ($50-100)

Total realistic range: $800-1,400/month on a lean budget; $1,400-2,200/month if you eat out often, live centrally, and use ride-hailing daily. Factor in a generator surcharge or backup power costs, since power cuts ("dumsor") are a real budget item for many residents.

Where to Work

Accra has a growing but still thin coworking scene. The best-known options cluster around Osu and the Spintex corridor.

  • Kukun (Osu) is the most-cited spot: a stylish cafe-workspace hybrid open 7am-10pm daily, with reliable fiber, air conditioning, and a solid food and coffee menu. It is also one of the priciest, around $90/week.
  • Impact Hub Accra (Osu) is the social-enterprise anchor of the scene, with monthly desks from around $50, strong community programming, and good WiFi. Hours are weekdays 8am-7pm.
  • ZuluDesk (Spintex Road, near Accra Mall) advertises 24/7 power and fast internet, with hot-desks from GHS 150/day. Useful if you are based on the east side of town.
  • BaseCamp Initiative is another creative-space option open daily 7am-10pm, popular with local entrepreneurs and the diaspora community.

Cafe-working culture exists but is not as well-established as in Southeast Asia. A handful of cafes in Osu and East Legon have strong enough WiFi and power to work from, but you should always confirm current connectivity before settling in. Mobile data on MTN or Vodafone Ghana is reliable as a backup; buy a SIM at the airport. Home fiber internet (50-100 Mbps) is available in most expat-friendly areas but takes a few days to set up.

Getting Around

Accra is not walkable in any meaningful sense outside of small pockets of Osu or East Legon. Heat, humidity, broken pavements, and the absence of pedestrian crossings make walking anything but short errands impractical.

The main options are trotros (shared minibuses), taxis, and ride-hailing apps. Bolt and Uber both operate in Accra and are the practical daily choice for nomads: fares are metered, routes are tracked, and you avoid price negotiation. A cross-town ride typically costs GHS 80-200 ($5-13) depending on distance and traffic. Traffic is severe, especially on routes through central Accra and the N1 highway corridor; build in 60-90 minutes for any significant journey during peak hours.

Trotros are the cheapest option (GHS 5-15 per trip) but routes require local knowledge and stops are not well signposted for newcomers.

Kotoka International Airport (ACC) sits just east of the central business district. Bolt or Uber from the arrivals hall to Osu or Labone typically takes 20-40 minutes and costs GHS 50-100 ($3-7). Avoid unmarked taxis at the terminal who charge 2-3 times that rate.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Ghana does not have a dedicated digital nomad visa. Most Western passport holders (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia) require an e-visa before arrival, applied for at ghana.gov.gh. Tourist visas are typically issued for 30-60 days. Yellow fever vaccination is required for all travelers aged nine months and older, and you must carry your vaccination card.

For stays beyond the initial entry period, you must apply for a residence permit extension through the Ghana Immigration Service. There is no easy "visa run" pathway: extensions are granted on a case-by-case basis. Technically, working remotely for a foreign employer on a tourist visa sits in a legal grey area; Ghana has not issued clear guidance for remote workers as of mid-2026. If you plan to stay more than two months, consult a local immigration lawyer.

Always verify current entry requirements with the Ghana Immigration Service (gis.gov.gh) before travel, as rules change.

Best Time to Visit

Accra has a tropical climate with two rainy seasons: a heavier one from April to June and a shorter one in September-October. The most comfortable and stable window for remote work is November through February, when temperatures sit around 27-30°C (81-86°F) with low humidity and minimal rain. Be aware that December-January brings the Harmattan, a dry Saharan wind that creates haze, dust, and very low humidity, which can irritate sinuses and dry out skin.

The wettest and most disruptive months are May and June, when heavy rains can flood roads, cut power, and make getting around difficult. August is also relatively dry despite being mid-year. If you visit during a rainy period, stay close to your base on heavy-rain days and confirm your coworking space has generator backup.

Pros and Cons

  • English is the official language, so daily life and business are fully accessible without learning a local language.
  • Strong African diaspora and "Year of Return" culture means a vibrant, welcoming community, especially for Black American and Caribbean visitors.
  • Good food scene with affordable street food and a growing range of restaurants spanning West African, Lebanese, Indian, and international cuisines.
  • Relatively stable and democratic by regional standards, with a calm political environment for an African capital.
  • Energetic nightlife centered on Osu, with live music and Afrobeats clubs open late on weekends.
  • Growing startup and tech ecosystem with regular networking events and a supportive entrepreneurial community.
  • Traffic is brutal and can consume 2-3 hours of your day on bad weeks.
  • Power outages (dumsor) are common outside peak-infrastructure areas; generator backup is often needed.
  • Air quality is poor, especially in the dry season. PM2.5 averages well above WHO guidelines year-round.
  • No nomad visa and limited formal options for stays beyond 60 days.
  • Heat and humidity outside the dry season make working without air conditioning uncomfortable.
  • Road safety is a real concern: Ghana has a high road accident rate; avoid driving at night.

Who Accra Is Best For

Accra rewards nomads who are genuinely curious about West African culture, food, and music, and who want to be part of a city building something. It is a strong fit if you are interested in the African tech and entrepreneurship scene, value English-speaking environments, or have personal or ancestral ties to West Africa. The diaspora community is large and welcoming, and the social calendar in Osu and East Legon is full.

It is less suited to nomads who need frictionless, plug-and-play infrastructure. If your work demands rock-solid connectivity at all hours, if you cannot tolerate heat, or if you get frustrated by slow bureaucratic processes, the daily friction of Accra will wear on you. Budget travelers expecting Southeast Asia-level value will also find costs higher than expected once you account for air-conditioned housing and reliable power.

Frequently Asked Questions About Accra

How much does it cost to live in Accra as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Accra is around $1,800 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Accra?

With a WiFi score of 5/10, connectivity in Accra is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Accra safe for digital nomads?

Accra scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Accra a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Accra is worth a look if you value English and low costs. Costs sit near $1,800 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
