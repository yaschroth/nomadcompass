Is Cali a Good Base for Remote Work?

The salsa capital where every night is a dance party. Plan on roughly $1,500 a month. Its strengths are nightlife and climate, while safety is where it scores lower.

Cost of Living in Cali

Cali is the most affordable major city in Colombia for remote workers. The exchange rate hovers around 3,800-4,000 COP per USD, so your dollar stretches noticeably further here than in Medellin or Bogota.

  • Rent, furnished 1-bed in Granada or El Penon: 1,500,000-2,700,000 COP ($400-700/month)
  • Rent, furnished 1-bed in cheaper areas (Miraflores, San Fernando): 900,000-1,500,000 COP ($230-400/month)
  • Groceries (cooking at home): 400,000-600,000 COP ($100-160/month)
  • Eating out, menu del dia lunch: 12,000-20,000 COP ($3-5 per meal)
  • Eating out, sit-down dinner for two: 80,000-150,000 COP ($20-40)
  • Coworking, hot-desk pass: 180,000-480,000 COP ($45-125/month)
  • Local transport (MIO bus): 2,100 COP per trip (~$0.55); Uber or InDriver ride in the city: 8,000-25,000 COP ($2-6)
  • Utilities + internet: 200,000-350,000 COP ($50-90/month)

Realistic monthly total: 3,500,000-5,500,000 COP ($900-1,400/month) for a comfortable life with coworking, eating out regularly, and the occasional night out. Budget-conscious nomads can come in under $800.

Where to Work

The coworking scene in Cali is smaller than Medellin's but functional. Enfoque Lab in the El Sindicato area is the city's most established dedicated coworking space, rated 4.6 stars and offering 1,500 square meters across multiple floors with fiber internet, air conditioning, a cafeteria, and private office options. Monthly hot-desk plans start around 480,000 COP ($125). Morrison Technologies in San Antonio runs a daily pass for about 50,000 COP ($13) and stays open until 10 PM, making it useful for evening sessions. Coworking Tequendama near the city center is another established option with reliable connectivity.

Cafe-work culture is real in Cali, particularly in Granada, El Penon, and San Antonio. Spots like Juan Valdes, Tierradentro Cafe in San Antonio, and a handful of independent coffee shops tolerate laptop workers without pressure. WiFi in cafes is hit or miss: many have fiber, but shared bandwidth during lunch hours can drag. Confirm speeds before ordering if you have a video call.

Residential fiber internet in a modern apartment in Granada or Ciudad Jardin runs 25-40 Mbps for $20-35/month and is generally reliable. If your apartment has fiber, working from home is often your best option in Cali.

Getting Around

The MIO (Masivo Integrado de Occidente) is Cali's bus rapid transit network and the backbone of public transport. A rechargeable card costs a few thousand pesos and each trip is about 2,100 COP. It covers most parts of the city where nomads tend to live and work, though it can be crowded during rush hour and the routes require some practice to learn.

Uber operates legally in Cali and is the most convenient option at night or when you have luggage. InDriver is also popular and often cheaper. Street taxis exist but agree on a price or insist on the meter before getting in; avoid hailing random taxis late at night.

Walkability is neighborhood-dependent. Granada and El Penon are reasonably walkable for daily errands. Larger parts of the city are not designed for pedestrians, and traffic can be aggressive.

Alfonso Bonilla Aragon International Airport (CLO) sits roughly 17 km northeast of the city center in the municipality of Palmira. An Uber or InDriver ride takes 25-40 minutes and costs 25,000-45,000 COP ($6-12) depending on traffic. A city bus option exists for around 10,000 COP ($2-3) but takes 40-50 minutes. Direct flights connect Cali to Bogota, Medellin, and a handful of international destinations including Miami and Panama City.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the US, Canada, UK, EU countries, Australia, and most of Latin America enter Colombia visa-free and receive a tourist stamp good for up to 90 days. You can extend this once at a Migración Colombia office for an additional 90 days, giving you up to 180 days in a calendar year before you need to exit and restart.

Colombia offers a dedicated digital nomad visa called the Visa Nomadas Digitales (V-ND). It allows a stay of up to two years, requires proof of remote work or freelance income of at least three times Colombia's monthly minimum wage (roughly $1,100-1,400 USD/month as of 2026), and permits legal remote work for foreign clients or companies. The approval rate has been below 60% in recent years due to discretionary review, so prepare your documentation carefully.

Always confirm current requirements directly with the Colombian Cancilleria or Migración Colombia before applying, as rules change.

Best Time to Visit

Cali sits at around 1,000 meters elevation and stays warm year-round, with daily highs between 28°C and 32°C (82-90°F). Rain does not follow a single wet season but instead comes in two rainy periods: April through June and October through November. Even during these months, rain usually arrives in the afternoon or evening, leaving mornings clear.

The two driest windows are December through March and July through September. July and August are the sunniest months and work well for remote workers who want reliable outdoor cafe time. December brings the famous Feria de Cali (December 25-30), a massive salsa festival with street parties and concerts that turns the city electric but also drives up accommodation prices and noise.

For pure productivity, January, February, July, and August offer the best combination of dry weather, manageable temperatures, and a functional city rhythm without major festival disruptions.

Pros and Cons

  • Affordable: One of the cheapest livable cities in South America for remote workers
  • Salsa culture: Nightlife, dance schools, and live music in a way no other city replicates
  • Food: Excellent local produce, fresh juice, Pacific Coast seafood, and growing restaurant scene
  • Warmth: Locals (Calenos) are known for being open, welcoming, and social
  • Climate: Warm but not oppressive, with enough breeze and shade to stay comfortable
  • Low competition: Far fewer nomads than Medellin, so you get a more authentic city experience
  • Safety requires vigilance: Petty theft and muggings happen; phone-snatching on the street is a real risk in many areas outside the main expat neighborhoods
  • Small nomad network: English-speaking co-working community is thin; making remote-work connections takes more effort than in Medellin or Bogota
  • Heat: The city can feel oppressive in the midday sun, especially outside air-conditioned spaces
  • Limited international flights: Getting to the US or Europe usually requires a connection through Bogota or Panama City
  • Tap water is not drinkable: Factor in bottled or filtered water costs
  • Traffic and pollution: Rush-hour air quality drops and noise on main roads can be significant

Who Cali Is Best For

Cali suits nomads who want to go deeper into Colombian culture rather than the established expat bubble. If you speak at least basic Spanish, can handle moderate safety precautions, and are drawn to salsa, Pacific Coast cuisine, and a genuinely local city rhythm, Cali will reward you more per dollar than almost anywhere in South America.

It works well for solo workers who are self-sufficient and comfortable researching neighborhoods, setting up their own routines, and building social connections outside the nomad circuit. Couples and slow travelers on a tight budget will find the value exceptional.

Skip Cali if you rely on a large English-speaking nomad community, need a dense coworking network, or are not comfortable with an elevated baseline level of street awareness. Travelers who want a curated nomad experience with meetups and English-first coworking are better served by Medellin.

Frequently Asked Questions About Cali

How much does it cost to live in Cali as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,500 a month in Cali for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Cali?

Cali rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Cali safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Cali is 3/10, patchy for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Cali a good city for digital nomads overall?

Cali stands out for nightlife and climate among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,500 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
