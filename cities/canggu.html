Canggu at a Glance for Remote Workers

Cost of Living in Canggu

Canggu is no longer cheap by Southeast Asian standards, and anyone expecting Bali prices from five years ago will be caught off guard. Here is what a realistic monthly budget looks like in mid-2026:

  • Rent, central Canggu (Batu Bolong, Echo Beach): IDR 12,000,000-18,000,000/month ($750-1,125 USD) for a private 1-bedroom villa with a pool. Shared co-living rooms start around IDR 5,000,000 ($310 USD).
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Pererenan, outer Berawa): IDR 7,000,000-11,000,000/month ($440-690 USD) for a comparable private room, with a 20-30 minute scooter ride to the busier parts of Canggu.
  • Groceries (cooking some meals at home): IDR 1,200,000-2,000,000/month ($75-125 USD).
  • Eating out: A warung meal runs IDR 25,000-50,000 ($1.50-3 USD). A sit-down meal at a mid-range restaurant costs IDR 100,000-250,000 ($6-16 USD). Budget IDR 2,500,000-5,000,000/month ($155-310 USD) if you eat out most meals.
  • Coworking hot-desk: IDR 280,000-400,000/day ($17-25 USD); monthly memberships range from IDR 800,000-3,550,000 ($50-220 USD) depending on the space.
  • Local transport (scooter rental): IDR 70,000-150,000/day ($4.50-9.50 USD), or IDR 500,000-700,000/month ($31-44 USD) on a long-term rental. Grab and Gojek rides average IDR 20,000-40,000 ($1.25-2.50 USD) per trip.
  • Mobile data (10 GB SIM): Around IDR 100,000/month ($6 USD).

Total monthly range: Budget nomads who rent shared rooms and eat mostly local food can manage IDR 12,000,000-18,000,000 ($750-1,125 USD). A comfortable independent lifestyle with a private villa, eating out regularly, and using coworking occasionally runs IDR 24,000,000-40,000,000 ($1,500-2,500 USD). Premium setups with a pool villa and restaurant dining push to $3,000+ USD. Rent costs climbed roughly 18 percent year-on-year in 2025-2026, and most landlords now require two months upfront.

Where to Work

Canggu has more coworking options per square kilometer than almost anywhere in Southeast Asia, so your biggest decision is what kind of environment you work best in.

Dedicated coworking spaces are the reliable choice for stable internet and a professional atmosphere. BWork in Berawa has a stunning triple-height atrium, a pool, and separate quiet and collaborative zones; day passes run IDR 280,000 and monthly memberships around IDR 3,550,000. Tribal in Pererenan is a community-run space with a pool and excellent wifi that you can access for free with a minimum food and drink spend of IDR 100,000 after three hours. Kinship Studio in Berawa is a smaller, design-focused space that draws a creative crowd; day passes start at IDR 250,000. Note: Dojo Bali, once Canggu's flagship coworking space, permanently closed in late 2022. Outpost's Canggu location also closed in 2024. Always verify before booking.

Cafe work culture is strong here. Many cafes have designated work zones with outlets and stable wifi. Zin Cafe in central Canggu has a specific quiet zone that discourages loud music. Betelnut Cafe and Miel Cafe on Batu Bolong are popular laptop spots with good air conditioning. The unspoken etiquette: buy something every couple of hours and don't occupy a prime table for six hours on one coffee during a lunch rush.

WiFi reality: Average speeds in Canggu hover around 25 Mbps, which is adequate for most remote work including video calls and cloud collaboration. Speeds above 50 Mbps are more the exception. Power outages during heavy rain are not uncommon, so a cellular backup plan using a local SIM from Telkomsel or XL is worth having. Grab a SIM at the airport on arrival.

Getting Around

Canggu is not walkable beyond a few hundred meters. The area is a sprawl of villas, rice fields, and narrow gang lanes that don't connect in any logical grid, and most of what you need is a 5-20 minute ride away.

Scooter is how most people get around, and renting one long-term is the most practical option. Rentals from local shops cost IDR 500,000-700,000 per month. You do need to have some comfort with traffic, as roads are narrow and motorbike-heavy. Batu Bolong and the main Canggu road grid lock between 4-7 PM daily.

Ride-hailing: Grab and Gojek work well throughout Canggu and nearby areas. Prices are low for short trips, and both apps show metered fares upfront. Use these if you don't want to ride a scooter. BlueBlue taxis are also available but cost more.

Airport transfers: Ngurah Rai International Airport (Denpasar) is roughly 45 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes from Canggu depending on traffic. Pre-arranged private transfers are the most reliable option and cost around IDR 180,000-250,000 ($11-16 USD) each way. Grab officially operates from the airport but pickup areas are a short walk from arrivals.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Indonesia's entry setup changed meaningfully in 2024-2025, so check the current rules before you book.

Visa-free: Citizens of 169 countries, including most Western nations, can enter Indonesia visa-free for up to 30 days. This is strictly non-extendable and cannot be converted to another visa type.

Visa on Arrival (eVoA): Most passport holders can purchase a Visa on Arrival at the airport for IDR 500,000 ($31 USD), granting 30 days. This can be extended once in person at an Indonesian immigration office for another 30 days, for a maximum stay of 60 days. As of May 2025, all extensions require an in-person biometric appointment at the local immigration office, which in Bali is in Denpasar.

Digital Nomad Visa (E33G): Indonesia launched a dedicated remote worker visa in 2024. It allows stays of up to five years with a minimum income requirement of approximately $60,000 USD per year. The application requires employment documentation, bank statements, and proof of foreign-source income. Costs are significant upfront but work out favorably for long-term stays.

Social Visa (C1): A 60-day single-entry tourist visa extendable up to 180 days total, processed through an Indonesian embassy before you arrive. Popular with long-stay visitors who don't qualify for the nomad visa.

Always verify current requirements with the official Indonesian immigration website at imigrasi.go.id before making plans.

Best Time to Visit

Bali has two seasons, and the difference matters for how you feel day-to-day.

Dry season (April to October) is the clear winner for remote work. Temperatures sit between 26-32°C (79-90°F) with low humidity, reliable sunshine, and almost no rain. May, June, and September are a sweet spot: excellent weather, fewer tourists than July-August peak, and slightly lower villa prices.

Rainy season (November to March) brings daily afternoon downpours, higher humidity, and occasional all-day rain. The bigger issue during this period is beach cleanliness: December through February see ocean debris and plastic wash up on Canggu's beaches in significant quantities, making beach time unpleasant. Mornings are usually clear, and afternoon work sessions are unaffected if you're indoors. Prices drop and crowds thin, which appeals to some.

For a first visit combining good weather, social energy, and reasonable prices, May-June and September are the best months.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: One of the world's densest concentrations of coworking spaces and laptop-friendly cafes in a compact area.
  • Pro: Large, active English-speaking nomad community making it easy to meet people quickly.
  • Pro: Excellent food scene spanning Indonesian street food to high-end Mediterranean and Japanese at relatively low cost.
  • Pro: Surf breaks directly in town, yoga studios everywhere, and beach access within walking distance of most accommodation.
  • Pro: Warm, friendly local community and a culture of genuine hospitality.
  • Con: Severe traffic congestion, especially on the main Canggu road from mid-afternoon. Short distances routinely take 30+ minutes by car.
  • Con: Rent has risen significantly and continues to climb; many nomads feel the value proposition has weakened compared to 2021-2022.
  • Con: Beaches collect significant plastic waste and debris, particularly November through February.
  • Con: Noise and party culture are constant: roosters at 5 AM, scooters all day, bars until late. Light sleepers and people who need quiet will struggle.
  • Con: Bali Belly (food-related stomach illness) is common; water quality is not reliable and bottled water is a daily necessity.

Who Canggu Is Best For

Canggu suits you well if you want to combine serious remote work with an active social life, you surf or want to learn, and you don't mind spending a bit more than you would elsewhere in Southeast Asia for access to infrastructure and community. It is ideal for people arriving solo who want to meet other nomads fast: the social density here is unmatched in Bali. It also works well as a short-to-medium term base, from one to three months, where the energy and variety feel like assets rather than exhaustion.

You should probably look elsewhere if you prioritize quiet and deep focus, prefer a slower pace with genuine cultural immersion, or are watching your budget closely. Ubud is the go-to for those wanting nature, a quieter pace, and Balinese culture on the doorstep. Sanur suits longer-stayers who want stability, reliability, and a calmer beach scene. If Canggu's reputation for being crowded and noisy rings alarm bells for you, those concerns are founded.

Frequently Asked Questions About Canggu

How much does it cost to live in Canggu as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $2,200 a month in Canggu for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Canggu?

Canggu rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Canggu safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Canggu is 7/10, good for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Canggu a good city for digital nomads overall?

Canggu stands out for a large nomad community and nightlife among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,200 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
