Cost of Living in Riyadh

Riyadh sits in a middle tier for the Gulf: pricier than Southeast Asia but noticeably cheaper than Dubai. You can live comfortably on a budget that scales significantly depending on how central you want to be.

  • Rent, central (Al Olaya, KAFD area): SAR 3,500-7,000/month ($930-1,870) for a furnished one-bedroom
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Al Rayan, Qurtubah, outer districts): SAR 2,000-3,500/month ($530-930)
  • Groceries (cooking at home): SAR 700-1,200/month ($185-320) for a single person shopping at local supermarkets; costs rise if you rely on imported Western brands
  • Eating out: SAR 30-50 ($8-13) for a casual meal, SAR 100-200 ($27-53) for a mid-range restaurant for two, SAR 250+ ($67+) at upscale spots
  • Coworking hot desk: SAR 600-1,200/month ($160-320); day passes from SAR 100-180 ($27-48)
  • Local transport (metro + Uber/Careem): SAR 300-600/month ($80-160); metro monthly pass costs SAR 140
  • Total realistic range: SAR 6,000-12,000/month ($1,600-3,200) depending on lifestyle and location

There is no personal income tax in Saudi Arabia. Alcohol is prohibited throughout the country, which removes one cost category entirely. Utilities and internet are reasonable, with a 60+ Mbps home broadband plan running around SAR 261/month ($70).

Where to Work

Riyadh's coworking scene has expanded fast under Vision 2030, and the city now has genuine options for remote workers.

Coworking Spaces

AstroLabs is the most established name, with a location in Al Malqa and a newer space inside the King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD). It attracts tech founders and offers month-to-month memberships from SAR 750/month. Spaces at Riyadh Park Mall offers a polished European-style environment with strong community management, coworking from SAR 669/month. Garagena in Al Rayan is a startup-focused space rated 4.9/5, open 24/7 and well-regarded in the local tech scene. Regus operates multiple locations including Kingdom Centre, covering all budgets.

Cafe Work Culture

Cafe culture is genuinely strong. Specialty coffee shops are everywhere, many open 24 hours, and laptop workers are a normal sight. Ashjar Cafe in Ar Rabi (rated 4.6/5), FOCUS in Namar (rated 4.8/5), and O'PO Specialty Coffee in Al Maliha (rated 4.7/5) are among the best-reviewed spots for focused work. Namq Cafe and Cacti both run 24 hours with multiple locations, useful for flexible schedules or late-night sessions.

WiFi Reality

Fixed broadband and mobile data are both fast: Saudi Arabia's median mobile speed hit 124 Mbps in 2025. Cafes and coworking spaces routinely offer 100+ Mbps connections. Video calls and large file transfers are not an issue in the city. The weak link is if you rely on hotel WiFi in budget accommodation, where speeds can be inconsistent.

Getting Around

Riyadh is a large, car-centric city by design, but the transport situation improved significantly in early 2025.

Riyadh Metro

The metro reached full operation on six lines in January 2025, covering 176 km across 85 stations. It is driverless, clean, and air-conditioned. A single journey costs SAR 4, a monthly pass SAR 140. The metro connects many business districts but does not cover all residential areas, so you will still need Uber or Careem for last-mile trips.

Ride-Hailing

Uber and Careem are both active and reliable. Prices are reasonable: a typical cross-city trip runs SAR 20-60. Traffic jams during peak hours (especially on the Eastern and Northern Ring Roads) can add significant time, so build buffer into your schedule for important calls or meetings.

Walkability

Most of the city is not walkable in practical terms. Distances are large, sidewalks are inconsistent, and summer heat makes outdoor walking dangerous from May through September. Near the metro stations and in some newer districts, conditions are better.

Airport

King Khalid International Airport is in the north of the city. Metro Lines 3 and 4 connect directly to the airport. A taxi or Uber to the city center costs SAR 70-150 and takes 30-40 minutes outside rush hour. The metro journey takes roughly 40-50 minutes and is significantly cheaper.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Saudi Arabia opened to tourism in 2019, and the entry process is now fairly straightforward for many nationalities.

Tourist eVisa and Visa on Arrival

Citizens of around 60 countries, including the US, UK, EU member states, Australia, Japan, South Korea, and several others, can get a one-year multiple-entry tourist visa. It allows cumulative stays of up to 90 days across all visits within that year. Apply via the Saudi eVisa portal before travel or get the visa on arrival at major airports.

Citizens of countries not on the main list may qualify if they hold a valid tourist or business visa from the US, UK, or a Schengen-area country.

Note from February 2025: Saudi Arabia restricted nationals of 14 specific countries to single-entry visas valid for 30 days only. Check which passport you hold against the current official list before planning a long stay.

Nomad Visa

Saudi Arabia does not currently offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. The tourist visa is the primary option for most remote workers visiting short-term. Staying beyond 90 days requires exit and re-entry or a separate visa category tied to employment or business registration.

Always verify entry requirements at the official Saudi eVisa portal (visa.visitsaudi.com) before booking travel.

Best Time to Visit

Climate drives everything here. Riyadh has a true desert climate with extreme summer heat and mild, pleasant winters.

Best Window: November through March

This is genuinely the sweet spot for remote work in Riyadh. Daytime highs sit around 20-26 degrees Celsius (68-79 F), evenings are cool and comfortable, and the city is at its most active. The Riyadh Season entertainment festival runs from October through early spring, bringing concerts, cultural events, and outdoor activities. This is when cafe terraces fill up and the city feels at its best.

Spring (April to May)

Temperatures rise quickly from late March. April is still tolerable but hot, and by May you are looking at 38+ degrees Celsius most days. Occasional sandstorms blow through in spring.

Summer (June to September)

Avoid if you need to move around outside. Temperatures regularly reach 45-50 degrees Celsius, outdoor air quality drops, and the city essentially retreats indoors. Remote workers who can stay inside a well-cooled apartment and coworking space can function, but quality of life is low.

October

Heat begins to ease in late October. Early October can still be very hot, so mid-to-late October marks the start of the good season.

Pros and Cons

  • Extremely low crime rate and strong personal safety in public spaces
  • Fast, reliable internet across the city, in cafes, coworking spaces, and homes
  • No income tax on personal earnings
  • Rapidly improving infrastructure including the new metro and world-class coworking venues
  • Excellent food scene with strong Middle Eastern, Asian, and international options across all price ranges
  • 24-hour cafe culture that accommodates unusual working hours
  • Alcohol is completely prohibited throughout Saudi Arabia, with severe penalties for violations
  • Extreme summer heat from June to September makes outdoor life difficult or dangerous
  • No dedicated nomad visa and 90-day cumulative cap on tourist stays
  • LGBTQ+ travelers face significant legal risks as same-sex relationships are criminalized
  • English proficiency is limited outside business districts and international hotels
  • Car dependency outside metro corridors makes daily logistics slow without a ride-hailing app
  • Air quality can be poor due to desert dust and urban pollution, particularly in summer
  • Social and political freedoms are restricted in ways that will feel constraining to many Western visitors

Who Riyadh Is Best For

Riyadh works well for remote workers who prioritize safety, fast infrastructure, and a high-quality food and cafe scene. If you are doing business development in the Gulf or attending regional conferences, Riyadh is an obvious base with excellent flight connections across the Middle East and Asia. Workers in tech, finance, or consulting who can benefit from the city's Vision 2030 networking environment will find genuine opportunity here.

It suits people who visit November through March, can stay within the 90-day tourist window, and are comfortable in a conservative Islamic environment without alcohol or overt nightlife. The city rewards those willing to explore its fast-changing local culture: new museums, excellent restaurants, and a genuinely welcoming population.

Skip Riyadh if you need a nomad community with years of infrastructure behind it, depend on alcohol as part of your social life, or identify as LGBTQ+ (the legal risks are serious and well-documented). Summer visitors who cannot tolerate being indoors most of the day should also look elsewhere. If you want cheap street food, walkable neighborhoods, and a beach nearby, Riyadh is the wrong city.

Frequently Asked Questions About Riyadh

How much does it cost to live in Riyadh as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $3,300 a month in Riyadh for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Riyadh?

Riyadh rates 8 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work excellent. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Riyadh safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Riyadh is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Riyadh a good city for digital nomads overall?

Riyadh stands out for fast WiFi and safety among remote workers. With a typical budget near $3,300 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
