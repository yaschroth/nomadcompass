Las Palmas for Digital Nomads: The Honest Take

Enjoy year-round spring weather on a volcanic island with a massive nomad scene. Las Palmas pairs climate with a large nomad community and a arid climate, all for about $2,400 a month. If anything, cost lags.

Cost of Living in Las Palmas

Las Palmas is one of the better-value nomad hubs in the EU, partly because the Canary Islands run a separate tax system with a 7% IGIC sales tax instead of mainland Spain's 21% VAT, which quietly lowers everyday prices. Rents have climbed with the island's popularity, but you still get a full-size Atlantic city for less than Lisbon or Barcelona. USD figures use roughly 1.08 to the euro; expect movement with the exchange rate. A realistic monthly breakdown for one person in 2025 to 2026:

  • Rent, one-bedroom near Las Canteras or the center: roughly EUR 900-1,300 (about $970-1,400). City-wide asking rent runs around EUR 13.8 per square meter, so an 80 sqm flat lands near EUR 1,100.
  • Rent, cheaper inland or edge neighborhoods: a studio away from the beach can start around EUR 350-600 (about $380-650), and a private room in a shared flat often runs EUR 350-500 ($380-540).
  • Groceries: around EUR 200-300 ($215-325). Fresh bread is about EUR 1.60, a coffee EUR 2.10, a beer EUR 3.
  • Eating out: a main course is about EUR 10 and a meal for two around EUR 50. Budget EUR 150-300 ($160-325) depending on how often you go out.
  • Coworking hot-desk: roughly EUR 20 per day, EUR 80 per week, or EUR 120-180 per month ($130-195) for a full membership.
  • Local transport: around EUR 20-30 ($22-32). Single fares are EUR 1.40, dropping to about EUR 0.42 per trip with a contactless Bono card.
  • Utilities and internet: around EUR 90 for a mid-size flat plus roughly EUR 40 for fiber and mobile.

All in, a solo nomad who cooks sometimes and shares or rents modestly can live comfortably on about EUR 1,500-2,200 per month ($1,600-2,400). A single person renting their own central one-bedroom and eating out often will land closer to EUR 2,200-2,800 ($2,400-3,000).

Where to Work

Las Palmas has one of the deepest coworking scenes in the Canaries, and most spaces cluster in the Guanarteme and La Isleta neighborhoods within walking distance of Las Canteras beach. Well-known options include White Forest (a quiet, modern space about a minute from the beach with 24/7 access), The House (bright, with covered roof terraces, free coffee and breakfast, 24/7 access), Soppa de Azul and Fico Coworking in La Isleta, the industrial-flavored Talleres Palermo, and Sky Coworking. Repeople, a local operator, also runs coworking and coliving packages if you want housing and a desk bundled together.

Cafe-work culture exists but is more relaxed than in a dedicated remote-work capital. Plenty of beachfront and center cafes tolerate a laptop for the price of a coffee, though outlets and reliable seating are hit or miss at peak times, so a coworking membership is the sane default for calls and deadlines. WiFi is genuinely good here: fast fiber is widespread in the beach and central districts, mobile data along the coast is reliable, and coworking spaces advertise high-speed connections as standard. If your work depends on stable video calls, book a place with fiber and keep a mobile data plan as backup.

Getting Around

The local buses are called guaguas, and the yellow Guaguas Municipales run the city network while the blue Global buses cover the wider island. City buses are clean, air-conditioned, cheap, and frequent. A single ride is EUR 1.40, but a contactless Bono card cuts the effective fare to around EUR 0.42 per trip, so load one on arrival. Journeys can be slow with frequent stops, but coverage across the city is solid.

Honestly, most nomads barely use the bus day to day. Las Palmas is flat and very walkable along the coast: the strip from Santa Catalina down to Las Canteras and through Guanarteme covers most housing, coworking, cafes, and the beach on foot. You will want transport for trips to the old town of Vegueta, the south of the island, or hiking in the interior.

The airport is Gran Canaria (code LPA), about 25 km south of the city. The direct airport bus, line 60, runs roughly twice an hour, costs about EUR 2.95, and reaches San Telmo station in the center in around 30 minutes (about 45 minutes to Santa Catalina). A taxi is faster but far pricier. There is no metro or tram on the island.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

If you hold a US, UK, Canadian, Australian, or similar passport, you can enter Spain visa-free under the Schengen rule of 90 days within any rolling 180-day period. The Canary Islands are part of Spain and the Schengen area, so this window is not separate from the mainland; time spent anywhere in Schengen counts.

To stay longer, Spain offers a digital nomad visa for remote workers employed by or contracting with companies outside Spain. As of late 2025 the income threshold rose to roughly EUR 2,763 net per month (about EUR 33,000 per year) for a single applicant, with extra required for dependents. Applied for at a consulate abroad it is typically issued for one year; applied for from inside Spain it can grant residence for up to three years, and it is renewable toward the five-year mark. A major draw of basing yourself here specifically is the Canary Islands' lower tax environment: the 7% IGIC sales tax instead of 21% VAT, and, for those who set up a qualifying company, the ZEC (Zona Especial Canaria) regime with a 4% corporate tax rate. The ZEC is aimed at businesses with real investment and hiring, not individual freelancers, so treat it as a reason to consult an accountant rather than an automatic perk. Visa income figures, tax residency rules, and ZEC eligibility change often, so confirm current details with official Spanish government sources or an immigration lawyer before you commit.

Best Time to Visit

This is the headline reason people come: Las Palmas has one of the most stable year-round climates anywhere, earning the Canaries their "eternal spring" reputation. The mean annual temperature sits around 19 C (66 F), and it rarely drops below 18 C or climbs above 30 C. Winters hover around 18-21 C, summers around 25-31 C, and the islands get around 3,000 hours of sunshine a year. Rain is scarce, especially in summer.

For remote work, there is no bad month, which is exactly the point: you can plan a long stay without gambling on the weather. If you want to swim comfortably at Las Canteras, the beach's calm, reef-sheltered water is warmest and most inviting from roughly June through October. If you prefer quieter, cheaper months with still-excellent working weather, aim for the shoulder seasons of spring and autumn. Note that it is genuinely windy here year-round, which keeps the heat pleasant but is worth knowing before you arrive.

Pros and Cons

The honest trade-offs:

  • Pro: Reliable spring-like weather nearly every day of the year.
  • Pro: Lower taxes than mainland Spain (7% IGIC) and solid value for an EU city.
  • Pro: Las Canteras, a long, calm, walkable city beach right where people live and work.
  • Pro: Deep coworking scene, fast fiber, and an established nomad community.
  • Pro: Real Spanish city life, not a resort bubble.
  • Con: Island isolation: getting anywhere off the island means a flight, and travel time to mainland Europe adds up.
  • Con: Persistent wind that some people love and others find wearing.
  • Con: Limited nightlife and a fairly quiet after-hours scene compared with big mainland cities.
  • Con: Rising rents and seasonal housing crunches driven by tourism and nomad demand.
  • Con: Swimming season at the city beaches is really only comfortable from about June to October.

Who Las Palmas Is Best For

Las Palmas suits you if you want a warm, beach-adjacent base with genuine city amenities, dependable internet, a ready-made coworking and nomad community, and the tax and lifestyle upside of the Canaries. It is a strong pick for medium to long stays, for solo travelers and couples who value routine and mild weather over constant novelty, and for anyone who wants to live outdoors year-round without leaving the EU.

Skip it if you need a buzzing nightlife scene, hate wind, or want the fast, spontaneous travel of a well-connected mainland hub where the next country is a train ride away. Digital nomads chasing the cheapest possible cost of living will also find Southeast Asia or parts of Latin America notably cheaper. If your priority is quick access to the rest of Europe, a mainland base will frustrate you less than an Atlantic island.

Frequently Asked Questions About Las Palmas

How much does it cost to live in Las Palmas as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Las Palmas is around $2,400 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Las Palmas?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Las Palmas is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Las Palmas safe for digital nomads?

Las Palmas scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Las Palmas a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Las Palmas is worth a look if you value climate and a large nomad community. Costs sit near $2,400 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
