Setting Up as a Nomad in Canberra

Australia's planned capital city. An oceanic climate and strong English make Canberra an easy base, and at about $2,740 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the size of the nomad scene in mind.

Canberra is Australia's most expensive city after Sydney, so set your expectations accordingly. As the capital, it attracts government workers and public servants, which keeps housing prices high year-round.

  • Rent (1BR, central Civic/Braddon): $1,400-1,760/month
  • Rent (1BR, outer suburbs like Belconnen or Tuggeranong): $1,050-1,330/month
  • Groceries: $280-350/month
  • Eating out (mid-range meal): $18-28 per meal
  • Coworking hot-desk (daily pass): $21-42/day; $195-455/month for dedicated desk
  • Local transport (MyWay+ monthly): $35-70/month

Monthly total range: $2,250-3,160 for a solo nomad living reasonably. Budget travelers sharing a house can get closer to $1,760, but it requires compromises on location. There is no shortage of cafes and restaurants, though eating out regularly will push your budget above $2,810 with ease.

Where to Work

Canberra has a small but solid coworking scene, with most spaces concentrated in the CBD and inner suburbs.

Coworking Spaces

  • Hub Civic Quarter (68 Northbourne Ave, CBD): Large, eco-designed space with 305 desks, private offices, meeting rooms, and an on-site cafe. Day passes from $41, monthly from $455.
  • Haven Workspaces (Northbourne Ave, Civic Quarter): Smaller boutique option with a strong community feel, walking distance to ANU and the CBD. Good for focused deep work.
  • JAGA Swanson Court (Allara St, CBD): Transparent all-inclusive pricing with flexible day passes at $21. Award-winning space with quiet focus areas and professional meeting rooms.
  • WOTSO Woden (Westfield Woden, Phillip): Best choice if you stay in the southern suburbs; 24/7 access, month-to-month, from $195/month.

Cafe Work Culture

Canberra's cafe scene is genuinely excellent, and laptop workers are tolerated in most spots during off-peak hours. Lonsdale Street Roasters in Braddon has an upstairs area popular with remote workers. The Marie Reay Teaching Centre on the ANU campus offers free public access Monday to Friday 8am-7pm with ample desk space and good WiFi, making it a reliable free option.

WiFi

NBN coverage across Canberra is strong in most areas. All major coworking spaces offer reliable, fast connections. Public WiFi exists in the CBD but is inconsistent; bring a backup data SIM if you rely on it for calls.

Getting Around

Canberra was designed for cars, and that reality has not changed entirely even with recent public transport improvements. You will cover more ground and save time with a car or scooter, especially if you plan to explore beyond the inner city.

Public Transport

The MyWay+ system covers buses and a light rail line running from Gungahlin through the CBD to the inner south. The light rail is fast and reliable. Buses are frequent on major routes but sparse in outer suburbs, with reduced evening and weekend service. A reloadable MyWay+ card or contactless bank card covers fares; a daily cap keeps costs predictable at $6.50.

Cycling

Canberra has Australia's most extensive shared cycling and walking path network. The inner city, lake circuit, and connections between major town centres are all well-served. E-bike hire is available and genuinely useful for the flat lake precinct.

Airport

Canberra Airport sits about 8 km from the city centre. Action Bus routes 11 and 11A connect both terminals to the city interchange every 15-30 minutes, with a fare of $3.50. A taxi or rideshare runs $18-25 and takes 15-20 minutes.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Australia has no dedicated digital nomad visa as of mid-2026. Your options depend on your passport.

  • Electronic Travel Authority (ETA, subclass 601): Available to passport holders from the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, and around 30 other countries. Grants up to 3 months per visit, multiple entries over 12 months. Applied for through the Australian ETA app. Low cost.
  • eVisitor (subclass 651): Free, for citizens of most EU member states and a handful of other European countries. Same conditions as the ETA: up to 3 months per visit.
  • Visitor Visa (subclass 600): Available to all nationalities. Allows stays of 3-12 months depending on circumstances, though the standard tourist grant is 3 months. More documentation required than the ETA.
  • Working Holiday Visa (subclass 417 or 462): If you are 18-30 (or up to 35 on some subclass 417 passports), this allows a 12-month stay with the right to work, renewable for a second and third year under certain conditions. Not technically a nomad visa, but widely used by remote workers.

You generally may continue working remotely for a non-Australian employer on a visitor entry, but you cannot provide services to Australian clients.

Best Time to Visit

Canberra has four distinct seasons and is one of Australia's coldest major cities in winter.

  • Autumn (March-May): The sweet spot. Mild days in the 15-22C range, cool nights, brilliant foliage colour, and fewer tourists. Ideal for working with the windows open and exploring on weekends.
  • Spring (September-November): Increasingly popular as temperatures climb back into comfortable ranges. Floriade, Canberra's major flower festival, runs through October and brings crowds but good energy.
  • Summer (December-February): Warm to hot with low humidity. Temperatures reach 28-35C and bushfire smoke can occasionally affect air quality during extreme heat events. Lively and busy.
  • Winter (June-August): Cold and grey, with overnight temperatures regularly dropping below 0C and frosts most mornings. Daytime highs of 10-12C. The city does not shut down, but outdoor life slows considerably. Only recommended if you genuinely love cold weather or want the lowest crowds.

For remote work, autumn offers the best combination of pleasant conditions, stable weather, and a city that feels energised without peak tourist pressure.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Genuinely excellent food and coffee scene, well above its size
  • Very safe city with low crime by any measure
  • World-class museums and galleries, most of them free
  • Fast, reliable NBN internet widely available
  • Clean, green, and uncrowded compared to Sydney or Melbourne
  • Easy day trips to the Snowy Mountains, South Coast beaches, and wine regions
  • Compact enough to explore fully in a longer stay

Cons

  • High cost of living, particularly rent
  • Car-dependent layout; getting around without a vehicle takes planning
  • Muted nightlife and entertainment compared to Sydney or Melbourne
  • Winters are cold and long for an Australian city
  • Small nomad community; less networking opportunity than coastal cities
  • No beach access; nearest coast is 90+ minutes by car

Who Canberra Is Best For

Canberra works best for nomads who value safety, clean infrastructure, and quality of life over social buzz or budget-friendly pricing. If you are a researcher, policy professional, or someone with an interest in Australian history, culture, or politics, you will find the city genuinely engaging rather than just liveable. It suits remote workers who want a calm, productive base and do not mind planning their social calendar in advance.

Canberra is a harder sell if you are chasing a large nomad community, warm beach weather, or a low cost of living. Digital nomads who thrive on spontaneous late nights, diverse street food, or backpacker social culture will find Canberra too quiet and too expensive. It is also not a great short-stay city; the attractions and rhythm reward staying at least three to four weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions About Canberra

How much does it cost to live in Canberra as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Canberra is around $2,740 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Canberra?

With a WiFi score of 8/10, connectivity in Canberra is excellent for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Canberra safe for digital nomads?

Canberra scores 9 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Canberra a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Canberra is worth a look if you value English and fast WiFi. Costs sit near $2,740 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
