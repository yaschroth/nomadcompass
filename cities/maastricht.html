Maastricht for Digital Nomads: The Honest Take

Where Netherlands meets Belgium, Burgundian lifestyle and European crossroads. For remote workers, Maastricht leans on safety and English, with a typical budget near $2,300 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Maastricht is one of the more affordable Dutch cities for remote workers, though it is not cheap by global standards. Expect to spend roughly EUR 1,800 to EUR 2,600 per month (approximately USD 1,950 to USD 2,800) as a solo remote worker, depending on your lifestyle.

  • Rent, central apartment (1BR): EUR 950 to EUR 1,400 per month
  • Rent, outside the center (Wyckerpoort, Nazareth, Sint Pieter): EUR 850 to EUR 1,100 per month
  • Groceries (self-catering): EUR 200 to EUR 300 per month at Albert Heijn or Lidl
  • Eating out: EUR 15 to EUR 20 for a single meal at a casual restaurant; a three-course dinner in the Wyck district runs EUR 35 to EUR 60
  • Coworking hot desk: EUR 65 to EUR 200 per month depending on the space and access level
  • Local transport (monthly bus pass): EUR 70 to EUR 85
  • Utilities (if not included in rent): EUR 150 to EUR 230 per month

Compared to Amsterdam, you save roughly 25 percent on rent and day-to-day costs. The historic center has a dense concentration of cafes and restaurants, so it is easy to overspend on food and drink if you lean into the city's famously social culture.

Where to Work

Maastricht has a genuine coworking scene, anchored by a university population and a growing number of freelancers and small businesses.

Coworking Spaces

ROOOMS on Boschstraat in the Sphinx Quarter is the most established independent coworking space, with a community of more than 60 companies and freelancers, reliable WiFi, printers, and a flexible strip-card system starting around EUR 65 per month. The Social Hub Maastricht (formerly The Student Hotel) on Sphinxcour offers day passes, monthly memberships, dedicated desks, and a lively atmosphere with good natural light. Regus also operates a serviced office location for those who need a more formal setup.

Cafes and Cafe-Work Culture

Cafe culture is strong here and most venues tolerate laptops, especially outside peak lunch hours. Coffeelovers Centre Ceramique, inside the public library on Avenue Ceramique, has long communal tables and free WiFi, making it a reliable backup without buying a coworking membership. Fixed Gear Coffee on Grote Gracht is a small specialty coffee shop that welcomes laptops. Alley Cat Bikes & Coffee clearly marks laptop-friendly tables.

WiFi Reality

The Netherlands has excellent broadband infrastructure and Maastricht benefits from that fully. Fiber is widely available in apartments. Cafe WiFi is generally solid for video calls, though shared spaces on busy afternoons can slow down. If you need guaranteed bandwidth, a coworking membership is worth the investment.

Getting Around

Maastricht's historic center is compact and very walkable. You can cross it on foot in about 20 minutes, and most daily needs, coworking spaces, restaurants, and the train station fall within a comfortable walk or short bike ride.

Cycling

Cycling is the dominant mode of transport. The city has well-maintained bike lanes and flat terrain across most of the center. Rental bikes are available through OV-fiets at the train station if you do not bring your own.

Buses

Arriva operates the city bus network. Buses connect the center with outer neighborhoods and suburbs. Pay with an OV-chipkaart for cheaper fares than single tickets. A monthly pass costs EUR 70 to EUR 85.

Train Connections

Maastricht Centraal connects to Amsterdam (roughly 2.5 hours), Utrecht, and Eindhoven via NS Intercity trains. Arriva also runs regional trains toward Heerlen and into Aachen, Germany, in about 45 minutes.

Airport Access

Maastricht Aachen Airport (MST) is small and limited in routes. Arriva bus line 30 connects the city center to the airport in about 30 minutes. For more flight options, Eindhoven Airport is roughly 70 km away by train and bus, and Brussels Airport is about 90 minutes by train via Liege.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

EU, EEA, and Swiss nationals can live and work in the Netherlands indefinitely with no additional permit required.

Non-EU nationals may enter the Netherlands and the broader Schengen Area visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period, depending on passport. Common visa-free passports include US, Canadian, Australian, UK, and Japanese citizens.

The Netherlands does not offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. The closest option for longer stays is the Self-Employed Residence Permit (MVV), which is designed for entrepreneurs establishing a business in the Netherlands. It requires demonstrating a viable business plan, adequate income (roughly EUR 1,300 gross per month), and international health insurance. The initial permit lasts one year. This route involves significant paperwork and is better suited to those planning to remain in the Netherlands long-term rather than those on a working holiday. Extensions are possible.

Always verify current entry requirements and permit rules at the official Dutch government immigration site (ind.nl) before travel, as rules change.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit

Maastricht has a sub-oceanic climate, wetter and cloudier than many nomads expect from Western Europe. The city lies in the far south of the Netherlands, so summers are slightly warmer than the north.

Late May through September is the best window for remote work visits. Temperatures sit between 18 and 25 degrees Celsius, terraces fill up on Vrijthof Square, and the city is at its most sociable. June, July, and August bring the famous outdoor André Rieu concerts and peak street life.

April and October are good shoulder options with fewer crowds and still manageable weather, though expect overcast days and light rain.

November through February is cold, damp, and frequently grey. The Christmas market on Vrijthof in December draws visitors, but working through winter months here requires tolerance for long dark days. Spring arrives slowly in March.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Genuinely beautiful historic city with one of the best preserved old towns in the Netherlands
  • Strong cafe and restaurant culture, excellent food and wine scene
  • More affordable than Amsterdam or Rotterdam for rent and daily costs
  • Highly walkable and cycle-friendly center
  • Close to Belgium and Germany, easy day trips to Liege, Aachen, and the Ardennes
  • Strong English fluency across most of the population
  • Safe, low-crime environment
  • Good coworking options for a city of this size

Cons

  • No dedicated digital nomad visa; 90-day Schengen limit applies to most non-EU passports
  • Rent has risen significantly in recent years and availability can be tight
  • Limited flight connections from local airport; getting out requires planning
  • Grey, wet winters make it a poor year-round base for those who need sunshine
  • Relatively small city; the social scene, while excellent, can feel limited over a long stay
  • Coworking options are adequate but sparse compared to larger Dutch cities

Who Maastricht Is Best For

Maastricht suits remote workers who want a high-quality, culturally rich European base without paying Amsterdam prices. It is a strong fit if you value walkability, a world-class food and cafe scene, and easy access to three countries. It works particularly well for EU passport holders who can stay as long as they like, and for non-EU travelers on a focused two to three month stint within their Schengen allowance.

The city also appeals to those who enjoy history, architecture, and outdoor cycling in the surrounding Limburg hills and valleys, which are unlike anything in the flat north of the Netherlands.

Skip Maastricht if you need a large, fast-growing nomad community with daily meetups, if you want a warm-weather winter base, or if flight connectivity is a priority for your work. It is also not ideal for those on a very tight budget, as the Netherlands in general requires a reasonable income to live comfortably.

Frequently Asked Questions About Maastricht

How much does it cost to live in Maastricht as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Maastricht is around $2,300 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Maastricht?

With a WiFi score of 8/10, connectivity in Maastricht is excellent for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Maastricht safe for digital nomads?

Maastricht scores 9 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Maastricht a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Maastricht is worth a look if you value safety and English. Costs sit near $2,300 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
