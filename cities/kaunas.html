Living in Kaunas as a Digital Nomad

Lithuania's hidden gem, art deco architecture and emerging creative scene. Plan on roughly $1,330 a month. Its strengths are safety and low costs, while the size of the nomad scene is where it scores lower.

Kaunas is one of the most affordable cities in the EU for remote workers. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in the city center (Naujamiestis or Old Town) runs roughly $460-635 per month. Move a few tram stops out toward Žaliakalnis or Aleksotas and you can find the same for $345-460.

Groceries for one person come to around $175-230 per month shopping at local Maxima or Rimi supermarkets. Eating out is cheap: a lunch set at a typical bistro costs $7-10, and a full dinner with a drink at a sit-down restaurant is $14-21. A flat white runs about $2.50-3.50.

Where to Work

Kaunas has a genuine coworking scene for a city its size. Workland Laisves on Laisves Avenue is the largest option with over 200 workstations, event space, and reliable fast fiber. Talent Garden Kaunas at Laisves al. 59 caters to the startup and creative tech crowd with a community-focused vibe. Pixel Hub near the Kaunas Lagoon district is a compact 300 m2 creative space with 30 desks, a photo studio, and a relaxation room. Zelmalab in the center is smaller (16 desks, 140 m2) and suits independent creatives who want a quiet, no-frills setup.

Cafe-working is also practical. CAFFEINE LT has multiple locations along Laisves Aleja with good WiFi and long hours (7:00-22:00). Vero Cafe locations are spread across the city, reliably quiet in the mornings, and fine for a few hours of work. The Kaunas Municipal Vincas Kudirka Public Library on Laisves Aleja is free, surprisingly well-equipped, and has consistent internet.

WiFi across coworking spaces is typically enough for video calls without issue, and the fiber infrastructure is solid. Cafe WiFi can be inconsistent at peak hours, so bring a local SIM as backup. Tele2 and Bite offer affordable prepaid data plans.

Getting Around

The city center, particularly Laisves Aleja (the main pedestrian boulevard) and the adjacent Old Town, is very walkable. Most cafes, restaurants, and coworking spaces are within a 15-20 minute walk of each other. Kaunas also has an extensive bus and trolleybus network running from around 5:30 to 23:00, with a modern real-time display system at major stops. A single ride costs $1; a monthly pass is around $32. The Bolt ride-hailing app works well and is cheap for late-night trips.

Two historic funiculars connect the city center with the Aleksotas and Zaliakalnis hillside neighborhoods. They are functional and genuinely used by locals, not just tourists.

Kaunas International Airport (KUN) is 15 km northeast of the city. Bus No. 29 runs directly from the airport to Laisves Aleja in around 35 minutes for $1. Taxis and Bolt are also available outside arrivals. Ryanair uses Kaunas extensively, making it a convenient flight hub for budget travel around Europe. Vilnius is about 100 km away by bus or train (90 minutes, roughly $8-10).

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Lithuania is a Schengen zone country. Passport holders from the US, Canada, Australia, UK, and most other Western nations can enter visa-free and stay for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Note that the EU's ETIAS pre-authorization system will require visa-exempt visitors to apply online before arrival even for short stays once it starts, and the European Commission has scheduled it for the last quarter of 2026.

For longer stays, Lithuania does not offer a purpose-built digital nomad visa under that name, but it does issue a temporary residence permit that remote workers use effectively. You will need to demonstrate sufficient income (at minimum roughly $1,150/month), valid health insurance, a clean criminal record, and accommodation in Lithuania. Permits are typically issued for one to two years and are renewable.

A separate pathway via the startup visa or self-employment registration also exists if you want to formalize your presence.

Best Time to Visit

The sweet spot for remote work in Kaunas is late April through September. Temperatures are mild to warm (15-25°C / 59-77°F), daylight hours are long (up to 17 hours in June), and the city is at its liveliest with outdoor cafes, riverside walks, and local festivals. May and early June are particularly good: fewer tourists, comfortable temperatures, and greenery at its peak.

July and August are the warmest months and slightly busier, but Kaunas never gets crowded in the way that major tourist cities do. Autumn (October-November) sees temperatures drop quickly and daylight shrink fast, though it stays workable through October. Winter (December-February) is genuinely cold, dark, and grey, with January averages around -3°C (27°F). The city does not shut down in winter, but it is harder to enjoy without good cold-weather gear, and the short daylight hours affect mood for some. If you are sensitive to seasonal affective patterns, plan your visit between May and September.

Pros and Cons

  • Very affordable by EU standards, especially rent and dining
  • Excellent internet infrastructure, fiber widely available
  • Safe city with low violent crime; comfortable to walk at night
  • Compact and walkable center with genuinely interesting interwar architecture
  • Good flight connections via Ryanair for budget travel across Europe
  • Authentic and unhurried compared to Vilnius; less touristy, lower prices
  • EU country, so strong rule of law and infrastructure
  • Dark, cold winters from November through March with minimal sun
  • Small nomad community; meeting other remote workers takes more effort than in hubs like Lisbon or Tbilisi
  • English gaps among older residents and in smaller shops; Lithuanian is hard to learn
  • Limited nightlife variety compared to larger European capitals
  • LGBTQ+ infrastructure is limited; the city is socially conservative by Western European standards
  • Fewer direct flights outside Europe; reaching North America or Asia requires a connection

Who Kaunas Is Best For

Kaunas works particularly well if you want an affordable, genuinely livable EU city without the tourist fatigue of Prague or Lisbon. It suits remote workers who prioritize low cost, fast internet, and a calm working environment over a buzzing social scene. History and architecture enthusiasts will find the interwar modernist streetscape genuinely distinctive. People visiting on a Schengen 90-day window will get excellent value here, especially in summer.

It is also a smart choice if you are testing European living before committing to a more expensive city: the infrastructure is solid, English is workable among the younger population, and flights home via Ryanair keep costs manageable.

Skip Kaunas if you need year-round warmth, a large existing nomad community to plug into immediately, or vibrant nightlife as a core part of your lifestyle. It is also not ideal if you are very sensitive to long, dark winters and plan to stay November through February.

Frequently Asked Questions About Kaunas

How much does it cost to live in Kaunas as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,330 a month in Kaunas for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Kaunas?

Kaunas rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work good. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Kaunas safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Kaunas is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Kaunas a good city for digital nomads overall?

Kaunas stands out for safety and low costs among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,330 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
