Setting Up as a Nomad in Stone Town

The old heart of Zanzibar City, a maze of coral-stone lanes and carved wooden doors where Swahili, Arab, Indian and European histories meet on the Indian Ocean shore. Expect $900 a month here. Culture and nature are the standout strengths, with the size of the nomad scene the main caveat.

Best Neighborhoods in Stone Town

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Shangani

$$$
polished, touristy, breezy

The tourist-friendly waterfront edge of the old town

Shangani sits on Stone Town's western tip, home to boutique hotels, rooftop restaurants, and the best sunset views over the harbor. It is the most polished and walkable part of the old town, with the highest concentration of laptop-tolerant cafes and reliable-ish wifi, though still patchy by global standards.

Best for: short stays, first-time visitors, sunset views
Pros
  • Best sunset and harbor views
  • Most laptop-friendly cafes in the old town
  • Easy walk to Forodhani Gardens night market
Cons
  • Most expensive part of Stone Town
  • Feels less authentic, heavily tourist-oriented

Mkunazini

$$
historic, residential, quiet

The historic heart, near the old slave market and cathedral

Mkunazini is central Stone Town, dense with coral-stone houses, the Anglican Cathedral built on the site of the old slave market, and quiet residential alleys just steps from the busier tourist routes. It offers a more local, lived-in feel with cheaper guesthouses, though wifi and power reliability are weaker here than in Shangani.

Best for: budget guesthouse stays, history and culture focus
Pros
  • Cheaper guesthouses than the waterfront
  • Deep historical significance, walkable museums
  • Genuinely local, less touristy streets
Cons
  • Weaker wifi and more frequent power cuts
  • Easy to get disoriented in the alley maze at night

Forodhani / Malindi waterfront

$$
lively, market-driven, transit hub

The night-market and port district on the northern shorefront

This stretch runs from Forodhani Gardens, famous for its evening street-food market, up to the Malindi area near the old dhow harbor and ferry port. It is lively at night with grilled seafood stalls and local families, and practical by day for ferry connections to Dar es Salaam, though it can feel congested and less private.

Best for: food lovers, short layovers before ferry connections
Pros
  • Best street food in Stone Town at Forodhani market
  • Walking distance to the ferry port for Dar es Salaam
  • Constant social energy in the evenings
Cons
  • Crowded and noisy near the port by day
  • Higher petty-theft risk in the market crowds

Paje

$$
beachy, active, social expat scene

The east-coast kitesurf and coworking beach town

About 45-60 minutes from Stone Town by road, Paje is where most Zanzibar-based nomads actually settle long-term, drawn by kitesurfing, real coworking spaces, beach bars, and a denser expat community than the old town offers. It trades cultural depth for reliable infrastructure and an active, social beach-town lifestyle.

Best for: long-term remote work, kitesurfing, community
Pros
  • Actual coworking spaces and better wifi
  • Strong long-term nomad and kitesurf community
  • Beautiful beach right outside your door
Cons
  • 45-60 minute drive from Stone Town's culture and ferry port
  • Can feel like a bubble, less authentically Zanzibari

Cost of Living in Stone Town

Stone Town is not the cheapest base in East Africa, but it is still affordable by nomad standards, roughly $1,200 to $2,200 a month for a comfortable single-person budget including rent. A one-bedroom apartment in or near the old town runs $400 to $900 a month, more if you want reliable air conditioning and a generator backup for the frequent power cuts. Guesthouse rooms and short-term rentals through local agents are common since long-term listings on the big platforms are thin. Food splits sharply in two: eating like a local at Forodhani Gardens or small Zanzibari cafes (rice, beans, mishkaki, Zanzibar pizza) costs $3 to $6 a meal, while the tourist-oriented rooftop restaurants around Shangani and Kiponda charge $12 to $25 a plate. Imported goods, sunscreen, wine, specialty groceries, are expensive because almost everything arrives by ferry or plane. Budget an extra buffer for bottled water, since tap water is not drinkable.

Where to Work in Stone Town

Stone Town itself has very little dedicated coworking infrastructure. Most remote workers park at hotel rooftop cafes and a handful of laptop-tolerant coffee shops in Shangani and around the old fort, working in short bursts around the heat and the wifi's mood swings. Emerson Spice's rooftop and a few boutique hotel lounges will let you nurse a coffee for a couple of hours if you order enough. The honest picture: this is not Bali or Canggu with rows of coworking desks, it is closer to "find a shaded corner with a plug and a signal and defend it." Many long-stay nomads based on Zanzibar actually work from the east-coast kite beaches (Paje, Jambiani) where a few real coworking spaces have opened, then come into Stone Town for a few days at a time for culture, food, and errands like visa runs or ferry connections. If you need videocalls or large uploads, keep a local SIM with mobile data as backup, since cafe wifi drops out often, especially during power cuts.

Getting Around Stone Town

The old town itself is entirely walkable, in fact cars barely fit through most of its coral-stone alleys, so daily life happens on foot, dodging bicycles and the occasional motorbike. For anywhere else on Unguja island, dala-dalas (shared minibuses) are the cheap, slow, crowded default, while tuk-tuks (bajaji) and metered-ish taxis are the faster, pricier option for direct trips to the airport or the beach towns, always agree the fare before getting in. Renting a scooter or small car is popular for reaching Nungwi, Paje, or Jambiani, roads are rough in places and driving is on the left. To reach the mainland, fast ferries run between Stone Town's port and Dar es Salaam in about two hours, book the reputable operators (Azam Marine is the established name) since safety standards vary and overloaded older boats have had serious incidents in the past. Zanzibar's Abeid Amani Karume International Airport has direct flights from several European hubs plus regional connections via Nairobi, Dar, and Addis Ababa.

Visas for Stone Town

Tanzania (which Zanzibar is part of) offers visa-on-arrival for many nationalities and a straightforward eVisa applied for online in advance through the official Tanzania immigration portal, typically issued as a single-entry 90-day tourist visa for around $50 to $100 depending on nationality, with US citizens paying more. Apply at least a few days ahead of travel and keep the approval PDF and a printed copy, since immigration at Zanzibar's airport and the Stone Town port both check it. Extensions are possible through the immigration office in Zanzibar Town but involve queuing and patience. There is no formal digital nomad visa for Tanzania as of writing, so long-stayers typically do visa runs or extensions to stay legal, and should not rely on remote work being an explicitly sanctioned visa category.

Best Time to Visit Stone Town

The most comfortable stretch is June through October, dry season with lower humidity, sea breezes, and the best diving and beach visibility on the island. December to February is also dry-ish and popular but hotter and more humid. Avoid, or at least plan around, the long rains from March to May, when Stone Town's narrow streets flood, humidity spikes, and many guesthouses close for maintenance, it is genuinely the low point of the year for both weather and infrastructure reliability. The short rains in November are lighter and more manageable, brief afternoon downpours rather than days of steady rain. Year-round, expect heat and humidity that make un-airconditioned upper floors uncomfortable at midday.

Pros and Cons of Stone Town

Pros: a genuinely unique UNESCO old town with carved doors, layered Swahili, Omani, Indian and colonial history, excellent fresh seafood, a real sense of place rather than a manufactured nomad bubble, easy access to world-class beaches and diving within an hour's drive, and a slower pace of life. Cons: wifi and power are inconsistent, with scheduled and unscheduled outages that disrupt calls and uploads, dedicated coworking is scarce, petty theft (phone and bag snatching, especially after dark in quieter alleys) does happen and is worth taking seriously, healthcare for anything beyond minor issues means a flight to Dar es Salaam or further, and the maze-like streets combined with poor lighting make solo night walks something to plan around rather than assume is fine.

Who Stone Town Is For

Stone Town suits nomads who want cultural immersion and history alongside their work, not a plug-and-play coworking scene, people who are comfortable improvising around patchy infrastructure and treat a good local SIM and a power bank as essential kit. It is a strong fit for slow travelers, writers, and anyone building a Zanzibar stay that mixes a few days of old-town culture with longer stretches on the coworking-friendlier east coast beaches. It is a weaker fit for anyone who needs guaranteed fast wifi for daily video calls, wants a large existing remote-work community on their doorstep, or is not willing to dress conservatively (covering shoulders and knees) out of respect for the predominantly Muslim old town.

Frequently Asked Questions About Stone Town

How much does it cost to live in Stone Town as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Stone Town is around $900 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Stone Town?

Stone Town rates 4 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is on the limited side for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Stone Town safe for digital nomads?

Stone Town scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Stone Town a good city for digital nomads overall?

Stone Town stands out for culture and nature and the outdoors among remote workers. With a typical budget near $900 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
