Setting Up as a Nomad in San José

Gateway to eco-adventures in the happiest country on Earth. A tropical climate and strong easy visas make San José an easy base, and at about $2,200 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the food scene in mind.

Cost of Living in San José

San José sits in the mid-range for Latin American capitals. Most essentials are priced in Costa Rican colones (CRC), though landlords and coworking spaces often quote in USD. The exchange rate hovers around 500-520 CRC per US dollar.

A one-bedroom apartment in central areas like Barrio Escalante or Los Yoses runs 900,000-700,000 CRC ($1,000-1,400) per month. In cheaper but still livable neighborhoods such as San Pedro or Tibás, expect to pay 250,000-400,000 CRC ($500-800). Most rentals aimed at foreigners are furnished.

  • Rent (central, 1BR): $900-1,400/month
  • Rent (outer districts, 1BR): $500-800/month
  • Groceries (local supermarket): $150-250/month
  • Eating out (local sodas, lunch casado): $4-7 per meal; mid-range restaurant $15-25
  • Coworking hot-desk: $100-200/month; $10-20 day pass
  • Local bus: roughly 650 CRC ($1) per ride; Uber averages $3-8 within the city
  • Total realistic range: $1,400-2,400/month depending on lifestyle

San José is notably more expensive than nearby beach towns for accommodation, but food and transport remain affordable if you eat local.

Where to Work

The coworking scene in San José is solid and growing. Barrio Escalante is the hub for nomad-friendly venues, with Gracias Coffee and Cowork standing out as the most popular option: great specialty coffee, reliable fiber internet, private meeting rooms, and a genuinely pleasant atmosphere. Monthly memberships run around $100-150. Republic Workspace in San Pedro offers a more corporate feel with 24/7 access, unlimited coffee, and strong internet across two city locations. Costa Rica Workstation near downtown is the budget option at around $1/hour, set in a colonial building with a pool.

WeWork operates a large location in the Escazú Village commercial complex on the city's western side, which suits those who need a polished corporate environment or drop-in access without a monthly commitment.

Cafe-working is viable in Escalante and Los Yoses. Places like Franco in Escalante welcome laptop workers during off-peak hours and serve excellent coffee. However, in smaller neighborhood sodas, expect spotty or no WiFi.

The real WiFi situation: fiber connections are widely available in central San José and most coworking spaces advertise 100-300 Mbps. Residential fiber from providers like Kolbi or Claro costs around $40-60/month. Power outages happen occasionally, especially during the rainy season, so a backup mobile data SIM (Kolbi or Movistar) is worth having.

Getting Around

San José's street grid is notoriously confusing. Addresses use a landmark-based system rather than street names, so navigation apps are essential. Walking in Escalante, Los Yoses, and Barrio Amón is reasonable, but footpaths are uneven and traffic loud. Beyond those pockets, the city is not particularly walkable.

City buses cover almost every neighborhood for around 650 CRC ($1) per ride, but the network is complex and stops are not always signed clearly. Uber is the practical choice for most trips, reliable and cheap: most crosstown rides cost $3-8. Official red taxis (with TAXI markings) are metered and legitimate; orange taxis operate at the airport only.

Juan Santamaría International Airport (SJO) sits about 18 km northwest of downtown in Alajuela. The drive takes 30-45 minutes without traffic but can stretch to 90 minutes during peak hours. Uber from downtown costs roughly $12-18. Official airport taxis are orange and metered; private shuttle services charge $35-50 per person. There is no direct rail link.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the United States, Canada, the UK, and most EU countries enter Costa Rica visa-free for up to 90 days (US citizens can technically receive up to 180 days at the officer's discretion, but 90 is standard in practice). You will need a valid passport, proof of onward travel, and nominally proof of sufficient funds ($100/month of intended stay).

Costa Rica launched a dedicated Digital Nomad Visa in 2022 that is still active. It requires proving a stable income of at least $3,000/month from foreign sources ($4,000 for those bringing family). The visa grants 1 year of legal residence, renewable once for a second year. You apply online through the official TramiteYa portal, pay a $100 fee, and must provide translated documents and proof of international health insurance. This visa exempts you from local income tax on foreign-earned income.

Border runs to Panama or Nicaragua are technically possible but immigration officers can deny re-entry if they suspect you are living in Costa Rica without proper status. Confirm current rules with the official Costa Rica immigration authority (DGME) before your trip.

Best Time to Visit

San José sits at roughly 1,100 meters elevation in the Central Valley, giving it a mild, spring-like climate year-round. Temperatures generally range from 16-27°C (61-81°F), far cooler than the coast.

The dry season runs December through April: clear mornings, low humidity, no afternoon downpours. This is the easiest period for remote work with fewer power interruptions. It is also peak tourist season with slightly higher prices and more social activity in the city.

The rainy season, May through November, brings afternoon rain most days, typically clearing by early evening. September and October are the wettest months and can see heavier, more disruptive storms. That said, mornings remain sunny and the city is far less affected by rain than coastal regions. Prices drop and the city feels more local during these months.

The best window for nomads who want reliable conditions and active social scenes is January through March. November and early December are a good secondary choice: transitional weather, lower costs, and the city is livelier as the dry season approaches.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Stable democracy, strong rule of law, and no military for over 75 years
  • Good coworking infrastructure concentrated in walkable Escalante and Los Yoses
  • Strong specialty coffee culture and a genuinely interesting restaurant scene
  • Year-round mild temperatures thanks to high-altitude Central Valley location
  • Easy access to beaches, cloud forests, and volcanoes as day or weekend trips
  • Digital nomad visa provides a legitimate long-stay path with tax benefits
  • Solid English proficiency in business districts and tourist-oriented areas

Cons

  • Central San José is noisy, congested, and has persistent petty theft; stay alert in crowded areas
  • More expensive than comparable cities in Central America or Southeast Asia
  • Getting around without a car or Uber is frustrating; the bus system is hard to decode
  • Rainy season power and internet outages can disrupt work schedules
  • The city lacks a compact, walkable downtown in the way that European or Southeast Asian capitals do
  • Air quality in the city center is poor due to bus and vehicle emissions

Who San José Is Best For

San José works well for nomads who want a base in the Americas with a stable, low-drama environment. If you value easy access to nature (beaches within 2 hours, active volcanoes, cloud forests), a genuinely good coffee and food scene, and a city that feels more cosmopolitan than its size suggests, San José delivers. It suits those on mid-range budgets who can spend $1,600-2,200/month comfortably, and remote workers who need reliable coworking infrastructure rather than improvising in beach cafes.

The digital nomad visa makes it a strong option for US and European workers wanting a longer Latin American base without constant border hopping. Costa Rica's political stability and functional public institutions remove a lot of the frustration common in neighboring countries.

Skip San José if your priority is a low cost of living (Southeast Asia or mainland Central America will be significantly cheaper), a beach lifestyle (the coast is better for that), or a compact and walkable city center. It also suits you less if you are noise-sensitive or want a nightlife-heavy scene: San José has decent bars and restaurants but is not a party destination.

Frequently Asked Questions About San José

How much does it cost to live in San José as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in San José is around $2,200 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in San José?

With a WiFi score of 6/10, connectivity in San José is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is San José safe for digital nomads?

San José scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is San José a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, San José is worth a look if you value easy visas and climate. Costs sit near $2,200 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
