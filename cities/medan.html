Cost of Living in Medan

Medan is one of Southeast Asia's most affordable large cities. A comfortable remote-work budget runs roughly IDR 10,000,000 to IDR 16,000,000 per month (about $600 to $1,000 USD), though frugal nomads can manage on less.

  • Rent, central areas (Medan Baru, Polonia, Kesawan): IDR 3,000,000 to IDR 5,500,000/month ($185 to $340) for a furnished 1-bedroom apartment. Serviced apartments with pools run higher, around IDR 6,000,000 to IDR 8,000,000.
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Medan Timur, Tembung, Helvetia): IDR 1,200,000 to IDR 2,500,000/month ($75 to $155). Expect basic furnishings and more local surroundings.
  • Groceries: IDR 800,000 to IDR 1,500,000/month ($50 to $90) if you cook occasionally and shop at local markets like Pasar Petisah.
  • Eating out: A warung meal costs IDR 15,000 to IDR 30,000 ($1 to $2). A full lunch at a mid-range local restaurant runs IDR 50,000 to IDR 80,000 ($3 to $5). You can eat well on IDR 1,500,000 to IDR 2,500,000/month ($90 to $155) if you mix local and occasional Western meals.
  • Coworking hot-desk: IDR 200,000 to IDR 350,000/day ($12 to $22), or IDR 1,390,000 to IDR 1,790,000/month ($85 to $110) at chains like Regus and GoWork.
  • Local transport (Grab/Gojek): IDR 400,000 to IDR 700,000/month ($25 to $43) for typical daily ride-hailing. Barely-used motorbike taxis keep costs low.
  • Total realistic monthly range: IDR 8,000,000 to IDR 16,000,000 ($500 to $1,000 USD). A comfortable life with a coworking membership, decent apartment, and eating out daily sits around IDR 12,000,000 to IDR 14,000,000 ($750 to $875).

Where to Work

Medan has a growing coworking scene anchored by national chains. The cafe-work culture is present but less developed than in Bali or Yogyakarta, and WiFi reliability varies significantly.

Coworking

GoWork at Sinar Mas Land Plaza on Jl. Diponegoro offers a polished hot-desk setup in the CBD, with stable fiber internet and a professional environment. Regus runs two locations: Forum Nine on Jl. Imam Bonjol (9th floor, great views) and Grand Jati Junction. CoHub.id on Jl. T. Amir Hamzah is a local option with a community feel and competitive monthly rates. Day passes at these spaces run IDR 200,000 to IDR 350,000 and monthly memberships from IDR 1,390,000.

Cafe Work Culture

Medan has a strong coffee culture with a growing number of specialty cafes. Miel Coffee Brewers in Petisah Tengah and Titik Koma in Cemara Asri are well-rated and laptop-friendly. Expect seating for a few hours without pressure, but do not count on consistent upload speeds for video calls. Coworking spaces are the safer bet for client calls or deadlines.

WiFi Reality

Average city-wide internet is around 9 Mbps, which is slow by regional standards. Coworking spaces use dedicated fiber lines and are meaningfully faster. Cafe WiFi is fine for browsing and async work, but you will encounter lag during peak hours. A local SIM (Telkomsel or Indosat) with a data plan is a sensible backup, with 4G coverage solid across the city center.

Getting Around

Medan does not have a metro or light rail within the city. The honest summary: you will spend a lot of time in traffic.

Ride-Hailing

Grab and Gojek are the primary way to get around. Both are reliable, cheap, and require no Bahasa Indonesia. A short city trip costs IDR 15,000 to IDR 40,000. Most nomads use GoCar or GrabCar rather than ojek motorbike taxis, though ojeks are faster in congestion.

Walkability

Medan is not a walkable city. Pavements are uneven or missing in many areas, heat and humidity are intense, and distances between areas of interest are large. The historic Kesawan district is the one neighborhood where a short walking loop makes sense.

To and From the Airport

Kualanamu International Airport (KNO) is about 30 km east of the city center. The Airport Rail Link is the best option: trains run regularly, take around 40 minutes, and a single ticket costs IDR 40,000 ($2.50). Grab to the city center costs IDR 150,000 to IDR 250,000 and takes 45 to 70 minutes depending on traffic. Avoid unmetered taxis at the terminal.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most passport holders enter Indonesia without a pre-arranged visa for short stays, or use the Visa on Arrival at Kualanamu Airport for longer ones.

  • Visa-free entry: ASEAN passport holders get 30 days, no extension possible.
  • Visa on Arrival (VoA): Available to citizens of roughly 90 countries at a cost of IDR 500,000 ($30). Valid for 30 days, extendable once for another 30 days at a local immigration office. Extension must now be done in person; online processing is no longer accepted.
  • B211A Tourist Visit Visa (C1): Apply before arrival for an initial 60-day stay, extendable monthly up to 6 months total. This is the standard long-stay option for nomads visiting Sumatra.
  • Digital Nomad Visa (E33G): Introduced in 2024, this allows a 1-year stay (renewable once) for remote workers employed by a foreign company. Requirements include USD 60,000/year income and USD 2,000 in savings. Tax-free on foreign-sourced income. You cannot work for Indonesian clients.

Always confirm current requirements at evisa.imigrasi.go.id or with an immigration agent before travel, as rules update frequently.

Best Time to Visit

Medan sits near the equator and is warm and humid year-round, with afternoon highs consistently around 31 to 33 C (88 to 91 F) and overnight lows rarely below 23 C (74 F). There is no true dry season, but some months are noticeably wetter than others.

The most comfortable window for remote work is February to July. February is statistically the driest month, June brings the most sunshine (roughly 7 hours per day), and humidity is slightly lower. August to December sees heavier rainfall and, critically, forest fire smoke from Sumatra and Kalimantan can push air quality to unhealthy levels from August through October. If air quality matters to you, avoid this window.

There is no tourist high season in Medan the way there is in Bali, so accommodation prices and availability are consistent throughout the year.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Exceptionally low cost of living, well under $900/month for a comfortable life
  • Outstanding food scene: Batak, Malay, Chinese, and Indian cuisines all converge here
  • Gateway to remarkable nature: Lake Toba (2 hours), Bukit Lawang orangutan reserve (3 hours), and Berastagi highlands
  • Coworking infrastructure is solid for a non-Bali Indonesian city
  • Far fewer tourists than Bali, Yogyakarta, or Lombok; life feels authentic
  • Direct international flights to Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, and Penang for easy visa runs

Cons

  • Average internet speeds are slow (around 9 Mbps) outside coworking spaces
  • Heavy traffic with no metro; getting anywhere takes patience
  • Air quality is a real concern from August to October due to peat fire smoke
  • English is limited outside hotels, coworking spaces, and expat-oriented businesses
  • No established digital nomad community; meetups and networking are sparse
  • Nightlife and social scenes are modest by regional standards
  • Heat and humidity are relentless; outdoor exploration requires effort

Who Medan Is Best For

Medan suits nomads who prioritize low cost and authentic cultural immersion over community and creature comforts. If you are happy working from a coworking space, eating at local warungs, and spending weekends exploring Sumatra's interior, you will find Medan rewarding and extremely affordable.

It also works well as a base for longer Indonesia stays: the B211A visa covers up to 6 months, the cost of living leaves room to save, and proximity to Malaysia makes border hops easy.

Skip Medan if you need fast, reliable home or cafe WiFi, a buzzing nomad community, comfortable walkability, or clean air in the second half of the year. Nomads who need daily video calls with clients, or who struggle in humid heat, will find the experience wearing. Bali, Yogyakarta, or Chiang Mai in Thailand are better-suited alternatives for those priorities.

Frequently Asked Questions About Medan

How much does it cost to live in Medan as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $800 a month in Medan for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Medan?

Medan rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Medan safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Medan is 6/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Medan a good city for digital nomads overall?

Medan stands out for low costs and climate among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
