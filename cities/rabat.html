Setting Up as a Nomad in Rabat

Morocco's cultured capital with Atlantic breezes and royal history. For remote workers, Rabat leans on low costs and easy visas, with a typical budget near $1,400 a month. The main trade-off is the language barrier.

Cost of Living in Rabat

Rabat is one of the more affordable capitals on the Mediterranean coast, though it costs more than Morocco's smaller cities. As a rough monthly budget for a solo remote worker:

  • Rent, central neighborhoods (Agdal, Hassan): 4,500-6,000 MAD (~$450-600 USD) for a furnished one-bedroom
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Youssoufia, Akkari, outer Salé): 2,000-3,500 MAD (~$200-350 USD)
  • Groceries: 1,000-1,500 MAD (~$100-150 USD) cooking mostly at home
  • Eating out: 30-70 MAD ($3-7 USD) at a local snack or cafe; 150-300 MAD ($15-30 USD) at a mid-range restaurant
  • Coworking hot-desk: 800-1,500 MAD (~$80-150 USD) per month
  • Local transport (tram, bus, petit taxi): 300-600 MAD (~$30-60 USD) depending on how much you walk
  • Total realistic range: 8,000-13,000 MAD (~$800-1,300 USD) per month, all in

A local SIM with data (Maroc Telecom or Orange) costs roughly 100-200 MAD per month for a 20-30 GB plan. Utilities including electricity and water typically add 300-500 MAD to rent in summer and more in winter if you use electric heating.

Where to Work

Rabat has a functional but not sprawling coworking scene. The best-known dedicated spaces include LA STATION in the Ocean neighborhood, which has fast fiber, a terrace, and round-the-clock access. Coworkinn in Agdal offers a quieter professional atmosphere and meeting rooms. Regus has multiple Rabat locations including Agdal and Hay Riad for more corporate needs.

Cafe work is viable but requires some trial and error. Connect Cafe in Agdal is consistently recommended for reliable WiFi and a comfortable setup. Edmonton Coffee House in the Yacoub El Mansour district is a specialty coffee shop with solid connectivity and a reputation among remote workers. Starbucks locations have decent WiFi but can get crowded. Many traditional Moroccan cafes do not have WiFi or will not welcome laptop workers for extended sessions.

Average fixed broadband speeds in Rabat sit around 40-50 Mbps, which is enough for calls and most remote work tasks. Mobile data on 4G is a reliable backup. In dedicated coworking spaces, speeds are generally fast and stable. In cafes, quality varies considerably, so test before committing to a long work session.

Getting Around

Rabat has a clean, air-conditioned tram system (Tramway de Rabat-Sale) that links central Rabat with the neighboring city of Sale across the Bou Regreg river. A single tram journey costs around 6 MAD. Petit taxis are small blue metered cars that cover short trips within city limits; the meter starts at roughly 7 MAD and most central rides cost 15-30 MAD. Grand taxis handle longer routes or trips to the suburbs.

The city center, Agdal, Hassan, and the Medina are all walkable in good conditions. The terrain is mostly flat, and the corniche along the river is pleasant on foot. Cycling infrastructure is limited but improving.

Rabat-Sale Airport (RBA) is about 8 km from central Rabat. An express shuttle bus connects the airport to the train stations and city center. A grand taxi from the airport to central Rabat costs roughly 80-120 MAD. Train connections link Rabat to Casablanca in about 45 minutes, which opens up the larger international hub at Mohammed V Airport if needed.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the US, UK, EU member states, Canada, and Australia can enter Morocco visa-free and stay up to 90 days. Most nationalities get their passport stamped on arrival with no prior arrangements needed.

Morocco launched a pilot remote-work visa program in 2025, which was extended into 2026. Requirements reported include proof of remote income of at least 2,000-2,500 EUR per month and valid health insurance. The program allows stays of up to 12 months. However, the program's official status and application process remained unclear at time of writing, and it is not yet available through a standard public channel. Always verify current requirements with the official Moroccan consulate or the Direction Generale de la Surete Nationale before planning a long stay.

For shorter stays, most nomads use the standard 90-day entry and then exit to Spain or Portugal before re-entering. Be aware that staying more than 183 days in a calendar year may trigger Moroccan tax residency rules.

Best Time to Visit

Rabat sits on the Atlantic coast, which keeps summers cooler than inland Morocco. July and August temperatures average 24-28 degrees Celsius (75-82 F) with almost no rain and sea breezes. This is comfortable compared to Marrakech or Fez, though the city becomes quieter as locals vacation.

The best months for remote work are March through June and September through November. Spring brings mild temperatures between 18-24 degrees Celsius, minimal rain after April, and a lively city atmosphere. Autumn is equally pleasant and less crowded with tourists. Winter (December through February) is the rainy season with temperatures dropping to 10-16 degrees Celsius in the evening, though it rarely gets cold enough to be disruptive. WiFi cafes and coworking spaces are busier and more useful in winter as outdoor work is less appealing.

Pros and Cons

  • Pros:
  • Among the safest cities in Morocco, with a visible police presence and low violent crime
  • Lower cost of living than Casablanca with a more relaxed pace
  • Atlantic coast location moderates summer heat
  • Good intercity train links to Casablanca and Fez
  • Rich history: Kasbah of the Udayas, Hassan Tower, Chellah, UNESCO-listed Medina
  • Functioning tram and public transport network
  • University city with a young, educated local population
  • Cons:
  • Smaller and quieter than Casablanca; nightlife and social scenes are limited
  • Coworking scene is modest compared to major European capitals
  • Cafe WiFi is inconsistent and requires testing before settling in
  • Visa situation for long stays lacks a clear, established process
  • English is less widely spoken than in Marrakech; French and Darija are essential
  • Harassment in the Medina and tourist areas, particularly for women traveling solo
  • Limited direct flight options compared to Casablanca

Who Rabat Is Best For

Rabat suits nomads who want a calm, genuine Moroccan city without the tourist saturation of Marrakech. If you value safety, walkable neighborhoods, and a lower cost of living over a buzzing social scene, Rabat delivers well. It works especially well for people who speak some French, since day-to-day life becomes much easier with it. The city is a good fit for solo professionals on a medium-to-long stay who want to actually live in a neighborhood rather than pass through a tourist zone.

Skip Rabat if you need a large, active nomad community to plug into quickly, or if you are looking for consistent nightlife and a packed social calendar. It is also not the right base if you want major international flight connections, since Casablanca's Mohammed V Airport is much better served. Anyone who finds slow-paced, administrative cities dull will likely get restless here within a few weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions About Rabat

How much does it cost to live in Rabat as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Rabat is around $1,400 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Rabat?

With a WiFi score of 5/10, connectivity in Rabat is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Rabat safe for digital nomads?

Rabat scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Rabat a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Rabat is worth a look if you value low costs and easy visas. Costs sit near $1,400 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
