Setting Up as a Nomad in Belgrade

Experience legendary nightlife and Balkan hospitality at unbeatable prices. A continental climate and strong nightlife make Belgrade an easy base, and at about $1,700 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep air quality in mind.

Belgrade is one of the most affordable European capitals for digital nomads. Your biggest variable is rent. In central neighborhoods like Stari Grad and Vracar, expect to pay 60,000-80,000 RSD ($550-730 USD) per month for a one-bedroom apartment. In slightly further areas like Dorcol or Zemun you can find similar quality for 40,000-60,000 RSD ($370-550 USD). Utilities add roughly 10,000-16,000 RSD ($90-150 USD) on top.

Groceries for one person run about 25,000-30,000 RSD ($230-275 USD) per month shopping at local markets and supermarkets. Eating out is genuinely cheap: a full meal at a local kafana costs 1,200-2,000 RSD ($11-18 USD) including drinks. A coworking hot desk runs 10,000-20,000 RSD ($90-185 USD) monthly at most spaces. Public transport in Belgrade became free for all passengers in January 2025, covering all buses, trams, and trolleybuses. Mobile data plans start around 1,200 RSD ($11 USD) for 10GB.

A realistic total monthly budget for comfortable solo nomad life: 130,000-175,000 RSD ($1,200-1,600 USD). You can stretch it down to $900 with discipline or up to $2,000+ if you eat out often and want a premium central apartment.

Where to Work

Belgrade has a solid and growing coworking infrastructure. Nova Iskra Workspace operates locations in Savamala (the creative neighborhood hub) and Dorcol, with fiber-optic WiFi and a strong community of freelancers and startup founders. ICT Hub at Kralja Milana 10 offers 24/7 access and a 1 Gbps connection, making it the top pick if speed is your priority. Anticafe Black Cat (Brace Baruh 6) runs a pay-per-hour model with free coffee and snacks, ideal for short or irregular work sessions.

Cafe-working culture is well-established. Kafeterija Magazin 1907 (Kralja Petra 16, Stari Grad) is housed in a stunning 1907 Art Nouveau building with multiple floors, ample power sockets, and good WiFi. It is explicitly laptop-friendly and heavily used by remote workers. Coffee, Tea and Sympathy in Dorcol and Red Bread in the center are both comfortable, smoke-free, and work-tolerant.

WiFi in coworking spaces is reliably fast. Cafe WiFi is generally usable for calls and regular tasks, though speeds vary. If you are staying in an apartment, confirm WiFi quality before booking as some older buildings still have slow connections. A local SIM from Telenor or SBB with a data plan is a sensible backup.

Getting Around

Belgrade has no metro. The network of buses, trams, and trolleybuses covers the city thoroughly, and since January 2025 all of it is completely free for everyone, including tourists. You simply board and ride. Google Maps handles Belgrade routing reliably.

The city is walkable in the central districts. Stari Grad, Vracar, and Dorcol are all comfortable to cover on foot, and the riverfront Savamala area is easy to walk. Taxis and Bolt (the dominant rideshare app) are inexpensive for longer trips or late nights.

Getting to and from Nikola Tesla Airport: the A1 minibus from Slavija Square costs 400 RSD (under $4 USD) and takes about 30 minutes, running every 15 minutes during the day. Bus 72 is a free option, running to Zeleni Venac in about 45 minutes depending on traffic. A taxi or Bolt to the center costs roughly 2,500-3,500 RSD ($23-32 USD).

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Serbia is not in the EU and has generous visa-free entry for most Western passport holders. Citizens of the US, UK, EU countries, Canada, and Australia can enter without a visa and stay for up to 90 days within a 180-day period. Some nationalities, including the US, are technically limited to 30 or 60 days on certain interpretations; in practice most travelers stay up to 90 days without issue, but check the official Serbian Ministry of Foreign Affairs website for the current rule for your specific passport before you travel.

Serbia does not have a designated digital nomad visa as of mid-2026. The formal long-stay option is a Temporary Residence Permit based on self-employment or company formation, which can be granted for up to three years and requires a minimum confirmed income of around 3,500 EUR per month, proof of accommodation, medical insurance, and registration at the local police station within 24 hours of arrival. Processing takes roughly 30 days. If you stay 183 or more days in a calendar year, Serbian tax residency rules apply. Confirm all current requirements with official sources before applying.

Best Time to Visit

Belgrade has a proper continental climate with hot summers and cold, damp winters. The sweet spots for remote work are April to early June and September to October. Temperatures sit in the 18-26C (65-79F) range, there is less rain than spring, and the city has an outdoor energy that makes it genuinely pleasant to be in.

July and August get intense, regularly hitting 32-35C (90-95F) with high humidity. Air conditioning is common in newer apartments but not universal in older buildings. Winter (December to February) is cold and grey, and Belgrade's air quality deteriorates significantly due to coal heating, wood burning, and traffic, with PM2.5 concentrations often well above WHO guidelines. If air quality matters to you, avoid November through February.

For the most balanced combination of good weather, manageable crowds, reasonable flight prices, and a lively local scene, late September and early October stand out as the best single window.

Pros and Cons

  • Very low cost of living relative to Western Europe, with strong infrastructure
  • Free public transport citywide since January 2025
  • Excellent nightlife and food scene, especially in Savamala and Skadarlija
  • 90-day visa-free stays for most Western passports, no bureaucracy on arrival
  • English widely spoken by younger Serbians, and the expat community is active
  • Growing coworking and startup scene with good internet speeds at dedicated spaces
  • Serious air quality problems in winter, regularly ranking among the worst in Europe
  • No metro, and surface transport can be slow during rush hour
  • No dedicated digital nomad visa: long stays require navigating the residence permit process
  • Older apartments can have unreliable WiFi and variable heating quality
  • Summers are genuinely hot and can make focusing difficult without reliable AC
  • Limited direct flight connections compared to larger European hubs, though this is improving

Who Belgrade Is Best For

Belgrade suits nomads who want a European city experience at a budget that actually makes sense. If you are coming from Western Europe or North America and find Lisbon or Barcelona too expensive for long stays, Belgrade delivers comparable energy at roughly half the cost. The city rewards people who lean into the local scene: kafanas, river clubs, Skadarlija, and the genuine bohemian feel of the older neighborhoods are part of the appeal.

It is a particularly good fit for freelancers in tech, design, or creative fields who will benefit from the growing startup community and coworking culture. The nightlife is genuinely world-class if that matters to you.

Skip Belgrade if air quality is a health concern for you, especially for winter stays. It is also a harder sell if you need seamless bureaucratic support for long-term residency, since the permit process requires legal help and meeting relatively high income thresholds. Travelers who prioritize outdoor nature activities year-round will also find the city limiting compared to coastal or mountain alternatives in the region.

Frequently Asked Questions About Belgrade

How much does it cost to live in Belgrade as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Belgrade is around $1,700 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Belgrade?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Belgrade is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Belgrade safe for digital nomads?

Belgrade scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Belgrade a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Belgrade is worth a look if you value nightlife and easy visas. Costs sit near $1,700 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
