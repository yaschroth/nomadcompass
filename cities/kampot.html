Setting Up as a Nomad in Kampot

Experience pepper farms and river sunsets, Cambodia's most charming small town. Kampot pairs low costs with nature and a tropical climate, all for about $1,100 a month. If anything, cleanliness lags.

Cost of Living in Kampot

Kampot is one of Southeast Asia's more affordable bases. A comfortable single-person monthly budget typically lands between $600 and $900 USD, with budget-conscious nomads getting by closer to $500.

  • Rent (central, riverside area): $250-$400 USD for a private bungalow or one-bedroom apartment
  • Rent (quieter outskirts): $150-$250 USD for basic but comfortable rooms or studios
  • Groceries: $80-$150 USD per month shopping at local markets (Phsar Kramat) and small supermarkets; fresh produce, eggs, and local staples cost a fraction of Western prices
  • Eating out: $2-$5 USD per meal at local Khmer restaurants; $6-$12 at Western-style cafes and restaurants; budget $150-$300 USD monthly depending on how often you cook
  • Coworking hot-desk: $5-$6 USD per half-day, $8-$10 USD per full day at spaces like Karma Traders or Safe and Sound; some cafes are essentially free if you buy a coffee
  • Local transport: Motorbike rental runs $5/day or $80-$120/month; tuk-tuks for short trips cost 4,000-8,000 KHR ($1-$2 USD); PassApp ride-hailing is available
  • SIM/data: ~$5 USD per week for 4G with Cellcard or Smart

Realistic monthly total: $600-$900 USD for a comfortable lifestyle with coworking access, regular restaurant meals, and occasional day trips.

Where to Work

Kampot's remote work infrastructure is small but functional. It suits those who prefer a quiet, low-distraction environment over a buzzing coworking scene.

Coworking Spaces

Karma Traders is the most-mentioned hub among nomads, offering 50 Mbps fiber WiFi, ergonomic seating, and private booths for $5/day or $80/month. It also hosts weekly digital nomad meetups on Friday mornings. Safe and Sound in Sovann Sakor village offers a formal office setup with a conference room, printer, free coffee, and balcony for $6 per half-day, appealing to those who need a quiet, professional setting.

Cafe Work Culture

Several cafes welcome laptop workers. Epic Arts Cafe near the old market is a long-standing favorite with reliable WiFi and a social, inclusive atmosphere. Simple Things doubles as a vegetarian cafe and casual workspace. Rikitikitavi on the riverfront has a breezy terrace and decent connectivity, though it gets busier in the evenings.

The Real WiFi Situation

Most coworking spaces and established cafes offer 30-50 Mbps, enough for video calls and standard remote work. The honest caveat: Kampot still experiences occasional outages during heavy rain, and power cuts happen a few times a month. Keep a local SIM card with 4G as a backup, and avoid scheduling critical video calls during afternoon thunderstorms in the wet season.

Getting Around

Kampot's town center is compact enough to walk in 20 minutes, but renting a motorbike or bicycle opens up the surrounding countryside, pepper farms, and nearby coast.

  • Motorbike: The default choice for most nomads. Daily rentals are $5 USD; monthly is $80-$120. You do not need a Cambodian license in practice, though you technically should have an international permit.
  • Bicycle: Many guesthouses rent bicycles for $2-$3/day; fine for central errands but limited for longer distances in heat.
  • Tuk-tuk and PassApp: Good for short trips or when you do not want to ride. PassApp (a Cambodian ride-hailing app) gives transparent pricing and is generally more reliable than flagging down a driver.
  • Getting to/from the airport: Kampot has no commercial airport. The nearest is Phnom Penh International Airport (PNH), about 140 km away. Options include a private taxi transfer (2.5-3 hours, $60-$80 USD) or a bus/minivan combination (4-5 hours, $8-$20 USD). Book the bus leg in advance via Giant Ibis or BookMeBus. Note: since June 2025, the Thailand land border is closed; regional travel between Cambodia and Thailand must be by air.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Cambodia is accessible for most Western passport holders with straightforward entry options, though the rules shifted meaningfully in late 2025 and 2026.

Tourist visa (T-class): Available on arrival or as an e-visa via evisa.gov.kh for around $36 USD, valid for 30 days single entry. Starting January 2025, all air arrivals must complete an e-Arrival Card within seven days before landing. A one-time extension of 30 days is possible through a local travel agent or immigration office.

Ordinary visa (E-class): A better option for longer stays. Obtain it on arrival for $35 USD, then extend it into a 1-year business (EB) visa through a local agent for roughly $290-$300 USD per year. Many nomads use this route: the EB visa has no minimum income requirement, no mandatory health insurance, and no age restriction.

No digital nomad visa: Cambodia does not currently offer a specific digital nomad visa. The EB route is the closest practical equivalent for remote workers who want to stay a year or more.

Important: Visa policies in Cambodia can change quickly. Always verify current requirements with the official Cambodia e-visa portal (evisa.gov.kh) or your nearest Cambodian embassy before traveling.

Best Time to Visit

Kampot sits in southern Cambodia near the coast, making its seasons a little more extreme than the interior, especially for humidity.

November to February is the sweet spot: dry, relatively cool (25-30C), low humidity, and clear skies. December and January are the most comfortable months overall and the easiest time to maintain a productive work routine without air conditioning running constantly.

March and April are hot and dry, topping out around 33C with humidity building. Manageable with AC, but evenings outdoors can be sticky.

May to October is the wet season. Rainfall peaks in August, when Kampot can see 400+ mm in a single month. Afternoons typically bring short, heavy downpours rather than all-day rain. The landscape is lush and guesthouses are cheaper, but power outages and brief internet drops are more common. If you are price-sensitive and do not mind the humidity, October is a decent compromise: the rains begin to ease and the countryside is still vivid green.

For remote work specifically, November to February offers the most reliable conditions: stable power, fewer storm-related outages, and a livelier expat community.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Genuinely low cost of living: comfortable life for $600-$800/month is realistic
  • Slow pace and low stress, easy to focus outside of work hours
  • Strong, welcoming expat and nomad community, especially in high season
  • Beautiful natural setting: the Teuk Chhou River, Bokor National Park, nearby Kep and the coast
  • Excellent and varied food scene for a small town
  • Friendly locals; basic English widely spoken in businesses and guesthouses
  • Long-stay visa options are simple and affordable with no income minimum

Cons

  • Coworking infrastructure is thin; the scene is growing but still limited compared to Chiang Mai or Bali
  • Internet outages and power cuts are a real issue during the wet season
  • No commercial airport: every arrival and departure requires a 3-4 hour journey from Phnom Penh
  • Heat and humidity for six months of the year can affect focus and energy without consistent AC
  • Petty theft (bag snatching, motorbike theft) occurs; the town is relaxed but not risk-free
  • Limited professional networking; better suited to established remote workers than those actively growing a local client base
  • The land border with Thailand has been closed since June 2025, limiting visa run options

Who Kampot Is Best For

Kampot works well for established remote workers who want a very low cost of living, a quieter pace, and genuine Southeast Asian character without the overdevelopment that has hit Sihanoukville. It is a good fit if you value natural surroundings, enjoy a tight-knit expat community, and have flexible hours that let you work around afternoon weather in the wet season.

It suits long-stay nomads (one month or more) better than short-term visitors: the town rewards settling in, building a routine, and discovering the surrounding countryside at a slow pace.

Consider skipping Kampot if you need fast, guaranteed-reliable internet for heavy workloads, or if you rely on being in a large tech or startup ecosystem for networking. It is also not ideal if you want the variety of a major city or need quick airport access for frequent regional flights. Those prioritizing professional development or a vibrant, diverse coworking culture are likely better served by Phnom Penh, Chiang Mai, or Bali.

Frequently Asked Questions About Kampot

How much does it cost to live in Kampot as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Kampot is around $1,100 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Kampot?

With a WiFi score of 4/10, connectivity in Kampot is patchy for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Kampot safe for digital nomads?

Kampot scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Kampot a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Kampot is worth a look if you value low costs and nature. Costs sit near $1,100 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
