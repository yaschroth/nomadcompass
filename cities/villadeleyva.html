Setting Up as a Nomad in Villa de Leyva

A whitewashed colonial town in the Boyaca highlands built around one of Latin America's largest cobbled plazas. Villa de Leyva pairs climate with culture, all for about $1,000 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Cost of Living in Villa de Leyva

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Villa de Leyva, so the $1,000 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 921 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Villa de Leyva is an affordable base by Western standards, though it sits a little above sleepy rural Colombia because it is a popular tourist town. A comfortable single nomad can expect to spend roughly $900 to $1,200 a month once rent, food, and small comforts are included. Rents rise around weekends and holiday peaks, when Bogota visitors pour in and short-term rates climb. Cooking with produce from the Saturday market keeps food costs low, while restaurants around Plaza Mayor charge tourist prices. These figures are editorial estimates, not fixed quotes, and your total will swing with rental choice and how often you eat out.

  • Monthly budget: roughly $900 to $1,200 for a single nomad (editorial estimate).
  • Rent: your largest cost, and noticeably higher on weekends and holiday peaks.
  • Food: cheap if you shop the Saturday market and cook; pricier around the square.
  • Extras: day trips, wine tastings, and taxis to fossil sites add up over a month.

Where to Work in Villa de Leyva

This is a small colonial town, not a coworking hub, so set expectations accordingly. There is no dedicated coworking space of note, and most remote workers rely on cafes around and just off the Plaza Mayor, plus their own rental. Several cafes serve good Boyaca coffee and will happily let you linger, though seating and plug access vary and weekends get crowded with day trippers. For focused work, a rental with a solid connection is your safest bet. Consider a mobile data plan with tethering as a backup, since public Wi-Fi can wobble. Mornings are quietest; by afternoon the square fills with visitors and noise.

Getting Around Villa de Leyva

The historic core is compact and made for walking, so most days you will not need transport at all. Be warned that the streets and the vast Plaza Mayor are laid with rough cobblestones, which are hard on ankles, wheeled cases, and anyone with mobility needs; flat, sturdy shoes are essential. For outlying sights like El Fosil, the Pozos Azules ponds, the vineyards, and Raquira, you can hire a taxi, join a tour, or rent a bicycle or scooter. Intercity buses connect Villa de Leyva to Tunja and onward to Bogota, roughly 2.5 to 3 hours away. There is no airport in town, so most arrivals come overland.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Villa de Leyva

Villa de Leyva follows Colombia's national immigration rules, so there is nothing town-specific to arrange. Many visitors arrive visa-free as tourists for short stays, which suits a trial visit. For a longer base, Colombia offers a digital nomad visa, the Visa V, aimed at remote workers earning from foreign clients or employers; it typically asks for proof of income and health cover, and is handled online through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Rules, thresholds, and processing times change, so confirm current requirements with an official source or a reputable immigration adviser before you commit. Give yourself buffer time, as small-town logistics can slow paperwork.

Best Time to Visit Villa de Leyva

Villa de Leyva enjoys a dry, sunny highland climate thanks to its 2,140m altitude and semi-desert setting, so it is pleasant much of the year. Days are mild to warm and nights cool sharply, so pack layers whatever the season. The drier spells around December to March and July to August bring the clearest skies and are lovely for hiking and fossil hunting. Remember that this is Bogota's favourite weekend escape, so the town swells on weekends, public holidays, and during festivals like the Festival of Lights and the kite festival. For calm and cheaper rates, come midweek and outside the main holiday windows.

Pros and Cons of Villa de Leyva

Villa de Leyva rewards those who want beauty, calm, and clean mountain air over big-city convenience. The whitewashed colonial streets, the huge Plaza Mayor, and the surrounding fossil sites, vineyards, and desert trails make it genuinely special. The trade-offs are practical: it is small, quiet, and weekend-busy, with limited English, a thin remote-work scene, and cobblestones underfoot everywhere. Weigh what you value before settling in for a longer stay.

  • Pros: stunning preserved colonial setting; dry sunny climate and clean air; safe, relaxed pace; superb nature and day trips nearby.
  • Cons: tiny community and no real coworking; limited English; crowded and pricier on weekends; punishing cobblestones and no local airport.

Who Villa de Leyva Is For

Villa de Leyva suits the independent, self-sufficient nomad who craves scenery, quiet, and a slower rhythm rather than a buzzing scene. It is ideal if you can work reliably from your own rental, enjoy hiking, history, and stargazing, and do not need nightlife or a ready-made expat crowd to feel at home. A little Spanish goes a long way, since English is limited. It is less suited to those who depend on a lively community, dependable coworking, or constant weekend peace, given the tourist influx. Come here to write, recharge, and explore Boyaca; look elsewhere if you want a dense, social hub.
