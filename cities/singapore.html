What Singapore Offers Digital Nomads

The garden city, efficient, clean, and Asia's business hub. A tropical climate and strong fast WiFi make Singapore an easy base, and at about $4,500 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep cost in mind.

Cost of Living in Singapore

Be honest with yourself before you book: Singapore is one of the most expensive cities in Asia, and housing is what makes it hurt. Prices below are in Singapore dollars (SGD) with rough US dollar conversions at about 1 SGD to 0.77 USD.

Monthly breakdown for one person

  • Rent, central condo (one bedroom): S$2,500 to S$4,000 (about $1,925 to $3,080). Prime areas near the CBD, Orchard, or Marina Bay sit at the top of that range.
  • Rent, cheaper area or HDB: A whole government HDB flat runs roughly S$1,800 to S$2,800 (about $1,385 to $2,155). A single room in an HDB flat is far cheaper, often S$600 to S$1,000 (about $460 to $770), and co-living rooms are a common middle option.
  • Groceries: S$120 to S$300 (about $90 to $230), buying at FairPrice, Sheng Siong, or the pricier Cold Storage.
  • Eating out: This is where Singapore stays livable. A full meal at a hawker centre costs S$5 to S$8 (about $4 to $6), so eating out three times a day can still land near S$500 (about $385) a month. Restaurant meals jump to S$20 to S$100 per person.
  • Coworking hot desk: A monthly membership runs roughly S$265 to S$400 (about $205 to $310); day passes are S$30 to S$70.
  • Local transport: S$100 to S$150 (about $77 to $115), since individual MRT and bus fares are cheap.

Realistic total: expect S$3,000 to S$5,500 (about $2,300 to $4,235) a month living alone in your own place, or closer to S$1,800 to S$2,800 renting a room and eating at hawker centres. There is no way around it: your rent line dominates everything.

Where to Work

Singapore has a dense, high-quality coworking scene concentrated in the CBD, Raffles Place, and Marina Bay. JustCo has many locations (Raffles Place, Shenton Way, One-North, Tampines) with hot desk plans from around S$265 a month. WeWork runs several CBD sites with memberships from roughly S$399. The Great Room is the premium, hotel-lobby-style option with day passes around S$70. If you want to pay per visit rather than commit, apps like Deskimo let you book hot desks on demand across the island.

Cafe and mall work culture is strong but competitive. Independent cafes and food courts fill up fast, and some cafes discourage laptop campers during peak hours. Malls and public spaces like libraries are reliable fallbacks. WiFi is genuinely excellent almost everywhere, and mobile data is fast and cheap, so a prepaid SIM as backup means you are rarely stuck.

Getting Around

Singapore has one of the best public transport systems anywhere. The MRT (metro) plus buses cover the whole island, run frequently, and are clean and air-conditioned, which matters in this heat. Tap in with an EZ-Link card or use SimplyGo to tap a contactless bank card or phone directly. Note that foreign cards on SimplyGo can carry a small daily admin fee, so a local EZ-Link card is often simpler for a longer stay.

Individual fares are low, usually a couple of dollars per trip. The core city is walkable and heavily connected by covered walkways and mall passages, though you will still sweat between stops. Grab (the local ride-hailing app) is easy and reasonable for late nights.

Getting to and from the airport is painless. Changi (SIN) connects directly to the MRT, and the ride to the city takes roughly 30 to 40 minutes for around S$2. Grab or a taxi runs more but drops you at the door.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Many passports enter Singapore visa-free, but the length depends on your nationality. Holders from the UK, US, Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, and South Korea typically get 90 days on arrival, while some others (India, for example) get 30 days. That visa-free entry is for tourism and business visits, not for working locally.

Singapore has no dedicated digital nomad visa. There is no long-stay category aimed at remote workers on foreign income. Staying long-term legally means qualifying for a work pass (such as an Employment Pass, which is employer-sponsored and salary-gated) or another specific pass, none of which are casual to obtain. Because entry rules and pass criteria change and vary by nationality, confirm your exact eligibility with Singapore's Immigration and Checkpoints Authority (ICA) before you travel.

Best Time to Visit

Singapore is hot, humid, and rainy all year. It sits almost on the equator, so temperatures barely move: highs around 30 to 32 degrees Celsius (86 to 90 Fahrenheit) every month, with heavy humidity and sudden thunderstorms that usually pass quickly.

The most comfortable window is roughly February to April, the drier stretch with slightly lighter rainfall. July is also relatively dry. The wettest period is the northeast monsoon from November to January, when December in particular sees frequent, longer downpours. None of this stops remote work, since you will spend most of your day in air conditioning anyway, but the drier months make evenings and weekends outdoors far more pleasant.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Excellent, fast WiFi and a deep supply of high-quality coworking spaces.
  • World-class public transport that makes a car pointless.
  • Very safe, clean, and efficient, with English widely spoken.
  • Cheap, outstanding hawker food that keeps your daily eating costs low.
  • Easy hub for onward travel across Southeast Asia.

Cons

  • Very high cost of living, driven mainly by expensive rent.
  • No digital nomad visa and no easy long-term route on foreign income.
  • Constant heat and humidity that can be draining.
  • Small city-state, so you can feel you have seen it quickly.
  • Strict rules and fines, with less of the loose, cheap backpacker vibe found elsewhere in the region.

Who Singapore Is Best For

Singapore suits remote workers with a solid budget who value reliability over savings: fast infrastructure, safety, cleanliness, and easy logistics matter more to them than stretching money. It is a strong pick if you want a comfortable, low-friction base for a few weeks, a launchpad for exploring the region, or a professional environment where everything simply works.

Skip it if you are optimizing for cheap long-term living, since your rent will dwarf what the same money buys in Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, or Bali. It is also a poor fit if you need a formal long-stay visa for remote work, or if you want a large, sprawling city with months of new things to discover.

Frequently Asked Questions About Singapore

How much does it cost to live in Singapore as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Singapore is around $4,500 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Singapore?

With a WiFi score of 10/10, connectivity in Singapore is excellent for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Singapore safe for digital nomads?

Singapore scores 10 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Singapore a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Singapore is worth a look if you value fast WiFi and safety. Costs sit near $4,500 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
