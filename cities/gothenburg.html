Gothenburg at a Glance for Remote Workers

Sweden's friendly west coast, seafood, archipelago, and laid-back vibes. Plan on roughly $3,800 a month. Its strengths are English and cleanliness, while cost is where it scores lower.

Cost of Living in Gothenburg

Gothenburg is one of the more expensive cities in Europe for remote workers. Expect to spend more here than in most Southern or Eastern European destinations, though it remains cheaper than Stockholm or major Nordic capitals.

  • Rent (central, e.g. Vasastan or Centrum): 12,000-15,000 SEK/month (~1,100-1,400 USD) for a 1-bedroom
  • Rent (cheaper areas, e.g. Majorna or Hisingen): 9,000-12,000 SEK/month (~840-1,100 USD)
  • Groceries: 3,000-4,500 SEK/month (~280-420 USD) shopping at ICA or Lidl
  • Eating out (casual lunch): 130-180 SEK (~12-17 USD) per meal; dinner at a mid-range restaurant 250-400 SEK (~23-37 USD) per person
  • Coworking hot desk: 2,500-3,500 SEK/month (~230-325 USD); drop-in day passes start around 250-350 SEK
  • Local transport (Vastrafik monthly pass): ~855 SEK/month (~80 USD) covering all trams, buses and ferries in the city
  • Total realistic monthly range: 17,000-28,000 SEK/month (~1,580-2,600 USD) depending on accommodation and lifestyle

The biggest cost driver is accommodation. The Swedish kronor (SEK) trades at roughly 10.7 SEK to 1 USD as of mid-2026. Budget travelers sharing apartments can trim costs significantly, but solo renters on the open market face tight supply and high prices.

Where to Work

Gothenburg has a solid but not overwhelming coworking scene, anchored by a tech and startup culture tied to Chalmers University and Volvo.

  • Convendum Kungsportsavenyn: A well-equipped multi-floor space on Avenyn with 24/7 access, fast wired and wireless internet, and a professional atmosphere. Good if you need a reliable daily base.
  • Flora Workspace (Gronsakstorget): A casual drop-in workspace attached to Hotel Flora in the city center. Comfortable and flexible with day-pass options.
  • G Lab21: A startup-focused penthouse coworking space in the city center. Energetic and community-oriented, better suited for founders and freelancers in tech than for pure heads-down focus work.

Cafe work is common and generally accepted. Viktors Kaffe near Avenyn is a standby for laptop workers with reliable wifi and a welcoming vibe. Brogyllen on Vastra Hamngatan is a classic choice if you prefer a quieter, more traditional setting. Expect to buy something every hour or two as a courtesy norm.

WiFi is broadly reliable in coworking spaces and most cafes. Speed at cafes varies but is typically adequate for video calls. If you need guaranteed high bandwidth, a coworking membership is the safer bet.

Getting Around

Gothenburg is very well served by public transit. The tram network is the backbone of getting around and covers most of the central city efficiently. Buses and a few ferry lines fill in the gaps.

A monthly Vastrafik pass costs around 855 SEK (~80 USD) and covers trams, buses, and ferries within the city zones. Single tickets cost 38 SEK (~3.50 USD) and are valid for 90 minutes. The To Go app handles ticketing on your phone.

The city center is walkable if you are based centrally. Many neighborhoods like Linnestaden, Vasastan, and Haga are comfortable on foot. Cycling is popular and there are dedicated bike lanes, with rental bikes available through the Goteborg City Bikes scheme (seasonal, April to November).

Getting to and from Landvetter Airport (GOT) is easy via Flygbussarna airport coaches, which run every 15-20 minutes and take around 40 minutes to the city center. A single ticket costs about 129 SEK (~12 USD). There is no direct train from the airport, though public bus connections exist with one transfer for around 70 SEK. Taxis run about 500 SEK (~47 USD).

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Sweden is a Schengen Area member. Citizens of the US, Canada, Australia, UK (post-Brexit), and most other Western countries can enter visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period across all Schengen countries, not just Sweden. This is enough for a short stay but limits longer stays without planning.

As of 2026, Sweden does not offer a dedicated digital nomad or remote worker visa. There is no straightforward path to legally working remotely from Sweden long-term unless you qualify for a self-employment permit, which requires owning at least 51% of a registered company and demonstrating at least 18,000 EUR (~19,500 USD) in personal funds to cover the first two years.

EU and EEA citizens have full freedom of movement and need no visa or permit.

If you spend more than 183 days in Sweden in a 12-month period, you may become liable for Swedish income tax, which is substantial. Always verify current requirements with the Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) or a qualified immigration lawyer before planning a long stay.

Best Time to Visit

The practical window for visiting Gothenburg as a remote worker is May through September. Summer (June to August) brings long daylight hours of up to 18 hours, temperatures of 20-23 degrees C (68-73 F), and a lively outdoor culture. The city comes alive with festivals, street food, and terraces. This is when Gothenburg is most enjoyable day-to-day.

May is a sweet spot: the weather is warming, crowds are smaller than peak summer, and prices for accommodation are lower. September is still pleasant and sees fewer tourists than July and August.

Autumn (October to November) brings reliable rain and dropping temperatures. Winter is cold, dark, and damp with limited daylight, which some find genuinely difficult for productivity and mood. The city functions perfectly well year-round from an infrastructure standpoint, but dark January days with only 7 hours of light can be a real adjustment if you are not used to Nordic winters. Spring (March to April) is variable but improves quickly.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Excellent and reliable public transport across the whole city
  • High safety, low crime, and a generally relaxed urban atmosphere
  • Strong English proficiency throughout the city; almost everyone speaks it fluently
  • Good food scene from casual fika cafes to multiple Michelin-starred restaurants
  • Active outdoor life with the coast, parks, and archipelago day trips all accessible
  • Manageable size; easier to navigate than Stockholm and with a more local feel
  • Clean air, clean streets, and high-quality infrastructure overall

Cons

  • High cost of living, especially rent, makes it expensive for remote workers on modest incomes
  • No digital nomad visa and the 90-day Schengen limit restricts long stays for most non-EU visitors
  • Winters are dark, cold, and rainy and can significantly impact mood and motivation
  • The coworking and nomad community is small; this is not a city with a strong nomad social scene
  • Rain is possible in any month; August is actually the wettest month of the year
  • Finding short-term rental accommodation is competitive and expensive compared to most of Europe

Who Gothenburg Is Best For

Gothenburg suits remote workers who already have a strong income and can absorb Scandinavian prices without stress. It is a good fit if you value safety, order, and quality of life above all else, and if you want a European city that is genuinely functional rather than just photogenic. You will do well here if you already travel within Schengen and can plan your 90-day window carefully.

It is also a strong match for people who genuinely want to experience Nordic culture, appreciate good food and design, or are drawn to the outdoor lifestyle of the Swedish west coast, including the Bohuslan archipelago to the north.

Skip Gothenburg if you are on a tight budget, need more than 90 days without EU citizenship, or prefer a warmer climate and a buzzing nomad community. Lisbon, Tbilisi, or Chiang Mai will serve budget-conscious nomads far better. If you want Scandinavia but prefer a larger city with more expat infrastructure, Stockholm is the more internationally connected option, though not cheaper.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gothenburg

How much does it cost to live in Gothenburg as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $3,800 a month in Gothenburg for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Gothenburg?

Gothenburg rates 8 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work excellent. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Gothenburg safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Gothenburg is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Gothenburg a good city for digital nomads overall?

Gothenburg stands out for English and cleanliness among remote workers. With a typical budget near $3,800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
