Setting Up as a Nomad in Matera

An ancient cave city of tufa-stone Sassi carved into a ravine, one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited places. Matera pairs culture with safety and a mediterranean climate, all for about $1,300 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Sasso Barisano

Historic, polished, atmospheric

Restored cave district of boutique stays

The more restored of Matera's two Sassi, Sasso Barisano faces north and is lined with cave dwellings converted into boutique hotels, restaurants and artisan shops. Its polished stone lanes make it the most comfortable and atmospheric base within the ancient town.

Best for: Travellers wanting boutique cave-hotel stays amid restored Sassi lanes

Sasso Caveoso

$$
Ancient, raw, dramatic

Wilder, ancient cave-dwelling quarter

The older, more raw of the two Sassi, Sasso Caveoso tumbles toward the ravine with rupestrian churches, cave-house museums and dramatic viewpoints. Less commercialised than Barisano, it offers the strongest sense of Matera's original cave-dwelling past.

Best for: History lovers wanting the most authentic ancient cave-dwelling atmosphere
✓ Pros
  • Most authentic Sassi feel
  • Rupestrian churches nearby
  • Dramatic ravine views
✗ Cons
  • Steep tough terrain
  • Fewer modern amenities

Civita

$$$
Central, ancient, scenic

Rocky heart between the two Sassi

The rocky spur crowned by Matera's cathedral, Civita is the oldest core rising between Sasso Barisano and Sasso Caveoso. Its narrow lanes and the panoramic Duomo terrace sit at the very centre of the ancient town, ringed by both cave districts.

Best for: Visitors wanting the cathedral, viewpoints and a central Sassi position

  • Central between both Sassi
  • Historic core setting

Cost of Living in Matera

Matera is affordable by Italian standards, noticeably cheaper than the big northern cities and much of Tuscany, though its fame as a UNESCO site and film location has pushed prices up from their once-rock-bottom levels. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $1,100 to $1,600 a month. A furnished apartment in or near the historic centre costs about $550 to $900, with atmospheric cave-house Sassi lets at the higher end. These are our editorial estimates in euros, with rent the main variable; southern Italian food and everyday costs stay gentle on the wallet.

  • Furnished apartment near the centre: roughly $550 to $900 a month
  • Pranzo or a plate of orecchiette at a trattoria: roughly $10 to $16
  • Espresso at the bar: roughly $1 to $1.50
  • Regional train or bus toward Bari: a few euros

Where to Work in Matera

Matera is a small heritage city rather than a nomad hub, but its stint as a 2019 European Capital of Culture left it more connected than its ancient looks suggest. Home fibre is available in much of the city and handles calls and everyday work, and there is a small coworking presence plus cafes where you can settle in with a laptop. A TIM, Vodafone or WindTre SIM adds cheap mobile data as backup. Matera shares Italy's Central European time zone, giving comfortable European overlap. For a scenic, slower base, the connectivity is adequate, if without a large coworking community.

Getting Around Matera

Matera's ancient Sassi districts are a labyrinth of stairways, alleys and cave dwellings tumbling into a ravine, so exploring them means walking, and steep walking at that, which is part of the magic but demanding day to day. The newer town above is flatter and more workaday. Local buses serve the city and the ravine viewpoints. Matera's rail links are limited: a private line, the FAL, connects to Bari in around an hour and a half, and Bari's airport, about an hour away, is the main gateway. A car helps for exploring Basilicata and Puglia.

Visas for Matera

Matera is in Italy and the Schengen Area, so most non-EU visitors can stay visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, while EU and EEA citizens face no limits. For a longer stay, Italy has introduced a digital nomad visa for skilled remote workers who can show sufficient income, alongside other residence routes, though the process can be bureaucratic. Requirements and the nomad visa's terms can change, so confirm the current rules with official Italian sources or a consulate before relying on them, and mind the 90-day Schengen limit if you enter visa-free.

Best Time to Visit Matera

Matera has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, cooler winters. The best times to visit are spring, from April to June, and autumn, from September to October, when the days are warm and pleasant and the pale stone of the Sassi glows beautifully in the softer light. Summer, from July to August, is hot and can be busy with visitors, with the stone city trapping the heat. Winter is mild but can be grey and chilly, with the Sassi looking atmospheric under low light. Aim for the shoulder seasons for comfort and softer crowds.

Pros and Cons of Matera

Matera offers a genuinely singular setting with the trade-offs of a small, out-of-the-way southern city.

  • Pros: an extraordinary UNESCO cave-city unlike anywhere else, deep history and atmosphere, excellent and affordable southern Italian food, genuine safety, lower costs than northern Italy, and a walkable, photogenic historic core.
  • Cons: poor rail connections and a somewhat remote location, a small nomad community, limited English, steep, demanding terrain in the Sassi, hot summers, and a quieter nightlife and amenities scene than a bigger city.

Who Matera Is For

Matera is for the culture-and-atmosphere nomad who wants to live somewhere truly unlike anywhere else, and who values history, beauty and southern Italian living over connectivity and a big scene. Anyone captivated by ancient stone, dramatic settings and slow, food-focused days will find it unforgettable, and it makes an inspiring base for focused creative work. It rewards travellers happy with some Italian and a quieter pace. It is a weaker fit for those who need excellent transport links, a large nomad community, or lively nightlife. Come in spring or autumn, base yourself near the Sassi, and let the cave city cast its spell.

Frequently Asked Questions About Matera

A comfortable nomad budget in Matera is around

,300 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Matera?

Matera rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Matera safe for digital nomads?

Matera scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.
