Cost of Living in Varanasi

Varanasi is extremely cheap, among the most affordable cities a remote worker could choose. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $650 to $1,000 a month. Guesthouse rooms and simple flats near the ghats or in quieter neighbourhoods rent long-term for about $120 to $350, and street food and thalis cost just a dollar or two. These are our editorial estimates in rupees, and even factoring in a decent room with reliable power and a backup connection, Varanasi remains very light on the wallet. The main costs beyond rent are food, the odd boat ride, and travel in and out.

  • Guesthouse room or simple flat, long stay: roughly $120 to $350 a month
  • Thali or street-food meal: roughly $1 to $3
  • Autorickshaw across town: roughly $1 to $2
  • Sunrise boat ride on the Ganges: a few dollars

Where to Work in Varanasi

Varanasi is one of India's most intense and spiritual cities, not a remote-work hub, and its infrastructure reflects that. Fibre and 4G reach the city and are cheap, but speeds can be inconsistent and power cuts happen, so test any room's connection and consider a backup before relying on it for calls. Dedicated coworking is essentially absent; a handful of traveller cafes, some with rooftop river views, serve as informal work spots. A Jio or Airtel SIM is an essential backup. Treat Varanasi as a place for focused, lower-intensity work between immersion in the city, rather than a base for constant video meetings.

Getting Around Varanasi

The heart of Varanasi is the labyrinth of narrow lanes behind the ghats, where no cars fit and you explore on foot, dodging pilgrims, cows and motorbikes. For longer trips, autorickshaws and cycle rickshaws are cheap once you agree a fare, and ride apps operate in parts of the city. The most memorable transport is a rowing boat along the ghats at dawn or for the evening Ganga Aarti. Lal Bahadur Shastri Airport, about 25 kilometres out, connects Varanasi to Delhi, Mumbai and other cities, and the railway station is a major hub across northern India.

Visas for Varanasi

Foreign visitors reach Varanasi on India's e-Tourist Visa, arranged online before arrival and issued in 30-day, one-year and five-year forms, with the longer versions allowing multiple entries and stays of up to 90 or 180 days at a time depending on nationality. A tourist visa does not permit local employment, though remote work for a foreign employer sits in the same grey area many long-stayers occupy. Rules and permitted durations change and vary by passport, so confirm the current terms on the official Indian e-Visa portal before booking, and keep copies of your visa and register if your stay triggers any requirement.

Best Time to Visit Varanasi

The best window for Varanasi is the cooler, drier season from October to March, when daytime temperatures are pleasant for wandering the ghats and lanes, though winter mornings can be foggy and chilly. Summer, from April to June, is punishing, often above 40 Celsius, which makes daytime exploring exhausting. The monsoon from July to September brings heavy rain and can flood the lower ghats as the Ganges rises. The city is at its most magical around the autumn and winter festivals, especially Dev Deepawali in November, when thousands of oil lamps light the ghats.

Pros and Cons of Varanasi

Varanasi offers unmatched intensity and meaning, with comfort and infrastructure a distant second.

  • Pros: one of the world's oldest living cities and the spiritual heart of Hinduism, extraordinarily cheap, endlessly photogenic, with the Ganga Aarti, ancient temples and a sensory depth found almost nowhere else.
  • Cons: serious air pollution and a heavily polluted river, crowds, noise and chaos, patchy internet and power, poor cleanliness, persistent touts, and an overall intensity that many find exhausting for a long stay.

Who Varanasi Is For

Varanasi is for the spiritually curious, culturally immersive traveller who wants profound experience over comfort, and who can handle India at its most intense. Photographers, writers, seekers and anyone drawn to ritual, history and the raw texture of life and death by the Ganges will find it unforgettable. It works best for shorter, deep stays rather than as a long-term work base, and rewards patience, an open mind and a tolerance for chaos and grime. It is a poor fit for those who need clean air, reliable infrastructure, a nomad community or any sense of calm. Come in winter, stay near the ghats, and let the city overwhelm you on its own terms.

Frequently Asked Questions About Varanasi

How much does it cost to live in Varanasi as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Varanasi is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Varanasi?

Varanasi rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Varanasi safe for digital nomads?

Varanasi scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Varanasi a good city for digital nomads overall?

Varanasi stands out for culture and affordability among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
