Setting Up as a Nomad in Marigot

The French half of an island shared with the Dutch since 1648, and no border post between them. What pulls nomads here is climate, backed by food and easy visas. Budget around $2,200 a month. The size of the nomad scene is the weak spot.

Best Neighborhoods in Marigot

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Marigot

$$
Town & Creole

The French capital under Fort Louis

The main town on the French side, built around a marina and a market that sets up on the waterfront several mornings a week, with Fort Louis on the hill above giving a view across the lagoon to Anguilla. It is the administrative centre of the collectivity and considerably quieter than the Dutch side.

Best for: Anyone who wants the market, the marina and the French side's centre
Pros
  • The waterfront market is the best on the island
  • Fort Louis and the view across to Anguilla
  • French supermarkets, bakeries and administration
Cons
  • Traffic through the town is heavy
  • Recovery from Hurricane Irma is still visible
  • Quiet in the evening compared with the Dutch side

Grand Case

$$$
Culinary & Beachfront

One street of restaurants and the lolos

A village on the northern coast that calls itself the gourmet capital of the Caribbean, and the claim holds up: a single beachfront street of French restaurants alongside the lolos, open-air barbecue grills serving ribs and snapper for a fraction of the price. The bay behind is calm and shallow.

Best for: Anyone who takes food seriously
Pros
  • The best concentration of restaurants in the eastern Caribbean
  • The lolos are cheap, excellent and genuinely local
  • Calm swimming directly off the village beach
Cons
  • Prices in the restaurants are European rather than Caribbean
  • Busy through the high season
  • Six kilometres from Marigot along a congested road

Terres Basses

$$$$
Exclusive & Quiet

The villa peninsula between two lagoons

The low western peninsula joined to the rest of the island by two causeways, holding large private villas, no shops and the island's quietest beaches at Baie Rouge, Plum Bay and Long Bay. It is entirely residential and is where most of the island's high-end rental stock sits.

Best for: Longer stays with the budget for a villa and a car
Pros
  • The quietest beaches on the island
  • Almost no through traffic
  • Close to the airport on the Dutch side
Cons
  • No shops, restaurants or services at all
  • Villa prices only
  • A car is absolutely necessary

Orient Bay

$$$
Beach Club & Lively

The long east coast beach with the beach clubs

A two kilometre beach on the Atlantic side, backed by a village of restaurants, beach clubs and rentals, with Green Cay and Pinel Island offshore. The northern end has been clothing-optional for decades. The trade winds blow steadily here, which makes it the island's windsurfing beach.

Best for: Anyone who wants a long beach with services along it
Pros
  • The longest and liveliest beach on the French side
  • Beach clubs, watersports and boats to Pinel Island
  • Steady trade wind keeps it cooler than the west coast
Cons
  • Busy with day visitors and cruise excursions
  • The Atlantic surf can be rough
  • Ten kilometres from Marigot

Cost of Living in Marigot

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Marigot, so the $2,200 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 951 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $2,200 a month on the French side, which is expensive by Caribbean standards and considerably cheaper than Saint Barthelemy next door. A one-bedroom runs roughly $900 to $1,400, with Terres Basses and the Baie Nettle strip at the top. Groceries in the French supermarkets are imported and pricey, though crossing to the Dutch side for some shopping is normal and legal, since there is no border post. Restaurants in Grand Case, which markets itself as the gourmet capital of the Caribbean, are excellent and expensive. The euro is the currency on this side and dollars are widely accepted.

Where to Work in Marigot

Connectivity is reasonable. Fibre reaches parts of Marigot and the main settlements, French mobile networks operate with free roaming for European visitors, and power is generally reliable, though the recovery from Hurricane Irma in 2017 took years and some infrastructure still shows it. There is limited coworking. The time zone is four hours behind GMT, which gives a good working overlap with the eastern United States all afternoon and with Europe in the morning, one of the more genuinely useful positions on this site for anyone with clients on both sides.

Getting Around Marigot

A car is the practical answer. The island is about eighty seven square kilometres shared between France and the Netherlands, with a single ring road and no border checks between the two halves, though the road narrows and the traffic backs up badly around Marigot and Simpson Bay. Shared minibuses run the main routes cheaply. Marigot itself is walkable. Princess Juliana International Airport on the Dutch side is the island's air link and one of the busiest in the eastern Caribbean, with direct flights to Europe and North America. Anyone crossing to the Dutch side regularly should also know that the two halves use different currencies and different plug standards, which catches people out more often than the border does.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Marigot

Saint Martin is a French overseas collectivity, part of France and the European Union but outside the Schengen area, so a Schengen visa does not admit you and time here does not count against a Schengen allowance. European Union citizens may live and work here without a permit. Most Western nationalities enter visa-free for ninety days. The Dutch side, Sint Maarten, is a separate country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands with its own rules, and although nobody checks the internal border, the two sides do keep separate records. Anyone planning a long stay should also weigh which side to base on, since the French side offers European Union rights and the Dutch side offers a lower cost of living.

Best Time to Visit Marigot

December to April is the dry season and the high season, warm at around twenty seven degrees with low humidity and the whole island busy. May, June and November are quieter and better value. Hurricane season runs June to November, and this island took the full force of Irma in 2017, which was the strongest Atlantic hurricane on record at landfall and destroyed a large part of the built environment. Recovery has been substantial but uneven. September is the quietest month and much of the tourist economy shuts. Carnival on the French side runs in February and March before Lent, while the Dutch side holds its own in April, so the island effectively gets two.

Pros and Cons of Marigot

The case for the French side is the food, which is genuinely a cut above anything else in the region at Grand Case, the beaches, the ability to live in France while working American hours, and the freedom of movement for European Union citizens. Two legal systems on one small island produce useful arbitrage in several directions. The case against is that Saint Martin has real crime problems by regional standards, the recovery from Irma is incomplete, traffic is bad, and the island is heavily dependent on a cruise and charter economy.

Who Marigot Is For

Saint Martin suits European Union citizens who want a Caribbean base with no immigration friction and a time zone that works for both sides of the Atlantic. It suits sailors, since the lagoon is one of the largest in the Caribbean and a major charter hub. It suits anyone who takes food seriously. It does not suit anyone wanting quiet, anyone uncomfortable with the security situation, or anyone hoping for a cheap island, because this is not one. It also suits anyone who enjoys the oddity of the arrangement itself, since the 1648 Treaty of Concordia dividing the island is among the oldest border agreements still in force anywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions About Marigot

How much does it cost to live in Marigot as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Marigot is around $2,200 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Marigot?

Marigot rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Marigot safe for digital nomads?

Marigot scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Marigot a good city for digital nomads overall?

Marigot stands out for great weather and its food scene among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,200 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
