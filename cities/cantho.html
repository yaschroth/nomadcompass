Is Can Tho a Good Base for Remote Work?

Mekong Delta's largest city with floating markets. Plan on roughly $800 a month. Its strengths are low costs and food, while the size of the nomad scene is where it scores lower.

Cost of Living in Can Tho

Can Tho is one of the most affordable cities in Vietnam for remote workers. Your biggest expense is rent. A basic furnished studio or one-bedroom apartment in the central Ninh Kieu district runs around 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND ($160-240) per month. Move a few kilometers out toward Cai Rang or Binh Thuy and the same space drops to 2,500,000 to 4,000,000 VND ($100-160). Most rentals include Wi-Fi; air conditioning is standard.

Groceries from local markets are very cheap. A week of fresh produce, protein, and staples costs around 300,000 to 500,000 VND ($12-20). Eating out is where Can Tho shines: a bowl of bun rieu or hu tieu at a street stall is 30,000 to 50,000 VND ($1.20-2.00), and a full sit-down meal at a local restaurant rarely exceeds 150,000 VND ($6).

Expect to spend 200,000 to 400,000 VND ($8-16) per day on coffee and cafe sessions if you work from cafes, though most cost 40,000 to 70,000 VND per drink and do not pressure you to keep buying.

  • Rent (central Ninh Kieu): 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND ($160-240/month)
  • Rent (outer districts): 2,500,000 to 4,000,000 VND ($100-160/month)
  • Groceries: 1,500,000 to 2,000,000 VND ($60-80/month)
  • Eating out (local): 1,500,000 to 2,500,000 VND ($60-100/month)
  • Coworking/cafe work: 500,000 to 1,500,000 VND ($20-60/month)
  • Local transport (motorbike rental): 1,000,000 to 1,500,000 VND ($40-60/month)
  • Total comfortable range: 10,000,000 to 16,000,000 VND ($400-650/month)

A frugal remote worker can manage on $400-500 per month. A comfortable lifestyle with a central apartment, eating out regularly, and occasional comforts sits around $550-700.

Where to Work

Can Tho does not have a large coworking scene. Dedicated coworking spaces are limited and change frequently; a Facebook-listed space called Co-working Space Can Tho has appeared in local directories, but verify it is still open before relying on it.

In practice, cafes are how most remote workers here get things done, and the cafe culture supports it well. Highlands Coffee has multiple branches across Ninh Kieu with reliable air conditioning, power outlets, and consistent Wi-Fi. Lego Coffee (58B-60B Tran Bach Dang, Tan An) has a quirky container design with outlets and solid internet. Cafe 1985 (138A Huynh Cuong, An Cu) is a popular evening spot with live music, good for evenings but can be loud once the band starts. Time Cafe (89A Vo Van Tan, Tan An) is a quieter option well regarded by locals for sustained work sessions. Tiem Tra Co Ngot on Le Lai Street offers a garden setting popular for long afternoon sessions.

Wi-Fi speeds across Can Tho cafes are generally strong, often 50 to 150 Mbps on fiber connections. Vietnam's overall internet infrastructure is solid. For backup, a local SIM card with data from Viettel or Mobifone costs around 150,000 to 200,000 VND ($6-8) per month for 30GB and delivers 4G reliably throughout the city.

Getting Around

Can Tho is not a walkable city for daily errands. The distances between places are manageable but spread out, and the heat makes walking uncomfortable for most of the year. A motorbike rental is the standard solution: expect to pay 1,000,000 to 1,500,000 VND ($40-60/month) for a basic scooter, and you will need an international driving permit if you plan to ride legally.

Grab (the regional ride-hailing app) is widely available and reliable for getting around without your own vehicle. A Grab bike across central Ninh Kieu costs 20,000 to 40,000 VND ($0.80-1.60). A Grab car runs 60,000 to 120,000 VND ($2.40-4.80) for most city journeys.

Can Tho International Airport (VCA) is about 10 kilometers from the city center. A Grab car to Ninh Kieu costs 100,000 to 150,000 VND ($4-6) and takes 15-20 minutes. Note that VCA currently handles only domestic routes; there are no scheduled international flights direct to Can Tho. To fly internationally you need to travel to Ho Chi Minh City (about 3-3.5 hours by bus or taxi), which is well-connected by frequent coaches from the Can Tho Bus Station.

Local buses exist but are slow and not practical for most visitors. The Ninh Kieu riverside area is pleasant to walk around in the evenings.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Vietnam offers an e-visa to citizens of all countries. The e-visa allows stays of up to 90 days and is available as single or multiple entry. You apply online at evisa.gov.vn; processing typically takes 3-7 business days, and the fee is around $25 USD. Can Tho airport is listed as a valid port of entry for e-visa holders.

Citizens of about 25 countries, including the UK, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, and several others, receive visa-free entry for 45 days. Citizens of some Southeast Asian countries have shorter visa-free allowances. US, Australian, Canadian, and most other passport holders need the e-visa.

Vietnam does not currently offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. If you want to stay beyond 90 days, your main options are exiting and re-entering (border runs to Cambodia are common via land border), or arranging a longer-term business visa, which requires local sponsorship. Extensions inside Vietnam are possible but limited. Always verify current rules at evisa.gov.vn or your nearest Vietnamese embassy before traveling, as visa policy changes periodically.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit

Can Tho has a tropical climate with two distinct seasons. The dry season runs from December through April, and the rainy season runs from May through November.

For remote work, the best window is December to March. Temperatures are more manageable (26-32 degrees Celsius), humidity drops, and skies are generally clear. You can walk along the river in the evenings without getting drenched. January and February are peak season for Vietnamese domestic tourism, so guesthouses fill up and prices rise slightly around Tet (Vietnamese New Year, usually late January or early February).

April and May bring the hottest temperatures of the year, regularly reaching 35-37 degrees Celsius, which makes going outside uncomfortable during the day. From May onward, afternoon thunderstorms become frequent, and by September to November rainfall is at its heaviest with occasional flooding in low-lying parts of the city. Working productively is still possible year-round since you will be indoors in air conditioning most of the time, but the dry season months make the city far more pleasant to live in.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Extremely low cost of living, one of the cheapest cities in Vietnam
  • Authentic Mekong Delta culture with very few tourists compared to Hoi An or Hoi An
  • Excellent and varied local food scene at very low prices
  • Riverside cafes with reliable Wi-Fi make for pleasant work environments
  • Friendly locals and a slower pace of life than Ho Chi Minh City
  • Easy Grab access and straightforward transport for daily needs

Cons

  • No dedicated coworking spaces to speak of; you depend on cafes
  • English is spoken less widely than in tourist-heavy cities; menus are often Vietnamese-only
  • No international flights; getting in and out requires a 3+ hour trip to Ho Chi Minh City
  • Limited digital nomad community, making it harder to meet like-minded people
  • Hot and humid for most of the year; flooding possible in rainy season
  • Nightlife and social scene are modest compared to larger Vietnamese cities

Who Can Tho Is Best For

Can Tho works best for solo remote workers or couples who are self-sufficient and not reliant on a community of fellow nomads. If your priority is keeping costs extremely low while living somewhere with real local character, Can Tho delivers. It suits people who enjoy exploring food culture, are comfortable navigating places where English is limited, and want a quieter pace of life than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

It is also a strong choice as part of a longer Vietnam trip. Spend a month here to save money and experience the Mekong Delta properly, then move on to more connected hubs for socializing and networking.

Skip Can Tho if you need regular coworking spaces with fast, reliable infrastructure, a ready-made expat social scene, or easy access to international flights. It is also a tough base if you are uncomfortable with language barriers or need frequent Amazon-style deliveries and services that larger Vietnamese cities handle easily. People on tighter timelines who want Vietnam's highlights quickly are better served by Da Nang or Ho Chi Minh City.

Frequently Asked Questions About Can Tho

How much does it cost to live in Can Tho as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $800 a month in Can Tho for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Can Tho?

Can Tho rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Can Tho safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Can Tho is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Can Tho a good city for digital nomads overall?

Can Tho stands out for low costs and food among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
