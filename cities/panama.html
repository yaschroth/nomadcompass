Why Panama City Works for Remote Work

A modern skyline, canal views, and a territorial tax system. Panama City pairs easy visas with fast WiFi and a tropical climate, all for about $2,400 a month. If anything, safety lags.

Panama City uses the US dollar (USD), which removes any currency conversion headache. Your costs depend heavily on which neighborhood you choose. In central areas like Marbella, El Cangrejo, or Punta Pacifica, expect to pay $900-1,500/month for a furnished one-bedroom apartment. Move out to areas like Betania or San Miguelito and you can find decent places for $500-800/month.

  • Rent (central, 1BR): $900-1,500/month
  • Rent (outer area, 1BR): $500-800/month
  • Groceries (self-catering): $200-350/month at supermarkets like El Rey or Riba Smith
  • Eating out (local spots): $5-10 per meal; a set lunch (menu del dia) around $4-6
  • Coworking hot desk: $120-200/month; day passes run $15-20
  • Local transport (MetroBus/Metro): $35-50/month; single ride is $0.35
  • Utilities (electricity, water): $80-150/month (AC drives costs up fast in the heat)

Total realistic monthly range: $1,400-2,200 for a comfortable nomad setup in a safe central neighborhood. Budget-conscious nomads staying further out can get under $1,000/month, but factor in Uber rides if you want to stay safe after dark.

Where to Work

Panama City has a solid coworking scene for its size. The standout options cluster around the financial district and Punta Pacifica. Workings runs three locations in major towers (including Torre de las Americas in Punta Pacifica) with fast internet, meeting rooms, and terrace access. The Cube Coworking near Via España and Metro Iglesia del Carmen is popular with nomads for its open-plan vibe and community events. Panama Coworking Center in Kenex Plaza offers some of the fastest connections in the city at roughly $150 for a 10-day pass. Selina Casco Viejo gives you a coworking day pass for around $10 in a beautiful historic building with a rooftop.

Cafe work culture exists but is uneven. Cafe Unido (multiple branches citywide) welcomes laptop workers and serves quality Panamanian specialty coffee. Sisu Coffee Studio in Casco Viejo is cozy and remote-worker friendly. That said, many local cafes are small and not designed for hours of work, so a coworking membership makes more sense as your base.

WiFi speeds are genuinely good by regional standards. Average download speeds in the city hover around 150 Mbps. Coworking spaces consistently deliver fast, reliable connections. Mobile data coverage via Claro or Movistar is strong across most of the city, which is useful as a backup.

Getting Around

Panama City is not particularly walkable outside a few pockets. The heat and humidity make long walks unpleasant, and sidewalks are inconsistent in many neighborhoods. That said, Casco Viejo itself is completely walkable once you are inside it, and parts of El Cangrejo around Via Argentina feel pedestrian-friendly.

The Metro is a genuine asset. Two lines cover major corridors of the city and run from 5:00 AM to 11:30 PM. A single ride costs $0.35 with a rechargeable card (also used on MetroBus). The MetroBus network runs over 140 routes and is air-conditioned, replacing the old chaotic "diablo rojo" buses. For everything else, Uber works well and is affordable.

Getting to and from Tocumen International Airport (PTY) is easy: Metro Line 2 has a direct branch to the airport terminal, making it one of the most convenient airport connections in Central America. A taxi or Uber from the city center takes 30-45 minutes depending on traffic, which can be bad during rush hours.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most Western passports, including US, UK, Canadian, EU, and Australian, enter Panama visa-free for 90 to 180 days depending on nationality. US passport holders typically receive 180 days on arrival as a tourist. Extensions are possible through the National Immigration Service but are granted case-by-case and not guaranteed.

Panama has a dedicated Digital Nomad Visa (Visa de Nómada Digital) valid for 9 months, extendable once for a total of 18 months. Requirements include proof of at least $3,000/month in foreign income, valid health insurance covering Panama, a clean criminal background check, and a valid passport. The application fee is $250 plus $50 for the visa card. You must use a licensed Panamanian legal representative to file, adding roughly $500-1,000 in legal fees. Processing takes around 30 days.

Panama's territorial tax system means income earned from clients or employers outside the country is not taxed locally, which appeals to many nomads considering longer stays.

Always confirm current entry and visa requirements with Panama's National Immigration Service or your country's embassy before travel, as policies change.

Best Time to Visit

Panama City sits on the Pacific coast and has two distinct seasons. The dry season runs from mid-December through mid-April. This is the most comfortable window for remote work: temperatures around 29-32C (84-90F), low humidity by local standards, and reliable sunshine. January through March is the sweet spot.

The wet season covers May through December. Rain typically arrives in heavy afternoon bursts rather than all-day downpours, so mornings are often clear. October and November are the wettest months. Working through the wet season is entirely feasible since you are indoors most of the day anyway, and accommodation costs can be lower with fewer visitors around.

Avoid travel over Christmas and Carnival (February/March) if you dislike crowds and higher prices. The shoulder periods of late November and early December offer decent weather and quieter streets.

Who Panama City Is Best For

Panama City works well for nomads who want a Latin American base without currency volatility, who value strong flight connections as a regional hub, and who appreciate a city with real urban infrastructure. If you earn in USD or euros and want a place where your money is straightforward to manage, Panama makes immediate sense. It suits people who are comfortable with heat and do not need a walkable neighborhood to feel at home.

It is a particularly good fit for those eyeing longer stays under the digital nomad visa, especially if the territorial tax treatment is relevant to their situation. The food scene, nightlife in Casco Viejo, and access to nearby nature (the Canal, rainforest, Pacific beaches) give it depth beyond a purely functional base.

Skip it if you need cool weather, prefer bike-friendly cities, or are on a very tight budget: Panama City is affordable by North American standards but is not the cheapest option in Central America. Also think twice if you need a tight-knit nomad community, as the scene is present but smaller than you find in cities like Medellin or Mexico City.

Frequently Asked Questions About Panama City

How much does it cost to live in Panama City as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Panama City is around $2,400 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Panama City?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Panama City is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Panama City safe for digital nomads?

Panama City scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Panama City a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Panama City is worth a look if you value easy visas and fast WiFi. Costs sit near $2,400 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
