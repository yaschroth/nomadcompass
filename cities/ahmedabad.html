Setting Up as a Nomad in Ahmedabad

Gujarat's UNESCO-listed heritage city, a textile-trading, largely vegetarian, dry-state metropolis with intricate old-town architecture and famously low costs. Expect $650 a month here. Low costs and food are the standout strengths, with nightlife the main caveat.

Best Neighborhoods in Ahmedabad

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Navrangpura

$$$
Central & Walkable

University-adjacent core with cafes and coworking

The old west-side hub around CG Road and Law Garden, packed with commercial towers, bookshops, chai stalls and the campuses of Gujarat University and CEPT. Students and office workers fill the pavements. It has the densest cluster of coworking desks and cafes you can reach on foot.

Best for: Nomads who want coworking, cafes and a metro stop all within a short walk
Pros
  • Most walkable commercial area in the city
  • Many coworking spaces and laptop-friendly cafes
  • Metro and buses connect the whole west side
Cons
  • CG Road traffic and horn noise are relentless
  • Older buildings with unreliable lifts and parking
  • Very little green space or quiet

Vastrapur

$$$
Balanced & Green

Lake, malls and IIM, the balanced west-side pick

Built around Vastrapur Lake with its walking path and open-air amphitheatre, this district mixes mid-rise apartment societies, the Alpha One mall and the IIM Ahmedabad campus. It attracts students, faculty and young professionals. Rents sit below Bodakdev while keeping cafes, gyms and the metro close.

Best for: Nomads who want a lakeside jog, a serviced flat and good value on the west side
Pros
  • Lake and parks for morning walks
  • Strong mix of cafes, malls and grocery delivery
  • Metro station and easy autos everywhere
Cons
  • Weekend crowds around the lake and malls
  • Society flats often demand long leases
  • Parking is a daily fight

Prahlad Nagar

$$$$
Modern & Upscale

Glass towers, garden and the modern expat pocket

A planned grid of new high-rises and corporate parks around the landscaped Prahlad Nagar Garden, just off SG Highway. Wide roads, gated societies with gyms and pools, plus a strip of upmarket restaurants and bakeries. It is where relocating professionals and long-stay foreigners generally land.

Best for: Remote workers who want a new serviced apartment with a gym, pool and fast fibre
Pros
  • Newest apartment stock with pools, gyms and backup power
  • Big landscaped garden and a jogging loop
  • Dense choice of restaurants and coworking on SG Highway
Cons
  • Most expensive rents in Ahmedabad
  • Long walks between blocks in brutal summer heat
  • SG Highway traffic is heavy at peak hours

Bodakdev

$$$$
Quiet & Leafy

Quiet bungalow lanes behind the business strip

A leafy pocket of bungalows, low-rise societies and the Thaltej Iskcon corridor, sitting immediately behind the SG Highway office towers. It is calmer than Prahlad Nagar but just as well served, with speciality coffee, delis and clinics. Popular with settled families and senior professionals.

Best for: Nomads staying months who want a peaceful street but corporate infrastructure nearby
Pros
  • Quiet tree-lined interior lanes
  • Speciality cafes, gyms and good hospitals close by
  • Short hop to the SG Highway coworking cluster
Cons
  • Expensive and mostly geared to families, not short stays
  • Sparse public transport inside the lanes
  • Little nightlife beyond restaurants

Old City

$
Historic & Cheap

Walled heritage lanes, pols and the cheapest rents

The walled east bank around Manek Chowk, Bhadra Fort and the carved wooden pols, where the market flips from vegetables by day to a street food riot at night. It is the cheapest place to stay and the most atmospheric, but infrastructure is old and lanes are tight.

Best for: Budget travellers and photographers who want heritage streets over comfort
Pros
  • Cheapest rooms and food in the city
  • UNESCO listed heritage lanes and the best street food
  • Very central for the riverfront and heritage walks
Cons
  • Congested, noisy lanes with poor air quality
  • Ageing buildings, patchy water and internet
  • Almost no coworking or laptop-friendly cafes

Cost of Living in Ahmedabad

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Ahmedabad, so the $650 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 951 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Ahmedabad is among India's better-value cities, running around $650 per month for a comfortable single nomad. A furnished one-bedroom in a decent area such as Navrangpura, Satellite, or near the riverfront costs roughly $200 to $350, with cheaper options further out. Food is very affordable, and Gujarati thalis, street snacks, and local eateries let you eat well on $150 to $220 a month, though note this is a largely vegetarian city so meat and especially alcohol are hard to find. A generous data SIM is about $3 to $5 monthly. Budget $35 to $65 for a coworking hot desk, plus utilities and heavy air conditioning use in the intense summer. Electricity bills spike badly from March through June. Groceries and domestic help are cheap. You can live comfortably on modest spending here, and the low rents make Ahmedabad one of the most economical metro bases in the country.

Where to Work in Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad has a growing but smaller coworking scene than the big tech metros, with spaces concentrated around Navrangpura, C.G. Road, Prahlad Nagar, and the SG Highway corridor offering hot desks and day passes. Fixed broadband in the city is reasonable, but reliability can dip during outages and the hot season, so keep a backup 4G or 5G SIM loaded with data for critical calls, which is wise across India. Mobile coverage is strong and cheap. Cafe culture is developing, with modern cafes along C.G. Road and in newer commercial areas where laptop work is tolerated, though the city is more business-driven than cafe-lounging in character. Being a dry state, do not expect cafe-bar hybrids for evening work sessions. Coworking spaces with backup power are the safest choice during occasional cuts. Basing near Prahlad Nagar or SG Highway gives good access to workspaces and amenities.

Getting Around Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad is relatively flat and has invested in transport, including a growing metro and the BRTS bus rapid transit system that runs on dedicated lanes and is cheap and reasonably efficient. Still, the city is spread out and summer heat makes long walks unpleasant much of the year. Ride-hailing via Ola and Uber works well and is affordable, and autorickshaws are plentiful, though you should use app meters or agree fares in advance. The metro is clean and expanding but does not yet reach every area. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport sits close to the city, typically 20 to 40 minutes from central areas, with good domestic links and some international routes to the Gulf. Two-wheelers are common among residents. For most nomads, a combination of app cabs, autos, and the BRTS or metro covers daily needs comfortably without owning a vehicle.

Visas for Ahmedabad

India offers no digital nomad visa, so visitors to Ahmedabad rely on the e-Tourist Visa, applied for online ahead of travel in 30-day, one-year, or five-year versions depending on nationality. It is simple to obtain and is the standard path for stays from a few weeks to several months. Working remotely for a foreign employer while on a tourist visa occupies a legal grey area that Indian rules do not clearly address, so understand you are not formally authorized to work and judge that risk for yourself. Any local employment or India-based paid work requires a proper employment or business visa instead. Allowed stay durations, entry rules, and possible registration steps can change over time, and certain longer stays require FRRO registration, so always check the current requirements on the official Indian government visa portal before booking and again just before you travel.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Ahmedabad

The comfortable season in Ahmedabad is November through February, when the weather turns mild and dry, making it the clear best window for visiting and working. Gujarat is a hot, dry state, and the pre-monsoon stretch from March through June is genuinely punishing, with high temperatures that make air conditioning essential and outdoor activity difficult, so avoid this period if you can. The southwest monsoon arrives around late June and runs through September, bringing relief and some rain, though the city stays drier than coastal India. Winter is the highlight for events too, with the famous Uttarayan kite festival in January and Navratri in autumn drawing crowds. The dry heat is intense but at least less sticky than coastal humidity. For a productive remote-work stay with tolerable evenings and cultural festivals, aim squarely at the November to February window and plan around the brutal summer.

Pros and Cons of Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad's strengths are very low costs, some of India's best vegetarian food, strong and improving public transport with the BRTS and metro, a nearby and convenient airport, and a rich heritage as a UNESCO-recognized historic city with the Sabarmati riverfront and Gandhi's ashram. It is safe, orderly, and business-friendly. The main drawbacks are extreme dry-season heat, a smaller coworking and international nomad scene than the tech metros, and the practical realities of a dry, largely vegetarian state, meaning no easy access to alcohol and limited meat options, which some travelers find restrictive. Nightlife is minimal by design. English is widely used in business alongside Gujarati and Hindi. The city is more industrious than glamorous, appealing to those who value substance and value over scene. Overall it rewards budget-focused, culturally curious nomads who do not need a party lifestyle.

Who Ahmedabad Is For

Ahmedabad suits budget-minded nomads who want an affordable, safe, and authentic Indian city with strong food and deep heritage rather than a nomad party hub. It is a natural fit for vegetarians, who will find some of the best plant-based food in the country, and for travelers happy in a dry state where alcohol is not part of the routine. It works for people drawn to history, textiles, architecture, and festivals like Uttarayan and Navratri, and for those who appreciate genuinely good public transport in an Indian metro. The very low rents make it excellent for cost-focused long stays. It is less ideal for anyone who needs nightlife, a large established international community, meat-heavy dining, or cool weather, since summers are severe. Solo travelers and couples who value calm, order, and value over buzz will feel at home here quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions About Ahmedabad

How much does it cost to live in Ahmedabad as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Ahmedabad is around $650 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Ahmedabad?

Ahmedabad rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is good for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Ahmedabad safe for digital nomads?

Ahmedabad scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Ahmedabad a good city for digital nomads overall?

Ahmedabad stands out for affordability and its food scene among remote workers. With a typical budget near $650 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
