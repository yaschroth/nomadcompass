Setting Up as a Nomad in Lucca

A graceful Tuscan city encircled by intact Renaissance walls, now a tree-lined promenade above piazzas, towers, and Puccini's streets. Lucca pairs food with safety and a mediterranean climate, all for about $1,600 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Cost of Living in Lucca

Lucca sits at the affordable end of Tuscany, cheaper than Florence yet full of the same beauty. A comfortable month for a single nomad lands roughly between $1,400 and $1,900, depending on how you rent and eat. These figures are editorial estimates rather than surveyed averages, so treat them as a planning guide. Renting a small flat inside or just outside the walls is your biggest cost, followed by groceries, the odd trattoria dinner, coffee at the bar, and a monthly mobile plan. Cooking at home with produce from the covered market and local shops keeps things low, while daily restaurant meals push the upper figure.

  • One-bed flat rent (monthly): roughly $750 to $1,100
  • Groceries for one: around $250 to $350
  • Espresso at the bar: about $1.20 to $1.50
  • Casual trattoria dinner for one: roughly $18 to $30

Where to Work in Lucca

Lucca is a small city, so your workspace is usually a good flat with reliable fibre plus a rotation of cafes. Dedicated coworking exists but is limited, so many remote workers book a desk in one of the shared offices near the centre or simply settle into a quiet cafe mid-morning before the lunch rush. The historic centre has plenty of characterful bars for a short session, though few expect you to camp for hours. For focused days, a home office with a decent chair beats hunting for outlets. In fine weather, the wide walls make an excellent spot to clear your head between calls.

Getting Around Lucca

Lucca is flat, compact, and made for bicycles, which is by far the best way to get around. The old town is largely pedestrian and cycle friendly, and you can pedal the entire circuit of the walls, about four kilometres, in well under half an hour. Most daily errands are a short walk or ride away, so a car is more burden than help inside the centre. For trips further afield, the train station sits just outside the southern walls, with regular services to Pisa in around thirty minutes, Pisa airport close behind, and Florence in roughly an hour and a half. Buses fill the gaps to nearby towns.

Visas for Lucca

Lucca is in Italy, part of the Schengen Area, so many visitors arrive under the standard ninety-days-in-any-one-hundred-and-eighty rule without a visa. For longer stays, Italy now offers a digital nomad and remote worker visa aimed at qualified people working remotely for employers or clients outside Italy, typically requiring proof of income, health insurance, and accommodation. Application is made through an Italian consulate before you travel, and rules and thresholds change, so always confirm current requirements with official sources. Once in Italy on a long-stay visa you generally apply for a residence permit locally. Requirements evolve, so verify everything with the consulate rather than relying on a summary.

Best Time to Visit Lucca

Late spring and early autumn are the sweet spots, with warm days, softer light, and fewer crowds than the peak of summer. May, June, and September bring pleasant temperatures ideal for cycling the walls and lingering in the piazzas. July and August turn hot and busier, with day trippers and the well-known Lucca Summer Festival drawing music fans into town. Autumn is atmospheric and quieter, and food lovers enjoy the seasonal produce. Winters are cool and damp rather than harsh, and the city feels calm and local, though some businesses keep shorter hours. For steady work and comfortable weather, aim for the shoulder seasons.

Pros and Cons of Lucca

Lucca rewards nomads who want beauty, calm, and authentic Tuscan life over big-city buzz. The intact walls, flat cycle-friendly streets, superb food, and easy trains to Pisa and Florence make it a lovely base. The trade-offs are scale and social scene: this is a small city, so the remote-work community is thin, English is only moderately spoken, and nightlife is gentle. Weigh what matters to you before committing.

  • Pros: stunning intact walls and historic centre, flat and superb for cycling, excellent Tuscan food, safe and clean, well connected to Pisa and Florence
  • Cons: small remote-work community, limited coworking and nightlife, moderate English, summer heat and crowds, rentals can be tight

Who Lucca Is For

Lucca suits the nomad who values a slower, rooted rhythm: someone happy to cycle to the market, work a focused day from a comfortable flat, and spend evenings walking the walls or sharing tordelli in a family trattoria. It is a strong pick for couples, solo travellers who do not need a party scene, and anyone drawn to art, history, and food. If you want a dense coworking hub, constant networking, and lively nightlife, a bigger city will serve you better. But if quiet beauty, walkability, and easy access to the rest of Tuscany sound ideal, Lucca is hard to beat.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lucca

How much does it cost to live in Lucca as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Lucca is around

,600 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Lucca?

Lucca rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Lucca safe for digital nomads?

Lucca scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Lucca a good city for digital nomads overall?
