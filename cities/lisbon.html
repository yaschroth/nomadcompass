Is Lisbon a Good Base for Remote Work?

Europe's sunniest capital with a thriving tech scene and endless pastéis de nata. Expect $3,500 a month here. Climate and a large nomad community are the standout strengths, with cost the main caveat.

Cost of Living in Lisbon

Lisbon is no longer the cheap European base it was five years ago. Housing costs jumped sharply through 2025, and rent is now the line that will define your budget. Here is a realistic monthly picture for one remote worker (prices in euros with rough USD equivalents at about 1.15).

  • Rent, 1-bed in the center: €1,200-1,600 ($1,380-1,840). Central neighborhoods like Príncipe Real and Chiado sit at the top of that range.
  • Rent, 1-bed outside the center: €900-1,100 ($1,035-1,265). A room in a shared flat runs roughly €600-900 ($690-1,035).
  • Groceries: €280-350 ($320-400) if you cook most days and shop at Pingo Doce, Continente, or Lidl.
  • Eating out: a simple lunch menu is €10-14, a mid-range dinner for one around €18-25, and a coffee (bica) about €1. Budget €200-350 ($230-400) monthly depending on habits.
  • Coworking hot-desk: roughly €150-250 ($170-290) for a monthly membership, or €15-25 per day drop-in.
  • Local transport: the Navegante Municipal pass covering the city is €30/month ($34); the metropolitan version is €40 ($46).
  • Utilities and internet: around €100-150 ($115-170) for a one-bedroom, with fast fiber included in that figure.

Total monthly range: a single remote worker renting solo in the center should plan for €2,000-2,800 ($2,300-3,220). Sharing a flat outside the center can bring you closer to €1,400-1,800 ($1,610-2,070).

Where to Work

Lisbon has a deep coworking scene. Second Home, inside the Mercado da Ribeira in Cais do Sodré, is a plant-filled, design-led space popular with internationals. Heden has locations in Chiado and Graça with hot-desks around €20/day and monthly memberships near €250 plus VAT. Avila Spaces, the oldest operator in the city, runs sites in Saldanha, Avenida da República, and Parque das Nações. Cafe-work culture exists but is more relaxed than in some nomad hubs; smaller independent cafes may not welcome all-day laptop camping, so a coworking membership is the reliable option for calls and focused work.

Connectivity is a real strength. Portugal has some of the best internet in Europe, with fast fiber standard in apartments and 5G widely available across the city. You will rarely struggle to run video calls.

Getting Around

Central Lisbon is walkable, though the hills are steep and the cobbled sidewalks (calçada) are hard on the knees and slippery when wet. Four metro lines, an extensive Carris bus network, historic trams, and suburban trains cover the rest, all payable with a rechargeable Navegante card. From Lisbon Airport (LIS), the red metro line runs directly from the Aeroporto station into the city in about 20-30 minutes with one transfer, which is by far the cheapest way in. Ride-hailing (Uber, Bolt) is cheap and widely used for late nights or luggage runs.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and around 60 other visa-exempt countries can stay in the Schengen Area, including Portugal, for up to 90 days within any rolling 180-day period without a visa. Those days are shared across all Schengen countries, not counted per country.

To stay longer, the main route for remote workers is the D8 digital nomad visa. As of 2026 it requires proof of remote income of at least €3,680 per month (four times the Portuguese minimum wage), plus savings of around €11,040. The D8 leads to a residence permit and can put you on a path toward longer-term residency over several years. You must actually live in Portugal (around 183 days a year) to keep the status. There is also the older D7 for those with passive or stable income. Rules and thresholds change often, so confirm current requirements with official Portuguese government sources (AIMA) or a qualified immigration lawyer before applying.

Best Time to Visit

Lisbon has a Mediterranean climate: warm, dry summers and mild, occasionally rainy winters that rarely turn truly cold. Summer (June to August) is hot and sunny, with August highs around 29°C (84°F) and occasional heat spikes past 35°C (95°F); it is also the most crowded and expensive stretch. Winters are gentle, with January lows around 8°C (46°F) and November the wettest month. The sweet spot for remote work is spring (April to June) and early autumn (September to October), when the weather is warm, the light is excellent, crowds thin out, and short-term rents ease.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Excellent, cheap, reliable internet and a mature coworking scene.
  • Pro: Mild climate, walkable center, and easy airport access.
  • Pro: Large international nomad community and widely spoken English.
  • Pro: A clear long-stay path via the D8 visa.
  • Con: Rent has risen fast and now eats a big share of any budget.
  • Con: Old apartments are often damp, poorly heated, and cold in winter.
  • Con: Steep hills and slippery cobbles, plus heavy summer tourist crowds.
  • Con: Bureaucracy (residency, banking) can be slow and frustrating.

Who Lisbon Is Best For

Lisbon suits mid-budget remote workers who want reliable infrastructure, a big international community, a mild climate, and a realistic path to staying long-term. If you earn a solid remote income and value comfort and connectivity over rock-bottom costs, it is one of Europe's strongest bases. Skip it if you are chasing cheap rent, need a quiet base far from tourists, or dislike cold, damp apartments in winter; a smaller Portuguese city or a lower-cost country will serve you better.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lisbon

How much does it cost to live in Lisbon as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $3,500 a month in Lisbon for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Lisbon?

Lisbon rates 8 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work excellent. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Lisbon safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Lisbon is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Lisbon a good city for digital nomads overall?

Lisbon stands out for climate and a large nomad community among remote workers. With a typical budget near $3,500 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
