Setting Up as a Nomad in Fes

Morocco's ancient imperial city, a labyrinthine medieval medina and the world's oldest university. Plan on roughly $950 a month. Its strengths are culture and food, while the size of the nomad scene is where it scores lower.

Cost of Living in Fes

Fes is one of Morocco's more affordable major cities, gentler on the wallet than Marrakech and far cheaper than Europe just across the strait. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $800 to $1,300 a month. A furnished apartment in the modern Ville Nouvelle or a restored room in a medina riad costs about $300 to $600, and street food and local restaurants are inexpensive. These are our editorial estimates in US dollars, priced against the dirham, and eating in local spots and choosing the Ville Nouvelle over a tourist-facing riad keeps daily costs comfortably low.

  • Furnished apartment, Ville Nouvelle: roughly $300 to $600 a month
  • Bowl of harira or a tagine at a local spot: roughly $2 to $5
  • Petit taxi across town: roughly $1 to $2
  • Train to Rabat or Casablanca: roughly $8 to $15

Where to Work in Fes

Fes is a heritage city rather than a developed nomad hub, so its work scene is thinner than Marrakech's or Rabat's. Internet is decent in the Ville Nouvelle, with fibre in many modern apartments, but connectivity inside the ancient medina can be patchy, so check carefully before renting there. Dedicated coworking is limited, but Morocco's strong cafe culture means plentiful places to sit with a coffee and a laptop, especially in the newer part of town. A Maroc Telecom, Orange or Inwi SIM gives cheap, reliable mobile data as backup. Fes suits a focused worker who values atmosphere over a bustling coworking community.

Getting Around Fes

Fes divides into the ancient medina and the French-built Ville Nouvelle, and getting around differs completely between them. The medina, Fes el-Bali, is a vast car-free maze of alleys where you walk and goods still move by handcart and mule, so a good sense of direction or a map app helps enormously. In the Ville Nouvelle and between districts, cheap petit taxis are the easiest option, with shared grand taxis for longer routes. Fes sits on Morocco's excellent rail network, with comfortable trains to Rabat, Casablanca, Marrakech and Tangier, and its airport connects to several European cities.

Visas for Fes

Morocco is straightforward for many travellers: citizens of the EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia and numerous other countries enter visa-free as tourists for up to 90 days. For longer stays, you can apply for a residence permit (carte de sejour) through the local authorities, which involves paperwork and proof of funds or purpose, or leave and re-enter, though relying on repeated entries is risky. Morocco does not yet have a dedicated digital nomad visa, so confirm the current rules and any extension process with official Moroccan channels or a consulate before planning a long stay, and note your permitted period to avoid overstaying.

Best Time to Visit Fes

Fes has a Mediterranean-influenced climate with hot summers and cool, wetter winters, shaped by its inland position. The best times to visit are spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to November, when temperatures are pleasant for exploring the medina on foot and the surrounding countryside is green or golden. Summer, from June to August, can be very hot, with the medina's enclosed lanes trapping the heat. Winter is cool and damp, with chilly nights and occasional rain, though rarely freezing. Aim for the shoulder seasons to enjoy the city and its endless walking in comfort.

Pros and Cons of Fes

Fes offers extraordinary living history at a low price, with the friction of an ancient city.

  • Pros: one of the world's best-preserved medieval medinas, a UNESCO-listed labyrinth of craft, food and history, very affordable living, superb Moroccan cuisine, excellent rail links, and an authenticity that touristy Marrakech has partly lost.
  • Cons: a small nomad community, limited coworking and patchy medina internet, limited English with French and Arabic dominant, persistent touts and hassle in the tourist areas, hot summers, and a conservative pace with modest nightlife.

Who Fes Is For

Fes is for the culturally immersive nomad who wants to live inside living history rather than a modern nomad bubble. Lovers of Islamic art and architecture, artisans, food enthusiasts and anyone captivated by the medieval medina will find it endlessly rewarding, and the low costs stretch a modest income far. It helps to have some French and a tolerance for hustle and getting lost in the lanes. It is a weaker fit for those who need a big coworking community, reliable medina internet, widespread English or a lively nightlife. Base yourself in the Ville Nouvelle for comfort or a riad for romance, come in spring or autumn, and let one of the world's great old cities unfold on foot.

Frequently Asked Questions About Fes

How much does it cost to live in Fes as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Fes is around $950 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Fes?

Fes rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Fes safe for digital nomads?

Fes scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Fes a good city for digital nomads overall?

Fes stands out for culture and its food scene among remote workers. With a typical budget near $950 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
