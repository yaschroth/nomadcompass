Cost of Living in Windhoek

Windhoek sits at the affordable-to-moderate end of the African capitals spectrum, though it is noticeably pricier than smaller Southern African cities. Prices are quoted in Namibian dollars (NAD); the exchange rate hovers around NAD 18-19 per USD as of mid-2025.

  • Rent, central (CBD/Klein Windhoek): NAD 7,500-15,000/mo (USD 400-800) for a one-bedroom apartment
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Khomasdal, Otjomuise): NAD 4,500-7,000/mo (USD 250-380)
  • Groceries: NAD 2,500-4,000/mo (USD 135-220) for a single person cooking at home
  • Eating out: NAD 80-160 per meal at a local restaurant (USD 4-9); sit-down bistros run NAD 200-350 (USD 11-19)
  • Coworking hot-desk: NAD 350-550/day (USD 19-29) or NAD 2,800-3,600/mo (USD 150-200) on a monthly plan
  • Local transport (combis + occasional taxi): NAD 900-1,800/mo (USD 50-100) depending on how often you use rideshares
  • Mobile data: NAD 300-800/mo (USD 16-43) for a generous MTC or Telecom Namibia prepaid data bundle

Total realistic range: NAD 16,000-28,000/mo (USD 860-1,500) covers comfortable solo living. You can dip below NAD 14,000 if you take a room share and cook most meals.

Where to Work

The coworking scene is small but functional. DoBox on Robert Mugabe Avenue is the most established dedicated space, with a direct fiber connection, standing desks, meeting rooms, and free coffee. Regus at Maerua Mall (third floor) offers a corporate-style alternative with day-pass access. WildWifi, based at Hotel Uhland in central Windhoek, runs coliving retreats with a 150 Mbit fiber connection, air-conditioned workspaces, and a pool; note it requires a minimum four-week stay and runs retreat-style with a set start date.

Cafe-work culture is growing but uneven. Seattle Coffee Company branches (Klein Windhoek on Independence Ave; The Grove Mall) have plugs and WiFi and are genuinely laptop-friendly. Photizo Patisserie in Klein Windhoek is quieter and well-rated for focused work. Krisjans Bistro is another good option with stable WiFi.

The real WiFi situation: MTC 4G is the most reliable connection in the city, hitting 20-40 Mbps in the CBD and Klein Windhoek. Cafe WiFi is functional but can slow to a crawl during busy lunch periods. A local SIM with an MTC data bundle is your best backup.

Getting Around

Windhoek's centre rewards walking during daylight hours. Independence Avenue and the streets around it are navigable on foot, but the city sprawls quickly once you leave the core and has no meaningful public bus grid.

Combis (shared minibus taxis) are the workhorse for locals, spreading from the central taxi rank to most neighborhoods for very low fares, but routes and schedules are informal and can be confusing for newcomers. Uber operates in Windhoek and is the most convenient option after dark or for cross-town trips; fares are low by international standards. Street taxis are available but negotiate the fare before you get in.

The airport (Hosea Kutako International) sits about 45 km east of the city centre. Pre-booked private transfers are the most reliable option and cost around NAD 500-700 (USD 27-38). Shared shuttles run NAD 300-400 per person (USD 16-22). A basic bus service exists on flight days for around NAD 150 (USD 8) but wait times can be long. There is no direct rail link.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Namibia changed its entry rules in April 2025. Visa-free entry for nationals of 33 countries was abolished; those citizens can now apply for an e-Visa online in advance. Many European, Southern African, and Commonwealth passport holders still receive a visa on arrival or e-Visa valid for 30 to 90 days depending on the inspector's decision and your planned length of stay.

For longer stays, Namibia launched a Digital Nomad Visa that allows remote workers to live and work in the country for up to six months. You need proof of at least USD 2,000/month in income from a non-Namibian source, a motivation letter from the Namibia Investment Promotion and Development Board (NIPDB), a police clearance certificate, health insurance, and a medical certificate. The visa fee is approximately NAD 2,200-3,300 (USD 120-180) payable on arrival after you receive NIPDB approval. Extensions beyond six months are not straightforward.

Always verify the latest rules with the official NIPDB site and your country's Namibian embassy before travel, as entry requirements have been changing.

Best Time to Visit

Windhoek sits at 1,700 metres above sea level in Namibia's central highlands, which tempers what could otherwise be a very harsh desert climate. This altitude means even the hot months are drier and less oppressive than coastal or lowland Africa.

May to September is the sweet spot for remote work. The dry, cool winter brings daytime temperatures of 20-25 degrees Celsius, zero rainfall, low humidity (as low as 23%), and clear blue skies. Power is more stable, internet infrastructure stays reliable, and the pleasant temperatures make a long desk day feel less punishing.

October to November is warm and still dry, good for work but getting hot (30+ degrees midday). December to March is the rainy season: afternoon thunderstorms roll through most days and temperatures push above 35 degrees Celsius. The rain rarely lasts more than an hour but heat and humidity spike. Connectivity holds in the city but it is not the most comfortable working environment. April is a transition month, warm and still partly rainy, but pleasant.

Pros and Cons

  • English is widely spoken and easy to navigate daily life
  • Political stability and one of Africa's most peaceful democracies
  • World-class nature on the doorstep: Etosha, the Namib, and the Skeleton Coast are all driveable
  • Small, manageable city with low traffic and no urban chaos
  • Fiber internet available in established coworking spaces
  • Good grocery selection including South African and European imports
  • Not walkable beyond the centre and you will need transport for most errands
  • Small nomad community: limited ready-made social scenes compared to Bali or Lisbon
  • Airport is 45 km away: transfers add cost and time to every trip
  • Petty crime is real: phone grabs, bag snatching, and car break-ins require active vigilance
  • Coworking options are thin: two or three real spaces, none with the density of a larger city
  • Internet outside coworking spaces can be unreliable; outages occur and cafe WiFi is inconsistent

Who Windhoek Is Best For

Windhoek is a strong fit if you want a calm, low-stress African base without the overwhelming density of Nairobi or Lagos. It suits nomads who genuinely want to explore Southern Africa: Namibia's landscapes are extraordinary, and using Windhoek as a hub gives you easy access to them on long weekends. It also works well for anyone who finds East African cities too hectic or West African cities too expensive.

You will do best here if you are comfortable working independently, comfortable building your own social circle rather than stepping into an existing one, and happy to rent a car for the best experiences. It rewards those who want something genuinely different from the beaten nomad trail.

Skip Windhoek if you need a buzzing co-working scene with daily events and easy networking, if reliable high-speed internet without a backup plan is non-negotiable, or if you want a tropical climate. It is also not ideal if you expect to walk everywhere or rely on public transport for longer stays.

Frequently Asked Questions About Windhoek

How much does it cost to live in Windhoek as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,500 a month in Windhoek for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Windhoek?

Windhoek rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Windhoek safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Windhoek is 6/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Windhoek a good city for digital nomads overall?

Windhoek stands out for nature and clean air among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,500 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
