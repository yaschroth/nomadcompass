Setting Up as a Nomad in Medellín

The city of eternal spring, where innovation meets Latin warmth. For remote workers, Medellín leans on climate and a large nomad community, with a typical budget near $1,800 a month. The main trade-off is the language barrier.

Cost of Living in Medellín

Medellín is one of the better-value nomad bases in Latin America, though prices in the popular areas have climbed as more remote workers arrive. At roughly 3,900 COP to 1 USD in 2025, here is a realistic monthly budget for one remote worker living comfortably.

  • Rent (furnished 1-bed): In a nomad-heavy part of El Poblado you'll pay around 3,900,000-9,000,000 COP ($1,000-2,300) on short-to-mid-term platforms. In Laureles or Envigado the same apartment runs closer to 2,700,000-5,800,000 COP ($700-1,500). Cheaper barrios like Belén go lower still.
  • Utilities and internet: Roughly 350,000-700,000 COP ($90-180). Fibre internet is widely available. Air conditioning is rarely needed, which keeps bills down.
  • Groceries: Around 700,000-1,200,000 COP ($180-310) if you cook regularly and buy produce at markets rather than only imported goods.
  • Eating out: A set lunch (menú del día) runs 15,000-25,000 COP ($4-6). A dinner for two at a mid-range restaurant is 90,000-160,000 COP ($23-40).
  • Coworking hot-desk: A monthly hot-desk membership is about 350,000-700,000 COP ($90-180). Day passes are 20,000-70,000 COP ($5-18).
  • Local transport: Metro rides are under 3,500 COP (well under $1). Budget 150,000-350,000 COP ($40-90) monthly including occasional ride-hailing.

A solo nomad with a mid-range lifestyle typically spends $1,200-1,800 per month. Choosing newer housing in El Poblado, a premium coworking plan, and frequent dining out pushes that to $2,200-3,500.

Best Neighborhoods for Remote Workers

El Poblado

The default landing spot for most nomads. You get the highest concentration of coworking spaces, international restaurants, cafes, and nightlife, plus the best English coverage. It is hilly, the priciest area, and can feel like an expat bubble with less local flavour. It suits first-timers who want convenience and an easy social scene.

Laureles

Flat, walkable, tree-lined, and more residential, Laureles gives you a more Colombian day-to-day at lower rents than El Poblado. It has a stable mid-term rental market, plenty of cafes and parks, and a growing set of coworking options. It suits nomads who want value and a local feel without giving up safety or amenities.

Envigado

A separate, quieter municipality just south of El Poblado, Envigado is calmer, more family-oriented, and less tourist-dense. It suits longer-stay remote workers who prioritise a peaceful base over walkable nightlife.

Manila

A small, leafy pocket bordering El Poblado, Manila is known for independent cafes and a more low-key vibe while keeping you close to the action. It suits cafe-workers who want the El Poblado location without the loudest streets.

Where to Work

Medellín has 50-plus coworking spaces. In El Poblado you'll find WeWork and Tinkko (known for panoramic valley views from upper floors of Golden Mile towers) for a corporate feel, and Selina near Manila for a relaxed, community-driven nomad crowd. Laureles has a good spread of smaller, community-focused spaces if you're based on that side of the city.

Cafe-work culture is strong, especially in Laureles and Manila, where independent coffee shops welcome laptops. On connectivity: fibre internet in apartments and coworking spaces is generally fast and reliable, but power and internet can drop briefly during heavy rain, so a coworking membership or a mobile data backup is worth having if your work can't tolerate interruptions.

Getting Around

Medellín has Colombia's only metro, and it's clean, cheap, and safe, with rides under 3,500 COP using a rechargeable Cívica card. The integrated Metrocable gondolas extend the network up into the hillside comunas (Line K to Santo Domingo, Line J to San Javier near Comuna 13, and Line L out to Parque Arví). The city also runs the free EnCicla bike-share from metro stations.

For door-to-door trips, use apps like Uber, DiDi, or Cabify, or InDrive, which are cheap (a cross-town ride is about $3-6) and safer than hailing a random street taxi. Laureles and Envigado are pleasantly walkable, while El Poblado is steep in parts.

Note the two airports. José María Córdova (MDE) in Rionegro handles international and most flights and sits about 40-45 minutes away via the Túnel de Oriente. An official white taxi into the city is a flat 118,000 COP (around $32), while the Combuses shuttle to Centro Comercial San Diego is roughly 20,000 COP. The small Olaya Herrera (EOH) airport is right in the city and handles regional turboprop flights only.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia, and many other countries get a tourist entry stamp on arrival, valid for up to 90 days. You can extend once for another 90 days online through Migración Colombia (about 143,000 COP, roughly $38), for a maximum of 180 days per calendar year. Apply about 10 days before your first 90 days end to avoid a fine.

For longer stays there's the Visa V for Digital Nomads, valid for up to two years. You'll need to work remotely for foreign clients or an employer, show income of at least three times Colombia's minimum wage (around 4,270,000 COP or about $1,100 per month in 2025), and carry health insurance covering Colombia. It cannot be renewed, so you re-apply or switch to another visa type when it expires. Always confirm current requirements with official Colombian government sources before you apply, as rules and fees change.

Best Time to Visit

Sitting at about 1,500 metres, Medellín earns its "City of Eternal Spring" nickname, with year-round temperatures around 18-27°C (64-80°F) and no real winter or summer. The altitude keeps it mild, so you rarely need heating or air conditioning.

The drier windows are roughly December to March and June to August, which are the most comfortable for getting around and working. The wetter stretches (April to May and September to November) bring frequent afternoon showers, though these are usually short and rarely derail a work day. For remote work, any month is workable, but December to February pairs the best weather with the liveliest festival season.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Excellent climate all year, with no need for heating or A/C.
  • Pro: Strong value, with rents and daily costs well below North America and Western Europe.
  • Pro: Large, established nomad community and plenty of coworking and cafe options.
  • Pro: Clean, cheap metro plus affordable ride-hailing.
  • Con: Safety varies by area and situation. Petty theft, phone snatching, and drink-spiking (including scopolamine) are real risks, especially around nightlife and dating apps. The local advice, "no dar papaya" (don't give an easy opportunity), applies.
  • Con: The experience is very neighborhood-dependent, and popular areas have grown pricey and touristy.
  • Con: Spanish is essential outside El Poblado, and Colombian bureaucracy can be slow.
  • Con: Rainy-season showers and occasional internet or power blips during storms.

Who Medellín Is Best For

Medellín suits the remote worker who wants great weather, low costs, a big social scene, and city energy without leaving Latin America. It's ideal if you'll base in Laureles, El Poblado, or Envigado, use ride-hailing at night, and put some effort into Spanish. It's a strong fit for first-time nomads who want an easy, well-trodden landing, and for social people who value community over solitude.

Skip it if you need guaranteed, uninterrupted connectivity for critical work, want a small quiet town rather than a big city, are uncomfortable staying street-smart about safety, or are looking for a beach base, since Medellín is landlocked in the mountains.

Frequently Asked Questions About Medellín

How much does it cost to live in Medellín as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Medellín is around $1,800 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Medellín?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Medellín is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Medellín safe for digital nomads?

Medellín scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Medellín a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Medellín is worth a look if you value climate and a large nomad community. Costs sit near $1,800 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
