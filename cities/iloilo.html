Why Iloilo Works for Remote Work

City of love with growing nomad scene. For remote workers, Iloilo leans on low costs and climate, with a typical budget near $900 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Cost of Living in Iloilo

Iloilo is one of the most affordable mid-size cities in the Philippines. A comfortable solo nomad budget sits between PHP 35,000 and PHP 60,000 per month (roughly $600 to $1,050 USD), depending heavily on how you rent and how often you eat out.

  • Rent, 1BR city center (Mandurriao, Jaro): PHP 12,000 to 18,000/month ($210 to $315 USD)
  • Rent, 1BR cheaper areas (La Paz, Lapuz): PHP 7,000 to 12,000/month ($120 to $210 USD)
  • Groceries: PHP 8,000 to 12,000/month ($140 to $210 USD)
  • Eating out, budget Filipino meal: PHP 100 to 180 per meal ($1.75 to $3.15 USD)
  • Eating out, mid-range restaurant: PHP 350 to 700 per meal ($6 to $12 USD)
  • Coworking hot desk: PHP 3,000 to 5,000/month ($52 to $87 USD); day passes around PHP 200 to 350
  • Local transport (jeepneys, tricycles): PHP 300 to 600/month ($5 to $10 USD)
  • Total realistic range: PHP 32,000 to 55,000/month ($560 to $960 USD)

Utility bills for a furnished apartment typically add PHP 2,000 to 4,000/month. Fast food and mall dining have crept up in price; cooking at home or eating at the public market keeps costs low.

Always verify current prices on arrival. Exchange rates shift and local inflation has been running at 3 to 5 percent annually.

Where to Work

Iloilo has a small but functional coworking scene anchored around the Mandurriao business district, with a secondary cluster in Jaro.

  • Bonds Coworking (Diversion Road, Jaro) is the most established option: unlimited coffee, ergonomic chairs, 24/7 access, and reliable fiber. Day passes and monthly plans available.
  • Co+Lab Coworking Laboratory (also Diversion Road, Jaro) is the most affordable, with student-friendly hourly rates from PHP 25 and good community energy.
  • Regus Iloilo (3F Festive Walk Mall, Mandurriao) offers a corporate-grade hot desk in a mall setting: professional but pricier at around PHP 8,000 to 10,000/month.
  • Thinkspace (Jalandoni Street, Jaro) is a relaxed study hub that also serves food, good for shorter sessions.

Cafe-working is possible but uneven. Chains like Starbucks have consistent but not fast wifi. Independent cafes such as Rainy Days Cafe and Dova's offer quieter environments, though speeds vary. Expect 20 to 50 Mbps at better cafes; peak hours slow things down. If you have video calls, book a coworking desk rather than gambling on cafe wifi.

Home fiber internet (PLDT or Converge) reaches 50 to 100 Mbps in most residential areas of Mandurriao and Jaro. Installation can take two to four weeks, so arrange it early if you plan a longer stay.

Getting Around

Iloilo is not a walking city in the traditional sense: the heat makes covering more than a few blocks on foot uncomfortable during midday. That said, within individual districts like Mandurriao or Jaro, most daily errands are manageable without a vehicle.

  • Jeepneys: 25 fixed routes cover the city. Fares start at PHP 13 to 15 per ride, making them the cheapest way to move between districts. Routes are posted at stops but the system takes a few days to learn.
  • Tricycles and pedicabs: Good for short hops within a barangay. Negotiate the fare before you get in; PHP 30 to 60 is typical for a short ride.
  • Grab: GrabCar launched in Iloilo in 2023. It works reliably in Mandurriao and the Business Park area, less reliably in outer districts. Fares run PHP 80 to 200 for most city trips.
  • Bike lanes: Iloilo has dedicated lanes along Diversion Road and the Esplanade, and is considered one of the more bike-friendly cities in the Philippines.
  • Airport to city: Iloilo International Airport is in Santa Barbara, about 20 to 25 km from Mandurriao. UV Express shuttle vans run to SM City Iloilo for around PHP 100 to 150. Grab from the airport costs roughly PHP 250 to 400 depending on destination. Taxis are available but metered fares to the city center often reach PHP 500 to 700.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

The Philippines grants visa-free entry on arrival to citizens of most Western countries, ASEAN nations, the US, EU, Japan, South Korea, and many others. The initial stay is typically 30 days, confirmed at the port of entry.

You can extend your stay at any Bureau of Immigration (BI) office, including the one in Iloilo City. Extensions are granted in increments of one to two months, with fees around PHP 3,000 to 4,000 per extension. The theoretical maximum under tourist status is 36 months, though most nomads extend a few months at a time.

There is no official digital nomad visa for the Philippines as of mid-2026. You stay as a tourist and extend locally. An onward ticket and six months of passport validity are required on entry.

Confirm current visa rules directly with the Philippine Bureau of Immigration (immigration.gov.ph) or your nearest Philippine embassy before travel.

Best Time to Visit

Iloilo has a tropical monsoonal climate with temperatures holding between 26 and 29 degrees Celsius year-round. What changes is the rain.

Best window for remote work: November through April. This is the dry season, with March and April being the driest months. Sunshine is reliable, typhoon risk is low, and the heat is manageable in the mornings and evenings.

The wet season runs May through October. July is typically the wettest month, with heavy daily rain and a real risk of typhoon disruption. Flooding can affect low-lying parts of the city and temporarily knock out power. If you arrive during the wet season, expect some downtime and keep a backup mobile data plan for power or connectivity outages.

The Dinagyang Festival (late January) draws large crowds and hotel prices rise. Book accommodation early if you plan to visit during this period. It is worth seeing at least once.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Low cost of living compared to Cebu, Manila, or most Southeast Asian alternatives
  • English is widely spoken and locals are genuinely welcoming to foreigners
  • Much safer than Manila, with crime dropping steadily in recent years
  • Strong local food culture centered on La Paz batchoy, fresh seafood, and Ilonggo cuisine
  • Bike-friendly infrastructure and a pleasant riverfront esplanade
  • Direct flights to Manila, Cebu, and a growing number of domestic routes
  • University city atmosphere: young, energetic, good cafe density

Cons

  • The coworking scene is small; options outside Mandurriao and Jaro are limited
  • Typhoon season (June to October) brings real disruption risk, not just rain
  • No direct international flights; you must connect through Manila or Cebu
  • Nighttime street safety requires more caution than daytime; walking alone after dark is not advisable in most areas
  • Airport is 20 to 25 km from the city center, adding friction to frequent travel
  • Internet infrastructure outside Mandurriao and Jaro can be patchy
  • Limited nomad community compared to Cebu or Chiang Mai

Who Iloilo Is Best For

Iloilo rewards nomads who prioritize a low monthly burn, genuine local culture, and a slower pace over a busy social scene or extensive coworking infrastructure. If you are happy to explore a mid-size Filipino city that most foreigners skip, budget PHP 35,000 to 45,000 a month, and can tolerate some logistics friction around flights and transit, Iloilo delivers excellent value.

It suits people doing solo deep-work stints, those already familiar with Southeast Asian city living, food-curious travelers who want to eat well cheaply, and anyone who wants Cebu-level amenities at a noticeably lower price point.

It is a harder sell if you need a large nomad community around you, depend on fast and reliable coworking infrastructure within walking distance of housing, require frequent international connections, or plan to arrive during typhoon season without a flexible schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions About Iloilo

How much does it cost to live in Iloilo as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Iloilo is around $900 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Iloilo?

With a WiFi score of 6/10, connectivity in Iloilo is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Iloilo safe for digital nomads?

Iloilo scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Iloilo a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Iloilo is worth a look if you value low costs and climate. Costs sit near $900 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
