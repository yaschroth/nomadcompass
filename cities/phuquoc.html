Phu Quoc at a Glance for Remote Workers

Vietnam's island paradise with 30-day visa-free for everyone. What pulls nomads here is nature, backed by easy visas and low costs. Budget around $1,300 a month. The culture scene is the weak spot.

Cost of Living in Phu Quoc

Phu Quoc sits below most Southeast Asian beach destinations in cost, but it has crept up as tourism infrastructure has expanded. Budget carefully and you can live well for under $1,000/month; spend more freely and $1,400 covers a comfortable setup with air-con, beach access, and regular restaurant meals.

  • Rent, central Duong Dong (long-term): 5,000,000-8,000,000 VND/month ($190-310), negotiated in person directly with landlords
  • Rent, quieter areas (An Thoi, Cua Duong): 3,500,000-5,500,000 VND/month ($135-210), often includes basic utilities
  • Groceries (self-catering): 1,500,000-2,500,000 VND/month ($58-96) at local markets and Vinmart
  • Eating out: Street food 30,000-50,000 VND ($1.15-1.90) per meal; sit-down Vietnamese restaurants 80,000-150,000 VND ($3-6); Western-style spots 200,000-400,000 VND ($8-15)
  • Coworking hot-desk: Focus Pocus charges approximately 7,500,000 VND/month ($290); cafe working is essentially free with a coffee purchase
  • Local transport: Motorbike rental 120,000-150,000 VND/day ($4.60-5.75); Grab ride within Duong Dong 30,000-60,000 VND ($1.15-2.30); monthly fuel if you rent long-term adds roughly 300,000-500,000 VND ($12-19)
  • SIM card with data: 180,000-260,000 VND/month ($7-10) for a generous data plan

Realistic monthly total: $700-1,000 on a lean budget; $1,100-1,400 living comfortably. Airbnb rates are significantly higher than locally negotiated rentals, so book at least one month ahead and contact landlords directly on arrival.

Where to Work

Phu Quoc is not a coworking hub. There is one dedicated coworking space on the island and a handful of cafes where remote workers park themselves for hours. Plan around this before you commit to a long stay.

Coworking Space

Focus Pocus is Phu Quoc's only established coworking space. Located on Tran Hung Dao Street in Duong Dong, it has a dedicated deep-work room, a rooftop terrace with ocean views, a kitchen, and fiber WiFi. It is a small community space and has a monthly membership model. The vibe is more boutique than corporate, which suits most nomads fine.

Cafe Working

Cafe culture on the island is growing but still thin compared to Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Your most reliable options are Highlands Coffee at 10A Hung Vuong in Duong Dong (consistent WiFi, open until 11pm, air-conditioned) and Nora's Cafe and Restaurant south of Duong Dong (spacious, calm, strong coffee, good connection). Like Coffee near the central park is another quiet spot favored by locals and a few nomads.

WiFi Reality

Hotel and guesthouse WiFi ranges from 20 to 100 Mbps, generally adequate for video calls. Cafe WiFi is less predictable. A local SIM from Viettel or Mobifone (around 50 Mbps 4G) is your safety net and costs under $10/month. Power outages are infrequent but not unheard of, especially during the wet season, so a backup battery and hotspot habit are worth forming.

Getting Around

Phu Quoc is not walkable beyond the Duong Dong town center. The island is about 50 km long and major attractions, beaches, and most accommodation are spread out significantly. You need wheels.

Motorbike rental is the standard choice. Shops near the airport and throughout Duong Dong rent semi-automatics for 120,000-150,000 VND/day ($4.60-5.75). Traffic is manageable outside peak season and the roads are in decent condition. Most rental shops do not ask for an international driving permit, though technically you need one.

Grab and Xanh SM (electric ride-hailing) both operate on the island and are reliable in Duong Dong. A ride within town costs 30,000-60,000 VND; airport to Duong Dong runs 175,000-225,000 VND ($7-9).

VinBus runs free electric buses connecting the airport, Duong Dong, and VinWonders. It is useful for specific routes but does not cover the whole island.

Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) is 10 km from Duong Dong. Direct connections serve Ho Chi Minh City (1 hour), Hanoi (2 hours), and select regional routes. Getting to the mainland by ferry (Rach Gia or Ha Tien) is also possible if you want to travel without flying.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Vietnam offers a special visa-free arrangement specifically for Phu Quoc: visitors of all nationalities can enter and stay for up to 30 days without a visa, provided they arrive directly from outside Vietnam (i.e., not transiting through the mainland first) and depart to a country other than Vietnam. This is unique to Phu Quoc and does not apply to the rest of the country.

For longer stays, your main option is the 90-day e-visa, available to most nationalities online before travel, with multiple entry permitted. This works for three months in Vietnam, including Phu Quoc.

Vietnam does not currently offer a dedicated digital nomad or remote work visa. Once your e-visa expires, you need to exit and re-enter or apply through an agent for a business visa extension. Extensions on tourist visas are possible but require processing in Ho Chi Minh City and take at least two weeks to arrange.

Always verify entry requirements with the official Vietnam Immigration Department or your country's embassy before travel, as policies change.

Best Time to Visit

Phu Quoc has a clear two-season climate. The dry season runs from November through April, with temperatures of 27-32°C, low humidity, almost no rain, and calm Gulf waters. This is the most enjoyable window for remote work: reliable power, lower risk of weather disruptions, and the beach is genuinely swimable every day.

The wet season runs May through October. Rain usually comes in short, intense afternoon bursts rather than all-day downpours, so mornings are often workable. However, humidity is high, some beach bars and smaller venues close or reduce hours, and the sea gets choppy. Internet reliability dips slightly with storm activity.

For remote workers, November to February is the sweet spot: stable weather, good coworking conditions, and the island is at its most photogenic. March and April stay dry but temperatures rise toward 35°C. The shoulder months of October and April offer decent weather with fewer tourists and lower accommodation prices.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Very affordable cost of living, especially on a negotiated long-term rental
  • 30-day visa-free entry for all nationalities (no pre-application needed)
  • Beautiful beaches and national park right on your doorstep
  • Good 4G coverage island-wide; fiber available in most guesthouses
  • Slow pace, low stress, easy daily rhythm for focused work
  • Direct flights to Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi keep you connected
  • Fresh seafood at low prices is genuinely excellent

Cons

  • Only one dedicated coworking space; cafe culture is thin by Southeast Asian standards
  • Not walkable; you need a motorbike or regular ride-hail expenses
  • Wet season (May-October) brings humidity, downpours, and some venue closures
  • Limited nomad community compared to Chiang Mai, Bali, or Ho Chi Minh City
  • Medical facilities are basic; serious health issues require evacuation to the mainland
  • Alcohol and imported goods are noticeably more expensive than mainland Vietnam
  • No digital nomad visa; 90-day e-visa is the ceiling without a business arrangement

Who Phu Quoc Is Best For

Phu Quoc works best if you prioritize nature, quiet, and low cost over professional infrastructure. If you are a solo freelancer or remote employee who handles mostly async work, needs only occasional video calls, and wants to spend evenings at the beach rather than at a rooftop bar networking event, Phu Quoc delivers at a price that is hard to match in Southeast Asia.

It suits people doing a slow-travel stint of one to three months, taking advantage of the 90-day e-visa or the 30-day visa-free entry before moving on. Couples looking for a quieter island base, writers, designers, and developers who work independently will find the pace genuinely productive.

Skip Phu Quoc if you rely heavily on in-person coworking, frequent client meetings in shared professional spaces, or a strong expat social scene. It is also a poor fit if you need reliable access to quality healthcare, fast mainland logistics, or a deep bench of co-founders and tech peers. For those needs, Ho Chi Minh City or Da Nang will serve you far better.

Frequently Asked Questions About Phu Quoc

How much does it cost to live in Phu Quoc as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,300 a month in Phu Quoc for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Phu Quoc?

Phu Quoc rates 4 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work patchy. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Phu Quoc safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Phu Quoc is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Phu Quoc a good city for digital nomads overall?

Phu Quoc stands out for nature and easy visas among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,300 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
