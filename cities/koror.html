Setting Up as a Nomad in Koror

The Rock Islands, Jellyfish Lake, and the first country to make its whole sea a sanctuary. Expect $2,100 a month here. Nature and safety are the standout strengths, with the size of the nomad scene the main caveat.

Best Neighborhoods in Koror

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Koror town centre

$$$
Commercial & Central

The commercial middle of the country

The main settlement of Palau along the road through Koror island, holding the shops, the banks, the government offices that remain here after the capital moved to Melekeok, the Belau National Museum and most of the country's restaurants and hotels.

Best for: Anyone who wants shops, restaurants and dive operators nearby
Pros
  • Everything commercial in the country is here
  • The Belau National Museum and the Etpison Museum
  • Most restaurants and dive shop offices within a short radius
Cons
  • The single main road carries all the traffic
  • Little architectural interest
  • Prices reflect a small island importing almost everything

Malakal

$$$
Working Port & Diving

The port island where the dive boats leave from

The small island connected by causeway southwest of Koror, holding the commercial port, the fuel depot, most of the dive operators and their boats, and a handful of hotels and restaurants along the water. Almost every dive in Palau starts from a dock here.

Best for: Divers, who are most of the people who come here
Pros
  • Every dive boat in the country leaves from these docks
  • Waterfront restaurants and hotels
  • Five minutes from Koror town by road
Cons
  • A working port, with the noise and traffic that implies
  • Limited residential accommodation
  • Little to do other than dive

Meyuns

$$
Residential & Local

A residential village on the western causeway

A quiet residential settlement west of Koror town on the way to Malakal, holding ordinary Palauan housing, a school and a small waterfront. It is where a good share of the country's population lives, and it is calmer and cheaper than the town centre.

Best for: Longer stays that want a settled street rather than a hotel strip
Pros
  • Cheaper than the town centre
  • A genuine residential community
  • Close to both Koror town and the Malakal docks
Cons
  • Very few shops or restaurants
  • Nothing aimed at visitors
  • You need transport for everything

Airai on Babeldaob

$$
Rural & Connected

The airport state across the bridge

The state on the southern end of Babeldaob, Palau's large volcanic main island, reached by the Japan-Palau Friendship Bridge and holding the international airport, a traditional bai meeting house and the start of the road that runs up the island to the new capital.

Best for: Anyone who wants space, greenery and the airport nearby
Pros
  • More space and lower rents than Koror
  • The airport and the road up Babeldaob start here
  • One of the surviving traditional bai meeting houses
Cons
  • Ten kilometres from Koror across the bridge
  • Almost no shops or restaurants
  • Aircraft noise near the airport

Cost of Living in Koror

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Koror, so the $2,100 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 981 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $2,100 a month as a solo remote worker in Koror, which is expensive and reflects a Pacific island nation of eighteen thousand people that imports almost everything. A one-bedroom place runs roughly $800 to $1,300. Groceries carry the full Pacific freight markup and fresh produce is limited. Palau uses the United States dollar, which removes any conversion. Diving is the line that dominates a real budget here, along with the environmental fees the country charges visitors. This is our own estimate rather than a reconciliation against measured data.

Where to Work in Koror

Palau's connectivity improved substantially with the Belau submarine cable and a second connection added since, and fibre reaches Koror at workable speeds, though prices are high and outages happen. Mobile data covers the populated islands. Power is reliable in Koror and expensive. There is essentially no coworking. Palauan and English are both official and English is used throughout government, business and education, which makes Palau one of the easiest Pacific nations to operate in. The country sits nine hours ahead of London. Anyone working with Australia will find the offset small, at an hour behind eastern Australian time, which makes that the practical business overlap.

Getting Around Koror

Koror is the country's main town and is compact enough to move around by taxi or the occasional bus, though most visitors rent a car. A causeway connects Koror to Babeldaob, the much larger and almost empty main island, where a single ring road passes the new capital at Ngerulmud, a purpose-built complex serving a country of eighteen thousand. Everything that matters, though, is by boat: the Rock Islands, the dive sites and Jellyfish Lake are all reached from the Koror harbours. Babeldaob also holds the Ngardmau waterfall and the stone monoliths at Badrulchau, both of which almost no visitor reaches.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Koror

Palau grants visa-free entry to citizens of most countries for thirty days, extendable in-country, and to United States citizens indefinitely under the Compact of Free Association. On arrival every visitor must sign the Palau Pledge, stamped into the passport, a commitment to act responsibly toward the environment addressed to the children of Palau, which is the only such requirement anywhere. There is no remote work visa. An environmental impact fee is charged on departure and a separate permit is needed for the Rock Islands. Anyone diving should budget for the Rock Islands permit and the Jellyfish Lake surcharge separately, since both are charged in addition to the dive itself.

Best Time to Visit Koror

Palau is warm and humid all year at around thirty degrees, and the calendar turns on rain and wind. November to April is the drier half with the calmest seas and the best diving visibility, and it is the busy season. May to October is wetter, and while Palau sits at the edge of the western Pacific typhoon belt it is struck far less often than the Philippines or Guam. Diving is possible year-round and the manta and shark aggregations at German Channel and Blue Corner are not seasonal. Anyone hoping to dive Blue Corner should be aware it is a current dive requiring a reef hook, and operators will ask about logged experience.

Pros and Cons of Koror

Palau made its entire exclusive economic zone a marine sanctuary in 2015, closing eighty per cent of it to commercial fishing, and the diving that results is regularly called the best in the world: Blue Corner, German Channel, the Ulong Channel and dozens of Second World War wrecks. The Rock Islands are a UNESCO World Heritage site and Jellyfish Lake holds millions of stingless jellyfish. Against that, it is expensive, very small, English-speaking but extremely remote, and there is essentially nothing to do that is not on or under the water.

Who Koror Is For

Koror suits a diver, and like Bonaire the honest answer is that it suits almost nobody else. For someone who wants world-class diving with English throughout and the dollar as currency, Palau is exceptional and the country has organised its entire environmental policy around keeping it that way. It is a poor fit for anyone who does not dive, anyone on a budget, and anyone who needs variety, since the islands are small and the flights in and out are few and long. It also suits anyone interested in Pacific war history, since the Peleliu battlefield south of Koror is largely as it was left in 1944.

Frequently Asked Questions About Koror

How much does it cost to live in Koror as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Koror is around $2,100 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Koror?

Koror rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Koror safe for digital nomads?

Koror scores 9 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Koror a good city for digital nomads overall?

Koror stands out for nature and the outdoors and safety among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,100 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
