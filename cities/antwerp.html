Cost of Living in Antwerp

Antwerp sits firmly in the expensive tier of Western European cities. Expect to spend more than in Lisbon or Tbilisi, though slightly less than Amsterdam or Paris.

Monthly breakdown for a single person:

  • Rent, central (Meir, Het Zuid, Eilandje): EUR 1,300-1,900 / USD 1,400-2,050 for a studio or one-bedroom
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Borgerhout, Berchem, Hoboken): EUR 800-1,200 / USD 860-1,300
  • Groceries (cooking most meals): EUR 250-350 / USD 270-380 at Albert Heijn or Colruyt
  • Eating out (mix of lunch spots and sit-down dinners): EUR 300-500 / USD 325-540
  • Coworking hot desk: EUR 150-250 / USD 160-270 per month
  • Local transport (De Lijn tram/bus unlimited monthly pass): EUR 50-65 / USD 54-70
  • Utilities and internet (if not included in rent): EUR 100-180 / USD 108-195

Total realistic range: EUR 2,000-3,200 / USD 2,150-3,450 per month depending on neighborhood and lifestyle. Budget travelers sharing a flat can push this toward EUR 1,400-1,700, but you will feel the squeeze.

Where to Work

Antwerp has a solid coworking scene. Silversquare Antwerp Tower (Frankrijklei 5, near Central Station) is the most prominent dedicated coworking hub, with flexible hot desks, fast fiber, meeting rooms, and a strong community calendar. Fosbury and Sons (Mechelsesteenweg 180) occupies the WATT Tower and leans toward a design-forward creative crowd, with 24/7 access and a premium feel. Clockwise Antwerp (Olijftakstraat 7) sits in the renovated Maritiem Huis near the waterfront and gets strong marks for natural light and staff. Day passes and drop-in options are available at all three.

Cafe work culture is real here. Caffenation (Mechelsesteenweg 16) is a well-established specialty roaster with seating, power outlets, and reliable WiFi that draws a regular laptop crowd. Normo (Minderbroedersrui 30) is a micro-roastery near the student quarter with a relaxed vibe and a reputation as a good spot to hunker down.

WiFi quality across the city is generally strong. Most cafes offer free connections but you will want a backup SIM or hotspot during peak hours at busier spots. Fiber speeds at coworking spaces are consistently fast.

Getting Around

The center of Antwerp is compact and very walkable. Most key neighborhoods, from the old town around the Cathedral to Het Zuid and Eilandje, are reachable on foot in under 30 minutes. Cycling is practical and the city has invested heavily in protected lanes, though bike theft is common so use a quality lock.

Public transport is run by De Lijn and covers trams, buses, and a pre-metro (underground tram) network. Core stations include Groenplaats, Meir, and Opera. A single trip costs EUR 3; a monthly unlimited pass runs around EUR 50-65 and is worth it if you move around regularly. Buy tickets in advance at newsagents or supermarkets to save about 20% versus buying on the vehicle.

For the airport, you have two options. Antwerp Airport (ANR) is small but convenient: bus lines 51, 52, and 53 connect it to Antwerp-Berchem station in about 10 minutes. Brussels Airport (BRU) is the main international hub and is linked to Antwerp Central Station by the Diabolo train service, running twice per hour on weekdays and taking about 35 minutes.

Trains to Brussels take around 40 minutes; Ghent is also under an hour. Antwerp Central Station is a working architectural landmark and one of the most beautiful train stations in Europe.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Belgium is part of the Schengen Area. Citizens of the US, Canada, Australia, the UK, and most other Western countries can enter visa-free and stay up to 90 days within any 180-day period across the whole Schengen zone, not just Belgium.

Belgium has no dedicated digital nomad visa. If you want to stay beyond 90 days, your main options are the Type D long-stay visa (requires a specific purpose such as work, study, or family reunification) or the Professional Card for self-employed individuals who intend to actively conduct business in Belgium, which requires demonstrating a minimum annual income of roughly EUR 26,000. Processing times for long-stay applications are typically around 120 days. A handling fee of approximately USD 300 applies as of mid-2026.

Confirm current requirements directly with the Belgian embassy or via belgium.be before you travel.

Best Time to Visit

Antwerp has a maritime oceanic climate with mild temperatures year-round, consistent cloud cover, and rain distributed throughout all four seasons. There is no true dry season.

May through September is the best window for remote workers. Average highs reach 20-25 degrees Celsius (68-77 F) in summer, outdoor terrace culture is in full swing, and the city's festival calendar is busiest. Late May and early September are particularly good: pleasant temperatures, longer daylight hours, and fewer tourists than July and August.

October through March is the harder stretch. Days are short, skies are frequently grey, temperatures drop to near-freezing at night in January and February, and strong winds off the North Sea arrive from November onward. The city does not grind to a halt and coworking spaces stay busy, but be honest with yourself if grey weather affects your productivity.

Pros and Cons

Pros:

  • World-class food scene at every price point, from street falafel to two-Michelin-star dining
  • English is very widely spoken; day-to-day life is easy without Dutch
  • Compact, walkable city with excellent architecture and genuinely interesting neighborhoods
  • Strong coworking infrastructure with multiple premium spaces
  • Excellent rail connections to Brussels, Amsterdam, Paris, and London
  • High safety levels compared to other major European ports; low violent crime rate
  • Vibrant fashion, design, and arts culture gives the city real personality

Cons:

  • Cost of living is high; central rent is not far behind Amsterdam or London
  • Weather is reliably grey and rainy for much of the year
  • No digital nomad visa; 90-day Schengen limit applies to most passport holders
  • Some bureaucratic friction if you need to register or deal with local administration in Dutch
  • Petty theft (pickpocketing) is a real issue around Central Station and the Meir
  • In 2026, metro renovation works are expected to disrupt tram and pre-metro services

Who Antwerp Is Best For

Antwerp works well for nomads who value culture, food, and a genuinely interesting city over cost savings. If you are coming from a tech or creative background and want to feel plugged into a real urban environment rather than a nomad bubble, Antwerp delivers that. It suits people who can work a full European schedule and enjoy exploring a city on evenings and weekends without needing organized nomad meetups to feel connected.

It is a strong base for anyone using Schengen days strategically: you get a functional, well-connected Western European city without the tourist overwhelm of Amsterdam or Brussels. The rail links make it easy to do day trips or weekend trips across Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern France.

Skip Antwerp if budget is your primary constraint, if extended grey weather depresses your output, or if you need a warm-weather, beach-adjacent base. Nomads looking for a large English-speaking remote work community or structured coworking meetups will find the scene quieter than in Lisbon or Barcelona.

Frequently Asked Questions About Antwerp

How much does it cost to live in Antwerp as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $2,800 a month in Antwerp for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Antwerp?

Antwerp rates 8 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work excellent. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Antwerp safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Antwerp is 6/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Antwerp a good city for digital nomads overall?

Antwerp stands out for fast WiFi and English among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
