Setting Up as a Nomad in Rijeka

A working port city on the Kvarner, cheaper and more lived-in than the coast south of it. For remote workers, Rijeka leans on climate and nature, with a typical budget near $1,300 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Best Neighborhoods in Rijeka

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Korzo and the Centre

$$
Central & Urban

The main promenade between the port and the city tower

Rijeka's central pedestrian street runs parallel to the harbour under Austro-Hungarian facades, with the city tower, the market halls and the cafes along it. The bars in the streets behind carry the city's nightlife, and the port and station are both a few minutes away.

Best for: Anyone who wants the market, the bars and the ferries within a short walk
Pros
  • The fish and produce market halls nearby
  • Bars and restaurants throughout the back streets
  • Port, station and bus terminal all close
Cons
  • The highest rents in the city
  • Port and traffic noise
  • Little green space

Trsat

$$
Hilltop & Panoramic

Up the stairs to the castle, with the whole bay below

The hill above the city, reached by a long flight of baroque pilgrimage steps or by bus, holding the castle, the pilgrimage church and the university campus. The view over the Kvarner gulf is the best in Rijeka, and the district is quieter and greener than anything below.

Best for: Stays that want the castle, the view and the university campus
Pros
  • The best views over the gulf and the islands
  • Castle grounds and the university nearby
  • Quieter and cooler than the centre
Cons
  • The stairs are a serious daily commitment
  • Fewer shops and restaurants
  • Bus-dependent in bad weather

Bulevard and Belveder

$$
Period & Elevated

The Austro-Hungarian villa slopes above the centre

The hillside immediately behind the Korzo, laid out in the late nineteenth century with stepped streets and substantial villas built when Rijeka was Hungary's port. It is a short but steep walk from the centre, quieter, and the housing stock is the most characterful in the city.

Best for: Longer stays wanting period apartments and a view, with stairs accepted
Pros
  • Handsome period buildings and sea views
  • Five to ten minutes above the Korzo
  • Quieter than the seafront
Cons
  • Steep stepped streets everywhere
  • Renovation quality varies considerably
  • Limited parking

Zamet and the Western Districts

$
Residential & Affordable

Residential blocks west of the centre, best everyday value

The residential belt running west along the coast, with post-war and newer apartment blocks, supermarkets, schools and a sports hall, plus bus links along the coastal road. Rents here are the lowest in Rijeka and some blocks look straight out over the gulf.

Best for: Longer stays wanting a normal flat with a sea view at a low rent
Pros
  • The lowest rents in the city, some with sea views
  • Full everyday shopping and services
  • Frequent buses along the coast
Cons
  • Fifteen to twenty minutes into the centre
  • Block architecture with little character
  • Little nightlife of its own

Opatija (15 km west)

$$$$
Resort & Belle Epoque

The Habsburg riviera resort along the coast

The nineteenth century spa resort fifteen kilometres west, built when Vienna's aristocracy wintered here, with grand hotels, a seaside promenade running for twelve kilometres and Ucka rising directly behind. It is prettier and considerably more expensive than Rijeka, with frequent buses between them.

Best for: Stays that want the resort promenade and the mountain, with the city close
Pros
  • The Lungomare coastal promenade and the parks
  • Ucka nature park directly behind
  • Frequent buses into Rijeka in twenty-five minutes
Cons
  • Considerably more expensive than Rijeka
  • Crowded in summer, quiet in winter
  • Older visitor demographic

Cost of Living in Rijeka

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Rijeka, so the $1,300 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 831 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget roughly $1,300 a month as a solo remote worker in Rijeka, which makes it markedly cheaper than Split or Dubrovnik for a city with better year-round infrastructure. A one-bedroom apartment near the centre runs about $500 to $700, and the hillside districts above the port drop that to $400 to $550. Because Rijeka is a working city rather than a resort, long leases are available on normal terms and do not vanish in summer, which is a real advantage over the Dalmatian coast. Groceries land around $250 to $320. A konoba lunch is $10 to $14, a restaurant dinner $22 to $32, and coffee about $2. A monthly transit pass is roughly $30. Utilities add $110 to $190, higher in the damp winter months.

Where to Work in Rijeka

Rijeka has a modest coworking scene, with a few spaces in and around the centre charging roughly $110 to $180 a month, and the city's designation as European Capital of Culture in 2020 left behind some renovated cultural venues that double as working space. The Korzo, the main pedestrian street, and the streets running off it have plenty of cafes where a laptop is unremarkable, and Croatian coffee culture is famously unhurried, so nobody will move you along. Fixed-line internet is fast and mobile data is good and cheap within the EU roaming area. The practical advantage over the tourist coast is that all of this works in February as well as in August, when Split's cafes are full of visitors and its rents have tripled.

Getting Around Rijeka

Rijeka is built on a narrow shelf between the Kvarner gulf and the hills, which makes it long, thin and steep. The centre is walkable end to end along the waterfront and the Korzo, but anything uphill is a serious climb and the city has stairs where other places have streets. An extensive bus network covers the rest, with a monthly pass at about $30. The main railway station connects to Zagreb in around four hours, which is slower than the bus at two and a half. Rijeka airport is on the island of Krk, 30km away, seasonal and small; Zagreb, Pula, Trieste and Ljubljana are all realistic alternatives within a few hours. Ferries run along the coast and to the Kvarner islands in season.

Visas for Rijeka

Croatia is in the European Union, the Schengen area since January 2023, and the eurozone since the same date, so EU and EEA citizens may live and work here freely. Most other Western passports get 90 days in any 180-day Schengen period. Croatia was an early mover on remote work and its digital nomad residence permit, available since 2021, grants up to one year for non-EU nationals working for foreign employers, with an income threshold and proof of accommodation and health insurance required. It is not renewable directly, requiring a gap before reapplying. Notably, income earned under it is not subject to Croatian income tax, which is unusual among such schemes and one reason the permit has been popular.

Best Time to Visit Rijeka

May, June, September and October are the best months in Rijeka: warm, bright and free of the July and August crowds that fill the Kvarner coast. Summer is hot, into the low thirties, and the city empties towards the islands and beaches at Opatija and Lovran along the coast. Winter is mild by continental standards, around 10 degrees, but wet, and Rijeka is known for the bura, a fierce dry wind off the mountains that can blow for days and closes the coastal road. February brings the Rijeka Carnival, one of the largest in Europe and the single most distinctive thing in the local calendar. Because the city works year-round rather than seasonally, rents and services stay stable in a way the Dalmatian resorts do not manage.

Pros and Cons of Rijeka

Rijeka gives you the Croatian coast without the resort economy: real neighbourhoods, year-round rents, a working port, a university, and a cultural scene that outlasted its Capital of Culture year. It is cheaper than Split, better connected to central Europe, and has the Kvarner islands and the Ucka mountains within half an hour. The digital nomad permit is one of the better ones in the EU. Against that, it is not conventionally pretty: the centre is handsome Habsburg but the surroundings are industrial and socialist-era. The bura wind is genuinely unpleasant. Beaches are pebbly and mostly a bus ride away. The nomad community is small, with most of Croatia's remote workers in Split, Zagreb or Zadar, and winters are wet.

Who Rijeka Is For

Rijeka suits remote workers who want to live on the Adriatic year-round rather than visit it in summer, and who would rather have a functioning city than a postcard. It is a strong choice for anyone on Croatia's digital nomad permit who wants stable rent, for people who value proximity to Slovenia, Italy and Austria, and for those who sail, hike or want island access without resort prices. It works for couples and solo workers who make their own social plans. It suits less well anyone chasing a large nomad scene, guaranteed sunshine, sandy beaches or the picture-postcard Dalmatia of Split and Dubrovnik, all of which are a few hours south and considerably more expensive.

Frequently Asked Questions About Rijeka

How much does it cost to live in Rijeka as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Rijeka is around

,300 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Rijeka?

Rijeka rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is good for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Rijeka safe for digital nomads?

Rijeka scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Rijeka a good city for digital nomads overall?

Rijeka stands out for great weather and nature and the outdoors among remote workers. With a typical budget near ,300 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
