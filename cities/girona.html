Girona for Digital Nomads: The Honest Take

Discover medieval gem near Barcelona, Game of Thrones streets and world-class dining. Girona pairs nature with safety and a mediterranean climate, all for about $2,400 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Cost of Living in Girona

Girona sits in a comfortable middle ground: noticeably cheaper than Barcelona, but not as cheap as smaller inland Spanish cities. Expect to spend roughly 1,700 to 2,400 EUR (about $1,850 to $2,600 USD) per month as a solo remote worker, depending on your habits and where you live.

  • Rent, city center (Barri Vell / Eixample): 1,100 to 1,500 EUR/month for a 1-bedroom
  • Rent, outer neighborhoods (Pont Major, Domeny): 650 to 900 EUR/month for a 1-bedroom
  • Groceries: 200 to 300 EUR/month if you cook regularly at home
  • Eating out, inexpensive meal: 14 to 18 EUR per person; a mid-range dinner for two runs 60 to 80 EUR
  • Coffee: 2.00 to 2.50 EUR for a cappuccino
  • Coworking, hot-desk: 90 to 150 EUR/month (part-time to full-time desk)
  • Local transport (monthly bus pass): 34 EUR
  • Utilities (electricity, water, heating for 85m2): 200 to 250 EUR/month
  • Home internet (60+ Mbps unlimited): 35 to 45 EUR/month

Note that Girona's rental market has tightened considerably, with asking prices up sharply since 2024. Central apartments at the lower end of the range move quickly. Outer neighborhoods like Pont Major offer significantly lower rents but require a bus or bike commute into the old town.

Where to Work

Girona has a small but functional coworking scene. Cossi Coworking on Carrer Nou occupies a 19th-century building in the heart of the city, with high ceilings and a quiet atmosphere; full-time desks run 150 EUR/month, with daily drop-ins at 14 EUR. Giworking on Carrer Manel Bonmati, near the train station, is larger and more corporate in feel, offering 1 GB fiber and 24/7 card access. B&Co Working Place on Carrer Santa Coloma is a 1,800m2 facility on an industrial estate, better suited to those who don't need to be in the old town daily.

Cafe culture in Girona leans toward leisure over laptop work. La Fabrica on Carrer de la Llebre (old town) is the city's iconic cyclist cafe, busy and welcoming, but it gets crowded. Federal on Carrer de la Forca and Le Bistrot near Sant Domenec are both quiet enough for a morning of focused work with a coffee in hand. Expect a genuine Catalan cafe culture: no one will rush you, but you also won't find power strips at every table.

WiFi in coworking spaces is reliable with fiber speeds common. Cafes are more variable. Mobile data coverage (4G/5G) is solid throughout the city as a backup.

Getting Around

Girona's historic center is compact and best explored on foot. The old town sits on a small hill above the Onyar River and most of what you need as a day-to-day resident is within a 20-minute walk. The city has an expanding network of cycle lanes and a public bike scheme called Girocleta, with 35+ stations across town, making cycling one of the most practical ways to commute between neighborhoods.

The city bus network runs 13 lines from around 7:00 to 21:30, with a single ticket at 1.90 EUR and a monthly pass at 34 EUR. Frequency on main routes is every 15 minutes; secondary routes run every 30 to 60 minutes.

Getting to Barcelona is straightforward: the Renfe Avant high-speed train covers the 38-minute journey for as little as 7 to 12 EUR, with around 25 daily departures. This makes day trips or co-working weeks in Barcelona entirely practical.

Girona Costa Brava Airport (GRO) is 13 km south of the city and served mainly by Ryanair. A direct bus connects it to Barcelona's Estacio Nord in around 75 minutes (roughly 20 EUR). For most flights, Barcelona's El Prat airport (BCN) is the better option, reachable by train from Girona in under an hour and a half total.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

EU/EEA passport holders can live and work in Spain indefinitely. Citizens of the US, Canada, UK, Australia, and most other Western countries can enter Spain visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period under the Schengen rules.

For longer stays, Spain's Digital Nomad Visa (launched 2023) is the main route for remote workers. It requires proof of remote employment or freelance income of at least 2,762 EUR/month (roughly $3,000 USD), health insurance, a clean criminal record, and an application at your nearest Spanish consulate before arrival. The initial permit lasts one year, renewable for up to five years, with a pathway to permanent residency. A notable perk is eligibility for the Beckham Law flat income tax rate of 19% for up to four years.

Spain also offers Non-Lucrative Visas for those with sufficient passive income. The Golden Visa (real estate investment route) was abolished in April 2025. Always verify current requirements and income thresholds directly with the official Spanish consulate or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs website before applying.

Best Time to Visit

Girona has a transitional Mediterranean climate: hot and dry in summer, mild and wet in autumn and spring, with cool but rarely harsh winters.

Best windows for remote work: mid-April to mid-June and September to October. Temperatures are comfortable (15 to 25C / 59 to 77F), the city is active without being overwhelmed by tourists, and daylight hours are generous. Spring can bring some rain, but nothing disruptive.

July and August are hot (regularly above 30C / 86F) and the old town fills with tourists. It is not unworkable, but expect noise, higher short-term rental prices, and a city running at a different pace.

November through February is quiet, cheaper, and genuinely mild by northern European standards (8 to 15C / 46 to 59F), with crisp air and uncrowded streets. This is an underrated period for focused work, though some smaller cafes and restaurants reduce their hours.

Pros and Cons

  • Fast train to Barcelona in 38 minutes, giving you big-city access without big-city costs
  • Compact, walkable old town with genuinely beautiful medieval architecture along the Onyar River
  • Safe and clean, with a notably low crime rate compared to larger Spanish cities
  • Strong cycling culture and easy access to Pyrenees trails and Costa Brava beaches within an hour
  • Excellent food scene out of proportion to the city's size, including a Michelin-starred restaurant within walking distance of most apartments
  • Cooler summers than southern Spain, making afternoon work more bearable
  • Small English-speaking nomad community; you will mostly be navigating life in Catalan and Spanish
  • Limited coworking supply; the best spaces are small and can fill up, especially in high season
  • Rental market is tight and prices have risen sharply; finding central housing at reasonable rates takes time
  • No international airport hub; most long-haul travel requires getting to Barcelona first
  • Catalan-first culture means some locals prefer Catalan over Spanish, which can be an adjustment
  • Quieter nightlife than Barcelona or Madrid; the city goes to bed earlier

Who Girona Is Best For

Girona rewards people who want the quality of a smaller European city without sacrificing access to a major hub. If you do focused solo work, cycle or hike on weekends, and do not need a large co-working community around you, it is close to ideal. The Barcelona train connection means you are never more than 40 minutes from a major airport, international clients, or a livelier social scene whenever you need it.

It suits those who already have some Spanish (and ideally some Catalan curiosity), appreciate food culture, and prefer cobblestone-and-river-views over beach-town energy. Writers, designers, developers, and consultants who thrive on quiet, beauty, and routine tend to do well here.

Consider somewhere else if you need a large nomad meetup scene, a buzzing co-working campus, or affordable central housing without a long search. It is also not the right fit if you need frequent long-haul flights and resent the extra leg to Barcelona El Prat. And if extreme heat in summer bothers you, plan your stay between September and June.

Frequently Asked Questions About Girona

How much does it cost to live in Girona as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Girona is around $2,400 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Girona?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Girona is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Girona safe for digital nomads?

Girona scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Girona a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Girona is worth a look if you value nature and safety. Costs sit near $2,400 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
