Setting Up as a Nomad in Telč

A UNESCO-listed Moravian town whose long central square is lined with pastel Renaissance and Baroque houses under arcades, ringed by ponds and a fairytale chateau. Telč pairs safety with culture and a humid continental climate, all for about $1,000 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Best Neighborhoods in Telč

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Stare Mesto (Historic Square)

$$
Historic, picturesque, touristy by day and calm by evening

The pastel heart of Telč, and the whole reason to come

The historic core built around namesti Zachariase z Hradce, ringed by Renaissance and Baroque arcaded houses, the chateau and fishponds. This is where nearly all restaurants, cafes and shops sit, and where day-trip tourists concentrate in summer. Staying here means postcard views out your window and everything within a two-minute walk, at the cost of some daytime foot traffic and slightly higher rents than the rest of town.

Best for: Short stays, first-time visitors, anyone prioritizing atmosphere over quiet
Pros
  • Walk to everything, including the chateau and fishponds
  • Stunning architecture right outside your door
  • Best restaurant and cafe access in town
Cons
  • Busiest with tour groups in summer daytime
  • Slightly pricier rentals than surrounding areas

Podoli

$
Residential, calm, local

Quiet residential streets just beyond the ponds

A low-key residential district a short walk south of the old town, separated from the square by one of Telč's fishponds. Podoli offers cheaper, more local housing with easy lakeside walking paths and a genuinely residential, non-touristy feel. It suits nomads who want proximity to the historic center without living inside the tourist zone itself, trading a five to ten minute walk for noticeably more peace and quiet.

Best for: Longer stays, budget-conscious renters, those wanting distance from tourist crowds
Pros
  • Cheaper rents than the old town
  • Direct access to fishpond walking paths
  • Genuinely quiet, local atmosphere
Cons
  • 10 to 15 minute walk to restaurants and shops
  • Very little going on locally after dark

Stepnice

$
Everyday, functional, low-key suburban

Telč's practical, everyday residential edge

A newer residential area on the edge of town, home to the supermarket, some schools and ordinary Czech family housing. Stepnice is the least scenic part of Telč but the most practical, useful for longer-term stays where daily errands and grocery access matter more than views. It is a ten to fifteen minute walk from the historic square, quiet, functional, and about as far from the tourist experience as Telč gets.

Best for: Long-term budget stays, families, anyone prioritizing practicality over charm
Pros
  • Closest area to the main supermarket
  • Most affordable housing in Telč
  • Genuinely local, non-touristy daily life
Cons
  • Least scenic part of town
  • Farthest walk from the square and chateau

Dacice

$
Small-town, practical, unpretentious

A nearby town for day trips, errands, and a change of pace

A larger town about 20 minutes from Telč by bus or car, useful as a practical satellite for bigger grocery runs, a wider choice of shops, and a bit more everyday bustle than Telč itself offers. Dacice is not a nomad base in its own right but a handy nearby option for anyone based in Telč who wants occasional variety, additional services, or a slightly bigger-town feel without traveling all the way to Jihlava or Brno.

Best for: Day trips, errands, a break from Telč's small-town pace
Pros
  • Wider shopping and services than Telč
  • Easy bus connection, about 20 minutes
  • Less touristy, more everyday Czech life
Cons
  • Lacks Telč's UNESCO architecture and charm
  • Not a realistic base, more of a supplementary trip

Cost of Living in Telč

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Telč, so the $1,000 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 771 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Telč is one of the cheapest places you can plant yourself in the Czech Republic. A one-bedroom apartment near the historic square runs roughly $430 to $620 a month, and that is before you even look at the cheaper streets a short walk from the center. Groceries at the local Albert or Coop supermarket, plus the small farmers market on the square, keep a weekly food bill low, and a sit-down lunch menu (denni menu) at a local restaurant typically costs $7 to $10. Eating out every night in Prague would strain a budget; doing it in Telč barely dents one. Coffee, beer and utilities are all priced for a small Moravian town, not a capital city, so a modest but comfortable single-person budget lands around $1,190 to $1,660 a month, including rent. The tradeoff for this affordability is obvious: there is very little to spend money on after groceries, rent and the occasional pastry from the bakery on the square.

Where to Work in Telč

Be honest with yourself about this one: Telč has no dedicated coworking space. There is no WeWork equivalent, no hot-desk operation, nothing built for remote workers. What you get instead is a handful of cafes around namesti Zachariase z Hradce with decent wifi and enough tables to park a laptop for a few hours, plus the quiet of your own rented apartment, which is honestly where most remote workers here end up doing the bulk of their focused work. Wifi in apartments and cafes is generally reliable, since Czech broadband infrastructure is solid even in small towns, and mobile data from Czech carriers works well as a backup. If you need a real office environment with meeting rooms and a community of other remote workers, you will need to travel to Jihlava (about 30 minutes away) or, better, Brno (roughly 1.5 hours), which has a proper coworking scene. Telč works well for people who want to hole up and write, code or plan in peace, not for people who need daily collaborative energy.

Getting Around Telč

The town itself is entirely walkable, small enough that you can cross it end to end in about fifteen minutes, and the historic core is pedestrian-friendly with cobbled streets. You will not need a car, and in fact a car is more of a liability than a help inside the old town. For getting elsewhere, regional buses connect Telč to Jihlava in about 30 to 40 minutes and to Trebic in a similar window, both useful for bigger grocery runs, medical appointments or a change of scenery. Direct buses also run to Brno (around 1.5 to 2 hours) and, less frequently, to Prague (roughly 2.5 to 3 hours), so weekend trips to either city are entirely doable without a car. There is no train station directly in Telč; the nearest useful rail connections require a bus transfer first, which makes buses the default way in and out. If you plan to explore the wider Vysocina and South Moravia region on your own schedule, renting a car for occasional trips is worth it, but daily life in town needs nothing but your own two feet.

Visas for Telč

The Czech Republic is a full Schengen member, so most nomads from the US, UK, Canada, Australia and similar passport countries can enter visa-free and stay up to 90 days within any 180-day period, counted across the entire Schengen zone, not just Czechia. That is plenty for a slow, quiet stint in Telč but not a long-term solution. For a longer stay, the Czech Republic offers a Zivnostensky list (trade license) route that some freelancers use to base themselves in the country legally, along with a more recent digital nomad visa option aimed at remote workers and contractors, both of which require paperwork filed through a Czech consulate or the Foreign Police and are easier to arrange with a Prague-based lawyer than to do solo from a small town. Because Telč has no immigration office of its own, any visa or residency administration means a trip to Jihlava or further, so factor that into your planning if you intend to stay past the 90-day mark.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Telč

Late spring through early autumn, roughly May to September, is when Telč is at its best: warm days in the low-to-mid 20s Celsius, long evenings for sitting by the fishponds, and the pastel facades on the square glowing in the light. July and August bring the most tourists, since Telč is a popular day-trip destination for visitors touring South Moravia, so the square can feel genuinely crowded with tour groups for a few hours around midday even though the town empties out again by evening. Shoulder months like May, June and September offer the same good weather with far fewer people. Winters are a different story: cold, grey, and often snowy, with temperatures regularly below freezing from December through February, and the town's tourist infrastructure, such as it is, largely shuts down. If you come for winter, come knowing you are choosing hibernation-mode quiet, not postcard charm.

Pros and Cons of Telč

The pros are real: Telč is stunning, a genuine UNESCO World Heritage town with one of the most beautiful squares in Central Europe, and living inside that beauty every day for a fraction of Prague's cost is a rare thing. It is safe, clean, unhurried, and easy to get your bearings in within a day. The cons are equally real and worth stating plainly. This is a town of about 5,000 people with almost no nightlife, a thin dining scene once you have tried the handful of restaurants on the square, no coworking infrastructure, and a remote-worker community that is essentially nonexistent. English is spoken at tourist-facing businesses but far less reliably once you step off that path. Days can feel very long and very quiet, especially outside the summer season, and anyone craving spontaneity, variety or a packed social calendar will feel that absence quickly.

Who Telč Is For

Telč suits a specific kind of nomad: someone who wants a deliberate, low-cost, low-stimulation month or two to focus on deep work, writing, or simply decompressing, and who is comfortable being mostly alone. It is a good fit for a couple, a solo worker who has already spent time in busier hubs and wants contrast, or anyone using it as a quiet home base for exploring the wider Vysocina and South Moravia countryside. It is a poor fit for anyone chasing a coworking community, an active nightlife, or a steady stream of new people to meet, and it is not a place to arrive without a plan for how you will fill your own days. Think of Telč as a peaceful retreat you visit deliberately, not a nomad hub you stumble into.
