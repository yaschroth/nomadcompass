A Remote Worker's Guide to Koh Samui

Discover tropical island paradise with wellness vibes and coconut palms. What pulls nomads here is nature, backed by easy visas and safety. Budget around $1,230 a month. Cleanliness is the weak spot.

Koh Samui sits in a middle tier for Southeast Asia: cheaper than Bali's premium enclaves, more expensive than Chiang Mai. Expect to spend more here than on the Thai mainland, partly because the island's private airport drives up flight costs and keeps tourist prices elevated.

A monthly breakdown for a solo nomad:

  • Rent (Chaweng or Lamai, 1-bed): $420-540 for a modern studio with air-con. Negotiate a 3-6 month rate directly with landlords and you can push this lower.
  • Rent (Bophut or interior areas): $240-360 for older local-style apartments. Less tourist infrastructure nearby but notably quieter.
  • Groceries: $120-210 depending on how much imported or Western food you buy. Local markets and Makro reduce this significantly.
  • Eating out (local): Street stalls and local restaurants run $2-3.50 per meal. You can eat well locally for $150-240 monthly.
  • Coworking hot-desk: $205-205 per month at dedicated spaces like Desk and Chair Samui in Chaweng.
  • Local transport (scooter rental): $90-150 monthly. A scooter is effectively mandatory here given the island's size and lack of metered taxis.
  • Total realistic range: $895-1,640 monthly depending on accommodation choice and lifestyle. Budget end requires local food and a cheaper area; the upper end covers a comfortable apartment plus a social life.

One cost to plan around: getting on and off the island. Bangkok Air operates a near-monopoly on Samui Airport flights, keeping ticket prices high. Budget nomads often ferry to Surat Thani on the mainland instead, which takes longer but costs a fraction of the price.

Where to Work

The coworking scene is small but genuinely functional. Desk and Chair Samui in Chaweng is the most established option, open 24 hours with seated and standing desks, focus zones, private call booths, and a rooftop terrace. Their monthly rate sits around $205. Mantra Work Lounge in Bophut is a lighter option, open 8am-6pm with complimentary refreshments and coastline views. For something more hotel-adjacent, The Exchange at Explorar Koh Samui operates 24/7 and caters to timezone-spanning schedules.

Cafe work culture exists but is thinner than in Chiang Mai or Bangkok. A handful of cafes, including Bao Bao Cafe (reportedly 900+ Mbps speeds) and The Road Less Travelled Cafe, actively welcome laptop workers with solid WiFi. Outside these known spots, many tourist cafes tolerate laptops but aren't set up for long sessions.

The real WiFi situation: fast fiber is available in modern accommodation and the dedicated coworking spaces. Speeds at the coworking spaces are generally excellent. In older guesthouses or more remote parts of the island, connectivity can be inconsistent. A local SIM card from AIS or DTAC as a backup makes sense, particularly if your work requires reliable uptime.

Getting Around

Koh Samui is not walkable between neighborhoods. The main ring road connects the coastal areas, but distances between Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut, and Choeng Mon mean you need a vehicle. A scooter rental is by far the most practical option at $4.50-9 per day or roughly $90-150 monthly. Road accident rates on Samui are genuinely high: wear a helmet every time, avoid riding at night when possible, and only rent from shops that include insurance in the price.

Red songthaews (shared pickup trucks) run fixed routes around the ring road cheaply but are slow and irregular. Regular taxis exist but negotiate all fares in advance as meters are not used. Short trips run $9-15. The Grab app works in Samui though drivers cannot pick up inside the airport.

From Koh Samui Airport to Chaweng is about 10 minutes by taxi at a fixed $15 or around $4 on a shared minivan shuttle. If you fly Bangkok Air you arrive directly; otherwise the main alternatives are the ferry from Surat Thani on the mainland (2 hours) or Nathon pier, which is considerably cheaper than flying.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of 93 countries currently enter Thailand visa-free. The previous 60-day exemption was in effect through mid-2026, but Thailand's Cabinet approved reducing this to 30 days, pending Royal Gazette publication. Until that change is officially gazetted, the 60-day limit remains. A single 30-day extension is available at a local immigration office for $57.

For longer stays, the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) is the most practical option for nomads. It costs $300, is valid for 5 years, and lets you stay up to 180 days per entry with one 180-day extension. You need proof of remote work status and savings of at least $14,950. There is no minimum income requirement, which makes it accessible for a range of nomads.

The Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa targets higher earners, requiring income of at least $80,000 annually from a foreign employer. Confirm current requirements at official Thai embassy or immigration sources before applying, as rules have shifted frequently.

Best Time to Visit

Koh Samui sits in the Gulf of Thailand and follows a different weather pattern than Phuket and the Andaman coast. The dry and most reliably sunny period runs December through April, with January and February the peak months for clear skies and calm seas. This is also high season, meaning higher prices and more tourists.

For remote work, the sweet spot is February through June. Conditions stay warm and mostly sunny, crowds thin out noticeably from March onward, and accommodation rates drop. May and June offer good value without serious weather risk.

October and November are the wettest months, with November typically the island's rainiest. Heavy downpours can hit for days at a time during this period, and flooding occasionally affects low-lying areas. Coworking indoors remains productive, but the experience outside is limiting. Avoid planning outdoor-heavy work trips during this window.

Pros and Cons

  • Established expat and nomad community with regular meetups centered around a few coworking hubs
  • High-quality coworking options including 24-hour access at Desk and Chair Samui
  • English is widely spoken in tourist and business areas, making day-to-day logistics straightforward
  • Good food diversity from cheap local stalls to internationally regarded restaurants
  • Air quality is generally good, better than many mainland Thai cities, with occasional seasonal smoke from Sumatran forest fires
  • Medical facilities are above average for a Thai island, with Bangkok Hospital Samui handling most expat healthcare needs
  • Higher cost than mainland Thailand, especially for flights on and off the island
  • Not walkable: you need a scooter or budget for daily taxis
  • Road safety is a real concern, with accident rates among the highest in the country
  • Limited coworking options compared to Bangkok or Chiang Mai; busy periods mean the best spaces fill up
  • October and November are heavily rainy and can feel isolating
  • Tourist pricing in restaurants and shops around Chaweng can feel inflated relative to quality

Who Koh Samui Is Best For

Koh Samui works well for nomads who want a beach-lifestyle base without fully roughing it. If you value reliable coworking infrastructure, English-speaking services, international food, and decent healthcare access alongside tropical surroundings, Samui delivers that combination more consistently than smaller Thai islands. It suits people on mid-range to comfortable budgets, particularly those planning a stay of one to three months during the dry season.

The DTV visa makes it a realistic medium-term base for nomads with verifiable remote work and some savings, particularly compared to grinding visa runs.

Skip Koh Samui if you are on a tight budget and need to keep monthly costs under $700: the island's relative expensiveness and mandatory transport costs make that difficult. It is also a poor fit if you need a dense coworking community comparable to Chiang Mai or Lisbon, or if you find tourist-resort energy grating rather than enjoyable. Those seeking a quieter, less commercialized island experience are often better served by Koh Phangan, 45 minutes away by ferry.

Frequently Asked Questions About Koh Samui

How much does it cost to live in Koh Samui as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,230 a month in Koh Samui for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Koh Samui?

Koh Samui rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Koh Samui safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Koh Samui is 7/10, good for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Koh Samui a good city for digital nomads overall?

Koh Samui stands out for nature and easy visas among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,230 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
