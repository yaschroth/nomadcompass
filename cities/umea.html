Setting Up as a Nomad in Umea

A young university city on the Gulf of Bothnia, with a river that freezes solid. Umea pairs fast WiFi with safety and a subarctic climate, all for about $1,700 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Best Neighborhoods in Umea

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Umea centre

$$$
Central & Planned

The grid of birches, rebuilt after the 1888 fire

The middle of Umea, laid out after a fire destroyed the town in 1888 with wide streets and around three thousand birch trees planted deliberately as firebreaks, which is why the place is called the City of Birches. The market square, the shops and the Vaven cultural centre on the river are all here.

Best for: Anyone who wants shops, culture and the river on foot
Pros
  • The Vaven centre and Bildmuseet are genuinely good
  • Everything central within a fifteen minute walk
  • The river path runs along the southern edge
Cons
  • The most expensive rents in the city
  • Very quiet in the dark months
  • Limited housing stock for a student city

Alidhem and the campus

$$
Student & Institutional

The student district around the university

The area east of the centre holding the university campus, the hospital and Alidhem, the large housing district built in the 1970s that accommodates a substantial share of Umea's thirty thousand students. It is where the student life is and where most of the affordable rental stock sits.

Best for: Students, researchers and anyone connected to the university
Pros
  • Most available rental stock in the city is here
  • Campus, hospital and the sports facilities together
  • Genuine student social life in term time
Cons
  • Architecturally plain 1970s housing
  • Three kilometres from the centre
  • Empties considerably outside term

Teg

$$
Suburban & Quiet

The residential south bank across the Ume river

The area across the river from the centre, connected by several bridges, holding a mix of houses and apartment blocks, schools and the everyday services of an ordinary Swedish suburb. It is quieter than the north bank and has the better river views back towards the town.

Best for: Longer stays that want a settled neighbourhood and a view
Pros
  • River views back across to the centre
  • Quieter than the centre and cheaper
  • Ten minutes into town by bicycle over the bridge
Cons
  • Few shops or restaurants of its own
  • The bridges are exposed and cold in winter
  • Suburban rather than interesting

Holmsund

$$
Port & Coastal

The port on the Gulf of Bothnia, south of the city

The harbour town about fifteen kilometres south where the Ume river meets the Gulf of Bothnia, and where the Kvarken ferry leaves for Vaasa in Finland across the narrowest part of the gulf. The archipelago around it is where Umea goes in summer and the land here is still rising out of the sea.

Best for: Anyone using the Finland ferry or wanting the archipelago
Pros
  • The Kvarken ferry to Finland leaves from here
  • Archipelago and beaches within reach
  • Cheaper housing than the city
Cons
  • Fifteen kilometres from Umea and its services
  • Very quiet outside the summer
  • Bus connections thin in the evening

Cost of Living in Umea

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Umea, so the $1,700 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 1000 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $1,700 a month. A one-bedroom runs roughly $700 to $1,100, and the university's thirty thousand students dominate the rental market, which means small flats are available and larger ones are not. Groceries are expensive by European standards, though the discount chains help. Eating out is expensive and the range is limited. Utilities are the northern reality: heating runs for seven or eight months and is a substantial annual cost, usually through district heating from the local plant. Sweden is largely cashless, and some businesses no longer accept cash at all.

Where to Work in Umea

Connectivity is excellent. Sweden has extensive fibre and Umea is well served, with comprehensive mobile coverage and entirely reliable power. There is coworking and a genuine startup presence around the university, which is a substantial research institution with a strong medical faculty. The time zone is central European, so European work is straightforward. Everything administrative runs on a personnummer, and life without one is workable but awkward: it gates bank accounts, some housing and much of the digital infrastructure that Sweden takes for granted. Anyone staying under a year should establish early whether they can get a coordination number instead of a personnummer, since that is what opens most of the practical services.

Getting Around Umea

Umea is flat, compact and built for bicycles, which people use through the winter on studded tyres rather than putting away. Buses cover the rest. The city centre is walkable. Umea Airport has frequent flights to Stockholm, about an hour, and the Botniabanan railway runs south along the coast. The Kvarken ferry crosses to Vaasa in Finland in about four hours, which is the narrowest point of the Gulf of Bothnia and a UNESCO site for the land rising out of the sea after the last ice age. Anyone cycling in winter should fit studded tyres, which is normal here rather than eccentric, since the city keeps the cycle paths cleared and expects them to be used all year.

Visas for Umea

Sweden is a member of the European Union and the Schengen area, so European Union and EEA citizens may live and work here without a permit, registering with the tax agency for a personnummer once staying more than a year. Everyone else needs a Schengen visa for short stays or a work permit tied to an offer of employment for longer ones. There is no digital nomad visa. Sweden's work permit rules have tightened, with a raised salary threshold, and the process for non-European nationals has become slower rather than easier.

Best Time to Visit Umea

June to August is the season, with temperatures around twenty degrees, the Ume river at its best and daylight that barely ends: Umea sits close enough to the Arctic Circle that midsummer nights are only dusk. September and early October give the autumn colour. Winter from November to March is cold, often minus ten or below, with reliable snow and a great deal of darkness, though the snow reflects what light there is. The northern lights are visible on clear winter nights, which is not something Stockholm can offer. Anyone hoping for the northern lights should plan for clear nights between September and March, and be prepared to leave the city's light for the best of them.

Pros and Cons of Umea

The case for Umea is a young, well-serviced Nordic city with genuine culture for its size, which is what the 2014 European Capital of Culture year was about, along with excellent nature immediately outside, northern lights in winter and a university that keeps the average age low. The case against is the winter, which is long and dark, the cost, which is Swedish, the limited flight connections, and the personnummer, which shapes how easy or difficult ordinary life is here more than anything else. Umea is also known nationally as a centre of vegan and straight edge hardcore music, which produced several internationally known bands in the 1990s and is still a live part of the city's identity.

Who Umea Is For

Umea suits researchers and students, since the university is the city's centre of gravity, and anyone who wants a northern Swedish base with real infrastructure. It suits people who enjoy winter sports and long cold months rather than merely surviving them. It suits European Union citizens especially, since the paperwork is minimal. It does not suit anyone who needs sunlight in December, anyone on a tight budget, or anyone from outside the European Union without an employer, since the work permit route has become genuinely difficult. It also suits anyone drawn to Sami culture, since Umea sits at the southern edge of Sapmi and the city has been a centre for Sami arts and institutions.
