Cost of Living in Zanzibar

Zanzibar is cheaper than most Southeast Asian nomad hubs on paper, but the reality is more complicated. The island's economy is built around tourism, which means long-term accommodation is genuinely hard to find at reasonable rates and groceries lean on imports.

All prices below are in Tanzanian shillings (TZS) with USD equivalents. The exchange rate hovers around 2,600 TZS per USD.

  • Rent (Stone Town, 1-bedroom): 800,000-1,200,000 TZS/month ($300-$460). Decent furnished flats exist, but long-term leases are rare and landlords expect tourist-rate premiums.
  • Rent (Paje or Jambiani, 1-bedroom): 600,000-900,000 TZS/month ($230-$345). Slightly more budget-friendly in the beach villages, especially off-season.
  • Groceries: 260,000-390,000 TZS/month ($100-$150). Local produce, coconut, fish, and rice are cheap. Imported goods (cheese, wine, oats) are expensive and spotty in availability.
  • Eating out, local spots: 10,000-20,000 TZS per meal ($4-$8). Zanzibar mix, biryani, and grilled seafood at street stalls are excellent value.
  • Eating out, tourist restaurants: 30,000-60,000 TZS per meal ($12-$23).
  • Coworking hot-desk: The Train's House charges $10/day or $180/month for a shared desk in Stone Town.
  • Local transport (dala dala): 500-1,000 TZS per ride ($0.20-$0.40). Scooter rental runs about 300,000 TZS/month ($115) long-term.
  • Utilities: Often included in accommodation. If separate, expect 100,000-150,000 TZS/month ($40-$60) for electricity, which can spike with AC use.
  • SIM data (Vodacom 20GB): ~26,000 TZS/month ($10).

Realistic monthly total: Budget nomad spending carefully: $1,200-$1,600/month. A comfortable lifestyle with occasional restaurant meals, a reliable coworking space, and scooter transport: $1,800-$2,400/month. Nomads who eat out frequently and stay in nicer guesthouses will easily hit $2,500+.

Where to Work

Zanzibar has a small but real coworking scene concentrated in Stone Town and Paje. These spaces exist precisely because home and hotel WiFi is unreliable, so they tend to invest in backup generators and better connections.

Coworking Spaces

  • The Train's House (Stone Town): The most established space on the island, operating from a historic building in Darajani. Hot desks run $10/day or $180/month. Generator backup, AC, printing, and a restaurant onsite.
  • Kokaya Space (Stone Town): A courtyard-style space on Mizingani Road with a cafe and cultural events. Good atmosphere if you prefer a more social environment over pure productivity.
  • Tarawanda House (Stone Town): Rooftop coworking with coliving rooms available. Hosts networking events and is popular with long-term nomads.
  • Migoz Pro Hub (Stone Town): More corporate in feel, on Nyerere Road with a barista counter and professional seating.
  • The Nest Coworking Place (Paje): Open 24/7 at a beachfront resort. The trade-off is a longer commute from Stone Town, but it suits nomads who want to base themselves in Paje.

Cafe Work Culture and WiFi Reality

Cafe work culture is less established than in Southeast Asia. You can find a handful of cafes with decent WiFi in Paje, but expect to be turned away or receive poor speeds if the connection is overloaded. Stone Town's better cafes offer 10-20 Mbps on a good day. Average speeds across the island sit around 10-15 Mbps download, which is fine for video calls and light cloud work but not for large file uploads. Power cuts happen and are genuinely disruptive. Coworking spaces with generators are a more reliable choice for client calls or deadline work.

Getting Around

Stone Town itself is compact and largely walkable, though the narrow alleys and midday heat make long walks uncomfortable. Beyond the old town, you need transport.

On the Island

  • Dala dala: Shared minibuses covering the main routes for 500-1,000 TZS ($0.20-$0.40) per ride. Crowded and infrequent after dark, but by far the cheapest way to cross the island.
  • Bolt: Launched on Zanzibar in mid-2025 and now the most convenient app-based ride option. Uber no longer operates in Tanzania.
  • Scooter rental: The nomad default for exploring the island freely. Long-term rentals run around 300,000 TZS/month ($115); daily rates are higher at 50,000-80,000 TZS ($20-$30). An international driving permit is advisable.
  • Taxis: Available but negotiate the price before you get in. Airport to Stone Town is a fixed $15 for up to four passengers and takes about 15 minutes.

Airport (ZNZ)

Zanzibar Abeid Amani Karume International Airport sits about 6km south of Stone Town. A dala dala costs TZS 400 ($0.15) but involves a walk and some patience. A taxi costs $15 and takes 15 minutes. The airport handles both direct international arrivals and connections via Dar es Salaam, Nairobi, and Doha.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Zanzibar is part of Tanzania, so you follow Tanzanian entry rules. Since January 2025, Tanzania no longer issues visas on arrival. You must obtain an eVisa before you travel.

  • eVisa (single entry, 90 days): $50 USD, apply at visa.immigration.go.tz. Processing typically takes a few hours to a few days.
  • Multiple-entry visa: $100 USD, valid for 12 months.
  • Mandatory travel insurance: Since October 2024, all visitors to Zanzibar must have travel insurance from Zanzibar Insurance Corporation. You pay this on arrival.
  • Extension: You can extend a single-entry tourist visa once at the Zanzibar Immigration Office for an additional 90 days ($50). Total maximum stay is 180 days before you must leave Tanzania.
  • Nomad visa: Tanzania has no dedicated digital nomad visa as of mid-2026.
  • Yellow fever: Vaccination certificate required if arriving from a country with yellow fever transmission risk.

Always verify current requirements at Tanzania's official immigration portal before travel, as rules have changed repeatedly in recent years.

Best Time to Visit

Zanzibar has two rainy seasons and two dry seasons. Getting the timing right matters a lot for both comfort and your connection to the outside world (power cuts spike during heavy rain).

  • June to October (long dry season): The best overall window. Temperatures sit at 22-28°C with low humidity, minimal rain, and consistent sunshine. July and August are peak tourist season with higher prices and more competition for accommodation. September and October give you the same good weather with notably smaller crowds.
  • December to February (short dry season): Warm at 28-32°C, mostly dry with occasional brief showers. A solid second choice, especially January and February when it is hot and dry.
  • March to May (long rains): Heavy afternoon downpours are daily. Accommodation is cheapest of the year and the island is quiet, but power outages are more frequent and some businesses scale back. Not recommended if reliable internet is a priority.
  • November: Short rains, variable and patchy. Not the worst time to visit but unpredictable.

For remote work, aim for June to October or January to February. September and early October offer the best combination of weather, lower prices, and manageable crowds.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Stunning beaches with some of the best kitesurfing in the world, particularly at Paje
  • Genuinely low cost of food when eating local, with excellent Swahili and spice-influenced cuisine
  • Stone Town is a living UNESCO World Heritage site, fascinating to explore
  • Warm, welcoming culture and English is widely spoken in tourist and business contexts
  • A small but real coworking scene with reliable generator backup
  • Good flight connections from Europe and the Middle East, Nairobi is a short hop away
  • No jet lag for European nomads; UTC+3 year-round

Cons

  • WiFi is genuinely unreliable outside of dedicated coworking spaces; power cuts are common
  • Long-term accommodation is hard to find and overpriced compared to comparable destinations in Asia
  • The island economy prices tourists heavily: eating out at nicer spots gets expensive fast
  • Petty theft is a real concern in Stone Town, particularly in crowded markets and after dark
  • No nomad visa; the eVisa caps you at 90 days (extendable once to 180 total)
  • Grocery selection for imported items is limited and inconsistent
  • Humidity and heat from November through March can make working without AC uncomfortable

Who Zanzibar Is Best For

Zanzibar works well for nomads who want a short-term tropical base, not a permanent one. If you are writing, designing, or doing async work that tolerates occasional connectivity hiccups, the island rewards you with a genuinely beautiful environment and rich culture. It suits people who can work from a coworking space on a schedule rather than relying on home or cafe WiFi for client calls. The June-to-October dry season is a particular draw for European nomads looking to escape winter.

Kitesurfers, divers, and anyone drawn to the Swahili coast's history will find Zanzibar more than a backdrop. Foodies willing to eat local will eat very well for very little.

Zanzibar is a harder fit if you need consistent multi-gigabit speeds, hold daily video calls on a strict schedule, or want a dense nomad community with regular meetups. It is also not ideal for anyone planning a stay beyond six months, since the visa situation requires either a border run or starting fresh with a new entry. Budget-focused nomads who compare it to Southeast Asia on a cost-per-experience basis will find Southeast Asia wins on internet reliability and accommodation flexibility, though not on culture or scenery.

Frequently Asked Questions About Zanzibar

How much does it cost to live in Zanzibar as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,500 a month in Zanzibar for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Zanzibar?

Zanzibar rates 4 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work patchy. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Zanzibar safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Zanzibar is 6/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Zanzibar a good city for digital nomads overall?

Zanzibar stands out for nature and low costs among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,500 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
