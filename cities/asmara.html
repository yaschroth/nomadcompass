Setting Up as a Nomad in Asmara

An intact Italian modernist city at 2,300 metres, and a UNESCO site because of it. Expect $800 a month here. Culture and climate are the standout strengths, with the size of the nomad scene the main caveat.

Best Neighborhoods in Asmara

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Harnet Avenue

$$
Modernist & Sociable

The palm-lined spine of the modernist city

The main avenue running through the centre, lined with palms and with the Italian modernist buildings of the 1930s along both sides, including the Cinema Impero with its original fittings. The evening passeggiata along here is a genuine daily ritual and the cafes stay full.

Best for: Anyone who wants the architecture and the evening ritual on their doorstep
Pros
  • The finest concentration of 1930s modernism anywhere in Africa
  • The evening passeggiata and the cafe culture
  • Everything central and entirely walkable
Cons
  • Photography of official buildings is restricted
  • Almost no internet worth the name
  • Limited accommodation, most of it dated

Medebar

$
Industrial & Extraordinary

The scrap market where everything is remade

A quarter east of the centre where hundreds of workshops turn scrap metal into buckets, stoves, tools and coffee pots, hammering old oil drums into new goods in the open air. It is loud, sooty and one of the most remarkable working markets anywhere.

Best for: Anyone who wants to see the most vivid part of the city
Pros
  • One of the great working markets in Africa
  • Everything is made and repaired here at local prices
  • A short walk from the centre
Cons
  • Loud, sooty and not a residential area
  • Photography needs permission and care
  • Nothing here in the way of accommodation

Tiravolo

$$$
Residential & Green

The garden villa quarter southwest of the centre

A residential district of villas and gardens laid out during the Italian period, holding embassies, the better houses and quiet tree-lined streets. It sits slightly above the centre and is where much of Asmara's small foreign and professional community lives.

Best for: Longer stays that want the best housing available
Pros
  • The best residential stock in the city
  • Quiet, green and genuinely pleasant to walk
  • Close to the embassies and the centre
Cons
  • The most expensive housing in Asmara, though still cheap
  • Very few shops within walking distance
  • Power cuts affect it like everywhere else

Godaif

$$
Residential & Ordinary

The southern residential district on the airport road

A large residential area south of the centre along the road towards the airport, holding ordinary housing, small markets and the bus depot for the routes out of the city. It is where a substantial share of Asmara's population lives and it is considerably cheaper than the centre.

Best for: Anyone who wants local prices and local life
Pros
  • The cheapest housing in the city
  • Local markets and the intercity bus depot
  • Close to the airport
Cons
  • A long walk or a bus ride from the centre
  • Very few services aimed at foreigners
  • Little of the architecture that makes Asmara remarkable

Cost of Living in Asmara

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Asmara, so the $800 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 951 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $800 a month, though as in Turkmenistan the figure needs a caveat: Eritrea has a closed economy, a fixed official exchange rate and strict currency controls, and what things cost depends heavily on which rate applies. A one-bedroom runs roughly $200 to $400 at official rates. Local produce and bread are cheap. Imported goods are expensive and often simply unavailable, which is the more relevant constraint. Eating out is inexpensive and the Italian legacy means genuinely good coffee and pasta alongside Eritrean food. Cash is essential and declaring foreign currency on arrival is compulsory.

Where to Work in Asmara

Connectivity is the worst in Africa and among the worst anywhere. Eritrea has the lowest internet penetration in the world, no meaningful mobile data for most of its history, and dial-up-era speeds in the internet cafes that serve as most people's access. Fibre exists in a very limited form. Power cuts are frequent. There is no coworking and no realistic way to run a remote job from here. The time zone is three hours ahead of GMT, which is academic given the connection. Anyone needing to reach clients should plan on doing so before arriving and after leaving, because the connection here cannot be relied on for anything time-sensitive.

Getting Around Asmara

Asmara is walkable and genuinely pleasant to walk, which is unusual for an African capital: the centre is compact, the streets are ordered on the Italian plan, and the altitude keeps it cool. Buses and shared taxis are very cheap. The Eritrean Railway to Massawa, built by the Italians and rebuilt by hand with original locomotives, runs occasional excursions down the escarpment. Asmara International Airport has limited connections, mainly to Cairo, Dubai and Addis Ababa since the 2018 rapprochement with Ethiopia. Anyone travelling beyond the capital should apply for the internal permit several days ahead, since it names specific destinations and is checked at posts on every road.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Asmara

Eritrea requires a visa of every nationality, applied for in advance at an embassy, with an invitation or a booking and a process that is slow and frequently refused. There is no visa on arrival and no e-visa. Travel outside Asmara requires a separate internal travel permit, applied for in the capital and issued for specific destinations, and this is enforced at checkpoints. There is no remote work visa. Photography is restricted and military and government buildings must not be photographed at all. Anyone applying should also expect to be asked for a detailed itinerary, and should understand that refusal without reason is common and carries no route of appeal.

Best Time to Visit Asmara

September to March is the best window, dry and mild at around twenty two degrees thanks to the altitude of about 2,300 metres, which makes Asmara far more comfortable than its latitude suggests. The main rains fall from June to August and are heavy but short. Nights are cool year round and a jacket is needed. The coast at Massawa is a different climate entirely and is brutally hot for most of the year, so the descent from the highlands is best made in the cooler months. Anyone visiting the Dahlak archipelago off Massawa should aim for the cooler months, since the Red Sea coast is among the hottest inhabited places on earth in summer.

Pros and Cons of Asmara

The case for Asmara is unique and worth stating carefully: the city is an intact Italian modernist and Art Deco capital, built in the 1930s and barely altered since, and it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2017 for exactly that. The Fiat Tagliero service station, shaped like an aeroplane, and the Cinema Impero are among the finest buildings of their period anywhere. The case against is the state: Eritrea has indefinite national service, extensive restrictions on movement and expression, and effectively no internet. The city's evening passeggiata along Harnet Avenue is also a genuine daily ritual, and it is the clearest surviving trace of the Italian period in ordinary life.

Who Asmara Is For

Asmara suits architects and anyone drawn to twentieth century modernism, for whom the city is close to unmatched, along with travellers who want somewhere genuinely unvisited and are prepared for the constraints. It suits people with family connections. It does not suit remote workers in any sense, because the internet cannot support the work, and it does not suit anyone who would find internal travel permits, checkpoints and photography restrictions difficult to accept. It also suits cyclists, since Eritrea is the most cycling-obsessed country in Africa, the Tour of Eritrea is a national event, and the sport is another Italian inheritance.

Frequently Asked Questions About Asmara

How much does it cost to live in Asmara as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Asmara is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Asmara?

Asmara rates 1 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is on the limited side for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Asmara safe for digital nomads?

Asmara scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Asmara a good city for digital nomads overall?

Asmara stands out for culture and great weather among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
