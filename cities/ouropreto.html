Setting Up as a Nomad in Ouro Preto

A perfectly preserved baroque colonial mining town in the hills of Minas Gerais, all cobbles, churches, and gold-rush grandeur. Plan on roughly $1,000 a month. Its strengths are culture and clean air, while the language barrier is where it scores lower.

Best Neighborhoods in Ouro Preto

Centro Historico (Praca Tiradentes)

$$
Historic, baroque, central

Baroque heart around the main square

The historic centre around Praca Tiradentes is Ouro Preto's UNESCO-listed core, packed with baroque churches, museums, cobbled hills, pousadas, restaurants and shops. It is the most walkable and amenity-rich base, though its steep slopes demand strong legs.

Best for: Travellers wanting sights, dining and pousadas within walking distance

  • Most restaurants and pousadas
  • Central and lively
Cons

  • Busier and pricier

Antonio Dias

$$
Traditional, quiet, residential

Historic parish around Matriz church

Antonio Dias is a historic eastern district built around the Matriz de Nossa Senhora da Conceicao church, where the sculptor Aleijadinho is buried. Quieter and steeply set, it offers traditional colonial houses and a more residential, local atmosphere close to the centre.

Best for: Visitors wanting historic character away from the busiest square
Pros
  • Rich colonial heritage
  • Quieter than Tiradentes
  • Close to the centre
Cons
  • Steep walks
  • Fewer restaurants

Pilar (Nossa Senhora do Pilar)

$$
Historic, studenty, calm

District of the golden church

The Pilar district surrounds the Basilica of Nossa Senhora do Pilar, one of Brazil's most gilded baroque churches. This western historic parish blends colonial houses, student housing linked to the university, and quiet cobbled streets a short walk from the centre.

Best for: Longer-stay visitors wanting heritage streets and student-town energy
Pros

  • Colonial atmosphere
Cons

  • Limited nightlife

Cost of Living in Ouro Preto

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Ouro Preto, so the $1,000 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 740 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Ouro Preto is affordable, cheaper than Rio or Sao Paulo and helped by its large student population, though its heritage status and tourism add a modest premium. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $800 to $1,300 a month. A furnished apartment or a room in the many student republicas costs about $300 to $600, and hearty Minas Gerais cooking is cheap and generous. These are our editorial estimates in US dollars, and because prices are set in the Brazilian real, the exchange rate strongly shapes how far a foreign income stretches here.

  • Furnished apartment or student-style room: roughly $300 to $600 a month
  • Prato feito or a plate of comida mineira: roughly $5 to $9
  • Cafezinho at a padaria: roughly $1
  • Bus to Belo Horizonte (about two hours): a few dollars

Where to Work in Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto is a historic university town rather than a nomad hub, but the presence of a major federal university means decent connectivity and a youthful energy. Home fibre is available in much of the town and handles calls and everyday work, and there are cafes and student-friendly spots where you can settle in with a laptop, though a dedicated coworking scene is minimal. A Vivo, Claro or TIM SIM adds cheap mobile data. Ouro Preto shares Brazil's eastern time zone, giving comfortable overlap with the Americas. The steep terrain aside, it is a workable, atmospheric base for focused work.

Getting Around Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto's defining feature for daily life is its terrain: the beautiful colonial town tumbles up and down impossibly steep, cobbled hills, so getting around is a genuine cardio workout and comfortable shoes are essential. The historic centre is walkable but demanding, and local buses help with the steeper climbs. For leaving town, frequent buses connect Ouro Preto to Belo Horizonte, about two hours away, which has the nearest airport and links to the rest of Brazil. Nearby colonial towns like Mariana and the baroque treasures of the region make easy day trips.

Visas for Ouro Preto

Brazil is fairly open. Citizens of the EU, UK and many other countries enter visa-free for 90 days, extendable by another 90, while US, Canadian and Australian visitors now need an affordable e-visa arranged online. For longer stays, Brazil offers a digital nomad visa for remote workers meeting a minimum monthly income, granting a year with the option to renew. Rules and the e-visa requirement have changed recently, so confirm the current terms on official Brazilian government channels before booking, and mind the 90-day limit if entering visa-free.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto sits at over 1,000 metres in the hills of Minas Gerais, giving it a mild highland climate with cool evenings year-round. The best time to visit is the dry season from April to September, when days are pleasant and sunny and the cobbled streets are at their most inviting, though winter nights can be genuinely cold. The wet season from October to March brings warm days and frequent afternoon rain that can make the steep streets slippery. The town is famous for its dramatic Holy Week and Carnival celebrations, both worth timing a stay around.

Pros and Cons of Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto is one of the Americas' great colonial treasures, with the quirks of a steep, out-of-the-way town.

  • Pros: a stunning, perfectly preserved baroque colonial townscape and UNESCO World Heritage site, extraordinary churches and gold-rush history, superb and cheap Minas Gerais food, a lively student atmosphere, a mild highland climate, and low costs.
  • Cons: punishingly steep, hilly streets, limited English, a small nomad community, a somewhat remote location, wet, slippery summers, and a small range of amenities compared with a big city.

Who Ouro Preto Is For

Ouro Preto is for the culture-and-history nomad who wants to live inside a baroque masterpiece and does not mind a daily workout on the hills. Lovers of colonial architecture, art, history and hearty regional food, and anyone who enjoys the energy of a lively university town, will be captivated, and it makes an inspiring, inexpensive base for focused creative work. It rewards travellers happy with some Portuguese and a quieter, steeper life. It is a weaker fit for those who need flat, easy walking, a large nomad community, beaches, or a big-city buzz. Base yourself in the historic centre, brace your legs, and let Ouro Preto's baroque beauty surround you.
