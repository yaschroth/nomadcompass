Setting Up as a Nomad in Tenerife

A volcanic island with eternal spring and dramatic landscapes. A arid climate and strong nature make Tenerife an easy base, and at about $2,400 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the culture scene in mind.

Cost of Living in Tenerife

Tenerife is cheaper than mainland hubs like Barcelona or Madrid, but the south (Costa Adeje, Los Cristianos, Playa de las Americas) has been pushed up by tourism, so your rent is where most of your money goes. Prices below are in euros with rough USD equivalents at about 1.15 to the euro. Treat them as a mid-2025 to 2026 snapshot, since the rental market moves fast.

  • Rent, one-bedroom in a central or resort area: roughly EUR 900 to 1,300 (about USD 1,035 to 1,495) per month for a long stay. Furnished short-term lets in Costa Adeje run higher.
  • Rent, cheaper area: a studio or one-bed in Santa Cruz, La Laguna, or an inland town runs closer to EUR 500 to 800 (about USD 575 to 920). Colivings that bundle a room, coworking, and community usually land at EUR 700 to 1,500 (about USD 805 to 1,725).
  • Groceries: about EUR 200 to 260 (USD 230 to 300) per month if you cook. Local produce and Mercadona keep this reasonable, though imported goods cost more because everything ships in.
  • Eating out: a cheap main is around EUR 15 (USD 17), a mid-range meal for two about EUR 50 (USD 58), and a coffee near EUR 1.60 (USD 1.85).
  • Coworking hot-desk: a monthly pass is roughly EUR 150 to 200 (USD 173 to 230); day passes sit near EUR 20 (USD 23).
  • Local transport: the TITSA resident monthly bus pass is EUR 19 (USD 22); most single rides cost a few euros with the ten+ card.

Realistic monthly total: a solo nomad renting solo can expect roughly EUR 1,600 to 2,600 (about USD 1,840 to 2,990). Sharing, choosing the north, or splitting a coliving pushes you toward the low end.

Where to Work

The coworking scene splits along the same north/south line as everything else. In the south, near where most nomads land, Zen Den in Costa Adeje is the main social hub, sitting close to the beach with fast WiFi, free coffee, and regular evening networking. Los Cristianos has beachfront options aimed at short stays. In the north, Santa Cruz has the most professional spaces, including Workeamos, a large two-level hub with meeting rooms, phone booths, and 24/7 member access. You will also find smaller community spaces in Puerto de la Cruz and La Orotava.

Cafe-work culture exists but is more relaxed than in a dedicated nomad city, so treat cafes as a backup rather than your daily office. On the internet, the island is in good shape: fibre is widely available in the main towns and coworking spaces, and speeds commonly run from 100 to 600 Mbps. Check the actual connection before signing any longer lease, since older or more rural buildings can lag.

Getting Around

The green TITSA buses cover almost the whole island and are the backbone of getting around without a car. Grab a ten+ card (about EUR 2 to buy, then topped up in five-euro increments), which cuts fares by roughly 20 to 30 percent and works on buses and the Santa Cruz to La Laguna tram. A short ride costs a few euros.

That said, a car is genuinely useful here. Buses connect the main towns well, but reaching quieter villages, Teide National Park, or the north coast on your own schedule is far easier with a rental, and rentals are cheap by European standards. Many nomads go car-free in the south and rent occasionally for trips.

There are two airports. Tenerife South (TFS) handles most international flights and is the one you will likely use, sitting close to the southern nomad zone; buses 111, 343, 40, and the night 711 link it to Los Cristianos, Las Americas, and Costa Adeje. Tenerife North (TFN) serves more inter-island and domestic routes and connects to Santa Cruz and La Laguna. The 343 express runs directly between the two airports.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

If you hold a passport with visa-free access to the Schengen Area (for example the US, UK, Canada, or Australia), you can stay up to 90 days within any 180-day period as a tourist. That window resets slowly, so you cannot simply leave for a weekend and return.

For longer stays, Spain's digital nomad visa lets non-EU remote workers live here legally. You generally need to work remotely for non-Spanish companies or clients (a small share of Spanish income is allowed), show a university degree or equivalent experience, and prove income around 200 percent of Spain's minimum wage, which was roughly EUR 2,368 per month for a single applicant in 2025 and higher in 2026. It is typically issued for one year from a consulate or up to three years if applied for from inside Spain, and it is renewable toward longer-term residency. A practical bonus of the Canary Islands is the lower IGIC sales tax, a general rate of 7 percent versus 21 percent VAT on the mainland, which trims the cost of many purchases. Visa rules change, so confirm current thresholds and paperwork with an official Spanish government or consular source before you commit.

Best Time to Visit

Tenerife is called the island of eternal spring for good reason: winters rarely drop below about 15C and summers sit around 25 to 30C, so there is no truly bad season for remote work. The catch is the north/south split. The south (Costa Adeje, Los Cristianos, Las Americas) is dry, sunny, and sheltered from the trade winds year-round, which is why most nomads base there. The north (Santa Cruz, La Laguna, Puerto de la Cruz) is greener but cooler, cloudier, and windier.

If you want the most reliable conditions, spring through early summer is a sweet spot: winds calm, temperatures are comfortable, and the crowds are thinner than peak season. Watch for occasional calima, a Saharan dust haze that can bring hot, hazy air for a day up to a week. For a long working stay, the south in almost any month is a safe bet.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: reliably warm, sunny weather all year, especially in the south.
  • Pro: lower cost of living than mainland Spanish cities, plus the reduced IGIC tax.
  • Pro: solid fibre internet and a growing coworking and coliving scene.
  • Pro: outdoor access to Teide, hiking, beaches, and diving on your doorstep.
  • Con: the southern nomad zones sit inside tourist-resort territory, which can feel generic and packaged.
  • Con: a car is close to essential for exploring beyond the main towns.
  • Con: island isolation and time away from mainland Europe; everything imported costs more.
  • Con: wind in the north and the occasional dusty calima can dull the paradise image.

Who Tenerife Is Best For

Tenerife suits you if you want dependable warm weather, outdoor sport, and a European base with lower costs and a lighter tax load, without the intensity of a big city. It works especially well for sun-driven nomads who will base in the south, dip into the coworking and coliving community, and rent a car when they want to roam.

Skip it if you crave a dense, walkable capital with nightlife and a large local scene, since the resort south can feel touristy and the real cities sit in the cooler, windier north. It is also a weaker fit if you refuse to drive, want to hop across mainland Europe cheaply and often, or need a hard-charging, high-energy nomad hub. For that, a mainland Spanish city may serve you better.

Frequently Asked Questions About Tenerife

How much does it cost to live in Tenerife as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Tenerife is around $2,400 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Tenerife?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Tenerife is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Tenerife safe for digital nomads?

Tenerife scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Tenerife a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Tenerife is worth a look if you value climate and nature. Costs sit near $2,400 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
