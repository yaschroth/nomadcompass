Why Marbella Works for Remote Work

Experience costa del Sol glamour, luxury marina, golf courses, and endless sunshine. A mediterranean climate and strong climate make Marbella an easy base, and at about $3,300 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep cost in mind.

Cost of Living in Marbella

Marbella is one of the pricier spots on the Iberian Peninsula. That said, it is still cheaper than London, Amsterdam, or Zurich, and the lifestyle you get for the price is hard to match in northern Europe.

Here is what a realistic monthly budget looks like:

  • Rent, 1BR in the center or Old Town: €1,200-1,600 (~$1,300-1,750 USD)
  • Rent, 1BR in San Pedro de Alcantara or Elviria: €800-1,100 (~$870-1,200 USD)
  • Groceries (cooking at home most days): €250-350 (~$275-385 USD)
  • Eating out (mix of local cafes and sit-down dinners): €300-500 (~$330-550 USD)
  • Coworking hot desk: €150-350 per month (~$165-385 USD), or around €15-25 for a day pass
  • Local bus transport (Bonobus card): €40-70 (~$44-77 USD)
  • Mobile data / SIM: €20-30 (~$22-33 USD)

Realistic monthly total: €1,800-2,800 (~$1,970-3,070 USD) for a lean-to-comfortable lifestyle, rising to €3,500+ if you eat out frequently or rent in premium areas. Summer months push prices higher across the board, especially for short-term accommodation.

Where to Work

Marbella has a small but solid selection of dedicated coworking spaces. WeCowork, near Puerto Banus, is the most established option with fiber internet, meeting rooms, and a professional atmosphere. It holds a 4.8-4.9 rating across review platforms. Our//Space, located in a commercial center on the N-340, offers a more casual setup with shared desks and private offices. Work in Marbella Coworking in the city center is another reliable choice. Hot desks run roughly €15-25 per day; monthly memberships start around €150 at budget spaces and reach €350 at premium ones.

Cafe-work culture exists but is patchy. Many cafes in the Old Town and along the Paseo Maritimo are tourist-facing, meaning ambient noise, slow turnaround on tables, and inconsistent WiFi. A few spots near the bus station and in San Pedro de Alcantara are more conducive to work sessions. If you need stable connectivity for calls, a dedicated coworking space is the safer bet.

Home fiber internet from providers like Avatel or Movistar routinely hits 600-1,000 Mbps in residential areas. Connection quality in your flat is generally not a concern, as long as you choose a modern building.

Getting Around

Marbella's Old Town and the strip along the Paseo Maritimo are walkable. However, the municipality as a whole is spread out along the coast, and many residential areas, coworking spaces, and beaches are not within easy walking distance of each other.

The local bus network run by CTSA-Portillo connects San Pedro de Alcantara, Puerto Banus, Marbella center, and Elviria. A rechargeable Bonobus card gives you discounted fares; a single journey runs under €2. Buses are frequent enough during the day but thin out in the evenings.

Taxis and ride apps (primarily Cabify) are reliable and not expensive for short trips, but they add up if you use them daily. Renting a scooter or small car is worth considering if you plan to live here for a month or more, particularly outside the center.

Airport: Malaga Airport (AGP) is about 50 km away. The Avanza bus from Terminal 3 Arrivals takes 45-60 minutes and costs roughly €8. There is no rail link directly to Marbella; the closest train station is in Fuengirola, from which you can take a bus. A taxi or private transfer from AGP runs €70-100 depending on traffic.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Spain is part of the Schengen Area. If you hold a US, Canadian, UK, or Australian passport you can enter visa-free and stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period. You cannot extend this limit within the Schengen zone by simply crossing a border.

Spain introduced a dedicated Digital Nomad Visa (Visado para Teletrabajadores Internacionales) that allows remote workers to legally live and work in Spain for up to one year, renewable for up to five years total. To qualify you must earn at least approximately €2,760 per month (net), work primarily for companies based outside Spain, hold a relevant degree or have three years of professional experience, and carry valid health insurance. The visa is applied for at a Spanish consulate before you enter the country.

Always verify current requirements and income thresholds at the official Spanish consulate website or via a qualified immigration lawyer, as these figures are updated periodically.

Best Time to Visit

Marbella averages over 320 days of sunshine per year and is genuinely pleasant in every season, but the best windows for a productive remote work stay are May to mid-June and September to October.

During these shoulder months, temperatures sit between 20-27 C (68-81 F), the sea is warm enough to swim, restaurant quality is high without the summer crush, and short-term rental prices drop noticeably from peak. You can actually get a table at a good restaurant without booking a week in advance.

July and August are hot (30-35 C / 86-95 F), extremely crowded with tourists, and significantly more expensive. Productivity suffers for many people when the city is in full party mode. Winter (November to March) is mild (12-18 C / 54-64 F) and quiet. It is a genuinely good time to live here cheaply and work without distraction, though some beach bars and seasonal venues close down.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Exceptional climate with warm, sunny weather almost year-round
  • Large, established English-speaking expat community makes integration easy
  • Strong fiber internet infrastructure in modern apartments
  • High quality of life: beaches, restaurants, golf, mountains all accessible
  • Good flight connections via Malaga Airport to most of Europe
  • Spain Digital Nomad Visa provides a legal path to stay beyond 90 days
  • Safer and cleaner than many comparably priced European cities

Cons

  • One of the more expensive cities in Spain, especially for rent in central or Golden Mile areas
  • Small coworking scene with limited options compared to Barcelona or Madrid
  • Car or scooter almost necessary if you live outside the core center
  • No direct train to Malaga Airport; transfers require a bus or taxi
  • Summer crowds and noise can seriously disrupt focus for light sleepers or those near tourist zones
  • Social scene skews toward wealthy retirees and seasonal vacationers rather than a startup or tech crowd
  • Limited spontaneous networking; the nomad community here is smaller than hubs like Lisbon or Valencia

Who Marbella Is Best For

Marbella suits you well if you value lifestyle balance as much as productivity. It works best for remote workers who already have stable income and an established workflow, and who want to pair focused mornings with afternoon swims and good food without sacrificing connectivity or safety.

You will get the most out of Marbella if you are in a slightly higher income bracket, comfortable with a smaller nomad community, and drawn to the Mediterranean pace. It is a particularly good fit for solo professionals or couples who prefer a relaxed, upmarket environment over a buzzy coworking-hub city.

Skip Marbella if you are on a tight budget and want to stretch your euros, if you need a dense tech or startup ecosystem to stay motivated, or if you are easily distracted by resort-town energy. Cities like Granada, Valencia, or Malaga itself offer a more affordable and community-rich alternative within Andalusia.

Frequently Asked Questions About Marbella

How much does it cost to live in Marbella as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Marbella is around $3,300 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Marbella?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Marbella is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Marbella safe for digital nomads?

Marbella scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Marbella a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Marbella is worth a look if you value climate and nightlife. Costs sit near $3,300 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
