A silver-boom colonial city of pink stone climbing a high desert ravine, crowned by a cable car to the Cerro de la Bufa. Expect $800 a month here. Culture and low costs are the standout strengths, with the size of the nomad scene the main caveat.

Cost of Living in Zacatecas

Zacatecas is one of the better-value colonial cities in Mexico, in part because it sees far fewer foreign nomads than San Miguel de Allende or Oaxaca, so prices have not been pushed up by outside demand. A solo remote worker living comfortably in or near the historic center can expect to spend roughly $900 to $1,600 per month, depending on how much you eat out and whether you rent short or long term. Housing is the biggest swing: a furnished one-bedroom near the cathedral costs meaningfully more per night on a short booking than a local lease negotiated in person. Everyday costs, market produce, comida corrida lunches, buses, and coffee, are genuinely cheap. These are editorial estimates, not surveyed figures, so treat them as a planning starting point.

  • Furnished 1-bed rental: roughly $400 to $700 per month
  • Comida corrida (set lunch): around $4 to $7
  • Local bus or combi ride: under $1
  • Coworking or cafe day: a few dollars in coffee, or a modest monthly desk fee

Where to Work in Zacatecas

Zacatecas is not a saturated coworking hub, so your setup will lean on laptop-friendly cafes plus a few dedicated desks. The historic center has a handful of reliable spots with decent wifi, strong coffee, and the kind of quiet mid-morning lull that suits calls. Home internet in central apartments is generally adequate for video meetings, though you should confirm the plan before committing to a longer stay, since older buildings vary. The altitude means cool interiors year-round, so a warm layer helps on winter mornings when cafes are slow to heat up. If you need a professional backdrop or a stable connection for a big call, book a desk in advance rather than assuming walk-in space.

  • Cafes along Avenida Hidalgo near the cathedral are the easiest daily option
  • Confirm apartment internet speed in writing before a long lease
  • Carry a backup mobile hotspot for important calls
  • Mornings are quietest; afternoons fill with students and locals

Getting Around Zacatecas

The historic center is walkable but genuinely steep. Zacatecas is built into a narrow ravine, so its callejones climb and twist, and a short map distance can mean a real hill. Sturdy shoes matter more here than in flatter colonial towns. For longer trips, local buses and combis are cheap and cover the city and neighboring Guadalupe. The teleferico cable car glides between two hilltops and is more a scenic ride than daily transport, but it is a lovely way to read the city's layout. General Leobardo C. Ruiz International Airport (ZCL) sits outside town with limited flights, so many travelers instead take comfortable long-distance buses to Guadalajara or Aguascalientes, both a few hours away, and connect onward from there.

  • Wear real walking shoes; the callejones are steep and cobbled
  • Buses and combis reach Guadalupe and the outer neighborhoods cheaply
  • The teleferico is scenic rather than practical daily transport
  • ZCL airport has limited routes; buses to Aguascalientes or Guadalajara widen your options
  • The altitude can leave you winded on hills at first

Visas for Zacatecas

Most visitors from the US, Canada, the UK, the EU, and many other countries enter Mexico as tourists without arranging a visa in advance. The tourist entry has historically allowed up to 180 days, but this is now set at the immigration officer's discretion, and shorter stamps have become common, so never assume you will get the full period. Check the number written on your entry record and plan around it. If you want to base yourself here for many months or longer, the temporary resident visa is the usual route, and it is generally started at a Mexican consulate abroad before you arrive. Rules and enforcement change, and individual cases differ, so treat this as editorial orientation and confirm current requirements with official Mexican sources or an immigration professional.

  • Tourist entry: up to 180 days, but often granted for less at officer discretion
  • Check the exact days written on your entry record on arrival
  • Temporary resident visa suits longer stays and usually starts at a consulate abroad
  • This is editorial guidance, not legal advice; verify with official sources

Best Time to Visit Zacatecas

At around 2,440 meters, Zacatecas has a high-altitude climate that stays cool at night all year, even in summer. Spring, from roughly March to May, is warm, dry, and bright, and is many people's favorite window. Summer brings the rainy season, with afternoon storms that green the surrounding hills and clear quickly. Autumn stays pleasant, while winter days are mild in the sun but genuinely cold once it sets, so pack layers regardless of season. Culturally, Semana Santa fills the city around Easter, and the Festival Cultural de Zacatecas is a major draw with music and performances in the plazas. Book accommodation early around either, since central rooms sell out and prices climb.

  • Spring (March to May): warm, dry, and clear, a strong all-round choice
  • Summer: afternoon rains, lush hills, quick-clearing storms
  • Winter: mild sunny days but cold nights; bring layers
  • Festival Cultural de Zacatecas and Semana Santa are peak, lively, and busy

Pros and Cons of Zacatecas

Zacatecas rewards travelers who want a strikingly beautiful, deeply Mexican colonial city without the tourist crush. The pink-stone baroque cathedral, the mines, the Coronel museums, and the nightly callejoneadas give it real character, and the low cost and warm locals make daily life easy. The honest trade-offs are practical: little spoken English, limited nomad infrastructure, a small international community, and the steep terrain and altitude that take adjustment. On safety, the historic center is generally calm and well touristed, but Zacatecas state has had serious cartel-related security concerns, so the sensible approach is to stay informed, keep to central and populated areas, avoid unnecessary intercity travel at night, and follow local guidance rather than assuming the whole state mirrors the peaceful center.

  • Pros: stunning architecture, low cost, rich food and culture, few crowds, warm locals
  • Cons: thin English, small nomad scene, steep hills, high altitude, limited flights
  • Safety: central areas are calm, but stay informed given wider state-level concerns

Who Zacatecas Is For

Zacatecas suits the curious, self-reliant nomad who values authenticity over convenience and is happy to function in Spanish. If you love colonial history, museums, mountain light, and evenings that end with a wandering street party rather than a rooftop bar scene, you will feel at home here. It is a strong pick for a focused month or two of deep work in a beautiful, affordable setting, or for a slower traveler weaving through central Mexico who wants somewhere less discovered than the usual stops. It is a weaker fit for those who need a ready-made expat network, extensive coworking, English everywhere, or flat and easy walking. Come with patience, a warm layer, some Spanish, and an appetite for one of Mexico's most underrated cities.

Frequently Asked Questions About Zacatecas

How much does it cost to live in Zacatecas as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Zacatecas is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Zacatecas?

Zacatecas rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Zacatecas safe for digital nomads?

Zacatecas scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Zacatecas a good city for digital nomads overall?

Zacatecas stands out for culture and affordability among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
