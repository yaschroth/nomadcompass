Cost of Living in Rhodes

Rhodes is one of the more affordable Greek islands for longer stays, though prices spike sharply in summer when tourist demand pushes short-term rents and dining costs up considerably.

A one-bedroom apartment in Rhodes Town center runs roughly 350-500 EUR ($380-545 USD) per month on a longer-term lease. In quieter residential neighborhoods like Zefyros or farther south along the west coast, you can find the same for 250-350 EUR ($270-380 USD). Be aware that annual leases are scarce; most landlords prefer seasonal contracts from April to October.

  • Rent (1BR, central): 350-500 EUR/month
  • Rent (1BR, cheaper area): 250-350 EUR/month
  • Groceries: 150-220 EUR/month cooking at home
  • Eating out: A sit-down taverna meal runs 12-20 EUR; a gyros wrap is 3-5 EUR
  • Coworking hot-desk: 16 EUR/day or around 250 EUR/month at Rhodes Project
  • Local transport (buses): 1-8 EUR per trip depending on distance; budget 40-60 EUR/month
  • Coffee at a cafe: 3-5 EUR

Realistic monthly total: 1,000-1,500 EUR ($1,090-1,630 USD) for a frugal-to-comfortable solo lifestyle. Off-season (November to March), you can live closer to the lower end. In peak summer, groceries and transport costs stay flat but eating out and any short-term accommodation will cost more.

Where to Work

Dedicated coworking infrastructure on Rhodes is thin but functional. The two confirmed spaces are Rhodes Project SCE (Chimarras 27, near the city center) and STAY Hotel's coworking floor in New Town. Rhodes Project offers hot-desk day passes at 16 EUR and monthly memberships at 250 EUR, with 100 Mbps fiber, ergonomic chairs, a garden patio, and a kitchen. STAY Hotel's space is open-plan with plug sockets at every desk and a private room for calls, included if you stay at the hotel or available separately.

Cafe work is genuinely viable in Rhodes Town. Coffee culture is strong here, Greek cafes stay open for hours, and most places have free WiFi. Speeds in town are generally reliable on fixed connections, though they can be inconsistent in village cafes or at the southern end of the island. Mobile data (4G) covers the main coastal strip well and is a solid backup.

Outside of Rhodes Town, working from a kafeneio in a village square is possible but do not count on it for deadline-critical work. The island has no major co-living infrastructure as of 2025.

Getting Around

Rhodes Town itself is very walkable. The Medieval Old Town is pedestrian-only inside the walls, and most of New Town's amenities sit within a 20-minute walk. Outside the town, you need a plan.

Two public bus networks serve the island: RODA covers the west coast and KTEL covers the east coast. Both depart from the central station near Mandraki harbor. Fares are cheap (1-8 EUR depending on distance), but services outside of peak summer run infrequently, and the last bus often leaves before evening. For serious island mobility, renting a scooter (15-30 EUR/day) or a small car (35-60 EUR/day) is the practical choice.

Airport to city: The airport sits about 14 km southwest of Rhodes Town. Bus line 5 connects the terminal to Mandraki for 2.20 EUR and takes roughly 40 minutes. Taxis cost 25-30 EUR for the same journey. There is no train on the island.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Rhodes is part of Greece, which is in the Schengen Area. Citizens of the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and most other non-EU countries can enter visa-free and stay up to 90 days within any 180-day period across the entire Schengen zone, not just Greece.

Greece offers a dedicated Digital Nomad Visa for non-EU, non-EEA citizens who work remotely for employers or clients outside Greece. It permits stays of up to 12 months (renewable once). Requirements include a minimum net monthly income of 3,500 EUR (roughly $3,800 USD), health insurance, a clean criminal record, and proof of remote employment. The application fee is approximately 225 EUR total. EU and EEA citizens have the right to live in Greece indefinitely and can register for a residence certificate.

Extensions beyond the 90-day Schengen limit without the nomad visa are not available. Always confirm entry requirements with the official Greek consulate or immigration authority before travel, as rules can change.

Best Time to Visit

Rhodes gets around 300 days of sunshine per year, which is the highest average in Greece, so most of the year is reasonable. That said, the experience varies significantly by season.

May and June are the sweet spot for remote workers: warm and sunny (22-27 degrees C), the island is lively but not yet overwhelmed, accommodation is easier to find, and prices are lower than peak summer. September and October are equally good, often better: the summer crowds have thinned, the sea is still warm from months of sun, and you can rent accommodation on more flexible terms.

July and August are relentlessly hot (33-35 degrees C) with the island packed with tourists, which strains transport, restaurants, and your focus. Winter (December to February) is mild at 15-17 degrees C with some rain, and most tourist infrastructure closes or scales back sharply. If you want a quiet, local feel and do not mind reduced amenities, winter works. Spring and autumn are the better all-round windows for productive remote work.

Pros and Cons

  • Outstanding natural setting with beaches, medieval architecture, and clear water
  • Affordable cost of living compared to most of Western Europe
  • Strong English proficiency throughout Rhodes Town
  • Good food scene with excellent fresh seafood and traditional Greek cuisine
  • Digital nomad visa available for non-EU citizens wanting to stay past 90 days
  • Safe, relaxed atmosphere with low crime
  • Thin coworking infrastructure with only two confirmed dedicated spaces
  • Heavily seasonal: many venues close in winter and transport becomes sparse
  • Getting around without a vehicle is limiting outside of Rhodes Town
  • Peak summer is crowded and hot, which makes productive work harder
  • Small nomad community compared to hubs like Athens, Lisbon, or Bali
  • Long-term rentals are hard to find as landlords favor short tourist lettings

Who Rhodes Is Best For

Rhodes suits remote workers who value lifestyle and environment as much as infrastructure. If you want a Mediterranean island base with genuine history, affordable food, good weather for most of the year, and a slower pace, Rhodes delivers. It works well for freelancers, writers, designers, or developers who are self-sufficient and do not need a buzzing nomad scene. The spring and autumn shoulder seasons are the ideal windows: you get warm weather, beach access, and a functional island without the summer chaos.

It is a harder sell if you rely on frequent coworking events or a built-in remote-work community, if you need to work reliably from outside Rhodes Town, or if you are planning a long winter stay and want a full social calendar. Athens is a better base for nomads who want city infrastructure, year-round options, and a larger international community.

Frequently Asked Questions About Rhodes

How much does it cost to live in Rhodes as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,800 a month in Rhodes for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Rhodes?

Rhodes rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Rhodes safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Rhodes is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Rhodes a good city for digital nomads overall?

Rhodes stands out for climate and nature among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
