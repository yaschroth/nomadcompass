Living in Bandung as a Digital Nomad

Java's cool highland, volcanic craters, tea plantations, and café culture. Plan on roughly $1,300 a month. Its strengths are low costs and climate, while air quality is where it scores lower.

Cost of Living in Bandung

Bandung is one of the most affordable mid-size cities in Southeast Asia for remote workers. A comfortable nomad lifestyle runs roughly IDR 9,000,000-18,000,000 per month (USD 550-1,100), depending on how you live.

  • Rent, central apartment (Dago, Braga, Lengkong): IDR 3,500,000-5,500,000/month (USD 210-340)
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Kiaracondong, Gedebage): IDR 1,800,000-3,000,000/month (USD 110-185)
  • Groceries (cooking most meals): IDR 800,000-1,500,000/month (USD 50-90)
  • Eating out (local Sundanese warungs): IDR 15,000-40,000 per meal (USD 1-2.50); western cafes run IDR 60,000-120,000
  • Coworking hot-desk (monthly): IDR 800,000-1,400,000/month (USD 50-85) at spaces like Creya or NextSPACE; daily passes IDR 50,000-150,000
  • Local transport (Gojek/Grab motorbike rides): IDR 300,000-600,000/month (USD 18-37) for regular use
  • Total range: USD 550-900/month for a lean but comfortable setup; USD 900-1,100 if you want a nicer apartment and eat out often

Bandung is noticeably cheaper than Bali for equivalent quality. The Sundanese food scene means you can eat extremely well for almost nothing if you stick to local spots.

Where to Work

Bandung has a solid, growing coworking scene and one of Indonesia's densest cafe cultures outside Jakarta. You have real options here.

Coworking Spaces

Creya Spaces in Lengkong is the most reliable dedicated coworking option, with 300 Mbps fiber, daily cleaning, and monthly hot-desks from IDR 800,000. NextSPACE by UnionSPACE on Jl. Trunojoyo is another well-reviewed option in the city center. BLOCK71 Bandung at the Innovation Factory caters to tech and startup workers and has a stronger community feel. Point Lab on Jl. Banda and H04 on Jl. Halmahera are smaller but highly rated by regulars (4.6-4.7 stars on Google).

Cafe Work Culture

Bandung may have more cafes per square kilometer than any other Indonesian city. The Dago, Setiabudi, and Cihampelas areas are especially dense. Most cafes welcome laptop workers and have WiFi; many have power outlets at every table. Popular spots include Sans. Co Space on Jl. Ir. H. Juanda, which straddles the coworking/cafe line, and dozens of independently run spots around Dago.

The Real WiFi Situation

WiFi quality is inconsistent across the city. Dedicated coworking spaces consistently deliver 50-300 Mbps. Cafes range from perfectly adequate for video calls to frustratingly slow during peak hours. The smart move is to connect and run a speed test before committing to a long session. Mobile data via Telkomsel or XL is fast, cheap, and a reliable backup for anything critical.

Getting Around

Bandung is a large, hilly city and not particularly walkable beyond individual neighborhoods. You will rely on ride-hailing for most movement.

Ride-Hailing

Gojek and Grab are the main options and cover the whole city. Motorbike (ojek) rides are cheapest at IDR 8,000-20,000 for short trips; car rides run IDR 25,000-70,000 for most cross-city journeys. Both apps work reliably and prices are fixed upfront.

Public Transport

Angkot minibuses cover fixed routes at IDR 4,000-7,000 per ride and can be hailed anywhere along the route. They are cheap but slow and route maps are hard to find. The Trans Metro Pasundan BRT system is a cleaner, more structured alternative for a few key corridors.

Getting to and from the Airport

Husein Sastranegara Airport (BDO) sits about 5 km from the city center. A Grab or Gojek car from downtown costs IDR 50,000-80,000 and takes 15-30 minutes outside of rush hour. The Whoosh high-speed rail connects Bandung to Jakarta (Halim station) in roughly 40 minutes, with tickets from IDR 300,000. This makes Jakarta day trips or onward travel genuinely convenient.

Walkability

The Braga-Asia Afrika corridor and parts of Dago are walkable for short errands. Beyond those pockets, uneven pavements, traffic, and hills make walking impractical for longer distances.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most Western passports (US, UK, EU, Australia, Canada) enter Indonesia on a Visa on Arrival (VOA) available at the airport for approximately USD 35. It grants 30 days and can be extended once at a local immigration office for another 30 days, giving you 60 days total. ASEAN passport holders and a handful of other nationalities get 30 days visa-free at no cost, with no extension option.

For longer stays, Indonesia introduced the E33G Remote Worker Visa (sometimes called the digital nomad visa), valid for 12 months and aimed at people working remotely for foreign companies. The main requirement is proof of annual foreign income of at least USD 60,000. It costs approximately USD 150 to apply and takes about 8-10 business days to process. It is not extendable but can be reapplied for.

Alternatively, an index visa (B211A) is a popular choice for those spending 60-180 days, though it requires a local sponsor or agent to process.

Always confirm current requirements with official Indonesian immigration sources or a licensed visa agent before traveling, as rules change.

Best Time to Visit

Bandung sits at 768 meters above sea level, so temperatures stay mild year-round at 20-26 C (68-79 F). You will want a light jacket for evenings regardless of season.

The dry season runs from June to September and is the best window for remote work. Lower humidity, more sunshine, and fewer power outages make daily life easier. The air is clearer and outdoor areas (parks, volcanic day trips) are accessible.

The wet season (October through May) brings heavy daily rain, especially November through March. Afternoon downpours are common, flooding can slow transport, and humidity climbs noticeably. That said, Bandung is still workable during the wet season: rain tends to fall in concentrated bursts rather than all day, and accommodation prices dip. Avoid December and January if you want to skip Indonesian school holidays, when domestic tourism peaks and prices rise.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Very low cost of living with good quality of life
  • Outstanding food scene, from IDR 15,000 warungs to creative restaurants
  • Cooler climate than Jakarta or Bali at altitude
  • Growing coworking scene and excellent cafe culture
  • Close to nature: volcanoes, tea plantations, and highland parks within an hour
  • High-speed rail to Jakarta in 40 minutes
  • Vibrant creative and tech startup community
  • Fewer tourists than Bali, feels more authentically Indonesian

Cons

  • Air quality is a real concern: vehicle exhaust and industrial pollution push AQI into unhealthy ranges on bad days
  • Traffic congestion is severe, especially on weekends when Jakarta residents arrive en masse
  • Walkability is poor outside a few central pockets
  • WiFi quality in cafes is inconsistent and requires testing each spot
  • English is less widely spoken than in Bali; day-to-day life requires some Bahasa Indonesia
  • Fewer international restaurants and nightlife options than Jakarta or Bali
  • The wet season can disrupt transport and outdoor plans for months at a time

Who Bandung Is Best For

Bandung suits remote workers who want a genuinely affordable Indonesian city without the tourist bubble of Bali. If you care about food, cooler weather, and being embedded in a real Indonesian urban environment rather than an expat enclave, Bandung delivers. It works especially well for people in tech or creative fields, since there is a real local community to plug into. The Jakarta rail connection makes it a smart base for anyone who needs periodic access to a major business hub.

You will thrive here if you are comfortable navigating a city with limited English outside of coworking spaces and international cafes, do not mind relying on ride-hailing for all transport, and are prepared to check air quality before deciding on outdoor plans.

Skip Bandung if you need a large English-speaking expat community, rely on walkability, or find heavy urban traffic and air pollution a dealbreaker. It is also a harder choice if your remote work requires near-perfect, uninterrupted internet: the backup situation (mobile data) is solid, but it requires planning. Bali or Chiang Mai are better fits for first-time Southeast Asia nomads who want a ready-made support network.

Frequently Asked Questions About Bandung

How much does it cost to live in Bandung as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,300 a month in Bandung for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Bandung?

Bandung rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Bandung safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Bandung is 7/10, good for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Bandung a good city for digital nomads overall?

Bandung stands out for low costs and climate among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,300 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
