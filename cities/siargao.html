Cost of Living in Siargao

Siargao sits comfortably in the middle of Southeast Asia's affordability spectrum. It is cheaper than Bali for accommodation, but pricier than Chiang Mai for food and coworking. Here is a realistic monthly breakdown for a solo nomad living in or near General Luna:

  • Rent, central General Luna (1-bedroom, AC): 20,000-30,000 PHP ($340-520)
  • Rent, outlying areas like Pacifico or Santa Fe (basic private room): 10,000-18,000 PHP ($170-310)
  • Groceries (wet market + supermarket mix): 5,000-8,000 PHP ($85-140)
  • Eating out (local spots + occasional Western meal): 8,000-14,000 PHP ($135-240)
  • Coworking hot-desk (monthly): 4,000-7,000 PHP ($70-120)
  • Scooter rental: 5,000-7,000 PHP/month ($85-120); fuel adds roughly 1,500 PHP ($25)
  • Utilities (if not included in rent): 2,000-4,000 PHP ($35-70)
  • SIM data or home internet: 500-3,000 PHP ($10-52)

Total monthly range: roughly 35,000-65,000 PHP ($600-1,100) for a budget-to-mid lifestyle. A comfortable setup with a private apartment and regular restaurant meals typically lands around 60,000-80,000 PHP ($1,000-1,400). Prices spike December through April when tourist season drives up short-term rental rates.

Where to Work

Siargao's internet situation improved dramatically after Starlink arrived in early 2023. Most coworking spaces, cafes, and many guesthouses now run on Starlink, which delivers reliable speeds for video calls, uploads, and general remote work. The days of hunting for a stable connection are largely over in General Luna, though power outages still happen and backup generators are not universal.

Dedicated Coworking Spaces

Coco Space is the island's main coworking hub, located between General Luna and Santa Fe. It has around 25 desks, private call booths, AC, a pool, a cafe, and solar power backup. Day pass is 500 PHP ($8); monthly membership runs 7,000 PHP ($120). It closes at 1 AM so it accommodates different time zones reasonably well.

Alter Space sits right in General Luna and runs 24/7 (with pre-booking required for late nights). It has both fibre and Starlink with a generator backup, making it one of the most reliable setups on the island. Day rate is 650 PHP ($11).

Cafe Work Culture

Working from cafes is socially accepted, especially during off-peak hours. Spotted Pig, The Brunch Spot, and Ohm Cafe are the most popular laptop-friendly spots, but outlets are limited and space fills fast by mid-morning. Most cafes have Starlink. If you plan to work all day, a dedicated coworking space is a better choice than competing for a corner table.

Getting Around

Siargao has no Grab service. Scooter rental is the default for most nomads and costs 300-500 PHP ($5-9) per day or around 5,000-7,000 PHP ($85-120) per month. Fuel is cheap at roughly 65 PHP ($1.10) per liter. Riding a scooter is the fastest and most flexible way to get anywhere.

For those who do not ride, habal-habal (motorcycle taxis) cover most routes. Within General Luna, tricycle rides cost 30-60 PHP ($0.50-1.00). For longer trips across the island, expect to pay 100-300 PHP ($1.70-5.20).

General Luna itself is walkable for basics: the beach, a cluster of cafes, and convenience stores are all within a short stroll if you stay centrally. Other parts of the island require wheels.

From the Airport

Sayak Airport (IAO) sits about 45 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes from General Luna depending on traffic and road conditions. Shared van transfers cost around 300 PHP ($5). A habal-habal runs 350-400 PHP ($6-7) and a private tricycle is about 600 PHP ($10) for the whole vehicle. There are no buses. Arrange a transfer in advance if you arrive late or with heavy luggage.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of most Western countries, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and EU nations, enter the Philippines visa-free for 30 days. You can extend your stay at the Bureau of Immigration office in General Luna for approximately 3,000-3,500 PHP ($52-60) per 29-day extension, stackable up to 36 months total for visa-waiver nationalities. After your first extension, you may need to apply for an ACR-I card (Alien Certificate of Registration). The Philippines does not currently offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. Indian nationals receive 14 days visa-free, non-extendable. Always confirm current requirements via the official Bureau of Immigration website (immigration.gov.ph) before you travel.

Best Time to Visit

Siargao has a distinct microclimate compared to the rest of the Philippines. The driest and most reliable months for remote work are March through May, when sunny days are consistent, seas are calm, and power outages are less frequent. October and November also offer a good balance: surf season peaks and the weather is largely clear, though typhoon risk is higher.

The wettest period runs from December through February, driven by the Amihan monsoon hitting Siargao from the northeast. This is when flooding, road damage, and power instability are most common. Budget accommodation is significantly cheaper off-season but connectivity suffers.

For remote workers who value stable internet and clear skies without peak-season crowds and prices, late March to June is the sweet spot. July through September brings occasional rain but is manageable and less expensive than peak months.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Starlink internet is now widespread, making reliable remote work genuinely possible
  • Pro: Low cost of living relative to Europe or North America
  • Pro: Stunning natural environment: surf, jungle, lagoons, and island-hopping all close by
  • Pro: Active and welcoming nomad community centred in General Luna
  • Pro: English is widely spoken; locals are friendly and accustomed to foreigners
  • Pro: Bureau of Immigration office on the island makes visa extensions convenient
  • Con: Power outages are common and can disrupt work without a backup-equipped space
  • Con: Getting there is genuinely inconvenient: connecting through Cebu or Manila adds significant travel time
  • Con: Scooter accidents are the leading safety risk; roads can be rough
  • Con: Typhoon vulnerability: Typhoon Odette in 2021 caused severe damage, and the island rebuilds slowly after major storms
  • Con: Peak season (December to April) pushes accommodation prices up sharply and beaches get crowded
  • Con: Limited options for specialists: no major hospital, no large pharmacy chains

Who Siargao Is Best For

Siargao works best for nomads who want nature and community alongside their work. If you surf, freedive, or want to spend mornings in the ocean and afternoons at a laptop, the rhythm here suits you well. It also appeals to people who want a real nomad hub without the mass-tourism feel of Bali or the urban pressure of a big city.

You will also do well here if you keep flexible hours. Most of the island runs on a slow, surf-oriented schedule, and the best cafes and spaces fill up by late morning. Early risers or late-night workers get the most out of the infrastructure.

Skip Siargao if you need 24/7 guaranteed uptime for work. Power and connectivity, while much improved, are still not as consistent as in Manila or Cebu. It also does not suit nomads who rely on frequent international travel, since every flight in or out requires a connection. And if you need access to specialist medical care, the island is not the right base for extended stays.

Frequently Asked Questions About Siargao

How much does it cost to live in Siargao as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Siargao is around $1,600 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Siargao?

With a WiFi score of 4/10, connectivity in Siargao is patchy for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Siargao safe for digital nomads?

Siargao scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Siargao a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Siargao is worth a look if you value nature and English. Costs sit near $1,600 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
