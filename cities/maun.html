Setting Up as a Nomad in Maun

The last town before the Okavango Delta, where the tar road ends and the mokoro starts. Plan on roughly $1,200 a month. Its strengths are nature and English, while the size of the nomad scene is where it scores lower.

Best Neighborhoods in Maun

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Maun Centre and the Mall

$$
Practical & Functional

The tar road through town, with the shops and the bank

The functional centre strung along the main road, with the Ngami Centre and Nandos mall, the supermarkets, the banks and the safari operators' offices. It is not attractive but it is where everything practical happens, and it is where anyone staying long does their shopping and their admin.

Best for: Anyone who needs supermarkets, banks and the safari offices close by
Pros
  • All shops, banks and services in one place
  • Safari operators and booking offices
  • Best connectivity in the town
Cons
  • Dusty, hot and unattractive
  • Traffic on the single main road
  • No character whatsoever

Thamalakane Riverfront

$$$
Riverside & Green

Along the river, where the lodges and the good houses are

The strip following the Thamalakane river northeast of the centre, where most of the lodges, guest farms and the better houses sit under big trees with the water in front of them. Hippos and elephants come through. It is the pleasant side of Maun and where almost every foreigner ends up.

Best for: Almost anyone staying more than a few days
Pros
  • River frontage with genuine wildlife passing
  • Lodges, restaurants and the sociable end of town
  • Shaded and cooler than the centre
Cons
  • The highest rents in Maun
  • Hippos and elephants are a real hazard at night
  • Ten minutes from the shops

Maun Airport Area

$$
Aviation & Transient

Around the airstrip, where the light aircraft leave from

The district around Maun's airport, which handles an extraordinary volume of light aircraft for a town this size because every delta camp is reached by air. Charter offices, pilot accommodation and a run of practical businesses sit around it. It is functional and convenient rather than pleasant.

Best for: Pilots, charter staff and anyone flying in and out repeatedly
Pros
  • Airport and charter offices on the doorstep
  • Practical services and vehicle hire nearby
  • Close to the town centre
Cons
  • Aircraft noise throughout the day
  • Nothing scenic or green
  • Transient population with high turnover

Boseja and the Village Side

$
Local & Affordable

The residential wards where Maun actually lives

The wards spreading south and west of the centre, where the town's Batawana population lives in family compounds among the mopane, with local shops and the everyday economy of the place. Rents are the lowest in Maun and this is where a long stay makes financial sense.

Best for: Long stays where the rent is the deciding factor
Pros
  • The lowest rents in the town
  • Genuine local community rather than a safari bubble
  • Local shops at ordinary prices
Cons
  • Sandy tracks rather than roads in places
  • No tourist services and limited English signage
  • Water and power interruptions

Okavango Delta Camps (by air, 30 to 200 km)

$$$$
Wilderness & Inaccessible

Inside the delta, reachable only by light aircraft or boat

The camps and concessions inside the delta itself, which is where the wildlife is and where almost everyone who comes to Maun is actually going. Access is by light aircraft from Maun airport or by mokoro and boat through the channels. There is no road, no grid power and no connectivity.

Best for: Short stays inside the delta rather than any kind of base
Pros
  • One of the great wildlife landscapes on earth
  • Mokoro and walking safaris from the camps
  • Complete disconnection, which some people want
Cons
  • Reachable only by air or boat, at real cost
  • No connectivity at all
  • Camp pricing is in dollars and is high

Cost of Living in Maun

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Maun, so the $1,200 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 921 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $1,200 a month as a solo remote worker in Maun, which is more than the town's remoteness suggests because almost everything is trucked in from Gaborone or across the border. A one-bedroom house or flat runs roughly $400 to $700. Groceries at the supermarkets are noticeably more expensive than in South Africa, and fresh produce is the worst affected. Safari-facing services are priced in dollars for tourists and bear no relation to local costs. Fuel is expensive and matters, since anything outside town involves driving. Botswana is one of the more expensive countries in the region.

Where to Work in Maun

Maun has no formal coworking and remote work happens from lodges, from accommodation or from the few cafes. Botswana's fixed broadband reaches the town but speeds are modest and reliability inconsistent, and the safari operators who need connectivity generally pay for satellite or dedicated links. Mobile data works in town and disappears within a short drive. Power cuts happen. Anyone with a demanding video schedule should test carefully before committing, and should assume that the delta itself, where most people actually want to go, has no connectivity at all. The practical arrangement most people here settle on is to do the demanding work in town and treat any trip into the delta as time genuinely offline.

Getting Around Maun

Maun is spread out and the tar road runs through the middle of it, with the airport, the shops and the river lodges strung along it. A vehicle matters here more than in most towns on this site: combis cover the main routes cheaply, but everything interesting requires either a four-wheel drive or a booking with an operator. The airport is unusually busy for a town this size because it is the gateway to the delta, with light aircraft leaving all day for the camps. Gaborone is a long drive or a short flight, and the Namibian border is a few hours west.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Maun

Botswana grants most Western nationals ninety days visa-free per entry within a twelve-month period, which is generous and straightforward. There is no Botswanan digital nomad visa. Extensions and longer stays are handled through immigration and are tied to work or residence, which is a bureaucratic process. Namibia, Zambia, Zimbabwe and South Africa are all reachable overland and the Kazungula crossing puts four countries within a short distance, which makes a border reset easy. Botswana has strict rules on importing meat and plant material because of foot-and-mouth controls. Anyone driving in should also budget time for the veterinary cordon fences, where vehicles are stopped and shoes are disinfected.

Best Time to Visit Maun

The dry season from May to September is both the best weather and, paradoxically, when the delta floods, because the water arriving from the Angolan highlands takes months to reach here. That combination of dry air, cool nights and high water is why July and August are peak season, with prices to match and cold mornings that surprise people. October is very hot, regularly above thirty-eight, before the rains break. The wet season from November to March brings afternoon storms, green landscape, dispersed game and much lower prices. Winter nights can drop close to freezing.

Pros and Cons of Maun

Maun is the gateway to the Okavango Delta, one of the great wildlife landscapes on earth, and living here means light aircraft over the river and mokoro trips as a weekend rather than a trip of a lifetime. Botswana is stable, safe, English-speaking and well run. Against that, it is expensive for the region, the town itself is functional rather than attractive, connectivity is weak, and there is no community beyond the safari industry. Medical facilities are limited and serious cases mean Gaborone or Johannesburg. Prices also swing hard with the safari season, so the same house can cost noticeably more in July than in February.

Who Maun Is For

Maun suits people who came for the delta and need a base between trips, and those working in conservation, safari operations or wildlife research, which is what most resident foreigners here do. It works for anyone whose priority is wildlife and space and who can tolerate a small, practical town. It is a poor fit for anyone needing reliable connectivity, urban amenities or an international social scene, and for budget travellers, since Botswana's deliberate high-value tourism policy keeps costs up rather than down. It also suits pilots and anyone in aviation, since the airport handles an unusual volume of light aircraft for a town of this size.

Frequently Asked Questions About Maun

How much does it cost to live in Maun as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Maun is around

,200 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Maun?

Maun rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Maun safe for digital nomads?

Maun scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Maun a good city for digital nomads overall?

Maun stands out for nature and the outdoors and widely spoken English among remote workers. With a typical budget near ,200 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
