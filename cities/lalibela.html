Setting Up as a Nomad in Lalibela

A highland pilgrimage town famed for its medieval churches hewn straight down into the rock, a living wonder of Ethiopia. A subtropical highland climate and strong culture make Lalibela an easy base, and at about $600 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the size of the nomad scene in mind.

Cost of Living in Lalibela

Lalibela is very cheap, one of the most affordable places a traveller could base themselves, reflecting Ethiopia's low prices and the town's small, rural highland setting. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $500 to $900 a month. Simple guesthouse rooms or basic flats cost little, often $100 to $300 for a longer stay, and local Ethiopian food is inexpensive. These are our editorial estimates in US dollars, priced against the Ethiopian birr, which has fluctuated considerably. Beyond a room and food, the main cost is the entry ticket to the churches and the occasional guide.

  • Guesthouse room or basic flat, long stay: roughly $100 to $300 a month
  • Plate of injera with wot: roughly $2 to $4
  • Traditional coffee ceremony: a modest sum
  • Church-complex entry ticket: a one-off, priced for tourists

Where to Work in Lalibela

Lalibela is a remote highland pilgrimage town, emphatically not a remote-work destination, and connectivity is a serious limitation. Internet is slow, patchy and unreliable, and Ethiopia has periodically imposed nationwide internet shutdowns, so a working connection cannot be counted on at all; anyone dependent on the internet should treat Lalibela as a place to visit rather than to work from. There is no coworking scene, and even basic mobile data through Ethio Telecom can be inconsistent. Come for the churches and the culture, plan your work around a visit rather than the other way around, and manage expectations accordingly.

Getting Around Lalibela

Lalibela is a small hillside town, and the famous churches are clustered within walking distance in the centre, so getting around means walking, often up and down steep, rough paths at over 2,500 metres, which takes some effort. Minibuses and bajaj auto-rickshaws help with longer or steeper trips. The town has a small airport with flights from Addis Ababa, which is by far the easiest way in and out; the overland roads are long and rough. Local guides, easily arranged, add enormously to understanding the churches and their living religious role.

Visas for Lalibela

Most visitors enter Ethiopia on an e-visa, applied for online before arrival and typically issued for 30 or 90 days, or a visa on arrival at the main airport for eligible nationalities. Extensions are handled through the immigration authorities in Addis Ababa. Ethiopia does not have a digital nomad visa. Given that entry rules and the security situation can change, confirm the current visa options and, importantly, the latest travel advisories for Ethiopia and the Amhara region before travelling, as conditions have varied, and note your permitted period to avoid overstaying.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Lalibela

Lalibela sits high in the Ethiopian highlands, giving it a mild, temperate climate despite its tropical latitude, with warm days and cool nights year-round. The best time to visit is the dry season from October to March, when clear skies make exploring the churches and the surrounding highlands comfortable. The main rains fall from June to September, bringing heavy afternoon downpours that can complicate travel on rough roads. The most extraordinary time is the Ethiopian Christmas, Genna, in early January, when tens of thousands of white-robed pilgrims converge on the churches in a moving spectacle.

Pros and Cons of Lalibela

Lalibela offers a living wonder of the world, with the realities of a very remote and basic town.

  • Pros: the astonishing rock-hewn churches, a UNESCO World Heritage site and living centre of Ethiopian Orthodox faith, extraordinarily low costs, a mild highland climate, dramatic scenery, and a deeply authentic cultural experience.
  • Cons: extremely poor and unreliable internet with the risk of national shutdowns, a very remote location, basic infrastructure and limited food variety, no nomad community, and the need to follow travel advisories carefully given Ethiopia's regional security situation.

Who Lalibela Is For

Lalibela is for the culturally and spiritually curious traveller who wants to witness one of the world's great wonders and a living religious tradition, and who is not trying to work online while there. History lovers, photographers and adventurous, off-the-beaten-path travellers will find it profoundly moving, especially around the Genna pilgrimage. It suits a shorter, immersive visit rather than any kind of work base. It is entirely unsuited to anyone who needs reliable internet, infrastructure, a community or comfort, and travellers should check current safety advice before going. Come for the churches, keep your plans flexible, and let Lalibela's faith and stone leave their mark.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lalibela

How much does it cost to live in Lalibela as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Lalibela is around $600 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Lalibela?

Lalibela rates 3 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is on the limited side for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Lalibela safe for digital nomads?

Lalibela scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Lalibela a good city for digital nomads overall?

Lalibela stands out for culture and affordability among remote workers. With a typical budget near $600 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
