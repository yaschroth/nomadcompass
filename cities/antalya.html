Antalya for Digital Nomads: The Honest Take

Experience turkish Riviera with ancient ruins, beaches, and nomad visas. For remote workers, Antalya leans on nature and easy visas, with a typical budget near $1,800 a month. The main trade-off is the language barrier.

Cost of Living in Antalya

Antalya is one of the most affordable Mediterranean cities for remote workers, though prices have risen with inflation and growing expat interest. Expect to spend around 15,000-25,000 TRY ($450-760) per month on a comfortable lifestyle, excluding rent.

  • Rent (city center, Muratpasa/Kaleici area): 10,000-18,000 TRY ($300-550) for a furnished 1-bedroom apartment
  • Rent (suburban areas, Kepez or outer Lara): 5,000-9,000 TRY ($150-275) for a 1-bedroom
  • Groceries: 4,000-6,000 TRY ($120-185) per month at local markets and supermarkets
  • Eating out (local restaurants): 100-250 TRY ($3-8) per meal; touristy Kaleici restaurants run 400-900 TRY ($12-27)
  • Coworking hot desk: 3,000-5,000 TRY ($90-150) per month; daily passes around 300-500 TRY ($9-15)
  • Local transport (bus/dolmus): 20-30 TRY ($0.60-0.90) per ride; monthly pass around 600-800 TRY ($18-24)
  • Mobile data (10 GB): roughly 300 TRY ($9) per month

Realistic monthly total: $800-1,400 as a single person, depending on your accommodation standard and how often you eat out. Alcohol is expensive relative to other costs, and imported goods carry a premium. Higher-end restaurants approach Lisbon or Madrid prices.

Where to Work

Antalya has a small but functional coworking scene centered on a handful of dedicated spaces and a growing number of laptop-friendly cafes.

Coworking Spaces

  • Coworking Antalya sits under the ancient city walls in Kaleici with a terrace overlooking the marina. It offers 24/7 access, ergonomic chairs, and consistent wifi. It is the social hub of the local nomad community and runs weekly events. Some users find it expensive for what you get physically, but the location and community value are real.
  • Creawork (two locations, including Sarampol Plaza) is more corporate in feel with high-speed internet, meeting rooms, and unlimited coffee. Good for focused, heads-down work.
  • Lara Coworking near Lara Beach offers sea-view terraces, 24/7 access, and meeting rooms in a quieter setting away from the tourist core.

Cafe Work Culture

Cafe culture is welcoming toward laptop workers. %100 Coffee in Kaleici is popular for its clifftop harbor views and reliable wifi. Big Chefs Konyaalti on the beach promenade has a wide terrace and solid connectivity. Times Coffee and Co-Working near Kaleici has a dedicated coworking section with its own pricing tier.

WiFi Reality

Fiber is available through providers like Turk Telekom and TurkNet, so apartment and coworking speeds are genuinely fast (100-1000 Mbps in good buildings). Cafe wifi varies but is usually adequate for video calls. SIM data is cheap and fast enough for backup use.

Getting Around

The historic center around Kaleici and Konyaalti Beach is walkable and compact. The wider city requires public transport or a car. Minibuses called dolmus run frequently and cheaply along fixed routes. Regular city buses cover most areas. Taxis are inexpensive by Western standards: a 3 km trip costs around $1-2. There is no metro.

The airport (AYT) is roughly 13 km east of the city center. Taxis take about 20-30 minutes and cost 300-500 TRY ($9-15). Airport buses (Havas) are cheaper at around 80-100 TRY. Antalya is a major international hub with direct flights to Europe, Russia, and the Middle East, so connectivity is excellent.

Between neighborhoods: Konyaalti (western beach strip) and Lara (eastern, newer area) both require transport from the old town. Many nomads settle in Muratpasa or Konyaalti specifically to stay on a single bus line.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most Western passport holders (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia) can enter Turkey visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Some nationalities require an e-visa obtained online before arrival (a simple form and small fee). Turkey introduced a formal Digital Nomad Visa in 2024, which allows stays of up to 12 months with renewal possible. Eligibility requires being aged 21-55, holding a university degree, earning at least $3,000 USD per month from clients or employers outside Turkey, and working remotely. You apply for a Digital Nomad Identification Certificate online, then attend a consulate appointment. Alternatively, if you are already in Turkey on a tourist entry, you can apply for a short-term residence permit through the local migration office.

The 90-day tourist allowance is common for shorter stays. Confirm entry requirements and current visa conditions via the official Republic of Turkey e-Visa portal and your nearest Turkish consulate before travel.

Best Time to Visit

Antalya gets around 300 sunny days per year, so it is livable for most of the calendar. That said, some periods are significantly better for remote work.

  • April to mid-June: The best window. Temperatures are 20-28C (68-82F), the sea is warming up, crowds are manageable, and accommodation is priced below peak. Cafes and outdoor terraces are pleasant for working.
  • September to October: Nearly as good. Heat drops to comfortable levels, the sea stays warm through October, and the summer crowds thin out. This is arguably the best shoulder season for nomads who want beach access without the peak-season chaos.
  • July to August: Very hot, often 35-40C (95-104F). The city is packed with tourists. Accommodation prices spike, cafes fill up, and working outdoors is difficult during midday. Air conditioning becomes essential.
  • November to March: Mild but rainy. Daytime temperatures of 15-20C (59-68F) are comfortable indoors, and the city is quiet. Good for budget stays and focused work but limited beach time. January is the wettest month.

For pure remote work quality, April-June is the sweet spot.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Low cost of living relative to Mediterranean peers in Spain, Italy, or Greece
  • Dramatic natural setting: limestone Taurus mountains behind, turquoise Mediterranean in front
  • Active nomad community centered on Coworking Antalya with regular meetups
  • 300+ sunny days per year and a long swimming season
  • Fast fiber internet in apartments and coworking spaces
  • Easy international flight connections from Antalya Airport
  • Rich history and culture in Kaleici (old town with Roman walls, temples, harbor)
  • Generally safe with low violent crime rates

Cons

  • English is limited outside tourist zones; everyday errands can require a translation app
  • Peak summer (July-August) is oppressively hot and crowded
  • Alcohol is expensive and subject to high taxes
  • Long-term apartment hunting is difficult, especially in high season when landlords favor short lets
  • Turkish lira has experienced significant inflation; savings in TRY erode quickly
  • Coworking supply is thin relative to the nomad demand; the main space has mixed reviews on value
  • City sprawl outside the old town is car-dependent and generic

Who Antalya Is Best For

Antalya suits remote workers who want a sunny, affordable Mediterranean base with genuine history and outdoor access, and who prioritize cost savings over a polished nomad infrastructure. If you want to swim in clear water in the morning, work from a terrace with harbor views in the afternoon, and eat well for $5, Antalya delivers that reliably during the shoulder seasons.

It works especially well for people comfortable navigating day-to-day life without English, those who enjoy socializing with a diverse international crowd (the expat community includes many Eastern Europeans and Russians alongside Westerners), and anyone who wants to explore Turkey's coast or interior from a well-connected hub.

It is a harder fit if you need a large, well-resourced coworking scene, expect English-speaking service in shops and offices, or are sensitive to intense summer heat. Travelers looking primarily for a buzzing Western-style nomad hub with plentiful coworking options would be better served by Lisbon, Budapest, or Tbilisi.

Frequently Asked Questions About Antalya

How much does it cost to live in Antalya as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Antalya is around $1,800 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Antalya?

With a WiFi score of 6/10, connectivity in Antalya is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Antalya safe for digital nomads?

Antalya scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Antalya a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Antalya is worth a look if you value nature and easy visas. Costs sit near $1,800 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
