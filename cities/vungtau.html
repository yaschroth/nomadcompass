Setting Up as a Nomad in Vung Tau

Saigon's nearest beach town, a breezy headland of seafood shacks, a hilltop Jesus statue and weekday calm once the weekenders leave. For remote workers, Vung Tau leans on low costs and climate, with a typical budget near $800 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Best Neighborhoods in Vung Tau

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Bai Sau (Back Beach)

$$$
Beachfront & Modern

The long beach strip where most foreigners land

Thuy Van Street runs for several kilometres along an open sand beach, lined with condo towers like Vung Tau Melody and The Song, seafood restaurants and a wide seafront promenade. Most serviced apartments and monthly rentals sit here. It is the default base for a remote worker who wants a swim before the first call.

Best for: Nomads who want a sea view apartment, a gym downstairs and sand two minutes from the desk.
Pros
  • Best supply of monthly serviced apartments
  • Swimmable beach and a flat promenade for running
  • Plenty of cafes and seafood along Thuy Van
Cons
  • Weekend crowds arrive by the busload from Ho Chi Minh City
  • Strong rip currents and litter after storms
  • Feels stretched out, you need a scooter to get around

Bai Truoc (Front Beach)

$$
Central & Walkable

Old harbour downtown facing the sunset

The original town centre, where colonial villas, a shaded seafront park and the Xom Luoi fish market face the harbour rather than the open sea. Banks, the post office, pharmacies and the cheapest coffee in town are all within a few blocks. Walkable in a way the rest of Vung Tau is not.

Best for: Anyone who wants to live without a scooter and be inside a real working town.
Pros
  • Everything practical is within walking distance
  • Sunset views over the harbour from the park
  • Cheap local food and a genuine fish market
Cons
  • The water here is not for swimming
  • Traffic noise on Quang Trung and Ha Long streets
  • Older buildings, fewer modern rentals

Khu A Chau

$$
Quiet & Expat

The small established expat pocket

A grid of quiet lanes between Phan Chu Trinh and Hoang Hoa Tham, nicknamed the Asia Area, that grew up around the offshore oil industry. Western bakeries, bars, a couple of international-style eateries and long-term house rentals cluster here. Ten minutes on foot to Front Beach, fifteen by scooter to Back Beach.

Best for: Longer stayers who want a house, a familiar bar and neighbours who speak English.
Pros
  • Central without the main road noise
  • Whole houses rentable long term, often under $400 a month
  • Small but real foreign community
Cons
  • Very few short-term or furnished options
  • Ageing expat bar scene is not for everyone
  • No beach access on foot

Bai Dau

$$
Leafy & Quiet

Quiet coastal road under Big Mountain

A narrow shoreline strip on the north-west side of Big Mountain, known for the hillside Christ statue viewpoints, small guesthouses and a coast road with almost no traffic. Rocky coves replace sand. The calmest place to base in Vung Tau, with sunset water views from most upper-floor rooms.

Best for: Writers and deep-focus workers who value silence over nightlife.
Pros
  • Genuinely quiet, even on weekends
  • Great sea and sunset views for the money
  • Scenic coast road for cycling and walks
Cons
  • No swimming beach, just rocks
  • Thin on cafes, restaurants and groceries
  • You need a scooter for anything at all

Chi Linh (Ward 10)

$
Residential & Cheap

Newer inland grid where locals actually live

A planned residential area north of the beaches with wide straight streets, new townhouses, local markets and a supermarket. Rents run well below the seafront towers and daily costs are local prices, not tourist ones. Roughly ten minutes by scooter to Back Beach and fifteen to Front Beach.

Best for: Budget nomads on a long stay who are happy to ride to the beach.
Pros
  • Cheapest decent housing in the city
  • Local market prices, no tourist markup
  • New, quiet streets with easy parking
Cons
  • No walkable beach or seafront
  • Little English spoken and few cafes to work from
  • Bland new-build streets with limited shade

Cost of Living in Vung Tau

At $800 a month a single nomad lives well in Vung Tau, a seaside city that is cheaper than Ho Chi Minh City while offering better air and a coastline. Furnished studios and one-bedroom apartments in Ward 1 near Front Beach or around Back Beach rent for $230 to $400, with sea-view units at the upper end and long-term deals common. Food costs $170 to $250: banh khot at $2 to $3, seafood plates at $4 to $8, and Western or Russian-influenced restaurants at $8 to $14. A Viettel or Mobifone SIM with plentiful data is $4 to $8. Coworking is limited, with desks near $60 to $100 where available, so many work from cafes at the price of a $1.50 coffee. Electricity, water and fibre total $40 to $80, spiking with air conditioning. Grab rides cost $1 to $3. Verdict: excellent coastal value.

Where to Work in Vung Tau

Ward 1 around Front Beach, Bai Truoc, is the most convenient base, walkable to the promenade, markets and the ferry pier; Ward 2 and the Back Beach strip, Bai Sau, suit surfers, kitesurfers and those wanting a wider beach but a longer walk to the centre. Cafe culture carries the working day here: Cong Caphe, Highlands Coffee and a dense set of independent sea-view cafes along Ha Long and Thuy Van streets all offer air conditioning, outlets and reliable wifi, and several stay open late. Purpose-built coworking has never established itself, so an apartment with a good fibre line is the sensible core setup. Speeds of 60 Mbps to 250 Mbps are normal, with occasional international slowdowns from undersea cable faults. Caveats: weekend crowds from Ho Chi Minh City fill cafes on Saturdays and Sundays, and coastal wind and beachfront noise make outdoor tables impractical for calls.

Getting Around Vung Tau

Vung Tau is compact and largely flat along the coast, so the centre is walkable and a bicycle covers most daily needs. Grab and the electric Xanh SM service dominate for anything longer: motorbike rides cost $0.60 to $1.50 and cars $2 to $5. A monthly scooter rental of $45 to $70 is worth it for the Nghinh Phong cape loop, Ho May cable car area and the beaches south of the city, though it is not strictly essential. The city has no airport; arrival is from Ho Chi Minh City, roughly 100km away. Limousine vans and buses from Saigon cost $6 to $10 and take two to three hours depending on the Long Thanh expressway traffic. The Greenlines DP hydrofoil from Bach Dang pier in District 1 is the more pleasant option at about $12 to $15 for a two-hour crossing, though sailings are weather-dependent.

Visas for Vung Tau

Vietnam's rules apply identically in Vung Tau. The universal e-visa, available to all nationalities since August 2023, grants up to 90 days single or multiple entry, costs roughly $25 or $50 respectively, and is applied for online with approval typically in three to five working days. Citizens of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, Spain and several other European and Asian countries also qualify for a 45-day visa exemption, with the eligible list revised periodically. Vietnam has no digital nomad visa, and remote work for a foreign employer while on a tourist entry is a widely practised grey area rather than an explicitly permitted category. In-country extensions exist through agencies but are unreliable and expensive; the standard long-stay pattern is a border run to Cambodia, Thailand or Singapore followed by a fresh e-visa. Local employment requires a work permit and temporary residence card obtained by a Vietnamese sponsor.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Vung Tau

Vung Tau follows southern Vietnam's two-season pattern and sits largely outside the main typhoon corridor, which is a meaningful advantage over central and northern coastal cities. The dry season runs from November to April: sunny, breezy, with highs of 29C to 33C, clean beaches and the best window overall. December to March is peak kitesurfing season at Back Beach thanks to consistent northeast winds. The wet season spans May to October, characterised by intense but usually short afternoon downpours rather than all-day rain, with humidity high and skies dramatic; September and October are the wettest and the sea is murkiest. Temperatures stay between 25C and 34C year round, so there is no cold season at all. Tet in January or February shuts many businesses and fills the city with domestic visitors, as do the Whale Worship festival and every Vietnamese public holiday weekend.

Pros and Cons of Vung Tau

Vung Tau's strengths are its balance: sea air and a long promenade, cleaner and cooler than Ho Chi Minh City, genuinely cheap rents with sea views, superb seafood and the local banh khot, a walkable and flat centre, decent fibre, a small but established expat presence rooted in the offshore oil industry, and a two-hour hydrofoil to Saigon for anything the city lacks. Kitesurfing from November to March is a real draw. The drawbacks are equally clear: weekends bring heavy domestic crowds and inflated prices, the beaches are pleasant rather than world class and can be littered after busy periods, there is no airport or formal coworking scene, the nomad community is small and transient, English is limited outside tourism, the wet season is humid and grey, and anyone wanting culture, nightlife or a large international network will be commuting to Ho Chi Minh City regularly.

Who Vung Tau Is For

Vung Tau fits the nomad who wants Vietnamese prices with a coastline and cleaner air, and who is happy to be near Ho Chi Minh City rather than in it. It works particularly well for kitesurfers and swimmers, for long-stay budget freelancers who need only a fast apartment connection, for couples and quiet-living remote workers, and for people cycling in and out of Saigon for meetings, flights and social life. It is also a sensible soft landing for a first long stay in Vietnam given how manageable the city is. Skip it if the priority is a dense nomad scene with coworking, meetups and constant new arrivals, if a top-tier beach is expected, or if an international airport on the doorstep is required. Those wanting more infrastructure and a bigger community should compare Da Nang; those wanting pure city energy should base in Saigon.

Frequently Asked Questions About Vung Tau

How much does it cost to live in Vung Tau as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Vung Tau is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Vung Tau?

Vung Tau rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is good for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Vung Tau safe for digital nomads?

Vung Tau scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Vung Tau a good city for digital nomads overall?

Vung Tau stands out for affordability and great weather among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
