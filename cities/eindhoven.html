Setting Up as a Nomad in Eindhoven

Experience design capital, Philips heritage, tech startups, and Dutch innovation hub. A oceanic climate and strong fast WiFi make Eindhoven an easy base, and at about $2,700 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep nature access in mind.

Cost of Living in Eindhoven

Eindhoven is noticeably cheaper than Amsterdam or Utrecht, but it is still a Western European city with Western European prices. Expect to spend between EUR 2,000 and EUR 3,200 per month (roughly USD 2,200 to USD 3,500) as a single remote worker, depending heavily on your housing choice.

  • Rent, central (city center, 1-bedroom): EUR 1,400 to EUR 1,700/month (USD 1,540 to USD 1,870)
  • Rent, cheaper area (outside center or suburbs): EUR 900 to EUR 1,150/month (USD 990 to USD 1,265)
  • Groceries (supermarket, Lidl/Albert Heijn): EUR 250 to EUR 350/month
  • Eating out (casual lunch or dinner): EUR 12 to EUR 20 per meal; a sit-down dinner with drinks EUR 30 to EUR 50
  • Coworking hot desk: EUR 175 to EUR 280/month (day passes typically EUR 15 to EUR 25)
  • Local transport (bus, OV-chipkaart): EUR 50 to EUR 80/month if you use it regularly; cycling is free if you bring or buy a bike
  • Total realistic monthly range: EUR 2,000 to EUR 3,200/month (USD 2,200 to USD 3,500)

Housing is the single biggest variable. You can shave EUR 400 to EUR 600 off your budget by living 15 to 20 minutes from the center by bike, which is entirely practical in this city.

Where to Work

Eindhoven has a genuine coworking culture, driven by its tech and design industries. You will not struggle to find a desk.

Coworking Spaces

Microlab (Strijp-S) is the most community-oriented option, with flexible nomad memberships from EUR 175/month, a rooftop bar, and regular events. Bouncespace (city center, near Central Station) sits under a spectacular glass atrium and is excellent for spontaneous day passes. Strijp-CS (Strijp-S, Torenallee) is small, industrial, and highly rated by regulars who want a quiet, focused atmosphere. Spaces Fellenoord offers 24/7 access and a more corporate setup if that suits your work style.

Cafe Work Culture

Laptop culture in cafes is accepted but not unlimited. Lucifer Coffee Roasters on Kleine Berg is the best-known spot for working over coffee, with strong WiFi and power sockets, though some locations occasionally ask laptop users to move on during busy afternoon hours. ZwartWit Koffie and Bakkie 040 are other well-regarded options with tables and outlets. If you plan to work in a cafe for several hours, order regularly and read the room.

WiFi Situation

Eindhoven's fixed-line and mobile internet infrastructure is excellent. Coworking spaces reliably offer fast symmetric connections. Cafe WiFi is generally solid at the spots listed above. The average city internet speed is well above 300 Mbps on fixed lines.

Getting Around

The bike is the dominant mode of transport and the best choice for daily life. The city is flat, compact, and has dedicated cycling lanes throughout. You can cover most of the inner city in under 15 minutes by bike.

For longer distances within the city and region, the bus network (operated by Connexxion and Hermes, using the OV-chipkaart) is reliable and covers all major areas. A tap-on, tap-off journey within the city costs roughly EUR 1.20 to EUR 2.80 depending on distance.

Eindhoven Central Station connects you to Amsterdam (1h 20m), Utrecht (55m), and Brussels (2h 30m) by intercity train. The Netherlands rail network (NS) is punctual by European standards, though it does have disruption periods.

For the airport: Eindhoven Airport (EIN) is 9 km from the city center. Bus lines 400 and 401 run from the Central Station bus terminal directly to the terminal in about 25 minutes. A single ticket costs around EUR 4.80, or EUR 2.74 with an OV-chipkaart. There is no direct train to the airport. Ryanair, Wizz Air, and Transavia use EIN heavily, so budget flights across Europe are easy to find.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

The Netherlands is part of the Schengen Area. Citizens of the US, Canada, Australia, the UK, and most other Western countries can enter visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period across the entire Schengen zone. This applies whether you are in Eindhoven for a week or three months.

The Netherlands does not have a dedicated digital nomad visa. For stays beyond 90 days, your main option is the self-employed residence permit (MVV), which requires a business plan, proof of income, and scoring on a government points system. This is not a quick or easy process and involves working with the Dutch immigration service (IND).

There is no straightforward short-term extension path for tourists. If you need more than 90 Schengen days, you either apply for the MVV well in advance or plan a Schengen exit before your 90 days are up. Always verify current requirements with the IND website (ind.nl) or the Dutch consulate in your home country before traveling.

Best Time to Visit

The best window for remote work in Eindhoven is May through September. Temperatures run between 18 and 26 degrees Celsius (65 to 79 degrees Fahrenheit), days are long (sunset past 9 pm in June), and the city comes alive with outdoor events including Dutch Design Week in October, which also makes late September into early October worth considering.

Winters (November to February) are grey, damp, and frequently overcast. Temperatures stay above freezing most of the time, but the combination of flat light, persistent drizzle, and short days makes this the hardest period psychologically. If you are sensitive to SAD or need sunlight to stay productive, winter in Eindhoven will test you.

Spring (March to April) is unpredictable, with cold snaps and rain, but improving by late April. Autumn (September to October) is crisp and manageable, with fewer tourists than summer.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Strong tech and design ecosystem; easy to connect with startups, creatives, and engineers
  • English spoken fluently and universally, including at government offices and shops
  • Excellent and affordable flight connections through Eindhoven Airport
  • Cheaper than Amsterdam while keeping the same quality of infrastructure
  • Highly bikeable, compact, and genuinely safe city
  • Good coworking supply with real community, not just hot desks
  • Air quality is excellent for a mid-size European city

Cons

  • Winter weather is persistently grey and wet for four to five months
  • Housing market is tight; finding a good long-term rental takes time and competition
  • Dutch people are friendly but reserved; social integration takes deliberate effort
  • Nightlife and food scene, while improving, is thinner than Amsterdam or Berlin
  • No dedicated digital nomad visa; 90-day Schengen cap applies
  • Cost of living has risen sharply since 2022, especially rent

Who Eindhoven Is Best For

Eindhoven suits remote workers in tech, product design, industrial design, or engineering who want proximity to a real innovation ecosystem. If you want to network with serious professionals, attend design and tech events, and live in a city that punches above its size, Eindhoven delivers.

It also works well for nomads who want a quieter, more affordable Dutch base than Amsterdam, with easy access to the rest of Europe via budget airlines and good train connections.

Skip Eindhoven if you need a warm or sunny winter climate, if you are chasing a buzzy nightlife scene, or if you are looking for the cultural density and variety of a capital city. It also rewards people who cycle; if you do not want to bike for daily errands, you will find the bus system workable but less convenient.

Frequently Asked Questions About Eindhoven

How much does it cost to live in Eindhoven as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Eindhoven is around $2,700 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Eindhoven?

With a WiFi score of 9/10, connectivity in Eindhoven is excellent for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Eindhoven safe for digital nomads?

Eindhoven scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Eindhoven a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Eindhoven is worth a look if you value fast WiFi and English. Costs sit near $2,700 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
