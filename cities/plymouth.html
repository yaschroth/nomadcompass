Setting Up as a Nomad in Plymouth

The Mayflower sailed from here, and Dartmoor starts twenty minutes north. A oceanic climate and strong English make Plymouth an easy base, and at about $1,450 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the size of the nomad scene in mind.

Best Neighborhoods in Plymouth

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

The Barbican and the Hoe

$$$
Historic & Waterfront

The Elizabethan quarter and the headland above it

The oldest part of Plymouth, which survived the 1941 bombing that removed most of the rest, with cobbled streets, the Mayflower Steps, a working fish market and the Gin Distillery that has operated since 1793. The Hoe rises above with Smeaton's Tower, moved here stone by stone from the Eddystone reef in 1877.

Best for: Anyone who wants the harbour, the Hoe and the pubs on foot
Pros
  • The only substantial pre-war fabric left in the city
  • Smeaton's Tower and the Hoe's view over the Sound
  • The best pubs and restaurants in Plymouth
Cons
  • The most expensive part of the city
  • Busy with visitors through the summer
  • Parking is very difficult

Royal William Yard and Stonehouse

$$$
Converted & Handsome

A Georgian naval victualling yard, converted

The nineteenth century naval supply depot on the western waterfront, a complex of enormous limestone and granite buildings by John Rennie that has been converted since 2000 into apartments, restaurants, galleries and a small cinema, with a ferry across to Cornwall from the slipway.

Best for: Anyone who wants the best modern housing in the city
Pros
  • Among the finest Georgian military architecture in Europe
  • Good restaurants and a cinema within the yard
  • The Cremyll ferry to Cornwall leaves from here
Cons
  • Expensive by Plymouth standards
  • A twenty minute walk from the city centre
  • Windy and exposed on the waterfront

Mutley and the university

$$
Student & Practical

The student quarter north of the centre

The area around Mutley Plain and the University of Plymouth, with student housing, a run of cheap eating and bars along the Plain, and the marine research institutions that give the city its academic character. It is busy in term and quieter out of it.

Best for: Students, researchers and anyone wanting cheap rent near the centre
Pros
  • Cheapest rents within walking distance of the centre
  • Cheap food and bars along Mutley Plain
  • The university and the marine research institutes
Cons
  • Student housing quality varies considerably
  • Empties outside term
  • Little green space

Yelverton and the Dartmoor edge

$$
Moorland & Village

The moor's southwestern gateway, fourteen kilometres north

A village on the southwestern edge of Dartmoor where the moorland begins directly at the last houses, with ponies grazing on the common and the road climbing onto open country beyond. Burrator reservoir and the tors above it are a few minutes further, and the moor runs uninterrupted north from there.

Best for: Anyone whose priority is Dartmoor rather than the city
Pros
  • Dartmoor begins at the edge of the village
  • Burrator reservoir and the tors within minutes
  • A genuine village with shops and a good pub
Cons
  • Fourteen kilometres from Plymouth and car dependent
  • Expensive for the area
  • Moorland weather arrives fast and is genuinely dangerous

Cost of Living in Plymouth

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Plymouth, so the $1,450 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 981 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $1,450 a month. A one-bedroom runs roughly $650 to $1,000, cheaper than Bristol or Exeter and considerably cheaper than the Cornish coast an hour west, where second homes have pushed prices well beyond local wages. Groceries are ordinary British prices. Eating out is moderate and the seafood, particularly the crab and the scallops landed at the Barbican, is very good. Utilities run through a mild damp winter. Council tax applies separately. The naval dockyard is the city's largest employer and shapes the housing market considerably. Anyone comparing with Cornwall should note that the second home effect across the Tamar has pushed coastal village prices well beyond Plymouth's, which is why many people commute in.

Where to Work in Plymouth

Connectivity is good. Fibre reaches most of Plymouth at reasonable speeds, mobile coverage is comprehensive and power is reliable. There is a coworking scene helped by the University of Plymouth and by a marine technology sector that is genuinely significant: the city hosts serious marine research and autonomous vessel work. The time zone is GMT with British summer time. Plymouth's position at the far southwestern end of the rail network means physical travel is slower than the connectivity suggests, which is worth weighing. Anyone in marine science should look at the Plymouth Marine Laboratory and the National Marine Aquarium, both of which anchor a research cluster that is genuinely internationally regarded.

Getting Around Plymouth

Plymouth is hilly and spread out, with the Hoe, the Barbican and the city centre walkable but the suburbs requiring buses, which cover the city adequately. The railway station has trains to London in about three and a half hours and to Penzance in two. Plymouth's airport closed in 2011, so Exeter or Bristol are the practical air connections. The ferry to Roscoff in Brittany and Santander in Spain leaves from Millbay, and the Cremyll and Torpoint ferries cross the Tamar into Cornwall. Anyone crossing into Cornwall should know that the Torpoint chain ferry runs around the clock and that the Tamar road bridge is the alternative if the ferry queue is long.

Visas for Plymouth

The United Kingdom requires most non-visa nationals to obtain an Electronic Travel Authorisation before arrival, and visa nationals need a standard visitor visa. Visitors may not work for a British employer, though remote work for an overseas employer is permitted within limits. There is no digital nomad visa. Longer stays require a sponsored work visa, the Global Talent route or the Graduate route. The naval dockyard and the marine sector are both areas where sponsored roles exist, though security clearance requirements apply to a good deal of it. Anyone considering dockyard or defence work should be aware that a great deal of it requires British nationality and security clearance, which narrows the route considerably.

Best Time to Visit Plymouth

May to September is the best window, with July around nineteen degrees and Devon and Cornwall at their best, though the school holidays in late July and August fill every road and beach in the southwest. April, May and September are the sweet spots. Winter from November to March is mild, wet and windy, with Atlantic storms arriving directly. Dartmoor is walkable year round and is genuinely dangerous in bad weather, which the moor's rescue teams are called out for regularly. Anyone walking on Dartmoor should carry a map and a compass rather than relying on a phone, since the moor has almost no landmarks and the weather closes in fast.

Pros and Cons of Plymouth

The case for Plymouth is position: Dartmoor twenty minutes north, Cornwall across the Tamar, the Devon coast east, and a natural harbour that has been Britain's western naval base since the sixteenth century. The Barbican survived the wartime bombing and the Hoe with Smeaton's Tower is a genuinely good piece of public space. The case against is that the city centre was flattened in 1941 and rebuilt as a postwar grid that few people love, and the naval dependence has left the economy narrower than it should be. Plymouth also has the largest naval base in western Europe at Devonport, which shapes the city's economy, its politics and a good deal of its social life.

Who Plymouth Is For

Plymouth suits sailors, divers and walkers above all, since the Sound, Dartmoor and the Cornish coast are all immediately available. It suits people in marine science or engineering, since that sector is real here. It suits anyone wanting a southwestern base at less than Cornish prices. It does not suit anyone who needs to travel frequently by air, since the nearest useful airport is over an hour away, and it does not suit anyone expecting a handsome city centre. It also suits anyone drawn to the Mayflower story, since the ship left from the Barbican in 1620 and the steps are still there with the surrounding streets largely intact.

How much does it cost to live in Plymouth as a digital nomad?

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Plymouth?

Plymouth rates 8 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is excellent for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Plymouth safe for digital nomads?

Plymouth scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Plymouth a good city for digital nomads overall?
