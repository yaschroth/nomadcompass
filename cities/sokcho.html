Setting Up as a Nomad in Sokcho

Seoraksan's granite peaks on one side, the East Sea and a raw fish market on the other. Expect $1,500 a month here. Fast WiFi and nature are the standout strengths, with the size of the nomad scene the main caveat.

Best Neighborhoods in Sokcho

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Jungang Market and the Downtown

$$
Central & Culinary

The town centre around the market, where the food is

Sokcho's compact downtown built around Jungang market, which is the town's real institution: dakgangjeong fried chicken, squid sundae and the fish stalls. Buses to Seoraksan and the express terminal are close, and everything a visitor needs sits within a few blocks.

Best for: Anyone who wants the market, the buses and the restaurants on foot
Pros
  • Jungang market and the town's best food
  • Buses to Seoraksan and the intercity terminal
  • The widest accommodation choice in town
Cons
  • The highest rents in Sokcho
  • Busy with visitors in the autumn foliage season
  • Little green space

Abai Village and the Port

$$
Historic & Waterside

The refugee settlement across the channel, reached by hand-pulled ferry

A spit of land across the harbour channel settled by refugees from north of the current border who expected to go home and never did, still reached by a small ferry that passengers pull across on a cable. Low houses, fish restaurants and a distinct history.

Best for: Stays that want the town's most distinctive quarter and the harbour
Pros
  • The hand-pulled ferry and a genuinely unusual history
  • Fish restaurants and the working harbour
  • Five minutes across the water to the centre
Cons
  • Very limited accommodation
  • Few shops or services
  • Exposed and cold in winter

Sokcho Beach and Daepo

$$
Coastal & Fishy

The sand north and the fish market south

The coastal strip either side of town: a long sand beach immediately north of the harbour, and Daepo port to the south with its raw fish market where you choose the fish and eat it upstairs. Accommodation faces the sea at rents below the downtown.

Best for: Stays that want the sea in view and the fish market a short ride away
Pros
  • Long sand beach and East Sea views
  • Daepo raw fish market to the south
  • Rents below the town centre
Cons
  • Ten to fifteen minutes from the market and buses
  • Cold and very windy in winter
  • Crowded in the July and August beach season

Seorak-dong

$$$
Park Gate & Alpine

At the national park gate, under the granite peaks

The settlement at the main entrance to Seoraksan, with hotels, restaurants and the cable car station, sitting directly beneath the granite walls. It is the base for anyone whose reason to be in Sokcho is the mountain, and it fills completely in the foliage weeks.

Best for: Stays where the mountain is the whole point
Pros
  • The park entrance and the cable car at the door
  • Trails to Ulsanbawi and Daecheongbong from here
  • Sinheungsa temple within the grounds
Cons
  • Twenty to twenty-five minutes into town by bus
  • Extremely crowded in the autumn foliage season
  • Resort pricing and little else to do

Joyang and the Residential South

$
Residential & Affordable

The apartment districts south of the lagoon

The residential areas around Cheongchoho lagoon and south towards the expressway, with Korean apartment complexes, supermarkets, schools and a settled local population. Rents are the lowest in Sokcho and the lagoon path is a good running route.

Best for: Longer stays where the rent matters more than the view
Pros
  • The lowest rents in the town
  • Lagoon path for running and cycling
  • Full everyday shopping
Cons
  • Fifteen minutes from the market and the beach
  • Uniform apartment-complex architecture
  • Nothing to walk to in the evening

Cost of Living in Sokcho

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Sokcho, so the $1,500 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 831 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget roughly $1,500 a month as a solo remote worker in Sokcho, which is well below Seoul while still being South Korea. A one-bedroom apartment or officetel near the centre or the beach runs about $450 to $700, and Korean leases traditionally involve a large deposit, the jeonse or wolse system, so short-term and foreigner-friendly options often come at a premium through agencies or serviced buildings. Groceries land around $350 to $450; Korean supermarket prices for fruit and imported goods are high. A bowl of noodles or a gimbap lunch is $6 to $9, a Korean barbecue dinner for two $30 to $45, and the fish market by the port is excellent and cheap. Utilities add $80 to $160.

Where to Work in Sokcho

Sokcho is a city of about 80,000 and has no meaningful coworking scene; that sits in Seoul and Busan. Remote workers here work from their accommodation or from cafes, of which Korea has an extraordinary density even in small cities, and Korean cafe culture positively expects long laptop sessions. Connectivity is the reason Sokcho works at all as a base: South Korea has among the fastest and most reliable internet anywhere, fixed-line and mobile alike, and that holds outside the capital. Public wifi is ubiquitous. English is limited in daily life, and while translation apps handle most transactions, anything administrative is considerably easier with a Korean-speaking friend.

Getting Around Sokcho

Sokcho is compact and spread between the beach, the port and the Cheongchoho lagoon, and much of it is walkable or cyclable on the flat. City buses cover the rest cheaply with a T-money card, and the same card works nationwide. Bus 7 and 7-1 run to Seoraksan national park, which is the main reason most people are here, in about twenty minutes. Express buses connect Sokcho to Seoul in around two and a half hours, and this is the standard route since the city has no railway station, though a KTX line to the east coast has been under development. Yangyang international airport is nearby with limited service; Incheon is the practical international gateway.

Visas for Sokcho

South Korea grants most Western passports 90 days visa-free, and requires many of them to obtain a K-ETA electronic travel authorisation before departure, though this requirement has been temporarily waived for a rotating list of countries, so check the current position. In 2024 Korea launched the F-1-D workation visa, a digital nomad visa for remote workers employed by foreign companies with more than a year of experience in their field and an income above roughly twice the Korean gross national income per capita. It grants one year with a possible one-year extension and allows accompanying family. It was a significant addition, as Korea previously had no route at all for remote workers.

Best Time to Visit Sokcho

Autumn, late September through November, is the outstanding season in Sokcho: clear skies, cool air and the foliage on Seoraksan, which is one of the most celebrated autumn sights in Korea and fills the park and the town at weekends. Spring, April and May, is the second window, mild and with cherry blossom. Summer is hot and humid, into the low thirties, and July brings the monsoon rains, but this is also when the east coast beaches are the point and Sokcho fills with Korean holidaymakers. Winter is cold, often below freezing, and this stretch of coast gets heavy snow, which makes Seoraksan spectacular and the roads occasionally difficult. Book far ahead for autumn weekends.

Pros and Cons of Sokcho

Sokcho gives you Korean infrastructure, which is to say some of the best internet, transport and healthcare in the world, in a small coastal city with a national park of granite peaks directly behind it and the East Sea in front. Costs are well below Seoul, the seafood is superb, and the country is exceptionally safe. Against that, English is genuinely limited and Korea is administratively demanding for foreigners: banking, phone contracts and rentals all involve hurdles. There is no foreign community in Sokcho and no nomad scene. The city is quiet outside the tourist seasons and busy within them. Winters are cold and snowy, and Seoul is a two and a half hour bus ride rather than a commute.

Who Sokcho Is For

Sokcho suits remote workers who want Korea without Seoul's cost and intensity, who hike or climb, and who are willing to operate in a mostly Korean-language environment. It is a strong choice for anyone on the new workation visa looking for somewhere quieter, for people drawn to Seoraksan, and for those who value infrastructure and safety very highly. It works for solo workers who want focus and for couples. It is a poor fit for anyone who needs an English-speaking environment, a foreign community, nightlife or a professional network. Seoul and Busan have all of that, and Busan in particular offers a coastal city with far more happening.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sokcho

How much does it cost to live in Sokcho as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Sokcho is around

,500 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Sokcho?

Sokcho rates 10 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is excellent for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Sokcho safe for digital nomads?

Sokcho scores 9 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Sokcho a good city for digital nomads overall?

Sokcho stands out for fast internet and nature and the outdoors among remote workers. With a typical budget near ,500 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
