Setting Up as a Nomad in Jeju Island

A volcanic island paradise with unique culture and Korean hospitality. Jeju Island pairs nature with safety and a humid subtropical climate, all for about $2,200 a month. If anything, the language barrier lags.

Cost of Living in Jeju Island

Jeju is noticeably cheaper than Seoul, though it is not rock-bottom cheap. Expect to spend roughly 1,500,000-2,500,000 KRW ($1,100-$1,850 USD) per month as a solo remote worker, depending on your housing choice and lifestyle.

  • Rent, central Jeju City (1-bedroom): 700,000-1,000,000 KRW/month ($520-$745)
  • Rent, quieter areas or villages: 500,000-750,000 KRW/month ($370-$560)
  • Groceries: 350,000-500,000 KRW/month ($260-$370); local markets and supermarkets are well stocked
  • Eating out: A simple local meal costs 8,000-13,000 KRW ($6-$10); a mid-range dinner for two runs 50,000-80,000 KRW ($37-$60)
  • Coworking hot-desk: Free at J-Space (government-supported); paid spaces like O-Peace run roughly 10,000-15,000 KRW/day ($7-$11)
  • Local transport: City buses cost 1,200-1,500 KRW ($1-$1.20) per ride; car rental runs 40,000-60,000 KRW/day ($30-$45)
  • Total monthly range: 1,500,000-2,500,000 KRW ($1,100-$1,850) excluding a rental car

Jeju's signature foods, particularly black pork and fresh abalone, are a genuine treat but add up quickly if you eat them often. Cooking at home or eating at local kimbap shops keeps costs comfortable.

Where to Work

Jeju has a small but real infrastructure for remote workers. South Korea's internet is among the fastest in the world, with nationwide average speeds above 100 Mbps and near-universal 5G coverage outdoors and in cafes.

Coworking Spaces

J-Space is a free coworking space and startup incubator in Jeju City, backed by the local government. It is fully equipped with high-speed WiFi and basic office facilities, though it is oriented toward Korean startups and can be quieter for international nomads. O-Peace Jeju, located in Jocheon-eup about 20 minutes from the airport, is a relaxed three-story building with ocean views, fast WiFi, specialty coffee, and an informal layout. Re:flow Jeju is a coliving property with 24-hour coworking access, better suited to longer stays.

Cafe Work Culture

Working from cafes is very common in South Korea, and Jeju is no exception. Most cafes tolerate laptop workers, particularly in the mornings on weekdays. WiFi is almost always available and fast. The catch is that cafe culture here leans toward aesthetics and Instagram appeal: seats can be limited, power outlets are not always accessible, and some popular spots have a high turnover expectation on weekends.

For a serious work session, dedicated coworking is more reliable. For a few hours of focused work, a cafe in Jeju City works fine. Outside of the city, especially in rural or coastal areas, options thin out quickly.

Getting Around

Jeju's attractions and neighborhoods are spread across a large island, and this is the main practical challenge for living here as a nomad.

Renting a Car

A rental car is the most practical option for exploring the island freely. Major rental companies run shuttle buses from Jeju International Airport (CJU). Costs run 40,000-60,000 KRW/day ($30-$45), and you need an international driving permit if you hold a non-Korean license.

Public Buses

Jeju has a reasonably comprehensive public bus network covering the city and major coastal routes. Buses cost 1,200-1,500 KRW per ride and stop announcements are in English. All buses have free WiFi. The downside is frequency: rural routes run every 30-60 minutes, and many smaller beaches or hiking trailheads require a long wait or a walk. Getting around without a car is workable but slow.

Taxis and the Airport

Jeju International Airport is located right at the edge of Jeju City, roughly 10 minutes and 5,000-8,000 KRW ($4-$6) by taxi from downtown. Airport limousine bus #600 connects to Seogwipo and southern areas every 20 minutes for 4,000-5,500 KRW. Daily flights connect Jeju to Seoul (Gimpo or Gimhae) in about an hour. There are also direct international flights to select Asian cities, though the selection is limited.

Walkability

Jeju City itself is walkable for daily errands and coffee shops. The broader island is not walkable as a primary transport mode; most things worth seeing are 20-60 minutes apart by car.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of most countries worldwide can enter Jeju visa-free for up to 30 days when arriving directly from abroad at Jeju International Airport (CJU). This is Jeju's special visa-free zone and is separate from South Korea's standard visa exemptions. If you plan to travel to the mainland during your stay, you must meet South Korea's regular entry requirements, which grant most Western passport holders 90 days visa-free on the mainland.

South Korea launched a dedicated Digital Nomad Visa (F-1-D), also known as the Workation Visa. It allows eligible remote workers to live legally in South Korea for one year, renewable once. Requirements as of 2025 include: age 18 or older, at least one year of experience in the same industry, and a minimum annual income of roughly 88,000,000 KRW (approximately $66,000 USD). You must work remotely for a non-Korean employer or client. Private health insurance covering at least 100,000,000 KRW in treatment is also required.

For most short-stay nomads on common passports, the 30-day Jeju visa-free entry or the 90-day mainland exemption is the practical route. Extensions beyond 90 days typically require departing and re-entering, or obtaining the F-1-D visa. Always confirm current entry conditions with the South Korean embassy or official immigration portal before travel.

Best Time to Visit

Jeju has a subtropical climate with warm, humid summers and mild winters. The island experiences four distinct seasons.

Spring (March to May) is the most popular window and for good reason. Temperatures sit between 10-20 degrees Celsius, rainfall is moderate, and the island is covered in cherry blossoms and canola flowers in March and April. Crowds build through May but remain manageable compared to summer.

Summer (June to August) is hot, humid, and rainy. Average August temperatures hit 27-30 degrees Celsius with high humidity. Typhoons are possible from July through September. Korean domestic tourism peaks in July and August, pushing accommodation prices up and beaches to capacity. For remote work, the heat and crowds are a drawback.

Autumn (September to November) is the second-best window. Temperatures cool to 15-25 degrees Celsius, rainfall drops, and the island's hillsides turn with autumn foliage. October and early November are particularly good for a balance of good weather, scenery, and fewer crowds than summer.

Winter (December to February) is mild by Korean standards (around 5-10 degrees Celsius) but can be windy and occasionally snowy on Hallasan. Accommodation is cheaper and the island is quiet. It is a reasonable time for a focused work stint if you are comfortable with grey skies and limited outdoor activity.

For remote work specifically, April to May and October to November offer the best combination of pleasant weather, lower prices, and a less chaotic environment.

Pros and Cons

  • Extremely fast and reliable internet virtually everywhere on the island
  • Safe with very low crime rates and a relaxed, clean environment
  • Stunning natural scenery including Hallasan, coastal cliffs, lava tubes, and beaches within easy reach of a work base
  • Affordable compared to Seoul or most Western cities, with good food quality for the price
  • Visa-free entry for most nationalities without any paperwork
  • Free coworking space (J-Space) reduces overhead for short stays
  • Domestic flights to Seoul take about one hour, keeping you connected to a major hub
  • Language barrier is real: English is limited outside tourist areas and younger demographics
  • Small English-speaking nomad community; networking opportunities are thin compared to Southeast Asian hubs
  • A car is near-essential for quality of life beyond Jeju City, which adds cost and complexity
  • Hot, humid summers with typhoon risk and peak tourist crowds
  • Limited nightlife compared to Seoul or regional hubs in Southeast Asia
  • The 30-day Jeju-specific visa-free period is short for those who want to explore beyond the island

Who Jeju Island Is Best For

Jeju suits you well if you want nature and clean air alongside reliable infrastructure, and you are comfortable with limited English-speaking social scenes. It is an excellent fit for nature lovers, hikers, and anyone who finds Seoul too loud and expensive but still wants Korean food culture, fast internet, and personal safety as baseline conditions. Couples and solo workers who are self-sufficient and happy to rent a car will get the most out of the island.

Jeju also works well for shorter workation stints of two to four weeks, taking advantage of the visa-free period to decompress in a genuinely beautiful setting without the logistical weight of a long-term base.

Skip Jeju if you rely on a large English-speaking community or co-working social scene to stay motivated. It is also a poor fit if you want to be car-free: outside Jeju City, the bus network is slow and infrequent. Those on a tight budget who need sub-$1,000 monthly costs will also find Jeju harder to stretch than comparable spots in Southeast Asia.

Frequently Asked Questions About Jeju Island

How much does it cost to live in Jeju Island as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Jeju Island is around $2,200 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Jeju Island?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Jeju Island is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Jeju Island safe for digital nomads?

Jeju Island scores 10 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Jeju Island a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Jeju Island is worth a look if you value nature and safety. Costs sit near $2,200 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
