Cost of Living in Pune

Pune is one of India's more affordable cities for remote workers, though costs have risen steadily as the tech sector has grown. Here is a realistic monthly breakdown for a single person.

  • Rent, central areas (Koregaon Park, Kalyani Nagar, Viman Nagar): INR 25,000-40,000 (~$300-480)
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Baner, Aundh, Kothrud): INR 14,000-22,000 (~$170-265)
  • Groceries: INR 5,000-8,000 (~$60-95)
  • Eating out (local thalis and street food): INR 80-200 per meal (~$1-2.50); sit-down restaurants in Koregaon Park run INR 500-1,200 for two
  • Coworking hot-desk: INR 5,000-8,000 per month (~$60-95)
  • Local transport (auto-rickshaw and Uber/Ola): INR 2,000-4,000 (~$24-48)
  • Phone SIM with 50 GB 4G data (Jio or Airtel): INR 400-500 (~$5)
  • Utilities and home internet: INR 2,500-5,000 (~$30-60)

Realistic total range: INR 50,000-85,000 per month (~$600-1,020). Living in a central neighborhood and eating well lands most nomads around $750. Going budget in an outer area and eating mostly local food, you can get by on $550.

Where to Work

Pune has a well-developed coworking scene with well over 400 active spaces, concentrated in Viman Nagar, Baner, Kalyani Nagar, Kharadi, and Hinjewadi. Hot-desk passes start at around INR 399 per day or INR 5,000 per month.

  • Innov8 (Baner, Kharadi, Viman Nagar): stylish spaces popular with startups; fast and reliable fiber Wi-Fi, ergonomic seating, meeting rooms
  • Awfis (multiple locations including Viman Nagar and Baner): practical and consistent; good for day passes if you need flexibility
  • The Hive Workspaces (Sangamwadi near Koregaon Park): rated 4.4 on Justdial; premium feel with a large floor plan
  • COWRKS (Baner): premium-tier pricing but reliable infrastructure

Cafe work culture is real and widely accepted in Pune's upmarket neighborhoods. The Flour Works in Baner is genuinely laptop-friendly with good coffee and fast enough Wi-Fi. FC Road Social is louder but reliable for a few focused hours. Starbucks on North Main Road has strong Wi-Fi and plenty of outlets, which makes it popular with students and freelancers. Avoid working in Irani cafes on FC Road during peak hours as tables turn quickly.

Home fiber internet through ACT or Airtel Xstream runs at 100-300 Mbps for INR 700-1,200 per month and is the most reliable option for video calls. Coworking spaces typically offer 40-100 Mbps. Mobile 4G with Jio or Airtel is a solid backup.

Getting Around

Pune is not a walkable city outside of a few micro-pockets. Pavements are inconsistent, traffic is heavy, and most errands require a vehicle. That said, getting around is cheap and relatively easy once you figure out the apps.

Auto-rickshaws are the standard short-hop option. Most drivers now use metered fares, but you should confirm before getting in or use Uber Auto or Ola, which give you a fixed price upfront. Rides across the city typically cost INR 80-200 ($1-2.50). Ola and Uber cabs are available and useful for longer trips, airport runs, or late nights.

The Pune Metro is operational on the Purple Line and the Aqua Line, but coverage is still limited and most nomad-friendly neighborhoods are not directly connected. It is useful for some specific routes but you cannot rely on it as a primary transport mode yet.

Pune Airport (PNQ) is about 5-10 km from Koregaon Park and Viman Nagar. An Uber or Ola cab costs INR 300-500 (~$4-6) to most central areas. PMPML operates an airport bus service to Yerwada Metro Station. Allow 30-45 minutes for the trip, more during peak traffic hours (8-10 am and 6-9 pm).

Renting a scooter is an option many longer-stay nomads use. Costs run INR 5,000-8,000 per month through local rental shops. You will need an international driving permit or an Indian licence.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most Western passport holders (USA, UK, EU, Australia, Canada, and many others) qualify for India's e-Tourist Visa (eTV), applied for online before arrival at indianvisaonline.gov.in. The most common option is the 1-year multiple-entry eTV, which allows stays of up to 90 days per entry and a total of 180 days per calendar year. A 30-day single-entry option is also available, as is a 5-year eTV that permits stays up to 180 days per visit.

India has no dedicated digital nomad visa as of mid-2026. The eTV is intended for tourism, recreation, and short-term activities. Most nomads treat it as a tourist visa and exit and re-enter via a trip to Sri Lanka, Thailand, or the UAE to reset their stay. Extensions inside India are not straightforward and require applying at a Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO), which is bureaucratically complex.

Always verify current entry requirements with the official Indian Visa Online portal and your country's embassy before booking.

Best Time to Visit

Pune sits at around 560 metres elevation on the leeward side of the Western Ghats, which gives it a milder climate than Mumbai or the coast. It has three distinct seasons that affect how enjoyable it is to work and live there.

Winter (November to February) is the clear best window for remote workers. Temperatures stay between 10-28°C (50-82°F), skies are mostly clear, and the city is at its most pleasant for exploring on evenings and weekends. January nights can feel genuinely cool.

Summer (March to June) gets progressively uncomfortable. By April and May, temperatures can reach 38-42°C (100-108°F). Air conditioning costs spike and outdoor time is limited to early mornings. March is still manageable but book an apartment with a good AC unit.

Monsoon (June to October) brings heavy rainfall from the Western Ghats. The city turns green and temperatures drop to 22-28°C, which is pleasant. However, flooding on roads, power outages, and waterlogged streets are real issues in July and August. If your work depends on rock-solid internet and power, the monsoon months carry more risk. October marks the end of the rains and the city becomes lively again.

For a first visit aimed at productive remote work, target November through February. If you like green landscapes and lower accommodation prices, late September and October are a reasonable compromise.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Very low cost of living relative to the quality of food, accommodation, and coworking infrastructure available
  • Large English-speaking population; you will have no difficulty getting by without Hindi
  • Thriving tech and startup ecosystem means good community events and professional networking opportunities
  • Excellent food scene ranging from cheap Maharashtrian thalis to well-executed Italian and craft beer
  • Pleasant winter climate and manageable monsoon compared to coastal Indian cities
  • Strong coworking supply across multiple neighborhoods, with day passes and flexible memberships widely available
  • Direct international flights to the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and some European cities via PNQ

Cons

  • Traffic is genuinely bad; crossing the city during peak hours can take 60-90 minutes for what looks like 8 km on a map
  • Air quality worsens in winter, particularly November through January, with PM2.5 levels that can trouble people with respiratory sensitivities
  • No digital nomad visa; the 90-days-per-entry cap on the standard eTV limits long uninterrupted stays
  • Pedestrian infrastructure is poor in most areas; daily life requires using apps for every errand
  • Reliable power is mostly fine in modern apartments, but monsoon-season outages can disrupt a work day
  • Water quality varies; budget for filtered or bottled water

Who Pune Is Best For

Pune suits nomads who want a genuinely affordable Indian city with real infrastructure and a large English-speaking professional community around them. If you are already familiar with urban India or are curious to spend a serious stretch in the country rather than just passing through, Pune is a strong base. The tech-heavy population means there are coworking events, startup meetups, and a social scene that does not require you to know anyone on arrival.

It is also a good fit for anyone who enjoys food exploration: the city has one of India's better restaurant cultures, with strong Maharashtrian street food, a growing craft beer scene, and a cluster of well-run international spots in Koregaon Park and Kalyani Nagar.

Skip Pune if you are looking for tropical beach vibes, consistently clean air, or a pedestrian-friendly city where you can wander without a plan. It is also not ideal for nomads who need to be on stable internet during monsoon without a dedicated coworking membership. If India itself feels too overwhelming for a first long-term base, Pune is not going to soften that experience significantly; it is a busy, complex Indian metropolis rather than a gentle introduction.

Frequently Asked Questions About Pune

How much does it cost to live in Pune as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Pune is around a moderate amount a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Pune?

With a WiFi score of 5/10, connectivity in Pune is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Pune safe for digital nomads?

Pune scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Pune a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Pune is worth a look if you value low costs and climate. Costs sit near a moderate amount a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
