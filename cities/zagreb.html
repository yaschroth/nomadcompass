A Remote Worker's Guide to Zagreb

Austro-Hungarian elegance meets Balkan warmth in Croatia's creative capital. Expect $2,000 a month here. Safety and easy visas are the standout strengths, with the climate the main caveat.

Zagreb is one of the more affordable European capitals for remote workers. Rent in the city center (Donji Grad, Gornji Grad, around Tkalčićeva) runs roughly 600-900 EUR ($650-$975) per month for a one-bedroom. Move a few tram stops out to areas like Maksimir, Špansko, or Dubrava and you can find decent one-bedrooms for 450-600 EUR ($490-$650).

Monthly expenses beyond rent typically look like this:

  • Groceries: 150-220 EUR ($165-$240), shopping at local markets like Dolac or discount chains like Lidl
  • Eating out: A sit-down meal at a local konoba runs 10-18 EUR ($11-$20); coffee at a cafe is 1.50-2.50 EUR
  • Coworking hot-desk: 115-300 EUR ($125-$325) per month depending on the space
  • Local tram pass: Around 50 EUR ($55) per month for a monthly ZET pass
  • Utilities (if not included): 80-150 EUR ($85-$165) depending on season

All-in, a comfortable remote worker budget in Zagreb lands between 1,400-2,000 EUR ($1,520-$2,170) per month. You can survive on less with cheaper housing and cooking at home; you can spend more with a central apartment and regular restaurant meals.

Where to Work

Zagreb has a small but solid coworking scene. HUB385 (Franje Petračića 6) is the most popular standalone space with reliable fiber, a good community feel, and day passes around 14 EUR. Impact Hub Zagreb (Basaričekova 11) skews toward social-impact startups and runs regular events. WESPA Spaces (Vjekoslava Heinzela 60) is large and modern, more corporate in feel. Day passes across most spaces run 11-15 EUR; monthly hot-desks start around 115-150 EUR at budget options and go up to 300 EUR at premium spaces.

Cafe work culture is genuinely good here. Zagrebians already spend hours nursing a single coffee, so sitting in a cafe with a laptop does not attract side-eyes. Filteraj and Cogito Coffee are the two most consistently recommended spots for reliable WiFi and enough outlets. Quahwa near the center has a dedicated upstairs area for focused work.

WiFi in apartments and coworking spaces is generally fast (50-300 Mbps is typical on fiber plans). Cafe WiFi is more variable, fine for video calls at the better spots but sometimes unstable during peak hours. Mobile data on a Croatian SIM is cheap and a good backup.

Getting Around

Zagreb is compact and walkable. The lower town core covering Jelačić Square, the main market, and most coworking spaces fits within a comfortable 20-minute walk. You can go days without using any transport at all if you stay central.

The ZET tram network is reliable and frequent, with 19 lines running from early morning until around midnight, then night trams on key routes. A single ride is around 1 EUR with a mobile ticket; a 30-day pass is roughly 50 EUR. Buses cover the outer neighborhoods the trams do not reach.

The airport (Franjo Tuđman Airport) is about 17 km south of the city. The Pleso Prijevoz airport bus departs every 30 minutes and drops you at the main bus station near the city center for around 7 EUR, taking 35-45 minutes. Taxis and ride-shares cost 20-30 EUR for the same trip.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Croatia is part of the Schengen Area. US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period, covering most of the Schengen zone. EU citizens have full freedom of movement.

For longer stays, Croatia offers a dedicated Digital Nomad Residence Permit aimed at non-EU nationals. As of March 2025, the permit was extended to a maximum of 18 months. You need to show a minimum monthly income of roughly 3,620 EUR (2.5 times Croatia's average net salary), proof of remote work with a non-Croatian employer or clients, travel insurance, and six months of bank statements. After your 18-month stay you must leave Croatia for at least six months before applying again. Importantly, the permit comes with a tax exemption as long as your income is from outside Croatia.

Always confirm current requirements directly with the Croatian Ministry of the Interior or a local immigration lawyer before applying, as thresholds update annually.

Best Time to Visit

Zagreb has a continental climate with real winters and warm summers. The best windows for remote work are April-June and September-October. Temperatures in May and early June sit around 18-24C (64-75F), the city is lively, cafe terraces are open, and the tourist crowds have not yet peaked. September keeps similar temperatures with fewer visitors and slightly lower accommodation prices.

July and August can push above 35C (95F) during heat waves. The city empties somewhat as locals head to the coast, which makes it quieter but also means fewer events and a slightly duller social scene. Winter (November-February) is genuinely cold, often grey, and can involve sleet and occasional snow. It is workable with the right mindset and a love of mulled wine at Christmas markets, but not the classic nomad sweet spot.

Pros and Cons

  • Affordable compared to Western Europe: solid infrastructure at a fraction of Berlin or Amsterdam costs
  • Very safe: low crime, comfortable walking at night
  • Excellent cafe culture suited to laptop workers
  • Good food scene with strong local cuisine and growing international options
  • Easy Schengen access for exploring nearby countries
  • Growing English fluency especially among younger locals
  • Smaller nomad community than Lisbon, Tbilisi, or Chiang Mai; takes more effort to meet people
  • Cold grey winters with limited daylight from November through February
  • No beach: the Adriatic coast is 2-3 hours away by car or bus
  • Digital nomad visa income threshold is high (3,620 EUR/month) for non-EU nationals wanting long stays
  • Nightlife is modest by regional standards outside of Tkalčićeva Street

Who Zagreb Is Best For

Zagreb works well for remote workers who want a livable, safe, and genuinely affordable European capital without the tourist noise of Dubrovnik or Split. It suits people who like a walkable city, real local culture, strong coffee, and a low-key pace. Europeans and EU passport holders get the most value since the Schengen 90-day window or EU free movement makes stays frictionless. It is a strong pick for writers, developers, and designers who want a routine-friendly base in Central Europe.

It is probably not the right fit if you need a large, already-established nomad scene to plug into immediately, crave year-round warm weather, or want to combine beach time with your work base. Those looking for a party-first city will also find Zagreb quieter than expected outside of festival season.

Frequently Asked Questions About Zagreb

How much does it cost to live in Zagreb as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $2,000 a month in Zagreb for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Zagreb?

Zagreb rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work good. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Zagreb safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Zagreb is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Zagreb a good city for digital nomads overall?

Zagreb stands out for safety and easy visas among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,000 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
