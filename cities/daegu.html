Setting Up as a Nomad in Daegu

Korea's fashion hub surrounded by mountains. A humid subtropical climate and strong fast WiFi make Daegu an easy base, and at about $1,700 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the size of the nomad scene in mind.

Cost of Living in Daegu

Daegu is the most affordable of South Korea's major cities. Rents run significantly lower than Seoul, and you can stretch a modest budget a long way without leaving the comforts of a fully connected urban environment.

A furnished studio or one-bedroom apartment in central neighborhoods like Dongseongno or Beomeo-dong costs roughly 500,000-650,000 KRW ($370-480 USD) per month. In outer districts such as Dalseo-gu or Dalseong-gu, the same quality drops to 300,000-430,000 KRW ($220-320 USD). Note that Korean landlords often require a large refundable deposit (jeonse or wolse system) rather than simple monthly rent; short-term furnished rentals and goshiwon rooms exist for stays under three months.

  • Rent (central, furnished studio): 500,000-650,000 KRW / $370-480 USD
  • Rent (outer district): 300,000-430,000 KRW / $220-320 USD
  • Groceries (home cooking): 200,000-300,000 KRW / $145-220 USD
  • Eating out (local meals and occasional restaurants): 200,000-350,000 KRW / $145-260 USD
  • Coworking hot desk: 130,000-200,000 KRW / $95-150 USD
  • Metro and bus transport: 50,000-80,000 KRW / $37-60 USD
  • Mobile data (10 GB SIM): 11,000 KRW / $8 USD

Realistic monthly total: 1,300,000-1,800,000 KRW ($950-1,350 USD) living comfortably, or up to $1,800 USD with a dedicated coworking space and a more international lifestyle.

Where to Work

Daegu has a small but functional coworking scene anchored by Regus, which operates a center at the Dongdaegu Trade Centre on the 8th floor of 489 Dongdaegu-ro in Dong-gu, a 10-minute walk from Dongdaegu Station. Hot desks run from around KRW 11,300 per day or roughly KRW 130,000-165,000 per month on a plan. Day passes cost about KRW 40,900. The city also lists numerous smaller shared-office providers, though many are oriented toward Korean startups rather than drop-in visitors.

Cafe work culture is deeply embedded here. Daegu's cafes are generally large, well-lit, and equipped with power outlets. The Dongseongno Cafe Alley in Jung-gu is the most concentrated strip, with places like Ryu Coffee Roasters and T Class Coffee popular among locals who spend hours over a single order. Slow Life near Beomeo Station is a multi-story cafe with natural light and generous seating that suits a long work session.

WiFi is fast and reliable across the city. Daegu averages around 34 Mbps on public and cafe networks. Most cafes provide a password on your receipt. There is no culture of being rushed out, though during weekend peak hours popular spots fill quickly. For reliable, uninterrupted connectivity, a dedicated coworking plan is worth the modest extra cost.

Getting Around

Daegu's subway system covers the city well with three lines. Line 1 (red) and Line 2 (green) form the core network. Line 3 (yellow) is an elevated monorail and the most scenic way to cross the northern parts of the city. A single fare costs 1,500 KRW ($1.10 USD) with a T-money transit card, which you load at any station. Monthly transport rarely exceeds 80,000 KRW if you live near a station.

Buses run frequently and complement the metro, with the same card usable across both. Taxis are cheap by Western standards: a 3 km trip averages around 2,700 KRW ($2). Kakao Taxi (the Korean equivalent of Uber) works seamlessly in Daegu.

Daegu International Airport (TAE) sits about 8 km northeast of the city center. Bus routes 401 and 718 connect it to the city for 1,500 KRW and run every 6-12 minutes. A taxi to the center costs around 7,000-10,000 KRW. Most international visitors arrive via Incheon Airport in Seoul and connect via KTX high-speed rail: trains depart Seoul roughly every 20 minutes from Suseo or Seoul Station and reach Dongdaegu Station in about 1 hour 40 minutes, with fares around 45,000-56,000 KRW one way.

Walkability is good in central neighborhoods. Dongseongno and the areas around Banwoldang Station are dense, flat, and easy to navigate on foot. Outer districts require the subway or a bus.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

South Korea offers visa-free entry to citizens of most Western countries for up to 90 days. US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders all qualify. You cannot work for Korean employers on a tourist entry, but remote work for foreign clients is not explicitly prohibited under tourist status, though the legal grey area is worth understanding before you stay long-term.

South Korea launched the F-1-D Digital Nomad Visa in January 2024, made permanent in 2026. It allows remote workers to live legally in Korea for up to three years. Requirements include: working remotely for a foreign employer or clients, annual income of at least the Korean GNI per capita (approximately $36,963 in 2025, with lower thresholds for applicants aged 18-34 residing outside the Seoul metro area), at least one year of professional experience in the same industry, and private health insurance of at least KRW 100 million ($75,000) coverage. High-income earners face a threshold of roughly $66,000 annually under the standard track.

Extensions within Korea are possible. Confirm all current requirements directly with the nearest Korean consulate or via the Korean Immigration Service (www.immigration.go.kr) before applying, as thresholds and rules have been updated recently.

Best Time to Visit

Spring and autumn are the clear best windows. Late April through late May brings mild temperatures (15-22°C), low humidity, and cherry blossoms. Mid-September through early November is equally pleasant, with clear skies, cool air, and autumn foliage in parks and around Apsan Mountain.

Avoid mid-June through August if possible. Daegu sits in a mountain basin and is famously the hottest major city in South Korea, locally nicknamed "Daefrica." Summers regularly hit 35-38°C with high humidity and a concentrated monsoon season in July and August. Working is fine indoors, but the heat makes exploring exhausting and the air quality can dip with yellow dust and pollution from China in spring.

Winter (December-February) is cold but dry and sunny, averaging 0-5°C. It is workable for remote setups but not the ideal introduction to the city.

Pros and Cons

Pros
  • Significantly cheaper rent and food than Seoul or Busan
  • Fast, reliable internet citywide
  • Efficient subway and cheap taxis
  • Large, comfortable cafes with power outlets and no time pressure
  • Excellent local food scene, especially street food and Korean BBQ
  • Less touristy and more authentically Korean than Seoul
  • Direct KTX access to Seoul and Busan in under 2 hours
Cons
  • English proficiency is lower than Seoul; navigating daily life takes more effort
  • Extremely hot and humid summers make outdoor time uncomfortable
  • Nomad community is small; most foreigners here are English teachers
  • Limited international flights from Daegu Airport; most arrivals are via Seoul
  • Nightlife and international social scene are modest by capital-city standards
  • The jeonse deposit system complicates short-term apartment rentals

Who Daegu Is Best For

Daegu suits nomads who want a genuine South Korean city experience without the costs, crowds, and pace of Seoul. If you are on a tight budget, focused on deep work, and genuinely curious about Korean culture beyond the tourist circuit, Daegu rewards the effort. The city is a good fit for solo workers who are self-sufficient and do not need a large English-speaking social scene to stay motivated.

It also works well for people already living in Korea, particularly those on the F-1-D visa who qualify for lower income thresholds by residing outside the Seoul metro area. Daegu provides real urban infrastructure without the capital-city premium.

Skip Daegu if you need a large international nomad community, rely on spontaneous English-language networking, or plan to visit during summer. Those seeking beach access, a buzzing nightlife, or frequent direct international flights will find Busan or Seoul a better match.

Frequently Asked Questions About Daegu

How much does it cost to live in Daegu as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Daegu is around $1,700 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Daegu?

With a WiFi score of 9/10, connectivity in Daegu is excellent for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Daegu safe for digital nomads?

Daegu scores 9 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Daegu a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Daegu is worth a look if you value fast WiFi and safety. Costs sit near $1,700 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
