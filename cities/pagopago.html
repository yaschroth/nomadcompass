Setting Up as a Nomad in Pago Pago

A drowned volcanic crater for a harbour, and the only US national park south of the equator. For remote workers, Pago Pago leans on nature and English, with a typical budget near $1,800 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Best Neighborhoods in Pago Pago

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Fagatogo and Pago Pago

$$
Administrative & Harbourside

The harbour head, where the government sits

The settlements at the inner end of Pago Pago harbour, with the fono legislature, the courts, the market and the small commercial strip along the water. Rainmaker Mountain rises directly behind, which is why this is one of the rainiest inhabited spots anywhere and why the cloud rarely lifts.

Best for: Anyone with business at the government offices or the market
Pros
  • Everything administrative in the territory is here
  • The market and the small shops are walkable
  • The harbour setting is genuinely dramatic
Cons
  • It rains more here than almost anywhere on earth
  • The cannery gives the inner harbour a distinctive smell
  • Very quiet in the evening and closed on Sundays

Utulei

$$$
Coastal & Institutional

The quieter side of the harbour mouth

The stretch along the southern shore towards the harbour entrance, holding the territory's main hotel, the governor's residence, the museum and a small beach. It catches more sun than the harbour head and is the most pleasant part of the immediate Pago Pago area to be based in.

Best for: Anyone who wants the harbour without the rain at its head
Pros
  • Noticeably drier and brighter than Fagatogo
  • The territory's main hotel and a small beach
  • Walkable to the government offices
Cons
  • Very little in the way of restaurants
  • Limited residential stock
  • The cable car to Mount Alava has not run for decades

Tafuna

$$
Suburban & Practical

The flat plain with the airport and the shops

The lava plain on the southwestern side of Tutuila, holding Pago Pago International Airport, the territory's largest supermarkets, the hospital and a great deal of ordinary housing. It is flat, sunny and considerably drier than the harbour, which is why so much of the population has moved here.

Best for: Longer stays that want the shops, the hospital and the airport
Pros
  • Drier and sunnier than the harbour side
  • The territory's main supermarkets and the hospital
  • Flat ground, which is rare on Tutuila
Cons
  • No character and nothing walkable in the evening
  • Aircraft noise near the approach
  • Twenty minutes from the government offices

Vatia

$$
Village & Spectacular

The national park village on the north coast

A small village on the north shore reached by a steep road over the ridge, sitting inside the National Park of American Samoa with Pola Island rising out of the sea offshore. It is one of the most striking settings on the island and one of the least visited parts of the territory.

Best for: Anyone who wants the national park at the door
Pros
  • Inside the national park with the Pola Island view
  • Genuinely quiet and traditional village life
  • Trailheads for the best walking on Tutuila
Cons
  • The road over the ridge is steep and slow
  • No shops beyond a small store
  • Village protocols matter here and should be respected

Cost of Living in Pago Pago

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Pago Pago, so the $1,800 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 1000 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $1,800 a month. A one-bedroom runs roughly $600 to $1,000, and the rental market is small enough that what is available at any moment matters more than the average. Groceries are expensive because almost everything is shipped in, and the local supermarkets carry American brands at a markup. Fish and taro from the market are cheap. Utilities are high, with power costing well above mainland United States rates. Eating out is limited and the options are mostly Samoan, Korean and Chinese at modest prices. American Samoa uses the United States dollar and the postal system is domestic United States mail, which is a genuine practical advantage.

Where to Work in Pago Pago

Connectivity has improved substantially. American Samoa was dependent on a single cable for years and the Hawaiki cable now provides a second route, so fibre in Pago Pago is workable and mobile data covers the main island. It is not fast by mainland standards and it is expensive. Power is reasonably reliable. There is no coworking of any kind and remote workers use their accommodation. The time zone is unusual and worth understanding: American Samoa is eleven hours behind GMT and does not observe daylight saving, which puts it a full day behind Samoa, forty kilometres away, across the international date line.

Getting Around Pago Pago

The aiga buses, privately owned and brightly painted, run along the main road around Tutuila and are cheap, frequent enough and part of the experience. A car makes life easier and rentals are available. Tutuila is small, about thirty kilometres long, and the road follows the coast with the ridge in the middle. Pago Pago International Airport connects to Honolulu twice a week and to Apia in Samoa several times a week on a short hop, and that is essentially the whole schedule. Getting here is the single hardest part of being here.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Pago Pago

American Samoa is a United States territory but it is outside the federal immigration system, which surprises people. A United States visa or ESTA does not admit you, and United States citizens are not automatically entitled to enter either. Most visitors need an entry permit, which for many nationalities is issued on arrival for thirty days provided you have onward travel. Longer stays require an application to the territory's immigration office in advance. It is also the only United States territory where people are born United States nationals rather than citizens.

Best Time to Visit Pago Pago

May to September is the dry season and the practical window, cooler at around twenty nine degrees with lower humidity. October to April is the wet season and the cyclone season, and Tutuila is genuinely wet: the mountains catch enormous rainfall and Pago Pago harbour is one of the rainiest inhabited spots in the world. The National Park of American Samoa is walkable year round but the trails are slippery in the wet months. Sea temperature is warm throughout. Sunday is worth planning around, since the territory observes it seriously and most things close.

Pros and Cons of Pago Pago

The case for American Samoa is a spectacular natural setting almost nobody visits: Pago Pago harbour is a drowned volcanic crater with ridges rising straight out of it, and the only United States national park south of the equator sits on the same island. Samoan culture here is intact and central to daily life. The case against is isolation, cost, very limited flights and almost nothing in the way of services. Rain is constant for half the year. There is no remote worker presence at all and the visa arrangement is more restrictive than people assume.

Who Pago Pago Is For

American Samoa suits anyone drawn to Polynesian culture in a place that has not been rebuilt around visitors, hikers who want a national park to themselves, and people with a genuine connection to the territory. It suits divers and snorkellers willing to work without an operator. It does not suit anyone who needs reliable services, anyone on a schedule that cannot absorb a cancelled weekly flight, or anyone who wants a social scene, because there is not one. It also suits anyone researching Pacific fisheries or climate, since the tuna cannery in the harbour is the territory's economic centre and its politics run through everything here.
