Setting Up as a Nomad in Salento

A colourful coffee-country village in the Andes, gateway to the towering wax palms of the Cocora Valley. A subtropical highland climate and strong nature make Salento an easy base, and at about $900 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the language barrier in mind.

Cost of Living in Salento

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Salento, so the $900 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 710 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Salento is affordable, a small coffee-country village where costs stay low despite its growing popularity with travellers. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $700 to $1,100 a month. Guesthouse rooms and simple flats in or near the colourful centre rent long-term for about $250 to $500, and local set-lunch meals cost just a few dollars. These are our editorial estimates in US dollars, priced against the Colombian peso, whose exchange rate can shift your real budget. Beyond rent and food, the main costs are jeep rides to the Cocora Valley and the occasional coffee-farm tour.

  • Guesthouse room or flat, long stay: roughly $250 to $500 a month
  • Set-lunch almuerzo or a trout dish: roughly $3 to $7
  • Willys jeep ride to the Cocora Valley: a couple of dollars
  • Coffee-farm tour with tasting: a modest sum

Where to Work in Salento

Salento is a small Andean village and a nature base rather than a remote-work hub, so its infrastructure is basic. Internet is the main limitation: fibre and 4G reach the village but speeds are modest and can drop, and the mountain setting and frequent rain do not help, so test any accommodation's connection carefully before relying on it for calls. Dedicated coworking does not exist; a handful of traveller cafes serve as informal work spots, and a Claro or Movistar SIM is a sensible backup. Salento suits someone with light connectivity needs who wants a beautiful, restful base rather than a place for constant video meetings.

Getting Around Salento

Salento is tiny and walkable, its colourful main street, the Calle Real, running up to a viewpoint over the coffee-country hills. The classic way to reach the star attraction, the Cocora Valley and its giant wax palms, is by the old Willys jeeps that gather in the main plaza, a fun, cheap shared ride. Buses connect Salento to the regional cities of Armenia and Pereira, each about an hour away, which have the nearest airports and links to Bogota, Medellin and the wider country. For coffee farms and nearby villages, jeeps and short bus rides do the job.

Visas for Salento

Most visitors enter Colombia visa-free as tourists, typically for up to 90 days, which can usually be extended to a maximum of 180 days in a calendar year. For a longer stay, Colombia offers a digital nomad visa for remote workers who can show sufficient income from foreign sources, granting a stay of up to two years, alongside other migrant visa options. Rules and income requirements can change, so confirm the current terms with official Colombian sources before relying on them, and keep track of your permitted days and any extension process to avoid overstaying.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Salento

Salento sits in the green Andean coffee region, which is lush precisely because it rains often, so the climate is mild and spring-like year-round rather than seasonal in temperature. The drier, clearer stretches tend to fall around December to February and July to August, which are the best windows for hiking the Cocora Valley and enjoying the views, though a shower can arrive in any month. The wetter periods bring frequent afternoon rain that keeps the hills emerald but can muddy the trails and cloud the scenery. Pack a light rain jacket whenever you visit and aim for the drier months for hiking.

Pros and Cons of Salento

Salento offers some of Colombia's most beautiful coffee-country scenery in a small, colourful package.

  • Pros: the spectacular Cocora Valley and its towering wax palms, immersive coffee-farm culture, a photogenic, brightly painted village, superb hiking and horse riding, a mild year-round climate, clean air, and low costs.
  • Cons: weak and inconsistent internet, a small village with limited amenities and no real nomad community, frequent rain, limited English, and heavy domestic-tourist crowds on weekends and holidays that can dent the tranquillity.

Who Salento Is For

Salento is for the nature-loving nomad who wants to wake up amid coffee farms and Andean hills and can work comfortably with light connectivity. Hikers, coffee lovers, photographers and anyone craving a colourful, laid-back village base will love it, and the Cocora Valley alone is worth the trip. It works best as a scenic, restful base for shorter stays or flexible, low-bandwidth work rather than a long-term hub, and rewards a little Spanish. It is a poor fit for those who need fast, reliable internet, a coworking community, or a lively social scene. Come in a drier stretch, base yourself near the Calle Real, and let the coffee country set the pace.

Frequently Asked Questions About Salento

How much does it cost to live in Salento as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Salento is around $900 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Salento?

Salento rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Salento safe for digital nomads?

Salento scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Salento a good city for digital nomads overall?

Salento stands out for nature and the outdoors and clean air among remote workers. With a typical budget near $900 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
