A Remote Worker's Guide to Puerto Escondido

A surf mecca on Oaxaca's wild Pacific coast. Plan on roughly $1,600 a month. Its strengths are nature and easy visas, while cleanliness is where it scores lower.

Cost of Living in Puerto Escondido

Puerto Escondido sits at the affordable end of Mexican beach towns. Most nomads land somewhere between $1,200 and $2,200 USD per month depending on how often they eat out and which neighborhood they pick.

  • Rent, La Punta or Zicatela (nomad hub): 9,000-18,000 MXN/month ($520-1,050 USD) for a furnished studio or one-bedroom
  • Rent, Centro or Bacocho (cheaper): 6,000-10,000 MXN/month ($350-580 USD) for a basic apartment
  • Groceries (self-catering week): 500-900 MXN ($30-53 USD); Chedraui and Mi Bodega Aurrera are the main supermarkets
  • Eating out, local comida corrida: 60-90 MXN ($3-5 USD) for a full set lunch
  • Eating out, mid-range restaurant: 200-400 MXN ($12-23 USD) per person
  • Coworking hot-desk: 1,700-3,000 MXN/month ($100-175 USD); day passes run 150-300 MXN ($9-17 USD)
  • Local transport (colectivo camioneta): 10-20 MXN per ride ($0.60-1.20 USD)
  • Total monthly range: 20,000-38,000 MXN ($1,200-2,200 USD) comfortable; under 17,000 MXN ($1,000) possible if you cook at home and skip coworking memberships

Prices spike noticeably in December through March when seasonal visitors drive up short-term rental demand. Locking in a monthly rate before high season saves you 20-30% on accommodation.

Where to Work

Puerto Escondido has matured into a genuine remote-work destination. Fiber optic from providers like TotalPlay and Izzi now covers most of the tourist zones, and Starlink is a common backup at coworking spaces and cafes. Speeds of 50-200 Mbps are typical; dedicated coworking spaces often hit 200-300 Mbps with backup power to handle brief outages.

Coworking Spaces

The main dedicated options are in Rinconada and La Punta. Casa Losodeli (Rinconada) operates 24/7 with fiber and Starlink, a pool, and a rooftop, making it a social hub for nomads. Ñuu Rinconada Cowork Community runs Monday-Friday with ocean-view balcony access and Starlink, suited to those who want a quieter, member-focused atmosphere. Pauline Creative Hub is a converted bowling alley in Rinconada with meeting rooms, regular events, and three internet connections. Selina Zicatela offers a large palapa-style coworking floor open 24/7 with AC and private offices.

Cafe Work Culture

Working from cafes is common and accepted, especially in La Punta. Amarisa Cafe in La Punta has Starlink, good coffee, and a relaxed vibe open daily until 9 PM. Espacio Cometa (La Punta) focuses on Pluma Hidalgo specialty coffee and fills up by mid-morning; arrive early. Sandala Cafe (between La Punta and Zicatela) has a garden setting and closes at 3:30 PM, so it works best for morning sessions. Most cafes expect a minimum spend of 100-150 MXN rather than a formal fee.

One honest caveat: power outages do happen, particularly in rainy season. A coworking space with backup power is worth the membership if you have calls or deadlines that cannot slip.

Getting Around

Puerto Escondido is not a walkable city in the conventional sense. The main neighborhoods (Centro, Rinconada, Zicatela, La Punta) are spread along a few kilometers of coastline, and roads often lack sidewalks. You need wheels or a ride to move between areas comfortably.

  • Colectivo camionetas: Shared pickup trucks that run set routes for 10-20 MXN per trip. They are cheap and reliable during daylight hours and are how most locals and long-stay nomads get around.
  • Taxis: Fixed-rate taxis operate from stands around town. A cross-town trip costs 60-120 MXN. Negotiate before you get in.
  • Uber: Available but with limited drivers; wait times can be long, especially outside peak season.
  • Motorbike or scooter rental: 300-500 MXN per day. Common among longer-stay visitors and the fastest way to move between neighborhoods independently.
  • Airport transfers: Puerto Escondido International Airport (PXM) is about 4 km from Zicatela. A taxi to most tourist areas costs 150-250 MXN ($9-15 USD). There is no Uber at the airport arrivals area consistently, so a pre-arranged transfer or taxi stand is the most reliable option.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Mexico does not require a tourist visa for most Western passport holders, including citizens of the US, Canada, UK, EU countries, and Australia. On arrival you receive an FMM visitor permit; immigration officers grant up to 180 days at their discretion, though recent reports indicate that many arrivals are being granted fewer days than the maximum. Ask at the border for the full 180 if that is your intention.

Extensions beyond the days stamped on arrival are not officially possible under the FMM system. If you want to stay longer than six months continuously, Temporary Residency is the legal path: apply at a Mexican consulate in your home country before arriving, show proof of sufficient income or savings, and you receive a permit valid for one year, renewable for up to three more.

Mexico has not implemented a dedicated digital nomad visa as of mid-2026. The Temporary Residency route is the closest equivalent for longer stays.

Always confirm current entry requirements with the official Mexican consulate or INM (Instituto Nacional de Migración) website before travel, as rules can change.

Best Time to Visit

Puerto Escondido has two clear seasons. The dry season runs November through May: sunny days, low humidity, daytime highs around 30-33°C (86-91°F), and almost no rain. The rainy season runs June through October, with afternoon downpours that can last a few hours, high humidity, and temperatures that stay warm but feel heavier.

For remote work, November through April is the most comfortable window. Internet and power are most stable, the heat is manageable with AC or ocean breezes, and the town has enough energy to make the community feel alive without being overcrowded. December through March is peak tourist season, so accommodation prices and availability tighten; book ahead and lock in monthly rates early.

The shoulder months of May and October offer cheaper rentals and quieter beaches with mostly stable conditions. June through September is genuinely rainy and more humid, which some nomads find oppressive, but the tradeoff is significantly lower prices, fewer crowds, and a more local atmosphere. If you handle heat and afternoon rain well, it is an underrated time to be here.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Affordable cost of living relative to most beach destinations
  • Pro: Growing coworking infrastructure with reliable fiber and Starlink backup
  • Pro: Strong nomad and expat community, especially in La Punta and Rinconada
  • Pro: World-class surf, bioluminescent lagoon, and natural beauty on your doorstep
  • Pro: Excellent food scene including a Michelin-recognized restaurant (Almoraduz)
  • Pro: Generally safe for visitors; petty crime is the main concern, not violent crime
  • Con: Not a walkable town; you need transport to get between neighborhoods
  • Con: Power outages are real, especially in rainy season; essential to have coworking with backup power for critical work
  • Con: Accommodation prices spike sharply in peak season (December-March)
  • Con: Limited direct international flights; most routes connect through Mexico City or Oaxaca City
  • Con: Spanish is the working language; English is common in tourist areas but limited elsewhere
  • Con: Ocean currents can be dangerous; Zicatela is a pro surf beach not suitable for casual swimming

Who Puerto Escondido Is Best For

Puerto Escondido suits you well if you want a genuine beach lifestyle without the polished resort price tag, you have flexibility in your schedule (so rainy season or shoulder periods are options), and you are comfortable in a town that is still more surf village than cosmopolitan city. It works particularly well for surfers who also work remotely, people who thrive in tight nomad communities, and anyone who wants Oaxacan culture and food without being landlocked in the city.

It is less ideal if you need a fully urban environment with fast airport connections, you require Japanese-standard power reliability for live trading or live broadcasts, or you want to spend most evenings at rooftop bars in a polished nightlife scene. The town closes early by big-city standards outside of high season. If you are looking for a high-rise coworking tower and Michelin-restaurant-per-block density, Puerto Escondido will feel small. But if you are happy trading that for morning surf checks and mezcal at sunset, it delivers reliably.

Frequently Asked Questions About Puerto Escondido

How much does it cost to live in Puerto Escondido as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,600 a month in Puerto Escondido for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Puerto Escondido?

Puerto Escondido rates 4 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work patchy. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Puerto Escondido safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Puerto Escondido is 5/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Puerto Escondido a good city for digital nomads overall?

Puerto Escondido stands out for nature and easy visas among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,600 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
