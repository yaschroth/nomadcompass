Cost of Living in Melbourne

Melbourne is not cheap, but it is more affordable than Sydney. Your biggest cost by far is rent. Note that Australians quote rent per week, so multiply by roughly 4.3 for a monthly figure. At the June 2026 rate, 1 AUD is worth about 0.70 USD.

  • Rent, central (CBD, Southbank, Docklands): a one-bedroom apartment runs around A$580-620 per week, roughly A$2,500-2,700 per month (about US$1,750-1,900).
  • Rent, cheaper inner suburbs (Brunswick, Footscray, Preston): a one-bedroom sits closer to A$340-480 per week, roughly A$1,500-2,100 per month (about US$1,050-1,470).
  • Groceries: budget A$500-850 per month (about US$350-600) for one person, depending on how much you cook.
  • Eating out: a meal at a casual restaurant is around A$25-35 (US$18-25), a flat white is A$5-6 (US$3.50-4.20).
  • Coworking hot desk: A$300-500 per month (about US$210-350) for a monthly pass, or A$25-45 per day for a drop-in.
  • Local transport: the Myki daily cap is A$11.40 (about US$8), though a temporary state discount has halved fares through December 2026. Budget A$120-160 per month at normal rates.

Realistically, a solo remote worker spends around A$3,500-5,000 per month (about US$2,450-3,500) all in, with rent choice being the main lever. Share a house in an inner suburb and you can push the low end well under A$3,000.

Where to Work

Melbourne has a deep coworking scene. In the CBD you will find WeWork (hot desks from about A$340 per month), The Cluster near the river, JustCo, and Regus locations if you want a corporate feel. For a more creative, community vibe, look at Inspire9 in Richmond, The Commons in Collingwood, and Hub Australia, an Australian-owned brand with several premium sites.

Cafe culture here is genuinely world-class, and working from a cafe over a long black is normal and welcomed at off-peak hours. Suburbs like Fitzroy, Brunswick, and Carlton are full of laptop-friendly spots, though the unspoken rule is to order regularly and free up your table over the lunch rush.

Connectivity is reliable. Fixed home internet (NBN) and cafe WiFi are solid across the inner city, and 4G and 5G mobile coverage from Telstra, Optus, and Vodafone is strong throughout the metro area. A prepaid SIM with a generous data allowance is cheap and easy to pick up on arrival.

Getting Around

Melbourne runs the largest tram network in the world, backed by trains and buses. You pay with a Myki card, which you can buy and top up at stations, convenience stores, and now via credit or debit card and phone tap on trains and trams. The standout perk is the Free Tram Zone: any tram trip that starts and ends inside the CBD and Docklands is completely free, with no need to tap on.

The inner city is flat and very walkable, and the grid layout makes it easy to navigate on foot. For the airport, be warned there is still no train line to Tullamarine. The SkyBus is the main option, running roughly every 10-15 minutes to Southern Cross Station for about A$24.60 one way (US$17). A rideshare or taxi is faster but costs A$60-80 into the city.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Australia has no dedicated digital nomad visa. Remote workers use standard visitor pathways instead. The ETA (subclass 601) suits many passports (including the US, Canada, Japan, and others), lets you visit repeatedly over 12 months, and allows stays of up to three months per visit. Europeans use the free eVisitor (subclass 651) on similar three-month terms, and the Visitor visa (subclass 600) can grant longer stays in some cases.

On these visas you may generally do limited remote work for an employer based outside Australia, provided tourism is your main purpose. Taking on Australian clients or local contracts breaches the conditions. If you want to stay longer, the Working Holiday visa (417 or 462) is an option for eligible under-35s. Visa rules change often, so confirm the current details with the official Department of Home Affairs before you book.

Best Time to Visit

Melbourne sits in the southern hemisphere, so the seasons are flipped: summer runs December to February and winter June to August. The city is famous for having four seasons in a day, and that reputation is earned, so always carry a layer and check the forecast. Winters are grey and cold rather than freezing, but they can be a slog if you are used to sun.

The sweet spot for remote work is autumn (March to May), with mild, stable days and thinner crowds. Spring (September to November) is also pleasant. Summer is warm and lively with festival season in full swing, though occasional heat spikes can be intense.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: outstanding coffee and food culture, and a genuinely creative city.
  • Pro: excellent public transport with a free tram zone in the centre.
  • Pro: reliable internet, safe streets, and a large community of remote workers.
  • Pro: walkable, flat inner city packed with cafes and coworking.
  • Con: high rent and overall costs compared with most nomad hubs.
  • Con: unpredictable, often grey weather, especially in winter.
  • Con: no easy visa built for long-term remote work.
  • Con: a long flight from almost everywhere and a punishing time zone for working with Europe or the Americas.

Who Melbourne Is Best For

Melbourne suits remote workers who value a rich, walkable city with great food, strong infrastructure, and a big creative community, and who can absorb a higher cost of living. It is ideal if your work aligns with Asia-Pacific hours or if you are self-sufficient on schedule. It also rewards people who plan to stay a few months rather than years, given the visa limits.

Skip it if you are chasing a low budget, need warm weather to feel good, or need to overlap heavily with European or American colleagues. In those cases, cheaper and better-connected hubs will serve you better.

Frequently Asked Questions About Melbourne

How much does it cost to live in Melbourne as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $4,000 a month in Melbourne for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Melbourne?

Melbourne rates 8 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work excellent. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Melbourne safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Melbourne is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Melbourne a good city for digital nomads overall?

Melbourne stands out for English and food among remote workers. With a typical budget near $4,000 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
