Setting Up as a Nomad in Puerto Vallarta

Experience pacific beach town with cobblestone charm and stunning sunsets. A tropical climate and strong nature make Puerto Vallarta an easy base, and at about $2,200 a month it fits a range of budgets.

Cost of Living in Puerto Vallarta

Puerto Vallarta sits in the mid-range for Mexican cities. You can live here on a tight budget or spend considerably more depending on your lifestyle and where you choose to rent.

  • Rent, central areas (Zona Romantica, Centro): MXN 20,000-32,000/month ($1,175-1,880 USD) for a 1-bedroom
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Versalles, 5 de Diciembre): MXN 14,000-22,000/month ($820-1,290 USD) for a 1-bedroom
  • Groceries: MXN 3,400-5,100/month ($200-300 USD) shopping at local markets and supermarkets like Walmart or Chedraui
  • Eating out: MXN 60-100 ($3.50-6 USD) for tacos at a local stand; MXN 170-340 ($10-20 USD) for a mid-range sit-down meal
  • Coworking hot-desk: MXN 4,000-6,800/month ($240-400 USD); daily passes run around MXN 240 ($14 USD)
  • Local transport: MXN 10 ($0.60 USD) per bus ride; Uber rides within the city run MXN 50-120 ($3-7 USD)
  • Utilities: MXN 1,700-3,400/month ($100-200 USD) including electricity, which runs high if you rely on air conditioning in summer

Total monthly range: Budget nomad MXN 13,600-20,400 ($800-1,200 USD); mid-range MXN 22,100-30,600 ($1,300-1,800 USD); comfortable MXN 34,000+ ($2,000+ USD). Prices have crept up over the past two years as the city has grown in popularity with remote workers, so build in some buffer.

Where to Work

Puerto Vallarta has a legitimate coworking scene rather than just a handful of hotel lobbies. Two dedicated spaces stand out.

Vallarta Cowork (Zona Romantica, near Rio Cuale) is the original and most established option. It offers fiber optic WiFi with VOIP priority, 24/7 access via smart lock, a meeting room, and even a hammock. Google rating of 4.9 from dozens of reviewers. Monthly memberships run around MXN 2,500 ($145 USD).

Angulo Cowork (Lazaro Cardenas/Versalles area) is the newer alternative with 1,000 Mbps fiber and a backup power supply, meaning it holds up even during outages. It has a small in-house cafe. Good for anyone who needs rock-solid connectivity for video calls.

Natureza Cowork (near Versalles) combines a cafe and workspace with a 4.5 Google rating, though reviewers note the WiFi is capped to a 2-hour window per purchase and can be inconsistent for long calls.

For cafe work, Cafe La Ventana (5 de Diciembre) opens at 7am and allows extended stays. The Coffee (Versalles) has a minimalist setup popular with laptop workers. Proyecto Cafe (Versalles) stays open until 10:30pm, which is rare in the area. Cafe culture in PV is generally nomad-tolerant but not universally so: popular brunch spots will want your table back by noon on weekends.

Home WiFi through providers like Telmex or Totalplay delivers reliable fiber in most central neighborhoods. Download speeds of 50-100 Mbps are typical for apartments in Versalles and the Romantic Zone. Connectivity is genuinely good, not just passable.

Getting Around

The city is compact along the bay, and the central areas where most nomads stay are walkable day and night. The Romantic Zone, Versalles, and Centro are all within 2-3 km of each other.

Local buses run the length of the coast for MXN 10 ($0.60 USD). They pass every 15-20 minutes during the day and are safe and reliable for the main routes. Always carry exact change.

Uber operates in the city and is reasonably priced for cross-town trips, though driver availability can thin out late at night. InDrive is widely used by locals and often has better availability than Uber, with the option to pay cash.

Official taxis use zone-based pricing rather than meters. Confirm the peso price before getting in. They are the most reliable option after midnight.

Airport (PVR): The airport is about 8 km north of the Romantic Zone. Uber is technically not allowed to pick up inside the airport zone: you need to cross the pedestrian footbridge outside the terminal and request your ride from the other side of the highway. Many travelers find this confusing. A licensed airport taxi to the Romantic Zone costs around MXN 300-400 ($18-24 USD). Shared shuttle vans run $10-15 USD per person with hotel drop-offs.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most Western passport holders (US, Canada, EU, UK, Australia) enter Mexico visa-free and can stay up to 180 days as a tourist. However, immigration officers at the airport no longer automatically stamp 180 days. In practice, 30-90 days is common unless you specifically request more and can demonstrate that you plan to spend money in Mexico. Be polite and direct about your plans if you want the full allowance.

Mexico does not have a dedicated digital nomad visa. The closest option is the Temporary Resident Visa, which lets you live in Mexico for up to one year with extensions available up to four years. You apply at a Mexican consulate in your home country before traveling. The income requirement is roughly $2,600 USD/month. This visa does not grant work authorization for Mexican employers but is legal for remote workers earning income from outside Mexico.

Always verify current requirements with the official Mexican immigration authority (INM) or your nearest Mexican consulate before traveling, as rules and income thresholds change.

Best Time to Visit

The dry season runs from November through April. Temperatures sit between 18-28°C (65-82°F), humidity is low, skies are mostly clear, and this is when Puerto Vallarta is at its most pleasant. December through March is peak tourist season, which means higher prices and more crowds.

The rainy season runs June through October. Rain typically arrives in short afternoon or evening bursts rather than all-day downpours, so mornings are often workable. July and August see the most rainfall. Heat and humidity are noticeably higher (30-34°C / 86-93°F). The upside: rental prices drop, the city is quieter, and you can often negotiate better monthly rates.

Best window for remote work: November through April offers the most comfortable working conditions. If budget is a priority, consider the shoulder months of May or October, which offer lower prices with only occasional rain. Avoid the heart of hurricane season (August-September) if you need consistent power and connectivity.

Pros and Cons

  • Reliable fiber internet in central neighborhoods and quality coworking options
  • Large English-speaking expat and nomad community, easy to meet people
  • Strong food scene from street tacos to fine dining, with good options at every price point
  • Walkable central areas with beaches, restaurants, and cafes in easy reach
  • Good flight connections to US and Canadian cities via PVR airport
  • Active outdoor options including whale watching, surfing, and jungle hikes nearby
  • Prices have risen sharply over recent years, particularly for short-term rentals in the Romantic Zone
  • Extreme heat and humidity from June through September makes outdoor work or walking uncomfortable
  • Tourist infrastructure dominates some areas, which can feel sanitized and pricey
  • Safety context requires awareness: petty theft occurs in busy areas and the US Embassy has flagged risks around certain apps and unfamiliar private locations
  • Limited daily bus frequency at night means you rely on apps or taxis after 10pm
  • Airport Uber situation is genuinely awkward for first arrivals

Who Puerto Vallarta Is Best For

Puerto Vallarta works well for nomads who want a beach-city lifestyle with real infrastructure. If you need reliable WiFi, a functioning coworking scene, English widely spoken, and access to good food without sacrificing the feel of a genuine Mexican city, this delivers all of that. It is particularly well-suited to people who have already done the cheaper budget-nomad experience and are ready to spend a bit more for comfort and community.

The city has a large LGBTQ+ expat community centered on the Romantic Zone, making it one of the more inclusive destinations in Mexico. Remote workers who enjoy outdoor activities like hiking, surfing, or whale watching will find opportunities close by.

Consider skipping it if you are working to a very tight budget (under $1,000/month all-in is genuinely difficult here now), if you need a visa-free entry for longer than 180 days without applying for residency, or if you find heavily tourist-oriented environments draining. Travelers who want a quieter, more authentically local Mexican experience will likely find the central zones too international in character. During rainy season, those with unreliable home setups should note that power outages, while not constant, do occur during heavy storms.

Frequently Asked Questions About Puerto Vallarta

How much does it cost to live in Puerto Vallarta as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Puerto Vallarta is around $2,200 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Puerto Vallarta?

With a WiFi score of 6/10, connectivity in Puerto Vallarta is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Puerto Vallarta safe for digital nomads?

Puerto Vallarta scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Puerto Vallarta a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Puerto Vallarta is worth a look if you value nature and easy visas. Costs sit near $2,200 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
