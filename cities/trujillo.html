Cost of Living in Trujillo

Trujillo is one of Peru's most affordable mid-sized cities. A one-bedroom apartment in the central districts of El Golf or Primavera runs roughly S/800-1,200 (USD 210-320) per month. Step further out toward California or San Andres and you can find decent one-bedrooms for S/600-900 (USD 160-240). Groceries from local markets run about S/400-600 (USD 105-160) monthly if you cook most meals. Eating out is cheap: a solid lunch menu at a local cevicheria or menu del dia costs S/12-20 (USD 3-5), and even a sit-down dinner at a nicer restaurant rarely tops S/60 (USD 16) per person with drinks.

A hot-desk day pass at Estacion Coworking or Centrika typically runs S/30-50 (USD 8-13); monthly memberships range from S/300-550 (USD 80-145). Getting around by combi or micro bus costs S/1-2 (USD 0.25-0.55) per ride. Uber and InDriver rides across the city average S/8-15 (USD 2-4). All in, a comfortable nomad budget for Trujillo lands around USD 1,000-1,400 per month, making it one of the cheaper cities in South America for remote workers.

Where to Work

Trujillo has a small but functional coworking scene. Estacion Coworking in San Andres is the main hub, offering fast fiber internet, a cafe, printer access, and a startup-leaning community. It hosts occasional events and is the closest thing the city has to a nomad focal point. Centrika Coworking on Jiron Ayacucho in the historic center rents offices and flexible desks and caters more to local professionals than nomads, but it is a solid backup option.

Cafe-working culture is emerging but not yet as established as in Lima or Medellin. Specialty cafes like Moche Cafes Especiales in the historic center and Elevaria Cafe near Av. Fatima welcome laptop workers and serve excellent Peruvian specialty coffee. Home internet via Claro or Movistar fiber delivers 50-100 Mbps at S/80-120 (USD 21-32) per month and is generally reliable in El Golf and Primavera. Power cuts are rare in the modern residential areas. Budget for a local SIM as a backup hotspot; both Claro and Entel offer 4G LTE with good coverage in the city.

Getting Around

The historic center is walkable and compact. Most attractions, cafes, and restaurants in the centro are reachable on foot within 20 minutes. Beyond the center, you will need wheels. Combis and micros cover the whole city for S/1-2 per trip and run from early morning until about 9 PM, though routes can be confusing at first. Uber and InDriver are both active in Trujillo and are the recommended options for evening travel or any time you are carrying a laptop bag, given occasional petty theft on public buses.

Taxis exist but always agree on a price before you get in; a cross-city ride should be S/8-15. The beach suburb of Huanchaco is a popular side base for nomads; colectivos run there for S/3-4, or a taxi costs around S/15-20. Trujillo's airport (TRU, Capitan FAP Carlos Martinez de Pinillos) is about 10 km from the center. A taxi to downtown costs roughly S/20-25 (USD 5-7). There are no direct bus routes that serve the airport entrance conveniently, so a taxi or ride-share is the practical choice with luggage.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the United States, Canada, the EU, the UK, Australia, New Zealand, and most of South America enter Peru visa-free and receive a tourist permit of up to 90 days on arrival. Your passport should have at least 6 months of validity remaining. You can extend your stay by 30 days by visiting the Direccion General de Migraciones; the process requires a return ticket and a small fee.

Peru passed legislation in late 2023 creating a dedicated digital nomad visa with a 365-day stay, but as of mid-2026 that visa has not been implemented by immigration authorities and was not included in updated TUPA publications. There is no workaround other than the standard tourist permit or exiting and re-entering the country. Always confirm current rules with the official Migraciones website (migraciones.gob.pe) before your trip, as regulations can change.

Best Time to Visit

Trujillo is a genuine year-round destination. The coast here is arid desert, so rain is almost never a factor. Temperatures stay between 16-29 degrees C (61-84 F) throughout the year. The warmest months run January through March, with afternoon highs touching 28-29 C (82-84 F) and occasional coastal humidity. June through October is drier and slightly cooler, averaging 20-24 C (68-75 F) during the day with fresh breezes off the Pacific.

For remote work, the sweet spot is April through November. Temperatures are comfortable, humidity is low, and the city runs at a calm pace outside of major festivals. If you visit in late January or early February, you will catch the Festival Internacional de la Primavera (Spring Festival) and the Marinera dance competition, which are culturally unmissable but also mean busier streets and higher short-term rental prices. The December-to-March window is perfectly fine to visit, just warmer and more festive.

Pros and Cons

  • Low cost of living compared to Lima, Cusco, or most Latin American nomad hubs
  • Mild, sunny climate year-round with virtually no rain
  • Rich archaeology: Chan Chan, Huaca del Sol y de la Luna, and Huanchaco are on your doorstep
  • Excellent northern Peruvian food, especially ceviche, cabrito, and seco dishes
  • Compact center that is easy to navigate on foot
  • Lower tourist saturation than Lima or Cusco, so a more authentic day-to-day feel
  • Limited coworking options: the scene is thin compared to Lima or Medellin
  • Little English spoken outside hotels and tourist businesses; functional Spanish is needed
  • Petty theft is a real concern in peripheral neighborhoods and busy markets
  • Small international community: fewer nomad meetups and networking events than larger hubs
  • No implemented digital nomad visa despite legislation passing in 2023
  • Air quality is moderate: Trujillo ranks among the more polluted Peruvian cities due to vehicle emissions

Who Trujillo Is Best For

Trujillo suits nomads who want a genuinely affordable South American base without the chaos of Lima or the tourist-trap pricing of Cusco. If you can work independently from a home office or are comfortable with a small coworking ecosystem, and you already speak basic Spanish, you will find the low costs, good food, and stable year-round climate rewarding. It is particularly good for people interested in pre-Columbian history, surf day trips to Huanchaco, or those doing longer slow-travel stays in Peru outside the beaten Gringo Trail.

Skip Trujillo if you need a large nomad community, frequent English-language networking, or a mature coworking infrastructure. It is also a poor fit if you are unwilling to take basic urban safety precautions, especially at night. Travelers who want consistent sun, low prices, and an authentic Peruvian city that has not been packaged for foreign visitors will feel right at home here.

Frequently Asked Questions About Trujillo

How much does it cost to live in Trujillo as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,000 a month in Trujillo for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Trujillo?

Trujillo rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Trujillo safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Trujillo is 5/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Trujillo a good city for digital nomads overall?

Trujillo stands out for low costs and climate among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,000 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
