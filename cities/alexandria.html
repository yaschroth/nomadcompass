Cost of Living in Alexandria

Alexandria is significantly cheaper than Cairo for equivalent accommodation, and dramatically cheaper than most European or North American cities. The Egyptian pound trades at roughly 49-50 EGP per USD as of mid-2026, so small amounts in local currency go a long way.

Expect to pay 6,500-9,000 EGP ($130-180) per month for a furnished one-bedroom in a central area like Sporting, Roushdy, or Sidi Gaber. Head further out toward Smouha or Saba Pasha and you can find similar apartments for 3,000-4,500 EGP ($60-90). Groceries from local markets run around 1,200-2,000 EGP ($25-40) per month if you cook Egyptian staples. A meal at a local foul and falafel spot costs under 100 EGP ($2); a full seafood dinner for two at a mid-range restaurant runs 600-1,000 EGP ($12-20). A hot-desk at one of the city's coworking spaces costs roughly 4,500-6,000 EGP ($90-120) per month. The tram and local buses are very cheap, with a monthly transit pass around 650 EGP ($13); taxis and ride-hail apps add perhaps 800-1,500 EGP ($16-30) per month depending on your habits. Home broadband averages around 600 EGP ($12) per month.

A comfortable but modest nomad budget lands in the range of $650-1,100 per month all-in, or $1,100-1,600 if you want a nicer apartment, regular restaurant meals, and coworking membership.

Where to Work

Alexandria has a small but functional coworking scene. Workstation in Smouha is one of the most established options, offering 50-plus seats, 200 Mbps internet, four private rooms, a printer, lockers, and a full kitchen. Hive Space in Roushdy provides a quieter atmosphere with complimentary drinks, a lounge, yoga studio, and a rooftop terrace, and draws consistent praise for its internet reliability. Flux Coworking Space on El-Horya Street in the Loran district is the largest single space in the city, with capacity for over 100 people, though some users report WiFi inconsistency during peak hours.

Cafe culture has grown considerably, and chains like Zanilli's Coffee (branches in Sporting, Montazah, and near City Center Mall) are popular among remote workers for their hours and seating. That said, many traditional coffeehouses do not have reliable WiFi and are better suited for reading than video calls.

The honest WiFi picture: home and coworking broadband in Alexandria delivers 15-40 Mbps download in better spaces, which is enough for Zoom and cloud work. Mobile data via Vodafone, Orange, or Etisalat SIM cards fills the gaps reliably. Public cafe WiFi is hit-or-miss, so carry a local SIM as a hotspot backup, especially if you are on calls.

Getting Around

Alexandria runs along a long coastal strip, which means much of the city is linear and navigable. The tram network (Raml Tram and Al-Madina line) is cheap, slow, and often crowded, but covers the central coast. City buses and minibuses serve most districts at very low fares but can be confusing to navigate without some Arabic. Ride-hail apps (Uber and Careem both operate here) are the most practical option for tourists and nomads; fares are low by Western standards.

The Corniche is pleasant to walk in good weather, and central neighborhoods like Raml Station, Roushdy, and Smouha are walkable within themselves. However, the city is not built for pedestrians across long distances, and the heat from June through September makes walking unpleasant for most of the day.

Getting to and from Borg El Arab Airport (Alexandria's main international airport, about 45 km west of center): a taxi takes roughly 50 minutes and costs around 250-400 EGP ($5-8). Bus route 555 runs from the airport to Al-Raml Bus Terminal for around 50 EGP ($1). There is no train link to the airport. If you are arriving via Cairo, the train between Cairo Ramses and Alexandria Sidi Gaber takes 2-2.5 hours and is comfortable, with frequent departures.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most Western passport holders (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia) can obtain a 30-day tourist visa on arrival at Borg El Arab Airport for $25-30 USD in cash, or apply in advance via the Egyptian e-visa portal. The e-visa is single-entry and also valid for 30 days. A multiple-entry e-visa (valid 6 months) is available but less straightforward to obtain.

Egypt does not have a formal digital nomad visa as of mid-2026. You can extend a tourist visa at the Mogamma building in Cairo or at the Passport and Immigration Office in Alexandria for a further 30 days; extensions beyond that require leaving and re-entering the country or applying for a resident permit, which requires more documentation and is mainly used by long-term expats.

The 2025 five-year multiple-entry visa program allows up to 180 days per visit, but this is primarily marketed at frequent high-spend travelers rather than standard tourist arrivals. Always verify current entry requirements with the official Egyptian e-visa portal or your embassy before travel.

Best Time to Visit

Alexandria has a Mediterranean climate moderated by sea breezes, which makes it meaningfully cooler in summer than Cairo, though still warm. The best windows for remote work comfort are October to November and March to May, when daytime highs run 20-28 degrees C (68-82F), evenings are pleasant, and the Corniche is enjoyable. The city is also at its least crowded outside of July and August.

July and August see Egyptian families flooding the city from the interior, accommodation prices rise, and daytime temperatures regularly hit 32-35 degrees C with humidity. If you arrive in summer, expect a noisier, more crowded city and plan to work during the cooler morning hours. Winter (December to February) is mild, around 15-20 degrees C by day, and quiet, making it a decent budget season, though some days are grey and windy off the sea.

Pros and Cons

  • Very low cost of living, one of the cheapest Mediterranean-adjacent cities for nomads
  • Rich history and culture: libraries, ancient sites, a genuinely distinct Egyptian coastal identity
  • Better air quality and sea access compared to Cairo
  • Good food scene with outstanding fresh seafood at local prices
  • Growing coworking infrastructure with several reliable options
  • Direct train link to Cairo opens up the whole country easily
  • Inconsistent internet outside dedicated coworking spaces
  • No digital nomad visa: you are limited to tourist extensions and re-entries
  • English is limited outside tourist-facing businesses and universities
  • Very limited nightlife due to conservative social norms; alcohol is available but not widely visible
  • Traffic and pedestrian environment can be chaotic and stressful
  • Smaller nomad community than Cairo; finding like-minded remote workers takes more effort

Who Alexandria Is Best For

Alexandria suits nomads who prioritize low costs and cultural depth over infrastructure and convenience. If you are comfortable navigating a large Egyptian city with limited English, want Mediterranean weather without Mediterranean prices, and enjoy seafood and a slower coastal pace, Alexandria delivers well. History enthusiasts, budget-conscious solo travelers, and anyone already spending time in Egypt will find it worth a month or two.

Skip Alexandria if you need rock-solid internet for heavy video production or engineering builds, want a buzzing expat social scene, or rely on nightlife and bars to decompress. Cairo is the better bet for those needing more digital infrastructure, a larger international community, or easier access to the full range of coworking options in Egypt.

Frequently Asked Questions About Alexandria

How much does it cost to live in Alexandria as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,100 a month in Alexandria for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Alexandria?

Alexandria rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Alexandria safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Alexandria is 6/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Alexandria a good city for digital nomads overall?

Alexandria stands out for low costs and culture among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,100 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
