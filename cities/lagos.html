Setting Up as a Nomad in Lagos

Africa's biggest city, chaotic, creative, and full of opportunity. For remote workers, Lagos leans on English and nightlife, with a typical budget near $2,000 a month. The main trade-off is air quality.

Cost of Living in Lagos

Lagos is one of Africa's most expensive cities for expats but genuinely affordable if you adapt to local rhythms. Rent is the biggest variable. A furnished one-bedroom in Lekki or Victoria Island costs between NGN 200,000 and 350,000 per month (roughly $130 to $230 USD at mid-2025 exchange rates), with premium spots pushing past NGN 500,000. On the mainland in neighborhoods like Yaba or Surulere, you can find comparable space for NGN 100,000 to 180,000 per month ($65 to $120 USD).

Beyond rent, expect to spend roughly:

  • Groceries: NGN 40,000 to 60,000 per month ($25 to $40) from local markets; significantly more from upscale supermarkets
  • Eating out: NGN 3,000 to 8,000 for a local meal; NGN 15,000 to 35,000 at a mid-range island restaurant
  • Coworking hot-desk: NGN 60,000 to 150,000 per month ($40 to $100), with day passes from NGN 5,000 to 15,000
  • Local transport (Uber/Bolt): NGN 2,000 to 5,000 per trip on the island; BRT bus fares are a fraction of that
  • Generator fuel: NGN 10,000 to 30,000 per month, an unavoidable cost given frequent power cuts

A realistic monthly total for a nomad living on the island runs NGN 350,000 to 700,000 ($230 to $470 USD). Mainland living cuts that by 30 to 40 percent. Anything imported, including many branded goods and international restaurant meals, costs close to European prices.

Where to Work

Dedicated coworking spaces are your most reliable option. Workstation NG on Victoria Island is open 24/7 and attracts freelancers and tech workers. 989 Workspaces has two Lekki-area locations with stable internet, meeting rooms, and a collaborative atmosphere. EridanSpace in Ikeja offers one of the lowest day-pass rates (around NGN 5,000) with fast WiFi and a gaming zone. HubOne in Yaba is the go-to for the Lagos tech community, though it lacks on-site food.

Cafe work culture exists but is concentrated in specific spots. Pause Cafe at the Palms in Lekki, Top Beans at the Wings Complex on Victoria Island, and Eric Kayser in Lekki Phase I are the most laptop-friendly options with reliable WiFi and enough outlets. Moonshine Cafe in Lekki Phase I offers a quieter setting.

The honest WiFi situation: coworking spaces in Lekki, Victoria Island, and Yaba generally deliver consistent speeds adequate for video calls. Cafe WiFi is more variable. Power cuts are frequent citywide, so coworking spaces with generators are worth the premium. Most nomads also carry a 4G/5G data SIM (MTN or Airtel) as backup. Home broadband plans start around NGN 15,000 to 25,000 per month.

Getting Around

Traffic in Lagos is legendary and often brutal. Your location determines your mobility. If you live and work on the island (Victoria Island, Lekki, Ikoyi), you can confine most daily movement to a small radius and use Uber or Bolt for short hops at NGN 2,000 to 5,000 per trip. Crossing between the island and mainland during rush hours (roughly 7 to 10am and 5 to 8pm) can mean 90 minutes or more in a car.

The BRT (Bus Rapid Transit) runs dedicated corridors connecting Oshodi, Yaba, Lagos Island, Lekki, and other major points. Fares are paid via a Cowry Card and cost a fraction of ride-hailing. The Lagos Blue Line metro, running from Marina to Mile 2, is air-conditioned and avoids road congestion entirely. Ferries connect key waterfront terminals including Five Cowries Terminal, and offer a faster, calmer alternative to road travel between the mainland and island.

Murtala Muhammed International Airport sits on the mainland in Ikeja. Getting to Victoria Island or Lekki takes 45 minutes to over 2 hours depending on traffic. Pre-book a ride via Uber or arrange a driver through your accommodation. Budget NGN 10,000 to 20,000 for an airport transfer to the island. Arriving off-peak significantly cuts travel time.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

As of May 2025, Nigeria replaced its Visa on Arrival system with an e-Visa platform. Citizens of 177 countries can apply online at the Nigerian Immigration Service portal. The tourist e-Visa (F5A) grants up to 30 days, while a separate application allows stays up to 90 days. Approval typically takes 24 to 48 hours and costs around $80 to $100 USD depending on nationality. The business e-Visa covers business-related visits but not formal employment.

Nigeria has no dedicated digital nomad visa. Most remote workers enter on a tourist e-Visa and renew by exiting and re-entering, though enforcement against remote work on tourist visas is minimal in practice. Extensions within Nigeria are possible through the Nigerian Immigration Service but are bureaucratically intensive.

Always confirm current requirements with the official Nigerian Immigration Service website (immigration.gov.ng) before travel, as policies have shifted frequently.

Best Time to Visit

Lagos has a tropical climate with temperatures staying between 21C (70F) at night and 33C (91F) by day year-round. The city splits into two seasons: a dry season from November to March and a rainy season from April to October.

For remote work, November through February is the sweet spot. The air is drier and slightly cooler, harmattan winds reduce humidity, and flooding-related traffic chaos is minimal. December is the most festive and expensive month, driven by the Lagos diaspora returning and a packed events calendar.

The wettest months are June and July, when heavy downpours cause flash floods and make traffic substantially worse than usual. September brings a brief drier window within the rainy season. If budget matters more than weather, the rainy season months command lower accommodation rates and fewer crowds, with the tradeoff of unpredictable commutes.

Pros and Cons

  • Low cost if you eat local and live on the mainland
  • Extraordinary food scene, from roadside suya to top-tier Pan-Asian fine dining
  • Growing tech and startup community in Yaba and Victoria Island
  • English is universal, spoken natively across business and daily life
  • Vibrant nightlife and arts culture year-round, especially December
  • Direct flights to major African, European, and Middle Eastern hubs
  • Frequent power cuts require generators and add to monthly costs
  • Traffic is severe and can consume multiple hours of your day
  • Safety requires real awareness: petty theft and armed robbery exist, and some areas are off-limits after dark
  • Air quality is poor, driven by generator fumes, traffic exhaust, and open burning
  • Coworking and cafe scene is thin outside a few island neighborhoods
  • No digital nomad visa and bureaucracy around extensions can be frustrating

Who Lagos Is Best For

Lagos suits you if you are drawn to a fast, loud, and genuinely exciting African megacity and can tolerate logistical friction in exchange for cultural immersion. It works well for nomads who already have connections in the Nigerian tech or creative scene, those doing business in West Africa, or travelers who want to base in a global city at well below global city prices. You get the most from Lagos if you are staying at least a month, living on the island, and working from a proper coworking space.

Skip Lagos if you need frictionless daily productivity, cannot handle unreliable power, are sensitive to air quality, or expect the cafe-work culture of Southeast Asia or Europe. First-time solo travelers to Africa may find it overwhelming as an entry point: start somewhere gentler and return once you have a clearer picture of what you want from the continent.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lagos

How much does it cost to live in Lagos as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Lagos is around $2,000 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Lagos?

With a WiFi score of 4/10, connectivity in Lagos is patchy for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Lagos safe for digital nomads?

Lagos scores 3 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is patchy for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Lagos a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Lagos is worth a look if you value English and nightlife. Costs sit near $2,000 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
