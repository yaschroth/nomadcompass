Living in Da Nang as a Digital Nomad

A beach city with perfect weather and Vietnam's best infrastructure. Expect $1,400 a month here. Low costs and nature are the standout strengths, with cleanliness the main caveat.

Cost of Living in Da Nang

Da Nang sits in the sweet spot for budget-conscious nomads: cheaper than Ho Chi Minh City for rent, with a beach lifestyle that would cost triple in Southeast Asian alternatives. Expect to spend around 17,000,000-27,000,000 VND ($700-1,100 USD) per month all-in.

  • Rent, central city or My An area: 4,800,000-9,700,000 VND ($200-400/month) for a studio or one-bedroom
  • Rent, beachfront or modern My Khe strip: 8,700,000-12,000,000 VND ($350-500/month)
  • Groceries (self-catering): 1,200,000-2,400,000 VND ($50-100/month) from local markets and supermarkets
  • Eating out: 6,000,000-9,700,000 VND ($250-400/month) mixing street food and occasional Western meals; a bowl of mi quang runs 35,000 VND ($1.50) while a Western dinner is 200,000-400,000 VND ($8-16)
  • Coworking hot-desk: 1,900,000-2,900,000 VND ($80-120/month)
  • Local transport (Grab + scooter rental): 1,200,000-1,900,000 VND ($50-80/month)
  • Total realistic range: 17,000,000-27,000,000 VND ($700-1,100/month) for a comfortable nomad life

Prices have been rising since 2024 due to growing nomad and expat interest. Budget travelers can dip below $700 by renting farther from the beach and eating exclusively local food. If you want a modern beachside apartment with frequent Western meals, budget toward $1,200.

Where to Work

Da Nang's coworking scene has expanded quickly. The main options are clustered near the beach and in the central Hai Chau district.

Coworking spaces worth knowing: Enosta Space (formerly Enouvo) near Son Tra offers 24/7 access and a beach-adjacent location; ACE Coworking Space spans three floors with phone booths and a juice bar; Seaview Coworking runs 24/7 from a beachfront building and the owner actively helps with visa questions; Coworking Da Nang is known for its community programming including weekly lunches. HIVE opened in mid-2024 with standing desks and a focused quiet zone. Hot-desks typically run 2,000,000-2,900,000 VND ($80-120/month), with day passes around 79,000-150,000 VND ($3-6).

Cafe work culture is strong. The An Thuong neighbourhood and the streets behind My Khe Beach have dozens of cafes with reliable power outlets, air conditioning, and speeds that handle video calls. Most owners are accustomed to laptop workers and will not push you out after one coffee.

WiFi reality: Home fiber and coworking connections regularly hit 50-150 Mbps down. Cafe speeds vary but a 4G backup SIM (Viettel or Mobifone, under $5/month for data) is worth having for video calls in busier spots. Power outages during heavy storms in October and November are occasional but real, so keep a backup plan during typhoon season.

Getting Around

Da Nang does not reward walking between districts. The city is spread out: the beach strip, city center, Han River, and Marble Mountains are each 5-15 km apart. Within An Thuong or the My Khe strip you can walk to dinner, but for anything else you need wheels.

Grab is the default for most nomads. A GrabBike (scooter taxi) costs around 15,000-18,000 VND ($0.65) for a 10-minute ride; a GrabCar runs 50,000-65,000 VND ($2-2.50) for the same distance. Wait times are almost always under 5 minutes.

Renting a scooter is the most flexible option if you plan to stay more than a week. Automatic scooters run 240,000-290,000 VND ($10-12/day) or cheaper by the month. Vietnamese traffic is fast and assertive; the beach road and riverside roads are manageable for cautious riders, but central intersections take adjustment.

Airport: Da Nang International Airport sits roughly 3 km from the city center and about 7 km from My Khe Beach. A Grab car to the beach costs 100,000-120,000 VND ($4-5). No taxi negotiation needed; just open the app on arrival.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Vietnam has become significantly more accessible. Citizens of many Western countries including Germany, France, the UK, Italy, Spain, and several Scandinavian nations qualify for 45-day visa-free entry under a policy in effect from August 2025 through August 2028. Check the official Vietnam immigration authority for the current list of eligible countries.

For everyone else, or for stays beyond 45 days, the e-visa is the main tool. It covers all nationalities, allows up to 90 days, can be single or multiple entry, costs $25-50 USD, and takes 3-5 business days to process. Da Nang International Airport is an approved e-visa entry point.

Vietnam does not have a dedicated digital nomad visa. The Talent Visa introduced in August 2025 targets senior academics, executives, and prominent artists, not typical remote workers. In practice, most nomads cycle on 90-day e-visas or do short border runs to Laos, Cambodia, or Thailand to reset. Extensions inside Vietnam are possible through agents but inconsistent. Always verify current rules with the official Vietnam Immigration Department or your embassy before travel.

Best Time to Visit

Da Nang splits cleanly into two seasons. The dry season runs roughly January through July; the wet and stormy season runs August through December with peak typhoon risk in October and November.

Best window for remote work: February through May. The rains from the previous wet season have ended, temperatures are comfortable (22-30C), humidity is lower, and you are ahead of the peak summer heat that pushes June and July past 35-40C. March and April are the most consistent months.

Avoid October and November if you need reliable internet and power. Floods are common, typhoons occasionally make landfall nearby, and coworking spaces sometimes lose power for hours. If your work can handle some disruption, rates drop significantly and the city is far quieter.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Long stretches of clean beach within 10 minutes of most accommodation
  • Pro: Low cost of living with a wide range of quality coworking options
  • Pro: Fast food delivery, reliable Grab, modern supermarkets
  • Pro: Safe, well-maintained city with low petty crime compared to larger Vietnamese cities
  • Pro: 90-minute drive to Hoi An for a change of scenery
  • Pro: Growing English-speaking cafe and coworking culture in the An Thuong area
  • Con: No dedicated digital nomad visa; you are relying on e-visas and border runs for long stays
  • Con: Typhoon season (October to November) brings real disruption risks
  • Con: Summer heat (June to August) can feel punishing outside air conditioning
  • Con: English is limited outside tourist areas; daily errands often require translation apps
  • Con: Prices rising faster than nearby cities as the nomad and expat base grows
  • Con: Car-dependent layout; not a city for walking between districts

Who Da Nang Is Best For

Da Nang works well for nomads who want a beach lifestyle without the premium of Bali or Phuket, and who can work reliably within a good coworking space rather than needing to move around constantly. It is a strong fit if you prioritize physical comfort (clean city, safe streets, fast food delivery, quality gyms) and you are happy living in the An Thuong or My Khe area where nearly everything you need is close.

It suits people on a 1-3 month rotation who can plan around the e-visa timeline. Families and couples benefit from the calm streets compared to Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Food lovers who want serious Vietnamese regional cooking alongside decent Western options will be satisfied.

Skip it if you need a deep, cosmopolitan city with diverse nightlife and cultural institutions: Da Nang is relaxed and relatively quiet. Also skip it if you are traveling October through November and cannot tolerate weather disruptions. And if you want to stay longer than 90 days without visa complexity, you will find the options limited until Vietnam introduces a clearer long-stay path for remote workers.

Frequently Asked Questions About Da Nang

How much does it cost to live in Da Nang as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,400 a month in Da Nang for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Da Nang?

Da Nang rates 7 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work good. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Da Nang safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Da Nang is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Da Nang a good city for digital nomads overall?

Da Nang stands out for low costs and nature among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,400 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
