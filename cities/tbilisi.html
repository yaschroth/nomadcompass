Setting Up as a Nomad in Tbilisi

Ancient wine country meets digital nomad paradise with a 365-day visa-free stay. A humid subtropical climate and strong easy visas make Tbilisi an easy base, and at about $1,600 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep cleanliness in mind.

Cost of Living in Tbilisi

Tbilisi is one of the cheapest capitals in Europe for a remote worker, though prices have climbed since 2022. Rent is your biggest variable. A furnished one-bedroom apartment in a central district like Vera or Sololaki runs roughly 1,800-2,200 GEL per month (about $650-800), while the same apartment in Saburtalo or further out drops to around 1,000-1,400 GEL ($370-520). Long leases and paying in advance get you better rates than short-term Airbnb pricing, which is inflated.

Here is a realistic monthly picture for one person:

  • Rent (1-bed, central): 1,800-2,200 GEL ($650-800)
  • Rent (1-bed, outside center): 1,000-1,400 GEL ($370-520)
  • Groceries: 500-800 GEL ($185-295)
  • Eating out: a filling local meal costs 20-35 GEL ($7-13), so budget 600-1,000 GEL ($220-370) if you eat out often
  • Coworking hot desk: around 410-525 GEL ($150-195) for an unlimited monthly membership, or 30-40 GEL ($11-15) per day pass
  • Local transport: under 60 GEL ($22), since metro and bus rides are 1 GEL each
  • Home internet: about 50 GEL ($18) for fast fiber

All in, a comfortable single person spends roughly 2,700-4,500 GEL ($1,000-1,650) per month. You can live on less in Saburtalo cooking at home, or push higher with a central apartment and frequent restaurant meals.

Where to Work

Tbilisi has a real coworking scene. Impact Hub, inside the Fabrika complex in Marjanishvili, is a large, light space popular with the creative crowd, with day passes around 40 GEL and monthly desks near 410-525 GEL. Terminal is the biggest chain in the Caucasus, with several locations across Vake, Saburtalo, and near Marjanishvili, offering day passes around 30 GEL and monthly desks near 425 GEL. Regus also operates for people who want a more corporate setup.

Cafe-work culture is strong and nobody rushes you out. Stamba and Fabrika are famous spots to plug in, and Vera is full of smaller cafes with outlets and decent WiFi. Connectivity is a genuine strength here: home fiber from Magti or Silknet delivers 100 Mbps and up for about 50 GEL a month, mobile 4G is fast and cheap, and cafe WiFi typically runs 20-40 Mbps, fine for calls but occasionally patchy for large uploads. Keep a mobile hotspot as backup for important video calls.

Getting Around

Tbilisi is cheap and easy to navigate. The metro has two lines and costs 1 GEL per ride, running from about 6am to midnight. Buses also cost 1 GEL. You pay both with a MetroMoney card, bought at any metro station for 2 GEL and topped up with cash, since foreign cards are not accepted at the gate. Marshrutkas (shared minibuses) fill the gaps and connect to other cities from the Didube terminal.

Bolt is the dominant taxi app and rides across the center usually cost 5-15 GEL. Yandex Go is a common alternative. The central districts (Vera, Sololaki, the Old Town) are very walkable, though hills, uneven pavements, and aggressive traffic take some getting used to. From the airport, bus 337 runs into the center for 1 GEL, or a Bolt costs roughly 25-40 GEL depending on demand.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Georgia is famous for one of the world's most generous entry rules: citizens of more than 90 countries, including the US, UK, EU, Canada, and Australia, can enter visa-free and stay up to 365 days on a single entry. That alone makes it a favorite base for long-stay nomads. There is an old "Remotely from Georgia" program, but it is outdated and unnecessary for most people given the visa-free stay.

Note an important recent change: from March 1, 2026, Georgia introduced new work-permit rules for foreign nationals. Remote workers earning from a foreign company or foreign clients remain exempt and do not need a permit, but working for a Georgian employer now generally requires one. Many long-stayers still reset their clock with a quick "border run" to Armenia or Turkey, though relying on this indefinitely is not guaranteed. Rules change, so always confirm your situation with official Georgian government sources before you rely on it.

Best Time to Visit

Tbilisi has a continental climate with real seasons. Summers are hot and can be uncomfortable: July and August regularly hit the high 30s Celsius (upper 90s Fahrenheit) and sometimes touch 40C, which makes midday work-from-cafe sessions with weak air conditioning rough. Winters are cold but rarely brutal, with daytime highs around 5-8C (low 40s Fahrenheit) and an atmospheric, quiet feel.

The best windows for remote work are late April to June and September to late October, when temperatures are mild, the light is beautiful, and the wine harvest brings the city to life in autumn. If you dislike heat, avoid a first stay in August.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: One of the longest visa-free stays anywhere, up to 365 days
  • Pro: Low cost of living and cheap, excellent food and wine
  • Pro: Fast, cheap internet and a genuine coworking and cafe scene
  • Pro: Simple 1% tax scheme available for freelancers via the individual entrepreneur registration
  • Con: Punishing summer heat and cold, grey winters
  • Con: Air pollution, heavy traffic, and chaotic driving
  • Con: Rising rents and inflated short-term prices for foreigners
  • Con: Shifting rules (like the 2026 work-permit change) and regional political uncertainty

Who Tbilisi Is Best For

Tbilisi is ideal for the budget-conscious, long-stay nomad who wants a genuinely affordable base with a year-long visa, great food and wine, fast internet, and an easy 1% tax setup for freelancers. It rewards people who like a bit of grit and character over polish, and who want to explore the mountains and Black Sea coast nearby. You should probably skip it if you need year-round comfortable weather, spotless modern infrastructure, or political and regulatory stability, since the heat, the pollution, and the shifting rules can wear on you over a long stay.

Frequently Asked Questions About Tbilisi

How much does it cost to live in Tbilisi as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Tbilisi is around $1,600 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Tbilisi?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Tbilisi is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Tbilisi safe for digital nomads?

Tbilisi scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Tbilisi a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Tbilisi is worth a look if you value easy visas and food. Costs sit near $1,600 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
