Setting Up as a Nomad in Erbil

A citadel mound occupied for six thousand years, in the calmest corner of Iraq. Erbil pairs culture with low costs and a semi-arid climate, all for about $900 a month. If anything, the size of the nomad scene lags.

Best Neighborhoods in Erbil

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

The Citadel and the Bazaar

$$
Ancient & Central

Six thousand years of occupation on one mound

The centre of Erbil is the citadel, a tell rising thirty metres above the plain that has been continuously inhabited for perhaps six thousand years and was inscribed by UNESCO in 2014. Below it the Qaysari bazaar and Shar Park form the city's public heart, and the ring roads radiate from there.

Best for: Anyone who wants the bazaar, the tea houses and the old city on foot
Pros
  • One of the oldest continuously occupied sites on earth
  • The bazaar and the tea houses are genuinely walkable
  • Everything radiates from here, so orientation is easy
Cons
  • Much of the citadel is still under restoration and closed
  • Traffic on the inner ring is heavy
  • Older housing with unreliable mains power

Ainkawa

$$$
International & Social

The Christian quarter, and where the internationals are

A historically Assyrian Christian district north of the centre, now the base for most of the international community, the consulates, the aid organisations and the bars and restaurants that serve them. It is the most cosmopolitan part of the city and the easiest place to arrive into.

Best for: Anyone working with the international sector, or wanting a drink
Pros
  • The best restaurants, bars and international services
  • Most consulates and organisations are based here
  • Easier to operate in with English than anywhere else
Cons
  • More expensive than the rest of the city
  • Somewhat separated from ordinary Kurdish Erbil
  • Traffic on the road into the centre is heavy

Italian Village and the compounds

$$$$
Gated & Serviced

The gated developments on the western ring

A cluster of gated residential developments west of the centre, built during the boom years with names like Italian Village, English Village and Dream City, offering villas, private generators, security and international schools behind a perimeter. They are where much of the expatriate population lives.

Best for: Families and anyone on a company housing allowance
Pros
  • The most reliable power and water in the city
  • International schools and facilities inside the compounds
  • Security and quiet
Cons
  • By far the most expensive housing in Erbil
  • Entirely car-dependent and cut off from the city
  • Little contact with Kurdish life outside the gates

Shaqlawa

$$
Mountain & Seasonal

The mountain town, about forty kilometres northeast

A resort town in the mountains northeast of Erbil, sitting at around a thousand metres with orchards, walnut trees and considerably cooler air, which is why the city empties towards it through the summer. The road continues to Rawanduz, the Gali Ali Beg gorge and the Hamilton Road.

Best for: Anyone escaping the summer heat, or heading for the mountains
Pros
  • Ten degrees cooler than Erbil in high summer
  • Orchards, walking and the road on to the gorges
  • A genuine local holiday town rather than a foreign one
Cons
  • About forty kilometres from Erbil on a busy road
  • Crowded through the summer weekends
  • Very quiet and cold in winter

Cost of Living in Erbil

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Erbil, so the $900 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 921 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $900 a month. A one-bedroom runs roughly $250 to $500, with the areas near Empire World and the newer compounds considerably higher and aimed at the international sector. Market produce is cheap and Kurdish cooking makes good use of it. Imported goods are available and reasonably priced given the trade with Turkey. Eating out is inexpensive and the kebab and the breakfast culture are both excellent. Utilities cost more than the headline suggests because private generator subscriptions are normal alongside the grid. The Iraqi dinar is used and United States dollars circulate widely.

Where to Work in Erbil

Connectivity is moderate. Erbil has fibre in the newer districts and the international compounds, with speeds workable for normal remote work, and mobile data covers the city. Power is the constraint rather than bandwidth: the grid does not supply full-time electricity and almost every building is also connected to a neighbourhood generator, which is billed separately and is simply how the city works. There is a small coworking scene. The time zone is three hours ahead of GMT, which gives a good European overlap. Anyone renting here should ask specifically about the generator subscription and its amperage, since that number determines what you can run during the daily grid outages.

Getting Around Erbil

Erbil is built in concentric rings around the citadel mound, which makes the layout unusually easy to understand and the traffic unusually circular. Taxis are cheap and plentiful and are the normal way to move, with ride-hailing apps available. The bazaar and Shar Park at the foot of the citadel are walkable. Erbil International Airport is about five kilometres west with connections to Istanbul, Dubai, Doha, Frankfurt and much of the region, and it has been the Kurdistan Region's link to the world for two decades. Anyone driving to Sulaymaniyah or Duhok should expect checkpoints along the way, which are routine, orderly and require a passport rather than causing any difficulty.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Erbil

The Kurdistan Region issues its own entry permits separately from the federal Iraqi government, and they are considerably easier to obtain. Citizens of the European Union, the United Kingdom, the United States and a number of other countries receive a visa on arrival at Erbil or Sulaymaniyah, valid for thirty days and extendable. A Kurdistan Region permit does not automatically allow travel to federal Iraq, which requires a separate federal visa. There is no remote work visa. Confirm the current position, since both regimes have changed repeatedly. Anyone intending to continue to Baghdad or the south should arrange the federal visa separately and in advance, since the regional permit does not cover that travel.

Best Time to Visit Erbil

March to May and October to November are the best windows, warm at around twenty five degrees, with the spring bringing green hills across the region that surprise people who expect only desert. Summer from June to September is severe, regularly above forty five degrees, and the city largely moves indoors or to the mountains at Shaqlawa and Rawanduz. Winter is cool and wet at around ten degrees with snow in the mountains. Newroz, the Kurdish new year on the twenty first of March, is the biggest event of the year.

Pros and Cons of Erbil

The case for Erbil is a citadel mound that has been continuously occupied for perhaps six thousand years, making it one of the oldest inhabited sites on earth and a UNESCO World Heritage site, sitting above a working bazaar in a city that is genuinely calm and inexpensive. The mountains north are beautiful. The case against is that this is Iraq, and while the Kurdistan Region has been far more stable than the rest of the country, the security situation is regional, changeable and requires current government advice rather than assumption.

Who Erbil Is For

Erbil suits people working in energy, reconstruction, diplomacy or humanitarian work, of whom there are a considerable number, and archaeologists, since the region holds an extraordinary density of sites. It suits travellers drawn to Kurdish culture and hospitality, which is genuine and much remarked on. It does not suit anyone uncomfortable with a changeable security picture, anyone who needs uninterrupted mains power, or anyone travelling in high summer. It also suits anyone drawn to Mesopotamian archaeology, since Nineveh, Nimrud and a dozen other sites are within a few hours and are slowly reopening to researchers.

Frequently Asked Questions About Erbil

How much does it cost to live in Erbil as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Erbil is around $900 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Erbil?

Erbil rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Erbil safe for digital nomads?

Erbil scores 4 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is on the limited side for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Erbil a good city for digital nomads overall?

Erbil stands out for culture and affordability among remote workers. With a typical budget near $900 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
