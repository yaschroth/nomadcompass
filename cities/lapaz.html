What La Paz Offers Digital Nomads

The world's highest capital with stunning Andean landscapes. A highland climate and strong low costs make La Paz an easy base, and at about $1,000 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the language barrier in mind.

Cost of Living in La Paz

La Paz is one of the cheapest capitals in South America for remote workers. A solo nomad can live reasonably well on $800-1,200 USD per month, or tightly on $600. Here is a realistic monthly breakdown in Bolivianos (BOB) and USD (1 USD is roughly 6.9 BOB as of mid-2026):

  • Rent, central (Sopocachi, San Miguel): 1,500-3,000 BOB ($220-440 USD) for a furnished 1-bed
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Obrajes, Miraflores): 1,000-2,000 BOB ($145-290 USD)
  • Groceries: 800-1,400 BOB ($115-200 USD)
  • Eating out (local set lunch menus, "almuerzo"): 15-30 BOB ($2-4 USD) per meal; mid-range restaurants 60-120 BOB ($9-17 USD)
  • Coworking hot desk: 500-1,000 BOB/month ($75-145 USD)
  • Local transport (teleferico + minibus): 100-200 BOB ($15-30 USD)
  • Total comfortable range: $700-1,100 USD per month excluding flights and travel

You will not find many long-stay furnished apartments listed online. Ask at your hostel or check Facebook groups like "Expats in La Paz" for short-term sublets.

Where to Work

La Paz has a small but functional coworking scene concentrated in the Sopocachi and Miraflores districts. Squemas Cowork (Av. Arce, near Miraflores) is consistently rated the best in town, with fast WiFi (reported 50+ Mbps), a friendly community, and hourly or monthly rates. Cowork Sopocachi on Calle Gregorio Reynolds offers a quieter, more local feel between two parks. EQ Spaces Cowork (Gabriel Rene Moreno) advertises 70 Mbps internet and meeting rooms.

For cafe work, HB Bronze Coffeebar in the historic Casona Patino building is the go-to specialty coffee destination with reliable WiFi. Cafe Epico in Zona Sur/Calacoto draws a health-conscious crowd and has solid connectivity. Experiment Coffee is a calm, Asian-aesthetic specialty shop popular with remote workers.

The honest truth about WiFi in Bolivia: speeds are acceptable but inconsistent. Fixed broadband in coworking spaces is generally dependable at 20-70 Mbps. Cafes can drop during peak hours. Buy a local SIM from Entel (best coverage nationally) the day you arrive and use mobile data as a backup. Tethering on a 4G Entel SIM typically gives you 10-30 Mbps and costs around $10-15 USD for a monthly data plan.

Getting Around

La Paz has one of the most unusual urban transit systems on earth: Mi Teleferico, a network of eleven cable car lines that connects neighborhoods across the dramatically uneven terrain. A single ride costs 3 BOB (under $0.50). It is fast, safe, and gives you spectacular views of the city and Illimani peak. For shorter flat distances, shared minibuses ("minis") are the cheapest option at around 2 BOB per trip, though routes can be confusing at first.

The city is not highly walkable. Steep gradients between neighborhoods and the altitude (3,625 m / 11,900 ft above sea level) make even short walks tiring until you acclimatize, which takes most people 2-4 days.

Getting to El Alto International Airport: the airport sits in El Alto at 4,061 m (13,325 ft), roughly 500 m above central La Paz. A taxi from Sopocachi takes 30-45 minutes and costs 60-100 BOB ($9-15 USD). The teleferico Red Line terminates near El Alto but does not reach the airport terminal directly; most travelers take a taxi or Uber for the final stretch. Book rides through the Uber app or use metered taxis from designated ranks, especially at night.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Bolivia offers generous visa-free entry for most Western passports. EU citizens can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. US citizens have been visa-free since December 2025, also for up to 90 days. UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders are also exempt from tourist visa requirements and receive 30-90 days on arrival depending on nationality and entry point.

There is no dedicated digital nomad visa for Bolivia as of mid-2026. Extensions beyond your initial stamp are possible at the immigration office (SEGEMIG) in La Paz, up to the 90-day annual maximum. Doing a border run to Peru or Chile resets the clock, though authorities may scrutinize frequent re-entries. For stays beyond three months, a temporary residence visa requires sponsorship or a local employer, making it impractical for most nomads.

You must complete SIGEMIG online pre-registration before arrival, and proof of yellow fever vaccination is required. Always confirm current requirements with official sources such as the Bolivian embassy or the US Embassy Bolivia page before travel.

Best Time to Visit

La Paz sits at high altitude and has a subtropical mountain climate, so temperatures stay mild year-round (average daytime highs of 14-17 degrees C / 57-63 degrees F). The key variable is rain.

  • Dry season (May to October): The best window for remote workers. Skies are clear and sunny, especially June and July. Nights get cold (below 5 degrees C / 41 degrees F), so pack layers. Power cuts during dry season are rare.
  • Wet season (November to March): Frequent afternoon downpours, occasional thunderstorms, and cloudier skies. January is the wettest month. Internet infrastructure can be affected during heavy rains.
  • Shoulder months (April and October): Rain begins tapering off or returning; milder nights than deep winter. A good compromise if dry-season crowds at Salar de Uyuni worry you.

The sweet spot for combining reliable work conditions with comfortable temperatures is May through August.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Very low cost of living, especially rent and food
  • Unique city character: high-altitude markets, cable cars, and diverse indigenous culture
  • Easy access to some of South America's most dramatic landscapes (Salar de Uyuni, Lake Titicaca, Death Road)
  • Warm and welcoming local population; Bolivians are generally friendly to foreigners
  • 90-day visa-free entry for most Western passports now includes US citizens
  • Affordable, abundant street food and fresh produce markets

Cons

  • Altitude at 3,625 m is serious: expect headaches, fatigue, and shortness of breath for the first few days, sometimes longer
  • WiFi reliability is lower than in Bogota, Mexico City, or Lima; carry a local SIM
  • English is rarely spoken outside tourist restaurants and upscale hotels; Spanish is essential
  • Air quality can be poor: high-altitude UV, vehicle emissions, and smoke from burning are all common
  • Political demonstrations and road blockades ("bloqueos") can disrupt transport with little warning
  • Nightlife and nomad networking community are thin compared to larger South American hubs

Who La Paz Is Best For

La Paz suits you if you are budget-focused and want to stretch a modest income while exploring a genuinely off-the-beaten-path South American capital. It is ideal for solo travelers who speak Spanish, are comfortable with some infrastructure roughness, and want to use the city as a base for weekend trips to extraordinary natural landscapes.

It also works well for people who find Medellin or Buenos Aires too polished or expensive, and who want to experience an unvarnished Andean city that is changing rapidly but has not yet been overrun by tourists.

You should probably skip La Paz if altitude sickness hits you hard, if reliable high-speed internet is non-negotiable for your work (video calls, large uploads), if you need a strong English-speaking social scene, or if you rely on a dense nomad community for accountability or networking. It rewards patience and adaptability more than any other city at this price point.

Frequently Asked Questions About La Paz

How much does it cost to live in La Paz as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in La Paz is around $1,000 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in La Paz?

With a WiFi score of 4/10, connectivity in La Paz is patchy for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is La Paz safe for digital nomads?

La Paz scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is La Paz a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, La Paz is worth a look if you value low costs and nature. Costs sit near $1,000 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
