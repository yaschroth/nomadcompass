Cost of Living in Mumbai

Mumbai is one of India's most expensive cities, but for international remote workers, it still undercuts most Western cities considerably. Expect real range here: your budget depends heavily on which part of the city you choose.

Rent: A furnished 1-bedroom apartment in central or South Mumbai (Colaba, Lower Parel) runs roughly INR 55,000-90,000/month ($650-1,070 USD). Shift north to Andheri, Powai, or Malad and that falls to INR 30,000-50,000/month ($360-600 USD). Short-term furnished rentals carry a premium; monthly deals through NoBroker or local agents are more realistic for stays over a month.

Groceries: Cooking at home costs around INR 5,000-8,000/month ($60-95 USD) for one person shopping at local markets. Imported goods from supermarkets like Godrej Nature's Basket or Foodhall push that noticeably higher.

Eating out: A meal at a decent mid-range restaurant costs INR 400-800 ($5-10 USD) per person. Mumbai's street food and local thali spots are exceptional value at INR 80-150 ($1-2 USD) a meal. Fine dining runs INR 2,500+ per person.

Coworking hot desk: INR 5,000-13,000/month ($60-155 USD) depending on operator and area. Day passes start around INR 300-600 ($4-7 USD).

Local transport: Local trains cost INR 5-60 per trip. Uber and Ola rides within the city average INR 150-400 ($2-5 USD). Auto-rickshaws are metered and cheap in the suburbs.

Total monthly range: A budget-conscious nomad based in the suburbs can live comfortably on INR 80,000-100,000/month ($950-1,200 USD). A more comfortable setup in a central apartment with coworking and regular dining out runs INR 130,000-180,000/month ($1,550-2,150 USD).

Where to Work

Mumbai has a solid and growing coworking ecosystem. WeWork operates across BKC, Andheri, Powai, Worli, and Goregaon, catering to professionals who want a polished, reliably-connected environment. 91Springboard covers five locations and has a strong startup community. Innov8 and Awfis are widely available, with Awfis especially strong in Andheri East near the airport.

For a more relaxed setup, Workbay in Bandra West blends cafe aesthetics with proper desk infrastructure including WiFi, meeting rooms, and tea and coffee. Silly in Khar doubles as a cafe and bar, with alfresco seating and solid connectivity during the day.

The cafe-as-office culture is alive in neighborhoods like Bandra and Colaba, and most cafes with table service will tolerate laptop workers during off-peak hours. That said, dedicated coworking is the more reliable choice: cafe WiFi in Mumbai is unpredictable, and connectivity can drop without warning.

For internet, Jio and Airtel offer the strongest 4G/5G coverage. Carrying a Jio or Airtel SIM with a hotspot plan (INR 300-500/month for generous data) as a backup is strongly recommended. Fixed fiber at home is fast where it's available, but installation and setup for short stays is rarely practical.

Getting Around

Mumbai's suburban rail network (the local trains) is the fastest way to travel north-south through the city and costs almost nothing. It is genuinely the backbone of Mumbai life. However, trains are extremely crowded during rush hours, and navigating them as a newcomer takes a few trips to feel comfortable.

Uber and Ola are available everywhere and are the practical choice for door-to-door trips, though traffic can make a 7 km ride take 45 minutes during peak hours. Auto-rickshaws operate in the suburbs north of Bandra and are metered and affordable. South Mumbai (below Bandra) is taxi and Uber territory.

Walkability is high within neighborhoods like Colaba, Bandra West, and parts of Andheri, but crossing between areas on foot is rarely practical given distances and heat.

Airport: Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM) sits in Andheri, roughly central in the metro. Metro Line 3 (Aqua Line) now connects both terminals directly to BKC, Dadar, and South Mumbai via dedicated CSMI Airport-T1 and T2 stations. An Uber to South Mumbai costs INR 400-700 ($5-8 USD) and takes 45-90 minutes depending on traffic. Andheri itself is a 10-15 minute ride.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Most nationalities (160+ countries) can apply for an Indian e-Tourist Visa online before arrival. The standard e-Tourist Visa comes in 30-day, one-year, and five-year variants. On the one-year or five-year visa, you can stay up to 90 days per visit (180 days for US and Japanese passport holders), exit, and re-enter as many times as you like within the validity period.

India does not have a dedicated digital nomad visa as of mid-2026. Remote workers typically use tourist visa rotations, which operates in a legal gray area for those actively earning income. A business e-Visa exists for those with legitimate business engagements in India.

Visa extensions inside India are possible but bureaucratically complex and rarely granted for straightforward tourism purposes. Most nomads simply exit to a neighboring country (Sri Lanka, Nepal, Thailand) and re-enter.

Always verify current visa rules at the official Indian e-Visa portal (indianvisaonline.gov.in) before travel, as policies change.

Best Time to Visit

The clear window for remote work is November through February. Temperatures sit at 18-28C (64-82F), humidity drops, and the city is at its most comfortable. This is peak season, so flights and accommodation are pricier, but the working conditions are substantially better.

March through May brings increasing heat and humidity, with temperatures pushing 34-38C (93-100F) by May. It is manageable with air conditioning but draining if you need to move around.

Monsoon season runs June through September. Mumbai receives some of the heaviest rainfall in India during July and August, with flooding that regularly disrupts public transport and can interrupt coworking commutes for days at a time. Internet reliability also dips. It is the cheapest time to visit, and the city's greenery is striking, but it is a genuinely difficult environment for sustained productive work.

October is a good shoulder option: the monsoon has largely passed, prices start to ease, and the air is relatively clear.

Pros and Cons

  • Enormous city energy with world-class food, culture, and nightlife
  • Well-developed coworking infrastructure and professional networking scene
  • Local trains make getting around fast and cheap once you learn the system
  • Excellent street food and restaurant diversity at every price point
  • English is widely spoken in business and service contexts
  • Strong airport connections to the rest of India and internationally
  • Traffic is genuinely brutal; cross-city trips can consume hours
  • Air quality is poor, especially November through January
  • Monsoon season (June-September) can disrupt work and commuting significantly
  • Rent in desirable central areas is high by Indian standards
  • No dedicated nomad visa; tourist visa rotation is a legal gray area
  • Noise and sensory overload can be hard to escape, especially in dense neighborhoods

Who Mumbai Is Best For

Mumbai suits remote workers who want a proper global city experience: professional coworking infrastructure, a strong expat and startup community, and a food and nightlife scene that can hold its own internationally. If you thrive on energy and density, Mumbai delivers in abundance. It is particularly good for those with client or business ties in India, since Mumbai is the country's financial and commercial capital.

It also works well for nomads doing medium-length stays of one to three months, visiting in the November-February window, and willing to budget carefully for the right neighborhood.

Skip it if you need tranquility to focus, struggle with pollution or heat, or are expecting the budget-ease of smaller Indian cities like Goa or Mysore. It is also a harder entry point for first-time India visitors: the city is rewarding but it does not ease you in gently.

Frequently Asked Questions About Mumbai

How much does it cost to live in Mumbai as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,800 a month in Mumbai for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Mumbai?

Mumbai rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Mumbai safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Mumbai is 5/10, workable for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Mumbai a good city for digital nomads overall?

Mumbai stands out for food and culture among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
