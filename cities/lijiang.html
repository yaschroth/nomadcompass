Setting Up as a Nomad in Lijiang

A UNESCO old town of canals, cobbled lanes, and Naxi culture in Yunnan, beneath the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. For remote workers, Lijiang leans on nature and culture, with a typical budget near $800 a month. The main trade-off is the size of the nomad scene.

Best Neighborhoods in Lijiang

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Dayan Old Town

$$
Historic, atmospheric, lively and crowded

The UNESCO heart of Lijiang

The main old town and a UNESCO World Heritage site, Dayan is a maze of cobbled lanes, canals and wooden Naxi buildings radiating from Sifang Square and the Mu Palace. It is beautiful and atmospheric, but also the busiest and most touristy corner of the city.

Best for: Culture seekers and first-time visitors who want to be in the heart of the old town
Pros
  • Unmatched old-town atmosphere with canals, bridges and Naxi architecture
  • Everything within walking distance, from teahouses to bars
  • Rich culture around Sifang Square and the Mu Palace
Cons
  • Very touristy, commercial and crowded, especially in peak season
  • Noisy bar streets and cobbles make it less practical for focused work

Shuhe Old Town

$$
Mellow, charming and slower-paced

A quieter old town with room to breathe

A smaller, more relaxed old town a few kilometres northwest of Dayan, Shuhe shares the same cobbled lanes, streams and Naxi charm but with a calmer, less frantic pace. It has become a popular base for travellers who want old-town character without Dayan's density and noise.

Best for: Nomads wanting old-town atmosphere with more calm and space to work
Pros
  • Genuine old-town character with noticeably fewer crowds than Dayan
  • Pleasant cafes and guesthouses suited to longer stays
  • Easy access to Dayan and the surrounding countryside
Cons
  • Still tourist-focused, with rising prices
  • Fewer services and less nightlife than the main old town

New Town (Gucheng District)

$
Modern, functional and everyday

Modern, practical and better value

The modern city surrounding the old towns, the new town in Gucheng district offers everyday conveniences: supermarkets, modern apartments, hospitals, banks and the main transport links. It lacks old-town charm but is cheaper, quieter for work and far more practical for a longer stay.

Best for: Longer-term nomads prioritising value, space and reliable amenities
Pros
  • Cheaper modern apartments and more space for the money
  • Full range of services, from supermarkets to hospitals
  • Closer to the railway station and transport hubs
Cons
  • Little of the historic charm that draws people to Lijiang
  • Less walkable and atmospheric than the old towns

Baisha Village

$
Rural, peaceful and traditional

The oldest Naxi settlement below the mountain

A tranquil village toward Jade Dragon Snow Mountain, Baisha is the oldest Naxi settlement in the area and once the Naxi capital. Known for its ancient frescoes and unhurried rural feel, it offers a glimpse of old Lijiang life with the mountain rising close on the horizon.

Best for: Slow travellers and mountain lovers seeking quiet and authenticity
Pros
  • Peaceful, authentic Naxi village atmosphere
  • Historic Baisha frescoes and traditional architecture
  • Closest base to Jade Dragon Snow Mountain and its trails
Cons
  • Very limited services and amenities
  • Further from the city, so you rely on transport for daily needs

Cost of Living in Lijiang

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers Lijiang, so the $800 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 921 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Lijiang is one of Yunnan's more affordable bases, and a comfortable single nomad can expect to spend roughly $700 to $1,300 per month depending on whether you live inside the atmospheric old town or in the cheaper, more practical new town. Rent is the swing factor: a guesthouse room or modern studio in Gucheng district costs far less than a boutique courtyard stay in Dayan. Naxi and Yunnan food is cheap and abundant, local transport is inexpensive, and daily costs stay low if you eat where locals eat rather than in the tourist lanes. Budget a little extra for a reliable VPN, occasional tours to the mountains, and warm clothing for the altitude and cold nights.

  • Modern one-bedroom in the new town: roughly $200 to $400 per month on a longer lease.
  • Guesthouse or courtyard room in or near the old town: often $400 to $700 for a monthly stay.
  • Local Naxi or Yunnan meal: about $2 to $5; tourist-lane restaurants cost noticeably more.
  • Coffee in a laptop-friendly cafe: around $3 to $5.
  • DiDi or taxi across the new town: usually $2 to $5 per ride.

Where to Work in Lijiang

Lijiang is scenic rather than a purpose-built remote-work hub, so temper your expectations. The biggest practical hurdle is the Great Firewall: many Western services, including Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram and much of the wider web, are blocked or unreliable, so a working VPN installed before you arrive is close to essential. Local fixed-line and mobile internet is generally decent and fast for anything hosted inside China, and most guesthouses and cafes offer usable wifi. The old town is beautiful but genuinely touristy and noisy in the day, which makes deep-focus work harder there. For steadier sessions, many nomads base themselves in the quieter new town or in Shuhe and treat the old lanes as a place to wander after hours.

  • Install and test a reputable VPN before entering China; do not rely on downloading one after you arrive.
  • Guesthouse wifi is convenient but variable; ask to test speeds before committing to a long stay.
  • Cafes around Sifang Square and Shuhe are pleasant but can be crowded and loud at peak times.
  • Buy a local SIM or eSIM with data as a backup for hotspotting.

Getting Around Lijiang

Getting around Lijiang is mostly straightforward and cheap. The UNESCO-listed Dayan old town is entirely pedestrian, a maze of cobbled lanes, canals and small bridges best explored slowly on foot, though the uneven cobbles are hard on wheels and heavy luggage. Beyond the old walls, the new town runs on affordable taxis, DiDi ride-hailing and local buses. Lijiang Sanyi Airport (LJG) connects to Kunming, Chengdu, Chongqing and other domestic hubs, while Lijiang railway station puts you on trains toward Kunming, Dali and Shangri-La. From here you can arrange tours to Jade Dragon Snow Mountain (Yulong Xueshan) and onward trips to Tiger Leaping Gorge. Remember the altitude of about 2,400 metres: pace yourself for the first days and drink plenty of water.

  • Dayan and Shuhe old towns are walkable but built on cobbles, so pack comfortable shoes.
  • DiDi and taxis are the easiest way around the new town; have your destination written in Chinese.
  • Lijiang station serves trains toward Kunming, Dali and Shangri-La; book popular routes ahead.
  • Day tours cover Jade Dragon Snow Mountain, Baisha and Tiger Leaping Gorge.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for Lijiang

China is one of the more demanding countries for entry, and Lijiang follows the national rules with no special local arrangements. Most visitors need a tourist (L) visa arranged in advance, which typically means an application with supporting documents such as itineraries and accommodation bookings, and processing through a consulate or visa centre. China has expanded visa-free and transit-exemption schemes for certain nationalities and specific entry points, and these change fairly often, so eligibility and duration vary a lot by passport and by where you land. There is no digital-nomad visa, and doing paid remote work on a tourist visa sits in a legal grey area. Treat this as an editorial overview, not legal advice, and always confirm the current rules with an official Chinese consulate before you travel.

  • The tourist (L) visa is the usual route; requirements and validity depend on your nationality.
  • Visa-free and transit-without-visa schemes exist for some passports but have strict conditions.
  • There is no dedicated remote-work or nomad visa in China.
  • Rules change frequently, so verify everything with an official consulate close to your trip.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit Lijiang

Lijiang's highland setting gives it a mild climate for its latitude, and the best windows are spring and autumn. From roughly March to May and again from September to November, the days are pleasant, the skies are often clearest, and you get the crisp mountain views of Jade Dragon Snow Mountain that draw people here. Summer, from June to August, is the rainy season: afternoon showers are common, the landscape turns lush and green, and crowds swell during Chinese holidays. Winter, from December to February, is cold and dry, with snow on the peak, bright light and noticeably thinner crowds, though nights are chilly and heating can be basic. Whenever you come, bring layers, as temperatures swing sharply between day and night at altitude.

  • Spring and autumn: mild, clearest mountain views, the most comfortable all-round conditions.
  • Summer: green and atmospheric but rainy, with peak-holiday crowds.
  • Winter: cold, dry and clear with snow on the peak and far fewer visitors.
  • Pack warm layers year-round for cold nights at 2,400 metres.

Pros and Cons of Lijiang

Lijiang is a genuinely special place to spend time, but it suits some people far better than others. On the upside, it is scenic, atmospheric and affordable, with deep Naxi and Dongba culture, dramatic mountain scenery on the doorstep, and a mild highland climate. The old towns of Dayan, Shuhe and nearby Baisha are lovely to wander, and food is cheap and distinctive. On the downside, the Great Firewall complicates everyday online work, English is limited, the tourist scene is heavy and at times commercialised, China visas are relatively hard to arrange, and the altitude affects some visitors. It is a place to weigh honestly against your work needs and travel style.

  • Pros: low cost, striking scenery, rich Naxi culture, walkable historic lanes, mild climate.
  • Cons: censorship and VPN dependence, limited English, heavy tourism, demanding visas.
  • Consider the altitude of about 2,400 metres if you are sensitive to thinner air.

Who Lijiang Is For

Lijiang rewards the culturally curious traveller who values atmosphere, scenery and slow exploration over the conveniences of a big international hub. It is a strong fit for nomads with flexible, China-friendly workflows, adventurous eaters, mountain lovers using the city as a gateway to Yulong Xueshan and Tiger Leaping Gorge, and anyone drawn to Naxi heritage and old-town life. It is a weaker fit for those who need frictionless access to Western tools and social platforms, who rely on widespread English, or who want a quiet, uncommercial base, since the old town stays busy and touristy. If you come prepared with a VPN, patience with the language barrier, and realistic expectations, Lijiang can be a memorable and affordable home for a while.

  • Great for: culture seekers, mountain lovers, adventurous eaters, slow travellers.
  • Fine for: flexible remote workers comfortable running a VPN and working around blocks.
  • Less ideal for: those needing seamless Western tools, strong English, or a quiet, uncommercial base.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lijiang

How much does it cost to live in Lijiang as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Lijiang is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Lijiang?

Lijiang rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Lijiang safe for digital nomads?

Lijiang scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Lijiang a good city for digital nomads overall?

Lijiang stands out for nature and the outdoors and culture among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
