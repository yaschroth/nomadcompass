Why Poznań Works for Remote Work

Poland's underrated tech hub with lively old town and student energy. A continental climate and strong safety make Poznań an easy base, and at about $1,700 a month it fits a range of budgets. Keep the climate in mind.

Poznań is one of Poland's more affordable major cities, though prices have risen steadily since 2023. A realistic monthly budget for a solo remote worker sits between 4,500 PLN and 7,500 PLN ($1,100-$1,850 USD), depending on your accommodation choices.

Total realistic range: 4,500-7,500 PLN/month ($1,100-$1,850 USD). Poznań is cheaper than Warsaw or Kraków for comparable quality of life, but noticeably pricier than smaller Polish cities like Lublin or Rzeszów.

Where to Work

Poznań has a solid and growing remote work infrastructure. The city centre and Jeżyce district offer the best concentration of coworking spaces and laptop-friendly cafes.

Coworking Spaces

Mundo Coworking (Garbary 35/9, Stare Miasto) combines a proper coworking setup with a cafe in a 19th-century tenement building. It draws a mix of freelancers and small startups. ClockWork Poznan Coworking (Słowackiego 55) is a more structured facility with dedicated desks, meeting rooms, and reliable fast internet. Business Link operates two Poznań locations (Nowy Rynek and Maraton) catering to corporate remote workers who want a professional atmosphere. Plus Jeden (Za Bramką 1) is a free coworking initiative funded by the city, open weekdays during business hours; it is good for occasional use but not a substitute for a dedicated membership.

Cafes

Jeżyce hosts the best cafe-work culture. SMASH is a specialty roastery with communal tables and strong WiFi. STRAG. on the same street is smaller and quieter. Minister Cafe in Srodmiescie is well known among locals for reliable WiFi and good coffee.

WiFi quality across cafes is generally good; speeds of 50-100 Mbps are common in better spots. Video calls work fine in most coworking spaces. The city-wide average is around 18-50 Mbps on home and cafe connections, which is comfortable for most remote work tasks.

Getting Around

Poznań is compact and highly walkable in the centre. Most daily errands in Stare Miasto or Jeżyce can be done on foot. The tram and bus network is extensive and runs from around 05:00 to 23:00, with limited night lines after that. A single ticket costs about 3.80 PLN ($0.95); a monthly pass runs 75-120 PLN depending on zones. The Jakdojade app handles route planning and ticket information well.

Bikes and e-scooters (Bolt) are practical from spring through early autumn. Cycling infrastructure has improved significantly in recent years, with dedicated lanes connecting most major districts.

Airport: Poznań Lawica Airport (POZ) sits 7 km west of the centre. Bus line 159 connects the airport to Poznań Główny train station in about 25 minutes, running every 15-20 minutes during the day. A taxi costs around 30-50 PLN ($7-$12). The main train station links Poznań to Warsaw (2.5-3 hours), Berlin (roughly 3 hours), and other major cities.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Poland is part of the Schengen Area. Citizens of the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and most other non-EU countries can enter visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period across the entire Schengen zone. Your stay in Poznań counts toward this shared limit, not just Poland's total.

Poland does not offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. The closest option is a Type D long-stay visa, which requires a specific purpose (study, family, business registration). Nomads wanting to stay beyond 90 days typically register as a sole proprietor in Poland, which qualifies for a two-year freelance Type D visa, but involves paying Polish taxes. EU/EEA citizens can live and work in Poland without any visa restrictions.

Always confirm current rules with the Polish consulate or an immigration lawyer before planning a long stay.

Best Time to Visit

Poznań has a moderately continental climate with real seasons. Winters (November through February) are cold, gray, and often damp, with temperatures regularly around 0°C (32°F) and some snow. Days are short and natural light is scarce, which affects motivation for many remote workers.

The best window for remote work is May through September. Temperatures in summer reach 22-26°C (72-79°F), parks are lively, and outdoor cafe terraces are open. July can be rainy, so late May, June, and September are the sweet spots: warm and dry without peak tourist crowds. Autumn (September-October) brings cooler but pleasant weather, lower prices, and a quieter atmosphere. Spring (April-May) is fresh and pleasant once the last cold snaps pass.

Avoid January through March if you are sensitive to short days, cold, and persistent overcast skies.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Significantly cheaper than Warsaw or Kraków for similar urban quality of life
  • Pro: Compact, walkable centre with strong cafe and coworking culture
  • Pro: Good train connections to Berlin and Warsaw
  • Pro: Lively university city with a young population and active food scene
  • Pro: Safer than most comparable European cities, low petty crime
  • Con: Air quality degrades noticeably in winter due to coal heating and traffic
  • Con: English proficiency is lower outside central tourist and coworking areas
  • Con: No digital nomad visa; 90-day Schengen limit applies to most non-EU citizens
  • Con: Gray, cold winters with minimal daylight can be hard for motivation
  • Con: Smaller international nomad community compared to Warsaw or Kraków

Who Poznań Is Best For

Poznań works well for remote workers from within the EU who can stay indefinitely and want an affordable, underrated Polish city with genuine local character. It suits people who enjoy a compact, walkable city where bars, cafes, and coworking spaces are within easy reach, without the tourist saturation or higher costs of Kraków.

It is a strong fit for those visiting in spring or autumn on a Schengen stay, looking for a cost-effective base with reliable infrastructure and good food. The city also appeals to anyone traveling with easy access to Germany, since Berlin is about three hours by train.

Skip Poznań if your main concern is a large English-speaking nomad community, if you need to be based in Europe long-term on a single non-EU passport without setting up a business, or if you find cold, dark winters genuinely disruptive to your work. Those who want a warmer, sunnier base with more international nomad infrastructure will be better served elsewhere.

Frequently Asked Questions About Poznań

How much does it cost to live in Poznań as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Poznań is around $1,700 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Poznań?

With a WiFi score of 7/10, connectivity in Poznań is good for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Poznań safe for digital nomads?

Poznań scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Poznań a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Poznań is worth a look if you value safety and low costs. Costs sit near $1,700 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
