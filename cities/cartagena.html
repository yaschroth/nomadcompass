Cartagena for Digital Nomads: The Honest Take

Experience colonial romance on the Caribbean with colorful streets and salsa. For remote workers, Cartagena leans on nightlife and easy visas, with a typical budget near $2,000 a month. The main trade-off is cleanliness.

Cost of Living in Cartagena

Cartagena is one of Colombia's pricier cities for nomads, mainly because the tourist economy inflates rent in the most desirable areas. Expect to spend between $1,100 and $2,400 USD per month depending on where you live and how much you run the air conditioning.

  • Rent, central areas (Walled City, Bocagrande): 2,800,000-8,000,000 COP/month ($700-2,000 USD) for a furnished one-bedroom
  • Rent, cheaper areas (Manga, Cabrero, Pie de la Popa): 1,200,000-2,800,000 COP/month ($300-700 USD)
  • Electricity (with daily A/C): 300,000-600,000 COP/month ($75-150 USD); the heat is relentless and A/C is a real cost
  • Groceries (mostly cooking at home): 400,000-700,000 COP/month ($100-175 USD)
  • Eating out (local spots and occasional mid-range restaurants): 300,000-600,000 COP/month ($75-150 USD)
  • Coworking hot-desk: 36,000-60,000 COP/day ($8-15 USD), or 360,000-720,000 COP/month ($90-180 USD) on a monthly pass
  • Local transport (taxis, Cabify, buses): 100,000-200,000 COP/month ($25-50 USD)
  • Total realistic range: $1,100-2,400 USD/month

Budget travelers who rent outside the tourist core and rely on Transcaribe buses can get by closer to $1,100. Living in Bocagrande or the Old City with A/C running all day pushes you toward $2,000-2,400.

Where to Work

Cartagena has a small but functional coworking scene. TuWork in Bocagrande is the most professionally polished option, with fiber-optic internet, private offices, showers, and a quiet outdoor terrace. It rates 4.7 out of 5 and is the best daily-rate choice. Cowork Cartagena sits in the Convention Center just outside the Walled City in La Matuna, offering rooftop harbor views and 3D printing alongside the usual desks. Virtualis Centro de Negocios in Manga is a smaller boutique option with high-speed fiber and a quieter residential atmosphere.

Cafe culture for work is real but patchy. Abaco Libros y Cafe in the Walled City is the classic pick: good espresso, books on the shelves, and a calm atmosphere that tolerates laptops. Juan Valdez branches in Bocagrande and Getsemani offer reliable A/C and consistent WiFi, which makes them practical backup spots. Avoid trying to work from the old-city tourist plazas as the ambient noise and intermittent street WiFi make it impractical.

Typical coworking WiFi runs 50-80 Mbps download, which is fine for calls and file work. Home fiber (for long-term stays) costs $20-40/month and is generally stable. The biggest WiFi risk is power outages during heavy rain, which happen occasionally in the rainy season.

Getting Around

The tourist core of Cartagena, covering the Walled City, San Diego, and Getsemani, is compact and walkable. You can cover most of it on foot in 20-30 minutes, though the heat and humidity make long walks in the middle of the day uncomfortable.

Beyond walking, Cabify and InDrive are the most reliable app-based options. Uber operates but with inconsistent availability. Local taxis do not use meters, so agree on a price before getting in; typical cross-city fares run 8,000-20,000 COP ($2-5 USD). Transcaribe, the city's bus rapid transit system, covers longer routes for around 2,000 COP ($0.50) per trip and is the cheapest way to commute between Bocagrande, Getsemani, and the center.

Airport: Rafael Nunez International Airport is roughly 10-15 minutes from the Old City by car. A taxi from the airport kiosk (use the official kiosk inside arrivals to get the correct rate) costs about 20,000-35,000 COP ($5-9 USD) to most central neighborhoods. Metrocar buses also connect the airport to downtown for around 2,000 COP if you travel light.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Citizens of the US, Canada, UK, Australia, most EU countries, Japan, and many others enter Colombia visa-free for up to 90 days. You can extend once at a Migración Colombia office for an additional 90 days, bringing your total to 180 days per calendar year. The extension fee is roughly 100,000-200,000 COP ($25-50 USD).

Colombia launched a Digital Nomad Visa (Visa de Nómada Digital) that grants up to two years of legal stay. You must prove a monthly income of at least three times Colombia's minimum wage (around $1,100 USD as of 2025), show proof of remote work for a foreign employer, carry Colombian health insurance, and submit a complete document package. Processing takes 2-4 weeks and the application is handled online through the Cancillería portal.

Always verify current requirements at colombia.travel or the official Cancillería website before applying, as rules and income thresholds change.

Best Time to Visit

Cartagena is a coastal Caribbean city that stays hot (26-33 C / 78-91 F) year-round. It has two rough seasons: a dry period from December through April and a wetter period from May through November, with October being the wettest month.

For remote work, the dry season (December through April) is the most comfortable window. You get clear skies, a reliable sea breeze, and fewer power interruptions. It is also peak tourist season, which means higher rents on short-term leases and busier neighborhoods.

The rainy season brings afternoon downpours, higher humidity, and the occasional connectivity hiccup, but it also means lower prices and thinner crowds. If you are renting for several months and can lock in a long-term rate, May through August is a reasonable compromise: the rains are lighter than September-October and short-term costs are softer.

  • Pro: Stunning colonial architecture and a genuinely beautiful historic core
  • Pro: Caribbean coast access with beaches a short taxi ride away
  • Pro: Rich food scene from cheap street ceviche to world-class fine dining
  • Pro: Walkable Old City with strong cafe culture for short work sessions
  • Pro: Shorter tourist visa for a quick trip; proper digital nomad visa available for longer stays
  • Pro: Direct flights from major US and European hubs
  • Con: Heat and humidity are intense year-round; high electricity costs if you rely on A/C
  • Con: Noticeably more expensive than Medellin or Bogota for rent in tourist areas
  • Con: Coworking scene is thin compared to Colombia's bigger cities
  • Con: Petty theft and tourist-targeting scams are common; street smarts required
  • Con: Rainy season can disrupt power and internet reliability
  • Con: Smaller expat and nomad community than Medellin, so it can feel isolating for long stays

Who Cartagena Is Best For

Cartagena works well for nomads who value atmosphere over amenity. If you want a visually striking base with Caribbean beaches nearby, excellent seafood, a colorful local culture, and do not mind paying a premium for a central apartment, you will enjoy it. It suits people on shorter stays of one to three months who want to absorb the city, and those who prioritize lifestyle over coworking infrastructure. It also works well as a wintering-in-place destination for people coming from cold climates who can handle the heat trade-off.

Skip it if you need a large, active nomad community, a deep bench of affordable coworking, or cool temperatures for focused work. It is also a poor fit for anyone on a strict shoestring budget who wants to stay in a central, comfortable neighborhood. Medellin or Bogota will serve that profile better.

Frequently Asked Questions About Cartagena

How much does it cost to live in Cartagena as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Cartagena is around $2,000 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Cartagena?

With a WiFi score of 6/10, connectivity in Cartagena is workable for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Cartagena safe for digital nomads?

Cartagena scores 5 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is workable for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Cartagena a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Cartagena is worth a look if you value nightlife and easy visas. Costs sit near $2,000 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
