Setting Up as a Nomad in Kas

A whitewashed, bougainvillea-draped harbour town on Turkey Lycian coast, a laid-back base for sea kayaking over the sunken city of Kekova, scuba diving, and hopping between quiet turquoise coves. Plan on roughly $800 a month. Its strengths are climate and nature, while the size of the nomad scene is where it scores lower.

Best Neighborhoods in Kas

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Town Center & Harbour

$$
charming, touristic, walkable

Whitewashed lanes, the marina, and the Lycian rock tomb overhead

The historic heart of Kas, all whitewashed houses, bougainvillea-draped stairs, and a working harbour lined with cafes and dive shops. The ancient Lycian rock tomb sits carved into the hillside right above the streets. Walkable, lively, and the base for most restaurants, boat trips, and daily errands, though busiest and priciest in peak summer.

Best for: first-time visitors, cafe workers, harbour and old-town lovers
✓ Pros

  • Best restaurant and nightlife concentration
  • Lycian rock tomb and old-town character on your doorstep
✗ Cons
  • Steep, hilly cobbled streets
  • Loud and crowded in July and August

Cukurbag Peninsula

$$
residential, quiet, scenic

A quieter, villa-lined peninsula with sea views on both sides

A narrow peninsula stretching west of the harbour, lined with villas, small apartment blocks, and boutique hotels overlooking the sea on either side. Popular with longer-stay visitors and those wanting a quieter base with a car or scooter, a short ride from the town center. Fewer shops and restaurants, more residential calm and sunset views.

Best for: longer stays, those with a scooter or car, sunset seekers
✓ Pros
  • Quieter than the harbour center
  • Sea views from many apartments
  • Good value long-term rentals
✗ Cons
  • Need transport for daily errands and dining
  • Fewer walkable amenities

Kucuk Cakil & Buyuk Cakil Beach Area

$$
beachy, relaxed, promenade-style

Kas's pebble-beach strip just east of the harbour

The pebble beaches of Kucuk Cakil (small) and Buyuk Cakil (big) sit a short walk east of the harbour, backed by a promenade of beach clubs, cafes, and casual restaurants. A good base for swimmers who want the sea within steps, with easier, flatter walking than the old town's slopes, and still close enough to reach the center on foot.

Best for: swimmers, beach-day regulars, easy walking access

✗ Cons

Kalkan

$$$
upscale, polished, boutique

A polished neighboring resort town, 25-30 minutes down the coast

A hillside harbour town about 25-30 minutes from Kas by dolmus or car, Kalkan is more upscale and boutique-hotel driven, with white Ottoman-Greek style buildings, fine-dining terraces, and a glossier international crowd. Many nomads base in Kas but visit Kalkan for a change of pace, a nicer dinner out, or to compare a quieter, pricier alternative base.

Cost of Living in Kas

Kas runs on the Turkish lira (TRY), and while Turkey overall remains one of the cheaper countries for remote workers, Kas is priced above inland Anatolian towns because it is a well-established Turquoise Coast resort. A studio or one-bedroom apartment in the town center or on the Cukurbag peninsula runs roughly 15,000-28,000 TRY a month in the summer season, dropping by a third or more if you sign a longer winter lease. A simple lunch of grilled fish or a pide runs 150-300 TRY, a coffee at a harbour cafe around 80-150 TRY, and a beer 150-250 TRY, all noticeably higher than Fethiye or Antalya city because Kas leans on tourist footfall. Groceries from the Friday pazar (market) and local markets are still good value: vegetables, olives, cheese, and bread cost a fraction of Western Europe. Budget nomads can live comfortably on 25,000-35,000 TRY a month outside July and August; during peak summer, expect costs to climb 30-50% as short-term rental demand spikes and restaurants raise prices for the crowds arriving by yacht and gulet.

Where to Work in Kas

Kas has no dedicated coworking space as of this writing, so most remote workers piece together a routine from harbour-view cafes with decent wifi, a handful of boutique hotel lobbies, and their own rented apartment. Cafes around the old harbour and the main square (Cumhuriyet Meydani) are the default daytime option, several with outdoor seating and usable, if not fast, connections. Wifi in Kas is generally mid-tier for Turkey standards: fine for email, video calls, and browsing, but liable to hiccup during peak summer when the town's population multiplies and everyone is streaming. If your work depends on rock-solid bandwidth, carry a local SIM (Turkcell or Vodafone Turkey both have reasonable 4G coverage in and around town) as backup, and test any apartment's connection before committing to a long stay. The upside is a relaxed, low-distraction pace: mornings on a cafe terrace overlooking the marina, afternoons free for a swim or dive, which is exactly the trade nomads come to Kas for.

Getting Around Kas

Kas itself is compact and steeply hilly, built on a slope down to the harbour, so most daily errands are done on foot, with sturdy shoes recommended for the cobbled inclines. For getting to Kas from outside, the nearest airports are Dalaman (about 2-2.5 hours by road) and Antalya (about 3-3.5 hours), both served by direct and connecting international flights; from either, you continue by intercity coach or a private transfer. Regular long-distance buses connect Kas to Antalya and Fethiye (roughly 2-3 hours each), run by companies like Kamil Koc and Pamukkale, with a change often required at Antalya's main terminal for onward international coach links. Locally, dolmus minibuses shuttle along the coast to Kalkan, Kaputas beach, and nearby villages frequently and cheaply. Taxis are available but pricier than dolmus. Renting a scooter or small car is popular for exploring Kalkan, Kaputas, and the Lycian Way, since Kas's own bus and ferry links to more remote coves are limited outside peak season, and having your own wheels opens up the coast considerably.

Visas for Kas

Most Western nomads (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia and many others) can enter Turkey visa-free or via the e-Visa system, typically granting a 90-day stay within any 180-day period; check your specific nationality on the official e-Visa portal (evisa.gov.tr) before booking, as requirements vary. This 90/180 rule is the key constraint for longer stays: once your allotted days are used, you must leave the Schengen-style rolling window before re-entering. For nomads wanting to stay beyond 90 days, Turkey's short-term residence permit (ikamet) is a realistic option and reasonably accessible by regional standards, requiring proof of address (a rental contract), health insurance, and a visit to the local immigration office, though Kas's small-town bureaucracy can mean trips to Antalya or Fethiye for appointments and paperwork. Always verify current rules directly with Turkish immigration authorities or your embassy, as e-Visa and residence permit policies have shifted in recent years.

Best Time to Visit Kas

Kas has a genuine warm season stretching from April through October, with the sweet spot for most nomads being May-June and September-October: warm enough to swim, dive, and kayak the Kekova coves, without the peak crowds, prices, or heat of July-August, when Mediterranean sun and tourist volume both spike hard. Winter (December-February) is a different town altogether: quiet, cooler and rainier, with many restaurants, dive shops, and boat tours shutting down or scaling back for the off-season, so it suits nomads who want solitude and low cost over lively social life or open dive centers. If diving, sea kayaking over the sunken ruins near Kekova, or paragliding off the nearby ridges are priorities, plan around the April-October window when operators are running full schedules.

Pros and Cons of Kas

Pros: a genuinely beautiful, whitewashed harbour town with bougainvillea-lined streets and a Lycian rock tomb carved into the hillside right above town; excellent diving, sea kayaking over the sunken city near Kekova, and paragliding on the doorstep; a relaxed, slower pace than the bigger Turkish resort towns; a growing and increasingly visible slow-travel and remote-work community; and genuinely good, affordable Turkish and seafood dining. Cons: extremely seasonal, with a lively, expensive summer giving way to a quiet winter where many businesses close; steep, hilly streets that are a real workout (and a challenge with luggage or mobility issues); wifi that is workable but not fast or fully reliable, especially in peak season; no dedicated coworking space; and a town that can feel overrun with day-trippers and yacht tourists in July and August, pushing prices and crowds up sharply.

Who Kas Is For

Kas suits nomads who want a coastal, outdoors-oriented lifestyle base built around diving, sea kayaking, swimming, and hiking the Lycian Way, and who are comfortable working from cafes rather than a formal coworking desk. It is a strong fit for those who can flex their travel dates around the shoulder seasons (spring and autumn) to get the best balance of good weather, lower prices, and thinner crowds, and who don't mind steep walks and modest wifi in exchange for genuinely striking scenery. It is less suited to nomads who need fast, guaranteed connectivity for demanding video calls, who want a large always-on international coworking scene, or who are visiting in deep winter expecting a lively social calendar, since much of the town's rhythm shuts down between November and March.

Frequently Asked Questions About Kas

How much does it cost to live in Kas as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Kas is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Kas?

Kas rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Kas safe for digital nomads?

Kas scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Kas a good city for digital nomads overall?

Kas stands out for great weather and nature and the outdoors among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
