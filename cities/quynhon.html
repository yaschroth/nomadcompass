Setting Up as a Nomad in Quy Nhon

Vietnam's next big thing, pristine beaches, seafood, and zero tourists. For remote workers, Quy Nhon leans on low costs and nature, with a typical budget near $800 a month. The main trade-off is the language barrier.

Cost of Living in Quy Nhon

Quy Nhon sits comfortably below major Vietnamese cities in price. A realistic monthly budget for a solo remote worker runs from about 10,000,000-17,000,000 VND ($400-680 USD), depending on how you live.

  • Rent, central area (Tran Phu, Xuan Dieu beachfront strip): 9,500,000-11,500,000 VND ($380-460) for a 1-bedroom apartment
  • Rent, cheaper residential areas (Le Loi, Nguyen Van Cu): 5,500,000-7,500,000 VND ($220-300) for a 1-bedroom
  • Groceries (self-catering light): 1,500,000-2,500,000 VND ($60-100)
  • Eating out: Local pho or banh xeo runs 25,000-60,000 VND ($1-2.50); a sit-down meal at a mid-range spot costs 80,000-150,000 VND ($3-6); a Western dinner with drinks at Sisters Tavern or the sports bar can reach 300,000-500,000 VND ($12-20) per person
  • Coworking hot-desk: Quy Nhon has very few dedicated coworking spaces; where available, expect 1,500,000-2,500,000 VND ($60-100) per month or 50,000-80,000 VND ($2-3) per day at a work-friendly cafe
  • Local transport (ride-share/motorbike hire): 500,000-1,000,000 VND ($20-40) monthly if you rent a motorbike; Grab rides in town run 20,000-40,000 VND ($1-1.60) each
  • Internet (home fibre): Typically 200,000-300,000 VND ($8-12) per month; speeds of 50-150 Mbps are common
  • Utilities (electricity, water, for 85sqm): Around 2,650,000 VND ($106) in the summer cooling months, less in cooler weather

Total realistic range: 10,000,000-17,000,000 VND ($400-680) per month covers comfortable living. You can go lower by eating local every meal and living outside the tourist strip.

Where to Work

Dedicated coworking spaces in Quy Nhon are limited compared to Da Nang or Ho Chi Minh City. The Binh Dinh Innovation Hub (bihub.vn) serves the local startup scene but is geared toward Vietnamese tech firms rather than passing nomads. In practice, most remote workers rely on the city's strong cafe culture.

ADIUVAT Coffee Roasters on Nguyen Hue Street is the go-to for serious laptop work: air-conditioned, reliable WiFi, own-roasted specialty coffee, and a spacious balcony. Retro Coffee Roastery on Tran Cao Van is a solid second option, open from 6am, with WiFi, AC, and a two-story layout that fills up but rarely feels noisy. Waseda Working Space Coffee in Nhan Binh leans into the study-friendly angle with a Japanese-minimalist interior and deliberately workable tables.

Home fibre internet is fast and cheap. Most apartments on short-term rental platforms come pre-connected at 50-150 Mbps. Cafe WiFi is generally good enough for video calls, though peak afternoon hours can slow things down at busier spots on the beachfront strip. A Vietnamese SIM card from Viettel or Vietnamobile gives you a solid 4G backup for under 200,000 VND ($8) per month.

Getting Around

The city center is compact, and many daily errands in the Tran Phu or Xuan Dieu zone are walkable in good weather. Outside the central strip you need wheels. Renting a semi-automatic motorbike costs about 800,000-1,500,000 VND ($32-60) per month from local shops, and it is by far the most practical way to reach beaches, markets, and restaurants scattered across town.

Grab (the regional ride-share app) works reliably in Quy Nhon for on-demand trips. Taxis from reputable firms like Mai Linh are also available and metered.

Phu Cat Airport is 31km north of the city. VietJet, Bamboo Airways, and Vietnam Airlines fly direct from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City multiple times daily. A Grab car or taxi from the airport costs 200,000-380,000 VND ($8-15). A cheaper shared shuttle bus runs for around 50,000 VND ($2) but schedules can be unreliable. Dieu Tri railway station is 10km west of center and sits on the north-south Reunification Express, connecting Quy Nhon to Da Nang (roughly 3.5 hours), Nha Trang (about 2.5 hours), and Ho Chi Minh City (overnight).

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Vietnam does not offer a dedicated digital nomad or remote work visa as of mid-2026. Your realistic options are:

  • Visa-free entry: Citizens of the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, and about 25 other European countries get 45 visa-free days (valid until August 2028). ASEAN nationals get 30 days. US, Canadian, and Australian passport holders are not on the visa-free list as of mid-2026.
  • E-Visa: Available to citizens of all countries. Single-entry costs $25 USD; multiple-entry costs $50 USD. Valid for up to 90 days. Applied fully online through the official Vietnam government portal.
  • Extensions and longer stays: The e-Visa's 90-day window is the practical ceiling for most nomads without a work or business visa. Doing a border run to Cambodia or Laos then re-entering on a fresh e-Visa is common but not guaranteed; Vietnam occasionally tightens re-entry at land borders. A Talent Visa launched in August 2025 targets skilled professionals but requires employer sponsorship.

Always confirm current rules with the official Vietnam Immigration Department website or a licensed visa agent before booking.

General information, not legal advice. Visa rules change often and turn on your nationality, income and reason for staying. Confirm the current requirements with the country’s immigration authority or a qualified immigration adviser before you book anything.

Best Time to Visit

Quy Nhon has two distinct seasons. The dry season runs March through September, with temperatures around 28-34 degrees Celsius (82-93F) and clear skies. February through early June is the sweet spot: temperatures are mild, sea conditions are calm, and rain is rare. July and August are hot and humid but still dry enough for beach days.

October and November are the worst months. The central coast receives the bulk of its annual rainfall then, often in concentrated heavy bursts. Flooding can affect low-lying streets, and some days are simply lost to rain. Typhoon risk is real in October. December through January brings cooler, overcast days (22-25C) with occasional grey spells, though rain is less severe than the October peak.

For remote work, the February-to-May window is ideal: stable electricity, no flooding risk, beach access after work, and a quieter city before Vietnamese summer domestic tourism picks up in June and July.

Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Genuinely affordable, including for beachfront living that would cost 5-10 times more in Thailand or the Philippines
  • Pro: Beautiful and uncrowded beaches within easy motorbike or walking distance of the center
  • Pro: Outstanding local seafood at low prices: fresh oysters, grilled prawns, banh xeo
  • Pro: Fast, cheap home internet and reliable cafe WiFi at the main work-friendly spots
  • Pro: Compact, low-stress city with minimal traffic chaos compared to Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City
  • Pro: Good air quality by Vietnamese standards
  • Con: Very few dedicated coworking spaces; cafe-working is the norm
  • Con: Small English-speaking expat community; integration with locals requires Vietnamese or patience
  • Con: Nightlife is limited to a handful of bars; not a social hub for nomads
  • Con: October and November weather can seriously disrupt work and travel
  • Con: No direct international flights; you connect through Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City
  • Con: Fewer cafes and restaurants than larger Vietnamese nomad hubs, so options feel repetitive after a few weeks

Who Quy Nhon Is Best For

Quy Nhon suits you if you want a slower pace, lower costs, and genuine beach access without the party-town energy of Mui Ne or the tourist saturation of Hoi An. It works well for independent workers who are comfortable relying on cafes for desk time, who enjoy cooking or eating local food daily, and who want to cover ground on a motorbike rather than rely on rideshares for every trip.

It is also a good fit for someone already traveling Vietnam who wants a few weeks off the main tourist circuit. The train from Da Nang or Nha Trang makes it easy to include without flying.

Skip Quy Nhon if you need a reliable coworking community with events and networking, depend on walking everywhere, require a wide range of international restaurants, or plan to arrive in October or November. If you want Vietnam's best nomad infrastructure and social scene, Da Nang or Ho Chi Minh City will serve you better.

Frequently Asked Questions About Quy Nhon

How much does it cost to live in Quy Nhon as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Quy Nhon is around $800 a month: a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. Live a little outside the center or eat local to spend less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Quy Nhon?

With a WiFi score of 4/10, connectivity in Quy Nhon is patchy for calls and focused work. A prepaid local SIM keeps you online when you are away from the apartment.

Is Quy Nhon safe for digital nomads?

Quy Nhon scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travel and long stays. Use normal precautions at night and with valuables, and choose well-reviewed areas.

Is Quy Nhon a good city for digital nomads overall?

For nomads, Quy Nhon is worth a look if you value low costs and nature. Costs sit near $800 a month and the community keeps growing, though, like anywhere, it will not match every checklist.
