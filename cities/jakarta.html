Cost of Living in Jakarta

Jakarta is more expensive than Indonesia's nomad favourites like Bali or Yogyakarta, but still affordable by global standards. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $1,000 to $1,600 a month. A furnished apartment in a central business district such as Sudirman, Kuningan or the leafier Kemang costs about $400 to $800, with serviced options higher. Everyday life is cheap: a plate of nasi goreng from a warung costs a dollar or two, and Grab and Gojek rides across town are inexpensive. These are our editorial estimates in rupiah, and where you land in the range depends heavily on how central and modern your building is.

  • Furnished apartment, central: roughly $400 to $800 a month
  • Warung meal (nasi goreng, mie): roughly $1.50 to $3
  • Grab or Gojek across town: roughly $2 to $5
  • Monthly coworking hot desk: roughly $80 to $150

Where to Work in Jakarta

As Indonesia's economic engine, Jakarta has strong work infrastructure. Fibre internet in modern apartments and condos is fast and reliable, and the city has a deep bench of coworking spaces, from international chains to homegrown brands, concentrated in the central business districts and Kemang. Cafe culture is huge, with countless laptop-friendly specialty coffee shops. A Telkomsel, XL or Indosat SIM gives cheap, fast mobile data. The real productivity killer is traffic, not connectivity, so most nomads pick a home and coworking spot close together to avoid losing hours in gridlock, and lean on the growing MRT where possible.

Getting Around Jakarta

Jakarta's traffic is legendary, and beating it is the key to daily life here. The clean, modern MRT runs a single north-south line through the central corridor with more under construction, and the TransJakarta bus rapid transit network and commuter rail fill gaps. For everything else, Grab and Gojek are indispensable, and their motorbike taxis weave through jams far faster than cars. The city is not walkable in the pedestrian sense, so plan around the transit spine. Soekarno-Hatta International Airport connects Jakarta to the region and the world, reached by an airport rail link or a long, traffic-dependent drive.

Visas for Jakarta

Most visitors enter Indonesia on a visa on arrival, granting 30 days and extendable once for another 30, or apply for a B211 social visa for longer stays. Indonesia has also introduced a remote-worker permit, the E33G KITAS, aimed at digital nomads who can show sufficient foreign income, allowing a longer, renewable stay. Rules and the new visa's terms are evolving, so confirm the current options and income requirements on official Indonesian immigration channels before relying on them. Overstaying carries daily fines, and agents in Bali and Jakarta commonly handle extensions for a fee if you prefer not to queue.

Best Time to Visit Jakarta

Jakarta is hot and humid all year, sitting just south of the equator, so the calendar is defined by rain rather than temperature. The dry season from roughly May to September brings the most reliable weather and the fewest disruptions. The wet season from November to March delivers heavy tropical downpours and, in bad years, serious flooding that can snarl the city, so it pays to choose accommodation away from flood-prone low areas. Temperatures stay in the low thirties Celsius throughout, with high humidity, so light clothing and air conditioning are year-round essentials whenever you visit.

Pros and Cons of Jakarta

Jakarta is a base for work and access rather than postcard charm.

  • Pros: a genuine economic and startup hub with strong coworking and fast internet, excellent and cheap food, very affordable Grab and Gojek transport, a growing MRT, and unmatched connectivity to the rest of Indonesia and Asia.
  • Cons: notorious traffic, serious air pollution, wet-season flooding, relentless heat and humidity, sprawl with little walkability, and a city that prioritises function over beauty. Most leisure-focused nomads still prefer Bali or Yogyakarta.

Who Jakarta Is For

Jakarta suits the nomad who needs a big-city base with real business infrastructure, whether that is meetings, clients, startup connections or a launchpad into the rest of Indonesia. Foodies who thrive on chaotic, diverse street eating and city people who like malls, nightlife and energy over nature will find plenty here. It rewards those who set up home and work close together and use Grab to skip the traffic. It is a poor fit for anyone chasing beaches, calm, clean air or a walkable town, who should look to Bali, Yogyakarta or a smaller Indonesian city instead. Base yourself centrally, near the MRT, and let the megacity's convenience outweigh its chaos.

Frequently Asked Questions About Jakarta

How much does it cost to live in Jakarta as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Jakarta is around

,300 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Jakarta?

Jakarta rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Jakarta safe for digital nomads?

Jakarta scores 6 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is average for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Jakarta a good city for digital nomads overall?

Jakarta stands out for its food scene and great weather among remote workers. With a typical budget near ,300 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
