Best Neighborhoods in English Harbour

Where to base yourself for the perfect nomad experience

Nelson's Dockyard and English Harbour

$$$
Historic & Nautical

The only working Georgian dockyard left in the world

The restored naval yard where Nelson was stationed in the 1780s, now a UNESCO World Heritage site with its stone buildings holding hotels, restaurants and a marina full of sailing yachts. The harbour itself is one of the safest anchorages in the eastern Caribbean, which is why the yard was built here.

Best for: Anyone drawn to sailing, or who wants to live inside the history
Pros
  • A UNESCO site you can walk through daily
  • Restaurants, a marina and provisioning all within the yard
  • The sailing scene runs year round, not only in season
Cons
  • Prices set by the yachting market rather than the island
  • Genuinely quiet from June to October
  • Very limited long-term residential stock

Falmouth Harbour

$$$
Marina & Social

The bigger bay next door, where the superyachts moor

The wider harbour a few minutes west, with two large marinas that take the largest yachts in the Caribbean and a strip of bars, chandlers and restaurants serving their crews. It is busier and more commercial than English Harbour and carries most of the area's ordinary daily life.

Best for: Anyone who wants restaurants and provisioning within walking distance
Pros
  • The best concentration of shops and restaurants in the south
  • Marina wifi and services are unusually good for the island
  • Walkable to English Harbour along the road
Cons
  • Yacht crew prices on almost everything
  • Half empty in the low season
  • No real beach on the harbour itself

Shirley Heights

$$$
Elevated & Scenic

The ridge above the harbours with the Sunday party

The fortified ridge on the headland east of English Harbour, with the ruins of the eighteenth century garrison and a lookout that holds the island's best known Sunday evening barbecue and steel band. A handful of houses sit along the ridge road with views over both harbours.

Best for: Anyone who wants a view and does not mind driving for everything
Pros
  • The best views on the island, over both harbours to Guadeloupe
  • Cooler than sea level with a steady breeze
  • The Sunday lookout party is a genuine island institution
Cons
  • Everything requires a car
  • Almost no rental stock
  • Sunday evenings bring traffic and crowds past your door

St John's

$$
Urban & Commercial

The capital, half an hour north across the island

Antigua's capital and only real town, about thirteen kilometres north with the cruise piers at Heritage Quay, the public market, the cathedral and the island's supermarkets and government offices. Anyone based in the south comes here for anything the harbours cannot supply.

Best for: Anyone who needs a real town rather than a marina village
Pros
  • The island's supermarkets, banks and offices are all here
  • Cheaper rents than the yachting south
  • A proper Saturday market rather than a provisioning shop
Cons
  • Thirteen kilometres from English Harbour over the hills
  • Crowded and hot when several cruise ships are in
  • Little of the sailing atmosphere that draws people south

Cost of Living in English Harbour

Where this figure comes from. No cost survey covers English Harbour, so the $2,000 a month above is our own estimate rather than a measurement. On 330 of our 951 city guides the figure is built from a published Numbeo basket, a one-bedroom rent in the centre plus one person's monthly costs without rent, converted to USD; this city is not one of them. Treat it as a starting point and check current rental listings before you budget against it. How we source everything.

Budget around $2,000 a month as a solo remote worker at English Harbour, which is expensive for the region and reflects a yachting economy. A one-bedroom place runs roughly $800 to $1,300, with anything on the water at the top of that and the villages inland considerably cheaper. Antigua imports nearly all of its food and the supermarket bill is the line most people underestimate. Eating at a local place in Falmouth or Liberta costs a fraction of the dockyard restaurants. The East Caribbean dollar is pegged at 2.7 to the dollar. This is our own estimate rather than measured data.

Where to Work in English Harbour

Antigua's connectivity is reasonable and better around English Harbour than the island average, because the yachting industry needs it: fibre reaches the dockyard area and Falmouth at workable speeds and mobile data covers the island. There is a small amount of coworking in St John's and around the harbour, some of it created for holders of the Nomad Digital Residence visa. Power cuts happen. English is the only language required, and the international sailing crowd means the working population here is unusually cosmopolitan for an island of ninety thousand. Anyone whose work involves the marine industry will find a genuine professional network here, in a way that is true of very few small islands.

Getting Around English Harbour

English Harbour and Falmouth Harbour sit next to each other at the southern end of Antigua and both are walkable, with Nelson's Dockyard, the restaurants and the marinas within twenty minutes of each other. Minibuses run to St John's, the capital, in about forty minutes for very little. Driving is on the left and a local permit is required. V. C. Bird International is the best-connected airport in the Eastern Caribbean, with direct flights to London, New York, Miami and Toronto, which is a genuine advantage of basing here. The walk up to Shirley Heights from the dockyard takes about forty minutes and is the standard way to reach the Sunday evening party.

Sorting transport? Book an airport transfer, or compare car rental on Auto Europe and QEEQ.

Visas for English Harbour

Antigua and Barbuda's Nomad Digital Residence is a two-year remote work visa for people employed or self-employed outside the country who can show an annual income of around $50,000, with a fee of roughly $1,500 for an individual and more for a family. Two years is among the longest terms offered anywhere and the application is handled online. Short visits are visa-free for most Western nationalities for up to three months. The country also runs a citizenship by investment programme, which is a separate matter. Anyone applying should know that the two years run from approval rather than arrival, so it is worth timing the application to the intended stay.

Best Time to Visit English Harbour

December to April is the dry season and the sailing season, with steady trade winds, daytime temperatures around twenty-eight and the harbour full. Antigua Sailing Week at the end of April is the largest regatta in the Caribbean and fills every bed in the south of the island. May to November is hotter and wetter and the hurricane season runs through it; Antigua was spared in 2017 while Barbuda, its sister island, was rendered uninhabitable by Hurricane Irma and evacuated entirely. Barbuda is reachable by ferry or a short flight and is slowly being rebuilt, with its frigate bird colony among the largest in the world.

Pros and Cons of English Harbour

Nelson's Dockyard is the only continuously working Georgian naval dockyard in the world, a UNESCO World Heritage site that is still a marina rather than a museum, and the Sunday sunset party at Shirley Heights above it has run for decades. Antigua has a two-year nomad visa, the region's best flight connections and a claimed beach for every day of the year. Against that, it is expensive, the nomad community is small and heavily overlapping with the sailing one, and the island empties considerably outside the season. The dockyard's Georgian buildings are also still in commercial use as hotels, offices and marina facilities, which is why it feels like a place rather than an exhibit.

Who English Harbour Is For

English Harbour suits a sailor, or someone who wants to be around sailing, with a two-year visa and the budget for a yachting town. The international crowd here is real and easy to join, which is rare on a small island. It works for a full year rather than a season. It is a poor fit for anyone on a tight budget or anyone wanting a city, since St John's is forty minutes away and modest. Anyone arriving in Sailing Week should book months ahead. It also suits anyone with a family, since Antigua's schools and medical facilities are better than most islands of comparable size can offer.

Frequently Asked Questions About English Harbour

How much does it cost to live in English Harbour as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in English Harbour is around $2,000 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in English Harbour?

English Harbour rates 6 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is English Harbour safe for digital nomads?

English Harbour scores 8 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is excellent for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is English Harbour a good city for digital nomads overall?

English Harbour stands out for widely spoken English and clean air among remote workers. With a typical budget near $2,000 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
