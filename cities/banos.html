Setting Up as a Nomad in Banos

An adventure town cradled in the Andes below an active volcano, all waterfalls, hot springs, and canyon swings. What pulls nomads here is nature, backed by low costs and clean air. Budget around $800 a month. The language barrier is the weak spot.

Cost of Living in Banos

Banos, formally Banos de Agua Santa, is very affordable, helped by Ecuador using the US dollar, which removes any exchange-rate worry for those earning abroad. A comfortable single-person budget runs roughly $650 to $1,050 a month. A furnished apartment or long-stay room in or near the small centre costs about $250 to $500, and hearty Ecuadorian almuerzos cost just a few dollars. These are our editorial estimates in US dollars, the actual local currency. Beyond rent and food, the main costs are the town's many adventure activities, from rafting to canyoning, which stay cheap by global standards.

  • Furnished room or apartment near the centre: roughly $250 to $500 a month
  • Set-lunch almuerzo: roughly $3 to $5
  • Entry to the thermal hot springs: a couple of dollars
  • Bus to Quito (about three hours): a few dollars

Where to Work in Banos

Banos is an adventure and backpacker town rather than a remote-work hub, so its infrastructure is basic. Internet is the main limitation: fibre and 4G reach the town but speeds are modest and can drop, and the deep Andean valley setting does not help, so test any accommodation's connection carefully before relying on it for calls and keep a Claro or Movistar SIM as backup. Dedicated coworking does not really exist; a handful of traveller cafes serve as informal work spots. Banos suits someone with light connectivity needs who wants an active, nature-filled base rather than a place for constant meetings.

Getting Around Banos

Banos is small and flat in its centre, and easily explored on foot, with adventure agencies, cafes and the thermal baths all close together. The classic way to see the surrounding gorge and its string of waterfalls, the Ruta de las Cascadas, is by rented bike or the open chiva buses that run the route to the mighty Pailon del Diablo falls. Frequent buses connect Banos to Quito, about three hours away, and to Riobamba and the wider Andes. The town sits below the active Tungurahua volcano, whose activity is monitored and occasionally affects local excursions.

Visas for Banos

Most visitors enter Ecuador visa-free as tourists for up to 90 days per year, which can sometimes be extended for a further period through the immigration authorities. For a longer stay, Ecuador offers a range of residence options and has introduced a digital nomad visa for remote workers who can show sufficient foreign income, granting a longer, renewable stay. Rules and requirements can change, so confirm the current terms with official Ecuadorian sources before relying on them, and keep track of your permitted days and any extension process to avoid overstaying your welcome.

Best Time to Visit Banos

Banos sits in a lush Andean valley at around 1,800 metres, giving it a mild, spring-like climate year-round rather than sharp seasons, though it is a notably rainy, green place. Drier, clearer spells tend to fall around the second half of the year, roughly September to January, which are good windows for the waterfalls and outdoor activities, but a shower can arrive in any month given the cloud-forest setting. The wetter periods keep everything emerald but can muddy trails and cloud the volcano. Pack a light rain jacket whenever you visit and expect changeable mountain weather.

Pros and Cons of Banos

Banos is a nature-and-adventure paradise with the trade-offs of a small, rainy mountain town.

  • Pros: a spectacular Andean setting of waterfalls, gorges and hot springs, an endless menu of adventure activities from rafting to canyoning and the famous canyon swings, very low costs, the convenience of the US dollar, clean air, and a lively traveller buzz.
  • Cons: weak and inconsistent internet, a transient backpacker crowd rather than a settled nomad community, frequent rain, limited English, a small range of amenities, and the background reality of an active volcano above the town.

Who Banos Is For

Banos is for the adventure-and-nature nomad who wants to fill their free time with waterfalls, biking, rafting and hot springs, and who can work comfortably with light connectivity. Thrill-seekers, hikers and anyone craving an active, outdoorsy base on a budget will love it, and the dollar economy makes budgeting effortless for those earning abroad. It works best as an energetic, scenic base for shorter stays or flexible, low-bandwidth work rather than a long-term hub, and rewards a little Spanish. It is a poor fit for those who need fast, reliable internet or a stable community. Come for the adventure, and let the Andes provide the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions About Banos

How much does it cost to live in Banos as a digital nomad?

A comfortable nomad budget in Banos is around $800 a month, covering a furnished rental, food, coworking, and getting around. You can spend less by living outside the center and eating local, or more in the most popular neighborhoods.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Banos?

Banos rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so connectivity is average for video calls and everyday remote work. A local SIM with a data plan is a smart backup for cafes, coworking, and travel days.

Is Banos safe for digital nomads?

Banos scores 7 out of 10 for safety in our ratings, which is good for solo travelers and long stays. Use the usual precautions with valuables and at night, and favor well-reviewed neighborhoods when you choose where to stay.

Is Banos a good city for digital nomads overall?

Banos stands out for nature and the outdoors and affordability among remote workers. With a typical budget near $800 a month and a growing nomad scene, it is worth a look if those priorities match what you want from a base.
