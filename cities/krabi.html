Living in Krabi as a Digital Nomad

Experience limestone paradise, island hopping, rock climbing, and Andaman adventures. Expect $1,700 a month here. Nature and safety are the standout strengths, with cleanliness the main caveat.

Cost of Living in Krabi

Krabi sits in a mid-range tier among Southeast Asian nomad hubs. You can live here comfortably, but it is not as cheap as Chiang Mai or inland Thai cities, especially if you want to stay close to the beach in Ao Nang.

  • Rent (Krabi Town, 1 bedroom): 8,000-15,000 THB/month ($230-$430 USD). Further from the beach, quieter, but with better local food options.
  • Rent (Ao Nang, 1 bedroom): 15,000-25,000 THB/month ($430-$720 USD). Prices spike in high season (December-March).
  • Groceries (self-catering): 5,000-8,000 THB/month ($145-$230 USD) at Big C or local markets.
  • Eating out: 80-150 THB ($2.50-$4.50) for a local meal; 300-600 THB ($9-$17) at a mid-range restaurant.
  • Coworking hot desk: 300 THB/day ($9 USD); 6,000-8,000 THB/month ($175-$230 USD) for a membership.
  • Local transport: 50-100 THB/trip ($1.50-$3) by songthaew; 200-350 THB/day ($6-$10) for a rented scooter.
  • Mobile data (10 GB): around 200 THB/month ($6 USD). SIM cards from AIS or DTAC work well.

Monthly total range: Budget-focused nomads in Krabi Town spend roughly 35,000-50,000 THB/month ($1,000-$1,450 USD). A comfortable lifestyle near Ao Nang with regular dining out and coworking runs closer to 55,000-80,000 THB/month ($1,600-$2,300 USD).

Where to Work

Dedicated coworking is thin on the ground in Krabi compared to Chiang Mai or Bangkok. What exists is genuinely good, and the cafe-work culture fills most of the gap.

Dedicated coworking spaces: Phansa Space in Ao Nang is the most accessible dedicated option, offering ergonomic furniture, an outdoor terrace, and fast fibre internet. KLW Coworking Coliving Workspace in Mueang Krabi is highly regarded for clean, quiet space and reliable connectivity, open 24/7 with pool access on site. Pricing at both starts around 300 THB per day ($9 USD).

Laptop-friendly cafes: Cafe 8.98 in Ao Nang is the most popular cafe workspace, with 25 Mbps WiFi, plentiful outlets, and long opening hours. Sprucy Cafe in Krabi Town has a dedicated coworking area in the back, strong coffee, and air conditioning. Zoo Coffee in Krabi Town is quiet and cheap, ideal for morning work sessions before 4 PM when it closes.

The real WiFi situation: Mobile 4G/5G coverage from AIS or True is reliable throughout Krabi Town and Ao Nang, so a local SIM is your backup when cafe WiFi disappoints. Speeds at dedicated coworking spaces comfortably support video calls. Beach bungalows and guesthouses vary wildly: always check recent reviews before booking if connectivity matters to you. Do not rely on free city WiFi.

Getting Around

Krabi is not a walkable destination. Krabi Town and Ao Nang are about 18 km apart, and most attractions, beaches, and restaurants require a vehicle or paid transport.

Songthaews (shared pickup trucks): The main public transit option. The white songthaew runs between Krabi Town and Ao Nang for 50 THB ($1.50 USD), operating from around 6 AM to 9 PM. Useful and cheap, but you wait for them to fill up.

Scooter rental: The practical choice for most nomads. You can rent for 200-350 THB/day ($6-$10 USD) from many guesthouses and rental shops. Roads in town and out to viewpoints are manageable, but the coastal road to Ao Nang can be busy in high season.

Tuk-tuks and taxis: Available in Krabi Town and Ao Nang. Always negotiate the price before you get in. Grab (rideshare app) works in Krabi Town, though coverage is lighter than in Phuket or Bangkok.

Airport connections: Krabi International Airport (KBV) is about 15 km from Krabi Town. A shared blue songthaew from the airport to town costs around 90 THB ($2.50 USD). Private taxis run 350-500 THB ($10-$14 USD) to Ao Nang. Shared minivans are a middle-ground option with set prices posted at the arrivals area.

Islands and beaches: Reaching Railay, Koh Phi Phi, or the four islands requires a longtail boat or speedboat ferry. Factor in transfer time and boat fares if you plan to island-hop regularly.

Visas and Staying Long-Term

Thailand's visa situation changed significantly in 2024-2026 and you should verify current rules before travel.

Visa-free entry: Citizens of around 93 countries previously received 60-day visa-free entry. As of mid-2026, Thailand's cabinet approved reducing this back to 30 days, though the rollout timeline may still be in flux. Check the official Thai immigration site before you book.

Tourist visa extension: You can extend your stay once at the Krabi immigration office in Krabi Town for 1,900 THB ($55 USD), adding 30 days. This applies whether you entered visa-free or on a tourist visa.

Destination Thailand Visa (DTV): Launched in July 2024, the DTV is designed for digital nomads and remote workers. It requires proof of income or funds (500,000 THB equivalent, around $14,000 USD), offers 180 days per entry, and has 5-year validity. It is a genuinely useful option for long-term stays if you meet the income threshold.

Always confirm current rules at official-thai sources such as thai.e-visa.go.th or immigration.go.th before booking travel.

Best Time to Visit

Krabi has a pronounced seasonal split driven by the southwest monsoon, and the timing matters a lot for remote workers who care about boat access, beach weather, and reliable power.

Peak season (November-March): This is the best window. Skies are clear, humidity is manageable, seas are calm, and longtail boats run reliably to all beaches. Temperatures sit around 25-32C (77-90F). Expect higher accommodation prices and more tourists, particularly in December and January.

Shoulder season (April-May): Still largely dry and hot (up to 35C/95F), with falling prices and thinner crowds. A solid choice for budget-conscious nomads who do not mind heat.

Wet season (June-October): The southwest monsoon brings heavy, frequent rain, choppy seas, and occasional boat cancellations to Railay and islands. Working from a well-equipped indoor coworking space is fine, but spontaneous beach days become unreliable. Prices drop 30-40% on accommodation, and you will have the place largely to yourself. September and October are the wettest months.

For remote work combined with beach access, November through February is the sweet spot.

Pros and Cons

  • Stunning natural environment: Limestone karsts, clear water, and some of Thailand's best beaches within easy reach
  • Lower cost than Phuket with a similar Andaman coastline
  • Good mobile data coverage from AIS and True across the main areas
  • Relaxed, unhurried pace compared to Bangkok or Phuket
  • Excellent local food scene in Krabi Town, with cheap southern Thai cooking
  • Direct international flights to Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, and several European cities in season
  • Very few dedicated coworking spaces; you will mostly work from cafes or your accommodation
  • No walkability between key areas; you need a scooter or paid transport constantly
  • Wet season can be disruptive: frequent power cuts and boat cancellations from June-October
  • Tourist infrastructure in Ao Nang can feel generic and overpriced compared to Krabi Town
  • Nomad community is small; harder to find coworking peers than in Chiang Mai or Bangkok
  • Short-stay tourist vibe makes settling into a routine feel less natural than in dedicated nomad hubs

Who Krabi Is Best For

Krabi works well for remote workers who can operate independently, do not need a strong coworking community, and want nature access built into their daily life. If your idea of a productive day is coffee in a quiet local cafe in the morning, a swim or kayak in the afternoon, and a cheap Thai dinner in town at night, Krabi delivers that better than almost anywhere in the region.

It suits nomads on flexible schedules who plan to stay through the dry season (November-March) and are comfortable on a scooter. It also works for people doing short working holidays who want to pair serious output weeks with weekend island trips.

Skip Krabi if you need a dense coworking scene for in-person collaboration, rely on daily video calls that demand consistently fast and stable fiber connections, or want to walk everywhere without renting a vehicle. It is also a poor fit if you are visiting June through October and expect beach access to be part of your routine. Longer-term nomads often use Krabi as a seasonal base alongside Chiang Mai in the north rather than as a year-round primary location.

Frequently Asked Questions About Krabi

How much does it cost to live in Krabi as a digital nomad?

Budget roughly $1,700 a month in Krabi for a furnished place, food, coworking, and transport. The most popular central areas cost more; suburbs and local spots cost noticeably less.

Is the internet fast enough for remote work in Krabi?

Krabi rates 5 out of 10 for WiFi in our scoring, so it handles video calls and daily remote work workable. Carry a local SIM with data as a backup for cafes and travel days.

Is Krabi safe for digital nomads?

Our safety score for Krabi is 8/10, excellent for nomads. As anywhere, watch your belongings, favor busier streets after dark, and research neighborhoods before you book.

Is Krabi a good city for digital nomads overall?

Krabi stands out for nature and safety among remote workers. With a typical budget near $1,700 a month and a growing nomad scene, it suits anyone whose priorities line up with those strengths.
